# Your Boss Is Probably Faking It

*Part I — Wake the F\* Up*

There's a rule that runs every office on earth, and nobody will say it out loud, so I will:

**People get promoted until they reach a job they're bad at — and then they stop.** They don't get promoted *out* of the job they're bad at, because, well, they're bad at it. So they stay. Forever. Marinating.

Which means the person sitting one level above you — the one who approves your time off, "gives you feedback," and gets visibly stressed by their own calendar — is, statistically, *at the exact job they are worst at.* That's not a tragedy.

That's the best news of your entire career.

## Let it sink in

You've been walking around assuming the org chart is a ranking of competence. Top = smart, bottom = still learning. That's the story they need you to believe, because the story is doing a *lot* of unpaid work for them.

Here's the actual situation: your boss was probably good at a completely different job — the one they had before this one — and then someone handed them people to manage and a problem to solve, and it turns out those are unrelated skills. They are not a genius gatekeeper standing between you and success. They are a person doing an improv scene called "In Charge," and they are hoping nobody asks a follow-up question.

The gap between their title and their actual ability? That gap is not your enemy.

**That gap is the whole opportunity. It's where you're going to live.**

## How to spot a faker (it's not subtle)

Fakers can't solve real problems, so they perform *around* them. Two tells:

**They love Things.** A Thing is anything tangible they can point at instead of thinking: a new dashboard, a reorg, a Slack channel called `#alignment`, a 40-slide deck, an offsite. Things feel like progress. Real problems — "our team doesn't trust each other," "nobody knows what we're actually building" — are *intangible*, and fakers are allergic to intangible. So they buy a Thing and hope the Thing does the thinking. Watch how fast a faker reaches for a tool when the honest move is a hard conversation.

**They love Hope.** Listen for it. *"I was hoping this would be further along."* *"I'm hoping the new process fixes it."* Hope is what people do instead of managing. A real boss makes you *more* able to do the job — clearer, better-equipped, unblocked. A faker just… hopes at you, from a distance, and then acts betrayed when hoping doesn't ship the project.

Once you can see Things and Hope, you can't un-see them. You'll clock it in the first ten minutes of any meeting. It's a genuinely fun party trick and it will make you insufferable at brunch. Worth it.

## The move: walk into the vacuum

Here's the part that separates you from the 90% who just complain about this in the group chat.

Competence, in a room full of fakers, is a **vacuum.** The problems your boss is performing-around are just… sitting there. Unclaimed. Nobody in the room *wants* the hard, undefined, intangible one, because it's scary and it might not work.

**Take it.** Not with a speech. With a question:

> "Quick gut-check before we build anything — what's the actual problem we're solving here?"

Watch the room. The fakers will get uncomfortable, because they don't know, and now everyone can see they don't know. And you? You just did the one thing your boss can't do, in public, in a way that looks helpful instead of aggressive. You didn't attack anyone. You just turned on a light. That's the entire game, and it's disguised as being a team player.

Do that once a week and within a year the higher-ups will start wondering why *your boss* isn't the one asking those questions.

## The point (and the vibe)

This isn't a revenge fantasy. Revenge is for people who are still intimidated — and you're done being intimidated. The person above you is a beatable level, not a supervillain. The bar is on the floor.

So stop looking *up* at them like they're weather you have to survive. Look *down* at the problem like it's a video game you just figured out. Then go take the wheel.

The final boss has a health bar. Aim for it.
