# Book 1 — Plot & Continuity Notes (for author decisions)

These are **structural** notes from the polish pass — the "organizing the plot"
help. None have been changed in the manuscript yet, because each is a story
decision that's yours to make. Each note has the problem, why it matters, and a
proposed minimal fix. Ranked by impact.

The good news first: the core mystery is sound, and the best beat in the book —
**Blazer learning Dr. Lee's scent off the peanut-butter note in Chapter 7, then
paying it off in Chapters 9 and 11** — is a genuinely well-built plant-and-payoff.
The notes below are about tightening the machinery around that.

---

## 1. The "Big Island trip" clock is set up but never pays off  ⚠️ highest impact

**Where:** Chapter 7 sets up a hard deadline — the family is leaving for the Big
Island "next weekend," and the twins' urgency is *"Once we leave, who knows what
could happen to the artifacts?"* That trip is positioned as the ticking clock.

**The problem:** the climax (Chapter 10) is triggered by a *different, smaller*
parent-absence — an ordinary afternoon "meeting with the client." The Big Island
weekend trip is never mentioned again. A reader who was told "the danger is when
they leave for the Big Island" waits for a payoff that never comes.

**Why it matters:** a planted deadline that doesn't fire is the #1 thing that
makes a plot feel loose. Every setup should pay off.

**Proposed fix (pick one):**
- **(A) Make the clock consistent — recommended.** Cut the Big Island trip and
  lean entirely on Dr. Lee's own deadline ("the shipping is arranged for tomorrow
  night"). That single clock is cleaner and already drives Chapters 8–10.
- **(B) Use the Big Island trip as the clock.** Move the climax so the twins have
  to solve it *before* the family departs for the Big Island — then the departure
  and Dr. Lee's shipping night line up as one deadline.

## 2. The ticking clock says "tonight," but the raid happens in daylight

**Where:** Chapters 9–10. The whole of Chapter 9 hammers *"Dr. Lee moves
everything tonight"* — but the kids raid the lava tube in the **afternoon**
(they're told to be "back by five o'clock").

**The problem:** it still works (they intercept *before* the night shipment), but
the prose keeps building night-time urgency and then resolves in daylight, which
slightly deflates the tension.

**Proposed fix:** add one line when they decide to go — e.g. *"If the shipment
leaves tonight, the artifacts have to be at the site right now — this is our only
window."* That reframes the daytime raid as beating the clock, not ignoring it.

## 3. Dr. Lee's key card is questioned but never explained

**Where:** Chapter 8 — Andrew literally asks, *"He has a key card? How did he get
that?"* It's never answered.

**Why it matters:** when a character asks the exact question the reader is
thinking, the reader remembers it and expects an answer.

**Proposed fix:** answer it in Mrs. Kalani's Chapter 11 explanation (she's already
the "here's how it all worked" character). One line — e.g. *"Dr. Lee used to
consult for the center years ago; his old access card was never deactivated."*
This also neatly explains how he knew the camera blind spots.

## 4. Make the petroglyph map a shared clue, not a coincidence

**Where:** the ancient petroglyphs lead the twins to the exact lava-tube chamber
where Dr. Lee has stashed the artifacts.

**The problem:** as written, it reads slightly like luck — the kids' ancient map
just happens to end where the villain is hiding.

**The fix (small, high-payoff):** make it explicit that **Dr. Lee decoded the
same petroglyphs** to find the sacred site — that's *why* he's there. The book
already hints at it (his notes about archaeological sites; his photos near the
lava tubes). One added line in Chapter 10 or 11 — e.g. Dr. Lee muttering that the
petroglyphs led him here too, or Mrs. Kalani noting it — turns a coincidence into
a satisfying "they were both following the same trail." This makes Amanda's whole
investigation feel *earned*.

## 5. (Minor) Two hiding places is a little tangled

**Where:** Chapter 11 reveals Dr. Lee moved artifacts first to a hidden room
*under the cultural center* (the spot Blazer found), then out to the lava-tube
site. Two stashes.

**Verdict:** not a hole — it's explained, and it pays off Blazer's discovery. Just
flagging that it's the most complex bit of the plot. Optional to simplify; safe to
keep.
