# Idea — Romance: City Quartets (the mega-ecosystem)

_Captured 2026-07-30 from author brainstorm sessions. Status: IDEA —
not yet a book directory. When greenlit, scaffold from
`studio/series-kit/` with these as the founding canon decisions._

## The architecture

- **Container = a city. Four books per city — one per season** (same
  town, one year: winter/spring/summer/fall). Market check: the
  seasonal quartet is prestigious-familiar (Ali Smith's Seasonal
  Quartet) and commercially proven in pieces (Hilderbrand's Winter
  Street unit, Colgan-style seasonal returns), but the strict
  same-town/one-year/one-couple-per-season romance quartet is
  unclaimed — brandable. Free mechanics: self-naming titles, box set
  per city, release calendar matched to real seasons, weather-beat
  rotation built into the spine.
- **The fractal bench rule (the author's engine).** Book scale: each
  book fields a rootable sub-character with a visible wound who
  becomes the next book's lead. City scale: the same rule one level
  up — a beloved secondary from City A RELOCATES and opens City B as
  its Book 1 lead. The hand-forward is a person, not a hint.
- **Romance inverts the millimeter rule.** Each couple completes
  identity→essence in ONE book (the HEA requires it); the CONTAINER
  arcs at series scale (the city's B-plot per quartet; the mega-arc
  across cities).

## The two threads across cities

1. **The matchmaker thread (the S01 transplant).** One deniable touch
   per book — the same figure in a different role each city (the
   photographer, the lawyer, the ticket seller) or a traveling object
   (ring, recipe card, postcard). RULES: budget exactly 1/book; never
   load-bearing (a reader who misses every touch loses nothing);
   reader pieces it together circa city 3. MEGA-FINALE, romance-
   native: the matchmaker's OWN love story, at a convergence event
   (a wedding reuniting every city's couples). The finale ADDS an
   HEA; it never withholds one — that wall makes the finale work in
   this genre.
2. **The small-talk thread (author, 2026-07-30): what everyone's
   watching.** Modern setting; "what are you watching?" is the
   default small talk, and characters bond, flirt, and confess over
   shows. Light theme, every book. Production rules: INVENT the
   shows (real titles date the book in two years and trip the
   fictionalize-brands gate) — a fictional prestige drama, a baking
   show, a dating show. Upgrade option: ONE fictional show franchise
   watched in every city, its in-world seasons progressing as the
   mega-series does — the shows-within-the-books become a second
   subtle connective tissue, and each book's featured show can rhyme
   with that book's trope (the fake-dating couple hooked on a dating
   show). [TK: decide whether the show thread stays texture or gets
   an S-thread ID and budget like the matchmaker.]

## Walls and warnings (genre edition)

- **Walls:** HEA/HFN every book; consent and agency; the heat band is
  locked series-wide and NEVER waivable; the couple earns the ending
  (a breakup caused by a missable phone call = solving a mystery by
  luck — the fair-play rule in genre costume); container canon
  (marriages, babies, geography).
- **Warnings (waivable per book):** cameo counts, gag touches, trope
  rotation cadence, seed timing, beat percentages.

## The ingredient checklist (drafted 2026-07-30, to be locked)

17 items in the lenses-not-scenes discipline — meet-collision, one
proximity device (logged, no back-to-back repeats), one primary trope
(rotation ledger), the rootable next lead, past-couples cameo (the
HEA holding), the communal set piece, competence-porn skill, weather
forces intimacy or truth, food scene, confidant sequels, the lie with
a felt cost, the gesture spends armor not money, an object/line echo,
running gags (book + series level), heat placement per the ladder, a
non-romantic B-plot the couple wins as a team, the epilogue
hand-forward. Full table in the session record; formalize into the
series' `02-story-engine` on greenlight.

## Economics rhyme

A quartet = a complete binge unit = the KU playbook's 4-book gate.
"No launch until City A is done"; each new city is a relaunch event
with a built-in read-through bridge (the relocating character). At
book-a-month: a city per four months, three cities a year, bench
refills every city — the format doesn't exhaust.
