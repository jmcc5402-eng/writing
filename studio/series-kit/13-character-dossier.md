# Template — The Character Dossier and the Rooting-For Ladder

_Author's ask, 2026-09-08: "I also like the idea of us doing a
character outline off-line before a book. That'll give me an
understanding of exactly who this character is where they've been and
where they're going. I love how our stories very slowly build up the
Romance arc, I think we need to do the same to make people want to
root for these characters and to do that we need a little more
details about who they are." Proven first on `books/campus-series/
book2/plots/dossier-aisha.md` and `dossier-dan.md`._

**What it is.** One document per lead, written BEFORE the book is
briefed (it joins the arc gate, PIPELINE §3b — the author approves it
with the arc docs), in three parts: WHERE THEY'VE BEEN (the
biography the page will draw on), WHO THEY ARE NOW (the daily person
the reader can like), and WHERE THEY'RE GOING (the arc doc's turns,
pointed to, not repeated). Then THE ROOTING-FOR LADDER: the details
and the small kindnesses, placed chapter by chapter, so the reader
learns who this person is as slowly and as surely as they watch the
romance climb. The arc doc says what the character DOES at each
turn; the dossier says who they ARE, and when the reader finds out.

**The rule it serves:** taste entry 15 — root for them before you
admire them. The calibration moment is a doctor dressing a Cub
Scout's blister at a bonfire: competent, kind, unrewarded, nobody
important watching, a few sentences.

**Canon first.** Every fact on a dossier is either on an accepted
page (cite `chNN:line`) or is NEW and marked so — the author strikes
new facts by comment; a bare merge accepts them. Nothing here is
invented silently. The name map (kit 03) and the furniture registry
stay the record of what has reached the page.

---

# <Lead> — the dossier

## 1. Where they've been

<The biography, in plain sentences, only as deep as the page will
ever need: where they grew up and what it was like; family (or its
absence, and why); school and training; the jobs and the towns, and
why each ended; the one friendship that lasted; money (what they
owe, what they earn, what they spend on); the wound's origin as an
EVENT with a place and a year; what they were like at twenty-five.
Mark each fact CANON (`chNN:line`) or NEW.

**The leads' history with each other before page one.** Name the
two or three times they bumped into each other before the book
starts — accidental, not the job: a stool, a dawn, a door held — and
where the first act SEEDS them (chapter and line). A reader who
watches two people get together needs to know how they already know
each other; a professional acquaintance on a sheet is not that.
Author, 2026-09-12, on 1.2 ch 17: "we need to seed more that they
bumped into each other earlier in the book or before the book
timeline. somehow that is part of how they already know each other."
Missing here = a finding at the arc gate.>

## 2. Who they are now

<The daily person — the reader's beer test. What they eat and where;
what they drive and how they keep it; what they wear off duty; their
phone habits; what they are good at that nobody asked for (the
blister); what they do when nobody is looking; their small pleasures
and their one vice; what they are bad at; what makes them laugh; how
they treat waiters, kids, dogs, the elderly, people who cannot help
them; the thing they would never say out loud that the reader will
know by chapter N. Mark CANON or NEW.>

## 3. Where they're going

<One paragraph pointing at the arc doc (kit 11): the wound, the false
belief, the turns by chapter, the moment of change. Do not repeat
the table; say what the dossier's details are FOR — which of them
the change will pay off (the coat she tries to return is the woman
who never unpacks; the board he reads is the man the town decided).>

## 4. The rooting-for ladder

<A chapter-by-chapter table, like the romance ladder: which detail
of who they are the reader learns in that chapter, and the small
kindness (for someone else, at a cost, unrewarded) that chapter
carries — or NONE, with the reason. The ladder climbs: early rungs
are competence and courtesy; middle rungs are private generosity
the other lead does not see; late rungs are the costly ones. Each
rung is a few sentences on the page, never a speech, never thanked
twice. The card's ROOTING FOR line and the brief's ROOTING FOR line
read from this table. The panel's beer test checks it.>

| Ch | What the reader learns about them | The kindness (for whom, at what cost, who sees) | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | | | on the page `ch01:line` / OWED / NONE (why) |
| … | | | |

## 5. Walls

<What the dossier must never let the page do: contradict the wound
(she still asks for nothing; he still reads the board); make the
kindness a speech; let anyone important watch; spend a ration (an
anchor, a chorus construction); tell the reader the character is
kind instead of showing a kindness.>

---

**Downstream.** The dossier is CANON on merge. The showrunner writes
the card's ROOTING FOR line from its ladder; the brief carries the
rung; the drafter puts a few sentences on the page; the panel's beer
test judges it; the fold marks the rung "on the page" with its cite.
The instrument-auditor's four-chapter run checks the ladder against
the pages the way the build check checks the romance.
