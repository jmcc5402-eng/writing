## 0. THE ANSWER, FIRST

**There is a plausible financial case, but not for four books.** The
quartet as a standalone project is, at median, a break-even-to-modest-
loss hobby with a nice cover. The financial case appears at roughly
book eight and becomes real at twelve — and the sweet shelf is one of
the few romance branches where a twelve-book backlist reliably pays,
because the readers are loyal, the competition is thinner, and
customer acquisition is cheaper than on the steamy shelf.

So the honest question is not "will the quartet make money." It is:
**is he prepared to write twelve to sixteen books in this world over
four to six years?** If the answer is yes, this is a credible
part-time second income with a long runway. If the answer is "four and
see," the correct expectation is: a few hundred to a few thousand
dollars gross, against $6–13k of costs, and he should do it because he
wants a book on the shelf, not because of the money.

One thing changes that math more than anything on the marketing side:
**the romance-delivery gap the two instruments found on 2026-09-14.**
Fixing it is cheap, is mostly free, and multiplies every number in
section 6. It goes first.

## 1. THE SHELF — where this book physically lands

### The three slots

Amazon allows **three categories per format** (down from ten; the
BISAC picker and the email-for-more pathway were both retired in
mid-2023), and **there is no limit on how often you change them**
[VERIFIED — KDP help + 2026 category guides]. Three slots, freely
re-testable, is the whole discovery surface.

The shelves that exist and fit:

| Slot | Category | Fit |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Romance › **Clean & Wholesome** | Exact. Amazon created it in 2016; it is the branch's home. |
| 2 | Romance › **Small Town** | Exact. Its own browse node and bestseller list. |
| 3 | Romance › **Later in Life** | Partial — see the warning below. |

All three are live Amazon browse nodes with their own bestseller and
new-release lists [VERIFIED — Amazon best-seller and new-release
pages for Clean & Wholesome Romance, Small Town Romance, and Later in
Life Romance; node IDs 11650867011, 120220981011, 17745834011].

**The Later in Life warning.** That shelf's occupants skew
substantially older than 43/47. The category's exemplar indie title —
Leeanna Morgan's *Bookstore on the Bay: A Sweet, Later-in-Life, Small
Town Romance* — has a **sixty-five-year-old widowed heroine**
[VERIFIED — Amazon/Google Play listing copy]. Shelving a 43/47 book
there is worth testing (it costs nothing and is reversible), but
expect some "these aren't later-in-life" friction in reviews. At 43
and 47 the book has **no dedicated browse node of its own**. It falls
in the taxonomy gap between New Adult and Later in Life. That is a
discovery fact with consequences in section 5.

### How competitive, versus steamy small-town

- Romance overall is the **#1 Kindle category by sales volume**; the
  top-100 romance titles averaged an estimated **~646 sales/day each**
  in April 2026 [VERIFIED as reported — K-lytics, vendor data, read
  via search summary; [CHECK] the figure and its basis].
- Kindle carries **1,000,000+ English romance titles** [VERIFIED —
  K-lytics]. Clean & Wholesome is a fraction of that, and the analyst
  convention names sub-niches under 2,000 titles "hot cells" and
  2,000–10,000 "hot niches" [VERIFIED — K-lytics framing].
- Clean & Wholesome ebook sales have **regrown 30%+ since the 2023–24
  Amazon category reset** [VERIFIED as reported — K-lytics; vendor
  source, [CHECK]]. Contemporary clean is **more than half** the
  clean/sweet market's value.

**Is lower competition a real, usable advantage for an unknown author?
Yes for cost, no for ceiling. Both halves matter.**

- **Real advantage:** a category bestseller badge is genuinely
  reachable on Clean & Wholesome at sales volumes that would be
  invisible in Contemporary Romance; Amazon and Facebook ad
  auctions are cheaper where fewer six-figure authors are bidding
  (book CPCs generally run $0.15–0.45, with competitive romance
  reaching $0.60+ [VERIFIED — 2026 ad benchmark guides]); and
  sweet readers filter *by* door, so a correctly labelled book
  reaches a pre-qualified buyer with no persuasion cost.
- **Smaller pond:** the ceiling per title is lower, and it is lower
  by a lot. The steamy shelf's chart velocity is not available here
  at any price.

**Net:** the sweet shelf lowers customer acquisition cost and raises
the probability of *some* traction. It does not raise the size of the
prize. For an unknown author with no list, lower CAC is worth more
than a higher ceiling he cannot reach anyway — so this is the right
shelf for this author, on the shelf's own terms.

### The three realities he asked me to separate

**(a) Unknown debut, first 12 months, four books on a 4–6 week

rather than against him: the same ad dollar now monetizes across eight

from four back-of-book calls-to-action becomes a launch asset.
- **Median: $2,000–9,000 gross for the year.**
- **Good: $10,000–25,000 gross for the year.**
- The jump between year one and year two is the whole argument for

**(c) The top of the branch.** USA Today-listing indie sweet authors

## 3. KINDLE UNLIMITED — does the sweet branch monetize the way steamy
## does?

**Short answer: yes, but with a different reader and one real caveat
the steamy branch doesn't have.**

**The evidence that it does:**
- Elana Johnson is a **Top 20 KU All-Star** writing clean and
  wholesome romance [VERIFIED — author site]. All-Star status is
  driven by page reads. Clean romance demonstrably generates KU
  volume at the top of the branch.
- Gussman, Isaacson and most modern indie sweet-romance catalogs sit
  in KU [VERIFIED — Amazon listings]; Amazon maintains a
  KU-eligible filter on both Clean & Wholesome and Later in Life,
  which only exists because the inventory is there.
- Romance and fantasy authors commonly report 60–70% of income from
  KU page reads, and leaving KU in those genres usually means a
  substantial drop [VERIFIED as reported — 2026 wide-vs-Select
  analyses; forum-sourced, so directional].

**The caveat, specific to this shelf.** The clean/sweet buyer skews
older than the KU-native binge reader — one third of romance readers
are over 45 (RWA 2017; **stale, nine years old, flag it**), and
roughly 40% of romance readers are 35–54 [VERIFIED as reported by
2026 aggregators; [CHECK] the underlying survey]. Older readers are
more likely to *buy* and more likely to read on non-Amazon devices,
and the clean branch has a real wide and library market that dark
romance does not. The shelf shows it: **Leeanna Morgan, a USA Today
sweet small-town author, is wide** — her new series is listed on
Google Play, Apple Books, Kobo/Indigo and B&N [VERIFIED — retailer
listings].

**Recommendation: KU-exclusive for the quartet's launch, revisit at
book five.** Reasons: a series monetizes one discovery event across
four titles and KU is where that compounding happens; the promo tools
(Countdown, Free Days) are the cheap launch levers an unplatformed
author needs; and **the commitment is 90 days, renewing — it is the
most reversible major decision in the plan.** Print is never exclusive
and goes everywhere regardless. Re-check before enrolling whether KU's
share of the romance Top 100 has kept drifting down (it fell through
2025; the 2026-09-03 doc already flags this as the one fact that
could flip the call).

## 5. THE DIFFERENTIATOR — 43 and 47 in a college football town

**This is the question that matters most, so here is the verdict
before the reasoning: the gap is real, demand exists, and the
differentiator is worth much more in retention than in discovery. It
will earn him loyal readers and good reviews. It will not, by itself,
get the book found.**

**Evidence the demand is real:**
- Amazon maintains a **Later in Life Romance** browse node with its
  own bestseller and new-release lists [VERIFIED]. Taxonomies follow
  money.
- A USA Today indie author spends title real estate on the phrase
  (§4, finding 1).
- One third of romance readers are over 45 (RWA 2017 — **stale**);
  ~40% are 35–54 [VERIFIED as reported, [CHECK]].
- Trade coverage describes a "wave of self- and traditionally
  published authors" embracing second- and third-act romance, and
  Entangled launched its **August** imprint for Gen-X readers with
  leads in their 40s [VERIFIED — Next Avenue, Women Writers Women's
  Books].
- Nan Reinhardt has an entire career on "grown-up love stories"
  [VERIFIED — author site].

**Evidence the gap is partly a gatekeeper artifact, which favours
indie:** when Reinhardt's agent pitched her first novel in 2019,
editors at several houses **asked her to knock two decades off the
leads' ages** [VERIFIED — Next Avenue reporting]. That is the shape of
a market that publishers under-serve rather than one readers reject.
Indie is the correct route to exploit exactly that kind of gap.

**Evidence to be honest about on the other side:**
- The subcategory is described by its own advocates as **"still
  relatively small"** with "an active community of enthusiasts"
  [VERIFIED — Next Avenue]. Enthusiast-scale, not romantasy-scale.
- **43/47 is in the taxonomy's blind spot.** The Later in Life shelf
  is populated by 55–70-year-old and widowed leads. There is no
  browse node for forty-something leads. A differentiator that
  doesn't map to a category slot or a high-volume search term cannot
  do discovery work.
- **The football town cuts both ways.** Sports romance is one of the
  fastest-growing romance subjects, with triple-digit print growth
  [VERIFIED — Circana 2025]. But that growth is athlete-hero, high
  heat, and mostly twenty-something. A closed-door book with a coach
  and a team physician in their forties is **not sports romance**; it
  is small-town romance with football furniture. The superfan report
  reached the same conclusion from the review side: "If the first
  line of the blurb has a quarterback in it, the wrong reader buys it
  and the right reader doesn't."

**What this means operationally.** Sell the book on **sweet +
small-town + second-chance + the town** — that is what has search
volume and category slots. Deploy **the ages and the football town as
the reasons to *choose this one*** — in the blurb's second paragraph,
in the keywords, on the series page, and in reviews. Age is the
retention asset, the review-generator and the word-of-mouth line
("finally, people my age"). It is not the acquisition hook. Any plan
that leads with the ages is spending its scarcest asset — the first
line of the blurb — on a differentiator with no traffic behind it.

## 6. THE VERDICT, AND WHAT WOULD HAVE TO BE TRUE

### The 24-month range for four books from a standing start

**[ESTIMATE] $1,000 – $12,000 gross over 24 months, with the median
near the bottom of that range (~$2,500–4,000), against $5,200–15,250
of cost.**

Assumptions, visible:
- Four books shipped on a 4–6 week ladder inside 24 months, all
  KU-exclusive, Book 1 at 99¢ or free after the launch window,
  Books 2–4 at $4.99.
- Genre-correct illustrated cover set, human proofread, front and
  back matter with a working series call-to-action.
- $150–400/month of ads from Book 2 onward.
- Read-through at the middle of the self-reported benchmark band.
- No platform, no list at launch, no virality.
- **The romance-delivery gap is fixed before launch.** If it is not,
  cut the whole range by roughly 40% — see below.

### The three things that most change the outcome

**1. Fixing the romance-delivery gap. The single largest financial
lever in this report, and it is nearly free.** Two blind instruments
independently found that the books under-deliver against what they
will be sold as: Book 1.2 demoted to **"borrow-worthy"** at **3.2
stars on romance delivery** against 4.6 on craft, with the reviewer
declining to preorder book three. Translated into money:
- **Read-through is the quartet's entire economic engine.** "I'd
  one-click book three, but I want to see the blurb first" is a
  read-through downgrade. Moving B2→B3 read-through from ~40% to
  ~60% roughly **doubles the value of every acquired reader**
  [ESTIMATE, using the study's own read-through bands].
- **Star average gates promotion.** Ad conversion, the also-bought
  algorithm, and BookBub Featured Deal acceptance all key off
  ratings. A 3.2-star series is a materially harder sell to a promo
  list than a 4.4-star one [ESTIMATE — BookBub publishes no
  threshold; [CHECK]].
- The survey's "highest-leverage ten" is **mostly FREE work**: ~40
  body sentences, chapter endings re-ordered, one awkward beat per
  book, four sentences of approach before the 1.2 ch 14 forearm, and
  a Kindle sample extended to 1.1 ch 5. **No ad budget buys what
  those forty sentences buy.**

**2. Whether books 5–12 get written.** The shelf's own winners have
50–130 titles. Four books is the cost of entry, not the business.
Everything about the year-two number depends on the quartet being the
first rung rather than the whole ladder.

**3. Cover and category execution at thumbnail size.** On a shelf
where the buyer filters by door before she filters by anything else,
a cover that reads "steamy" or "young" mis-sells the product and earns
the false-advertising one-stars the 2026-08-01 study warns about.
Commission the four as a set; test the thumbnails against the live
Clean & Wholesome and Small Town bestseller grids before paying the
balance.

### Trad versus self, for this book

**Recommendation: self-publish the quartet, and submit one
standalone-shaped manuscript to Harlequin Heartwarming in parallel if
he wants the validation and the editorial experience.**

- **Self, honestly:** control, speed, all rights, the quartet ships
  when he says so, and the ladder-and-KU mechanics that make a series
  pay are available immediately. Cost: he funds and runs a small
  publishing operation, and the median outcome is section 2(a).
- **Trad-lite (Heartwarming), honestly:** open to unagented
  submissions, the product spec matches almost exactly, an advance in
  the low four figures [CHECK], real editorial, and print
  distribution into channels he cannot rent. Cost: he loses the
  series as *his* asset, the timeline runs to years, category romance
  books have short shelf lives, and the leads' ages and the
  invented-college worldbuilding may both get edited toward the line's
  house style. Tule, the better-fitting house, is closed until 2028.
- **Reversibility is the deciding argument, and it points the same
  way it did for MYBYB:** self-publishing the quartet does not
  foreclose a future Heartwarming or Tule submission with a
  *different* manuscript, and the 2025 self-pub-to-trad pipeline was
  ~80% romance with demonstrated sales — this is the one genre where
  indie numbers are a pitch. Submitting first costs 6–18 months of
  waiting during which nothing ships.

## 7. THE ALTERNATIVE, PRICED — what the steamy branch would pay

**[ESTIMATE] Roughly 2–4x the sweet outcome at equal catalog depth
and equal effort, with materially higher competition and acquisition
cost.** The reasoning: the Kindle romance money list skews clearly
steamy; ~44% of $10k+/month indie authors write romance concentrated
in high-steam niches; dark romance, romantasy and sports romance are
the triple-digit-growth categories in Circana's 2025 print data; and
BookTok — the discovery engine an unplatformed author most needs —
overwhelmingly amplifies open-door titles [VERIFIED as reported —
WWM 2025, Circana 2025]. Against that: the steamy shelf is where every
well-capitalised indie is bidding, CACs are higher, the reader churns
faster, and it would mean abandoning 131,000 finished words and
writing a book this author has said he does not want to write. **The
trade is real and it is not close on raw ceiling — but a higher
ceiling in a market he can't afford to buy into is not worth more than
a lower ceiling in one where his dollar goes further.** Not
recommended as a pivot; recorded so he can see the price of the lane
he chose.

## 8. SEQUENCED RECOMMENDATION

**Principle: fix the product before buying traffic for it; make the
two blocking decisions that cost nothing; then ship four and decide at
a gate with a number on it.**

### The first three concrete moves

**1. Run the romance-delivery fix pass.** Book 1.2 ch 1–13 and Book
1.1 ch 1–15, working the survey's highest-leverage ten. Roughly 40–80
inserted sentences, no structural change, no canon touched.
- **Cost: ~10–18 hours of author and agent time.**
- **Displaces: ~2 weeks of Book 1.3 drafting.**
- **Why first:** it is the only move in this report that raises
  revenue without spending money, and every later move is worth more
  after it.

**2. Lock the pen name and the series title; open the KDP account and
do the tax interview.**
- **Cost: ~3 hours plus a few days of bureaucratic latency.**
- **Displaces: nothing.**
- **Why:** the cover brief, the blurb, Author Central and every
  listing queue behind these, and they are decisions, not work. The
  2026-09-03 doc has flagged both as blocking-and-cheap for twelve
  days.

**3. Commission the four-book cover set, gated on a storefront test.**
Brief illustrated, warm, adult-rendered, town-and-stadium iconography,
series branding legible at thumbnail. Before paying the balance, put
the thumbnails in a grid beside the live Clean & Wholesome and Small
Town bestseller lists and ask whether it reads as the same shelf.
- **Cost: $600–3,200, plus ~4 hours of briefing and review.**

### Then, in order

4. Finish Books 1.3 and 1.4 to the fixed romance standard (the
   density survey's findings become drafting inputs, not later
   repairs).
5. Human proofread on all four.
6. Front and back matter, with the series call-to-action and the
   newsletter signup live before Book 1 ships.
7. Launch on a 4–6 week ladder, KU-exclusive, Book 1 as the funnel.
8. Ads from Book 2 onward; promo-site stacking on Book 1 discounts;
   BookBub Featured Deal applications once Book 1 has review volume.

**Steady-state marketing ask once live: 3–5 hrs/week.** That is time
taken directly from drafting books 5–8, and it is the real cost of
the machine.

### The decision gate, with a number

**Six months after Book 4 ships: if the series is not grossing
$300+/month, the twelve-book path will not pay at this author's
production rate, and the honest choice is to keep writing it because
