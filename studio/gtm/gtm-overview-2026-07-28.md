### Audit: the existing Spytwins roadmap (`roadmap/go-to-market.md`)

The roadmap's instincts are right — smallest increment first, beta
readers as cheapest signal, formats stack, let readers vote. Four
findings against 2026 reality:

1. **Stage 1's self-publish option is a broken market test.** The
   roadmap treats "publish Book 1 on KDP and let sales + reviews be the
   market test" as a valid probe. In middle grade it isn't: discovery
   runs through adult gatekeepers, not storefront browsing (see §2), so
   a quiet KDP launch measures the absence of a marketing machine, not
   the quality of the book. A failed test that can't distinguish "bad
   book" from "invisible book" is not a test. It also spends the debut
   card: agents rarely take on an already-self-published book unless it
   posts thousands of sales [ESTIMATE — long-standing industry
   consensus; base it on the fact that the 2025 self-pub-to-trad deal
   wave was ~80% romance/romantasy with proven sales, PW 2025].
2. **Stage 2 is directionally right but unpriced.** MG graphic novels
   really are the category's bright spot — roughly a quarter of MG
   sales and rising [VERIFIED — PW/SLJ reporting, 2024-2025]. But the
   roadmap prices "commission sample art" as an increment without
   noting that a full MG graphic novel is a five-figure illustration
   commitment, and that color print-on-demand costs can exceed $12/unit
   [VERIFIED — KDP cost structure, 2026], which kills self-pub margins.
   Stage 2 is only real as a *traditionally sold* format or with
   capital.
3. **Stage 3 (webcomic to build a kid fanbase) is COPPA fantasy as
   written.** Platform minimum age is 13; 8-12s are not legally
   addressable there [VERIFIED — COPPA, platform terms]. A webcomic
   builds an *adult* comics audience, which is a different asset than
   "Spytwins readers." Flagged per this agent's remit.
4. **Stage 4 (screen) is fine as a horizon and should generate zero
   hours in the next 24 months.**

Verdict: keep the staircase, replace Stage 1's fork with the trad-first
sequence in §3, and re-cost Stages 2-3 when Stage 1 produces signal.

## 2. CHANNEL ANALYSIS

Each channel, per book, never averaged. Contrarian comps per variance
card A4: for each recommendation, the comp that argues the opposite,

### 2a. Amazon KDP — ebook + Kindle Unlimited

**The verified economics (all KDP official or trade-tracked, 2026):**
- Ebook royalty: 70% at $2.99-$9.99 list (minus ~$0.15/MB delivery),
  35% outside that band. [VERIFIED — KDP help pages]
- KU pays per page read: ~$0.0045/page average across 2025, $0.00482 in
  April 2026. A 300-page book read cover to cover earns ~$1.45.
  [VERIFIED — KDP fund trackers, 2026]
- Median self-published author income: ~$13,500/yr in the 2025 ALLi
  survey — but ~75% of self-published authors earn under $1,000/yr.
  The median is carried by authors with deep backlists: 25+ titles
  correlates with ~$3,000/month median. Self-pub income is a *catalog*
  business. [VERIFIED — ALLi 2025 survey reporting; indie income
  aggregators, 2026]
- What sells self-pub: romance + fantasy = ~48% of self-pub ebook
  sales; fantasy readers are the most indie-friendly audience.
  [VERIFIED — PublishDrive, 2026]

**Spytwins: NO as primary channel.** The 8-12 reader does not browse a
Kindle store, hold a KU subscription, or click ads; the buyer is a
gatekept adult. Industry advice is blunt: don't self-publish MG or
chapter books without a built-in, underserved audience [VERIFIED —
Brent Hartinger via Jane Friedman's blog; note the piece is a few years
old, but every 2024-2026 signal (B&N shrinking MG shelf space,
librarians ignoring cold pitches) points the same direction].
- *Contrarian comp (argues it SHOULD work):* the backlist math above —
  a planned 40-book series is exactly the 25+-title shape that pays
  $3k/month medians, and the story-engine design supports rapid
  release. *Answer:* that math is built on adult genre readers who buy
  their own books inside Amazon's flywheel (KU, also-boughts, ads). MG
  buyers are outside it — page-read income requires adult heavy
  readers, and ads can't target children. Reaching the 25-book
  threshold in MG means roughly a decade of writing before the
  mechanism that pays adult indies even applies. The comp's engine is
  real; this category can't run it.

**MYBYB: YES — primary channel.** The concept doc already defines an
Amazon flywheel: edgy title acquires, substance retains, $9.99-$14.99
gift pricing, a 22-30 phone-native buyer. That is a KDP-shaped book.
One economic caveat: at ~15k words the book is maybe 80-120 KENP pages,
so a full KU read-through earns roughly $0.40-0.60 [ESTIMATE — word
count from BACKLOG length target x verified per-page rate]. KU is
near-worthless for a short gift book; price for 70% sales (ebook
$4.99-6.99 as the sampler, paperback as the real product) and decide
KU enrollment on data, not default.
- *Contrarian comp:* *Go the F\*ck to Sleep* — the defining edgy gift
  book — was traditionally published (Akashic, 2011) and exploded via
  retail and a pre-pub viral leak; gift books are checkout-counter
  impulse products, and KDP gets you no checkout counter. *Answer:*
  conceded for the *illustrated retail edition* — that is a later,
  trad/hybrid play (§3). But the text-first edition's buyer, per
  CONCEPT.md, shops gift guides and "funny gift for coworker" searches
  online, where the profane-self-help lane was proven at scale by
  *The Subtle Art* (2016) — a lane this manuscript predates (2014,
  per POSITIONING.md). KDP-first risks no shelf; trad-first risks two
  years and a likely pass on an unplatformed debut. Only one of those
  is reversible.

**Young Nicholas: NOT DECIDABLE YET; leaning no-as-primary.** No ending
exists; no channel decision is actionable (§3). Noting for later: it is
adventure-fantasy-adjacent, the most indie-friendly macro-genre
[VERIFIED — PublishDrive, 2026], but it is a seasonal all-ages myth,
not romantasy, and Christmas books are disproportionately physical
gifts bought at retail [ESTIMATE — from the gift-purchase pattern of
the comp shelf: Klaus, Haig].

### 2b. Print (POD paperback / hardcover / retail)

- KDP paperback: 60% of list minus print cost (40% via expanded
  distribution); B&W print costs run a few dollars a unit; color can
  exceed $12. [VERIFIED — KDP, 2026]
- Self-pub print's ceiling: POD reaches Amazon easily, bookstores and
  schools barely. Indie-to-bookstore placement is store-by-store
  handselling [VERIFIED — bookstore-placement guides, 2024].

**Spytwins:** print matters (MG is a print category; the slump data is
*print* data) — which is precisely the trad argument, since school/
library/bookstore hardcover distribution is what a publisher owns and
an indie can't rent.
**MYBYB:** paperback IS the product (gifts are physical). At $12.99
list, ~120-page B&W print cost ≈ $2.50-3.00 → ~$4.80-5.30/unit royalty
[ESTIMATE — KDP calculator ranges x verified 60% formula]. Healthy.
The illustrated edition breaks this math (color cost); that edition is
a trad pitch, not a KDP upload.
**Young Nicholas:** deferred with the book.

### 2c. Audio

- Production: $150-400+ per finished hour narration, ~+$100 PFH for
  editing/mastering; ACX royalties 40% exclusive / 25% non-exclusive;
  a typical indie production needs ~1,200+ unit sales to break even;
  Audible holds ~63% of the US market and moved all new titles to a
  new royalty model in May 2026. [VERIFIED — ScribeCount and
  production-cost guides, 2025-2026]

**All three books: NOT NOW.** Audio is a backlist amplifier for books
already selling. Spytwins audio (family/library listening) becomes
interesting only with a publisher or proven sales; MYBYB's runtime
(~1.5-2 hrs) would be cheap (~$500-800 [ESTIMATE — verified PFH rates x
runtime]) but gift buyers don't gift audio files.
- *Contrarian comp:* audio is publishing's growth segment year after
  year, and waiting means missing it. *Answer:* growth accrues to
  titles with discovery already solved; a $1,500 production against
  zero audience is the worst-sequenced dollar this portfolio could
  spend. Revisit per book at 1,000 copies sold or a trad deal.

### 2d. YouTube channel

The author named this candidate directly, so bluntly: **NO for all
three books, as a channel build.** The observable "AuthorTube" successes
are publishing-advice channels (Dale Roberts 100k+, Kindlepreneur 130k+
subscribers) monetizing courses and services to *writers* — not fiction
authors moving fiction to *readers* [VERIFIED — the channels; the
inference that fiction channels rarely convert is ESTIMATE, but no
counterexample surfaced in research]. A channel is a second unpaid job
(5-10 hrs/wk for years) that displaces the portfolio's actual
bottleneck: unfinished manuscripts.
- **Spytwins specifically:** a channel cannot address 8-12s (COPPA;
  platform age minimums; YouTube kids-content ad restrictions)
  [VERIFIED — COPPA/platform policy]. A teacher/librarian-facing
  channel is a slow institutional-trust play, not a sales channel.
- **MYBYB is the one partial exception** — as *short-form clips*
  (Shorts/Reels/TikTok skits of the frameworks: the Donut Move, the
  9:47pm Slack message), made as launch ad-creative on 2-3 hrs/wk, not
  as a subscriber build. Success metric: email signups and sales, not
  subscribers.
- *Contrarian comp (argues a channel SHOULD work):* authors who did
  build audience-first empires (the self-help/creator lane —
  platform-first authors whose books launch to a warm list). *Answer:*
  those authors built platform for years *before* the book, in
  audience-first niches; adopting that path here means delaying all
  three books behind a media startup with its own dismal base rates.
  The portfolio's scarce input is writing hours; the books are closer
  to done than any channel is to 10k subscribers.

## 3. TRAD VS SELF — THE FORK, PER BOOK

Three books, three different answers. That is expected, not
indecision.

### Spytwins — QUERY TRADITIONAL FIRST

- **Why:** every distribution asset MG needs (school/library hardcover,
  book fairs, bookstore shelf, institutional review coverage) is
  publisher-owned; self-pub MG is the acknowledged worst category for
  indies (§2a). The series engine — repeatable formula plus serialized
  secret — is the sales shape publishers still buy in a down market.
- **Base rate, honestly:** dismal. Per-agent odds are well under 1%;
  most queried manuscripts never land representation [ESTIMATE — from
  verified 100+/week query volume vs ~1 signing/year]. The MG slump
  makes 2026 a below-average year to sell MG. The number goes in the
  report: expect rejection as the modal outcome.
- **Timeline:** 3-12 months querying → if signed, 6-18 months on
  submission → ~18 months to shelf. Realistic shelf date on success:
  2029. [VERIFIED components; ESTIMATE assembly]
- **Reversibility — the deciding argument:** querying first costs ~1
  hr/week and forecloses nothing. Self-publishing first largely
  forecloses trad for Book 1 (the 2025 self-pub-to-trad pipeline was
  ~80% romance/romantasy with demonstrated sales — no MG lane exists).
  Do the irreversible thing last.
- **Pitch discipline:** query Book 1 with "series potential," arcs in
  the synopsis back pocket. Never say "40 books" in a query [ESTIMATE
  — convention; a 40-book commitment from a debut reads as naivety].
- **Decision gate:** 40-60 personalized queries over 9-12 months. If no
  offers: self-publish with recalibrated expectations (hobby-economics,
  write-for-the-shelf) or park Book 1 and reassess when MYBYB's machine
  exists.

### MYBYB — SELF-PUBLISH FIRST (KDP), TRAD LATER FOR THE ILLUSTRATED EDITION

- **Why:** the buyer is online, adult, and impulse-driven; the book is
  priced and shaped for Amazon in its own concept doc; humor/gift
  acquisition at trad houses is platform-weighted, and platform is
  exactly what this author may not have [AUTHOR DATA NEEDED, Q1].
  A trad path spends 2-3 years to maybe-$10k against a book whose
  whole thesis is speed and self-reliance.
- **Base rate, honestly:** the self-pub median is a hard number — 75%
  of indie authors earn <$1,000/yr, and a single short humor title
  with no list is below-median positioned. Realistic year-1 outcome
  without ad spend or virality: hundreds of copies, not thousands
  [ESTIMATE — from verified income distributions]. The upside case is
  gift-season and gift-guide concentration: this category spikes in
  December and graduation season.
- **Timeline:** manuscript-complete by ~October at 4-6 hrs/wk (8-10
  chapters to draft plus adoption of Part I) → edit, title, cover →
  launch late November 2026 (Secret Santa window) or, safer, April
  2027 (graduation). [ESTIMATE — drafting pace from the 2026-07 runs]
- **Reversibility:** fully reversible upward — Amazon sales data is
  the *pitch* for a trad illustrated gift edition (the Go-the-F*-to-
  Sleep lane in POSITIONING.md). Failure costs the cover + edit budget
  (~$1-3k [ESTIMATE]) and teaches the whole portfolio the KDP machine.

### Young Nicholas — DECISION DEFERRED; LEANING TRADITIONAL

- **Why deferred:** ~45% of a manuscript with no ending cannot be
  queried or published. Any channel debate now is displacement
  activity. The only GTM-relevant work is finishing: Grace's second
  act, the rebuilt climax, the lineage decision (TODO.md's stars).
- **Why leaning trad (when finished):** Christmas books are physical,
  seasonal, retail-table products — trad's exact strength; the comp
  shelf (Klaus; *A Boy Called Christmas*, itself a bestseller-to-film)
  proves recurring appetite for Santa origins. The never-says-the-name
  device (SC1) is a genuine differentiator on that shelf.
- *Contrarian comp:* Haig already owns the modern Santa-origin shelf —
  "it's been done." *Answer:* category appetite is the evidence *for*:
  this shelf rewards new entries each season, and no comp runs the
  trick in reverse (survival-adventure register, name never printed).
  Crowded-shelf risk is real and answerable only by execution quality
  — which is another argument for finishing before deciding.
- **§8 ladder audit:** the picture-book-first rung now conflicts with
  the locked older/all-ages register (bible §7 decision 8) and carries
  the plan's own "honest note" about illustration cost. Recommend the
  novel leads; picture book becomes a post-success license, not rung 1.
  Flag, not a change — the bible owns this. The §12 four-volume shape
  (BRAINSTORM) is GTM-friendly: four is pitchable, forty was not.
- **Earliest decision point:** mid-2027 at current trajectory
  [ESTIMATE].

## 4. SEQUENCED RECOMMENDATION

The portfolio sequence, driven by one principle: **ship the least
gatekept book first, query the most gatekept book in parallel, and
protect writing hours for the unfinished one.**

1. **Now → Sept 2026:** Spytwins Book 1 adoption read; then the query
   package; querying begins and runs in the background all year.
2. **Now → Nov 2026:** MYBYB is the main writing lane: finish Part II
   + closer, lock the title, launch on KDP (Nov 2026 stretch, Apr 2027
   safe). This launch is also the portfolio's GTM classroom: covers,
   categories, ads, newsletter — learned on the book whose category
   forgives indies.
3. **Dec 2026 →:** Young Nicholas becomes the main writing lane once
   MYBYB is in production; no GTM until it has an ending.
4. **No YouTube channel. No pre-launch social. No audio.** Revisit
   audio per book at 1,000 copies or a deal; revisit social only as
   MYBYB launch support.

### First three concrete moves, costed

1. **The Spytwins adoption read** (author): read the twelve v2
   chapters, ratify or reject, trigger the one adoption pass. One-time
   ~6-8 hours. Everything downstream of the portfolio's only finished
   asset is blocked on this.
2. **MYBYB drafting cadence**: two chapters/week at **4-6 hrs/wk** for
   ~5 weeks, plus the title decision (1 hour, against a
   market-pitch-agent title memo). Displaces: nothing — this is the
   existing lane, now with a launch date attached.
3. **Query launch for Spytwins**: market-pitch-agent produces the
   package (below); author personalizes and sends the first batch of
   10-12 queries. Setup ~4 hours once, then **~1 hr/wk** tracking and
   batching thereafter.

Total steady-state ask: ~6-8 hrs/wk writing + ~1 hr/wk querying. If
the author's real budget is lower [AUTHOR DATA NEEDED, Q2], cut moves
from the bottom of this list, never the top.

### What to ask market-pitch-agent to produce, and when

- **Now (gated only on the adoption read):** Spytwins query letter,
  1-page synopsis, and a comps refresh — 2024-2026 MG comps that
  survived the slump (series, engine-driven, non-graphic-novel).
- **Now:** MYBYB title/subtitle candidates (edgy-now test, retail-safe
  variant) + Amazon category/keyword map.
- **At MYBYB manuscript lock (~Oct):** back-cover blurb, Amazon listing
  copy, launch-week plan inputs.
- **Not yet:** anything Young Nicholas; anything screen/graphic-novel.

Answers change the plan; none were assumed.

1. **Platform inventory:** do you have ANY existing audience — email
   list, social accounts, YouTube, a professional network that maps to
   MYBYB's reader? Sizes, even approximate.

6. **MYBYB title authority:** who decides the edge level, and is the
   retail-safe variant (B1+CH styling) in or out?
7. **Camera/voice:** for MYBYB short-form, are you willing to be on
   camera or voice, or is it text/graphics only?
8. **Kid-world network:** do you know teachers, librarians, or

   roadmap's cheapest-signal move, still unexecuted)?
9. **Date sensitivity:** does hitting December 2026 with MYBYB matter
