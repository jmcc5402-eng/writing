# Romance Series Market Study — Amazon Kindle / KDP

**Purpose:** ground a prospective new romance series in what actually works
on Amazon Kindle right now, and work backwards from a goal of **$50,000
gross royalties by end of year two**.

Compiled 2026-07-31 from two research passes (market landscape + KDP
economics). Sources are linked throughout; anything self-reported or
unverifiable is flagged. Key public data sources: Romancing the Data
(weekly Kindle romance Top 100 scrapes), Circana BookScan (print trend
direction), Written Word Media indie author surveys, KDP's own royalty
docs, and author-community benchmarks (20BooksTo50K / Author Nation,
Kindlepreneur).

---

## The four questions, answered

### 1. Subgenre or broad? — Niche down. The data is unambiguous.

"Broad contemporary romance" is not really a shelf anymore. The Kindle
Top 100 is ~71% contemporary, but that share is itself composed of
niches — sports (~11%), rom-com (~9%), small-town/western (~8%), mafia,
billionaire ([Romancing the Data, July–Aug
2025](https://blog.romancingthedata.com/p/amazon-kindle-store-romance-best)).
Readers browse and buy by trope and micro-niche. With over a million
romance titles on Kindle, a genre-generic book by an unknown has no
discovery surface: cover, blurb, and keywords must signal an exact
trope package or the book is invisible.

**Temperature by subgenre (mid-2025 → 2026):**

- **Hot / growing:** romantasy and sports romance (triple-digit print
  growth in 2025, Circana), dark romance (named growth category),
  cowboy/western (the 2025–26 riser), monster romance (crossed from KU
  niche to NYT list). Dark romantasy is the predicted 2026 riser.
- **Saturated but selling:** hockey (past its search peak), billionaire,
  mafia — mature, high-volume, high-competition staples.
- **Declined from peak:** classic vampire/urban-fantasy paranormal,
  which ceded ground to romantasy and dark romance.

**The winning move for a new entrant:** a niche that is *proven but not
peaking* — the established readership of monster/demon or cowboy, not
the top of the hockey wave.

### 2. Does it have to be steamy?

The money list skews clearly steamy: dark romance, spicy romantasy, and
monster titles drive the charts, and ~44% of $10k+/month indie authors
write romance, heavily in high-steam niches ([WWM 2025
survey](https://www.writtenwordmedia.com/2025-indie-author-survey-results-insights-into-self-publishing-for-authors/)).

But **clean/sweet romance is a real, quieter, commercially viable
ecosystem of its own** — Amazon maintains dedicated Clean & Wholesome
and Amish bestseller categories, and full-time indie careers exist in
sweet small-town, clean western, and Christian romance. Lower ceiling
per title, but much less competition and voracious readers.

**Steam is subgenre-locked, and that's the real rule:**

| Subgenre | Steam expectation |
|---|---|
| Dark, mafia, monster/demon, why-choose | Explicit — effectively mandatory |
| Romantasy | Mid-to-high ("spice" is the marketing language) |
| Sports, rom-com | Mid-to-high dominates; closed-door exists |
| Small-town | Splits into steamy and sweet branches |
| Amish / Christian / clean historical | Closed-door by definition |

What *doesn't* work is mismatching — a closed-door book in steamy-genre
packaging (or vice versa) earns "false advertising" one-stars. Pick a
lane and label it. There is no established shelf for a clean demon
romance; if closed-door is the preference, the honest play is a
different niche entirely. [CHECK: no rigorous public dataset quantifies
revenue share by heat level; conclusion is from chart composition.]

### 3. Demons and dark paranormal — yes, a genuinely proven niche

This is arguably the strongest recent indie-to-mainstream pipeline in
romance, proven at every scale:

- **Ruby Dixon, *Ice Planet Barbarians*** — self-pub 2015, BookTok
  explosion 2021, picked up by Berkley. The foundational monster-romance
  proof.
- **Harley Laroux, *Souls Trilogy*** — spicy dark **demon** romance,
  self-pub, TikTok breakout, print deal with Kensington. The closest
  comp for "dark demon love interest, indie, series."
- **Kimberly Lemming, Mead Mishaps** — cozy-funny spicy demon romantasy,
  acquired by Orbit. Proof the niche supports a *comedic* register.
- **Katee Robert, *A Deal With A Demon*** — six interconnected books,
  indie, all in Kindle Unlimited.
- **Opal Reyne, *Duskwalker Brides*** — pure-indie KU monster/demon
  staple, 5+ books.
- Trad side: Sarah Hawley's demon rom-coms at Berkley — New York now
  buys demon romance in multiple tones.

The niche is KU-native, still growing via the dark-romantasy wave, has a
well-defined trope vocabulary (fated mates, touch-her-and-die, the
monster who stays a monster) and cover language, and normalized the
45–60k-word short novel — shorter books, faster releases, more borrows.
[CHECK: no reliable dollar figure exists for demon romance specifically;
size inferred from chart presence, KU density, and trad pickups.]

### 4. Series or standalones? — Series, decisively.

The assumption is correct, and the *architecture* matters:

- **Economics:** KU pays per page read, so one discovery event on Book 1
  monetizes the whole series. Romance read-through can exceed 70% on
  later books; a reader's lifetime value runs 4–5x the first sale.
  Standalones "rarely get checked out" in KU relative to series. Series
  enable the core promo mechanics: 99¢/free first-in-series, box sets,
  sell-through ads.
- **Dominant structure — interconnected standalones:** one couple per
  book, each book a complete arc with its own HEA, all in one world (a
  town, a pack, a bargain, a hell-adjacent inn). Readers can enter
  anywhere; past couples cameo; future couples are seeded as secondary
  characters. This is the default in contemporary and paranormal.
- **The exception:** romantasy and some dark romance run one couple
  across a duet/trilogy with the HEA at the end. Readers accept
  mid-series HFNs there; in contemporary-adjacent subgenres,
  cliffhanging the central couple is punished in reviews.

**Genre contract (non-negotiable):** HEA/HFN, first-person dual POV as
the modern default in steamy contemporary/dark/monster, trope-forward
blurbs, subgenre-correct covers (photographic/moody for dark and
paranormal; illustrated for rom-com/romantasy), content warnings in
dark romance.

## The $50k math, backwards

$50k over 24 months is not $2,083/month from day one. Realistically it
is **≈ $8–12k in year one and $38–42k in year two**, i.e. ~$3,300/month
by months 18–24.

**Revenue mechanics (per 60–80k-word book, KU-exclusive):**

- Ebook sale at 70% tier, after delivery fee: $2.99 → ~$2.03 ·
  $3.99 → ~$2.73 · $4.99 → ~$3.43.
- Kindle Unlimited pays per page: rate ~$0.0040–0.0045 [CHECK: Amazon
  publishes only after month-end; sources disagree at the 4th decimal].
  A full read of a 60k-word book ≈ $1.50; 80k ≈ $2.00.
- The Kindle romance Top 100 is ~63% self-published and ~75% enrolled
  in KU. For a new romance pen name on this timeline, KU-exclusive is
  the standard opening play; going wide is a slower list-building game.

**Series read-through (community benchmarks, self-reported):** paid
Book 1 → 2 runs 40–65% (50% is very good); later books 70–80%+. Each
Book 1 reader consumes ~2.5–3 books of a 6-book series.

**The Amazon algorithm rewards cadence:** new releases get elevated
visibility for 30 days (cliffs at 30/60/90). Hence the KU romance meta:
release every 4–10 weeks so something is always in a window, and bank
3–4 finished books before launching Book 1 to make the pace survivable.

### Three scenarios

| | A: Standard | B: Rapid | C: Two trilogies |
|---|---|---|---|
| Books | 6 × 70–75k @ $4.99 | 10 × ~50k @ $3.99 | 6 × 75k @ $4.99 + 2 box sets |
| Cadence | every ~10 weeks | every ~7 weeks | 6-week bursts, banked |
| Value per Book 1 reader | ~$7.25 | ~$5.40 | ~$7.25 + 10–15% box sets |
| Book 1 readers needed | ~6,900 | ~9,300 | ~6,000–6,500 |
| Character | The proven model | Max algorithm leverage, burnout risk (~500k words in 18 mo) | Most sustainable pace; two "series complete" moments; best setup for going wide later |

**Bottom line:** $50k gross in 24 months means **6–10 books in one
subgenre, on a ≤10-week cadence, ~45–55% read-through, and roughly
6,000–9,500 acquired series readers**. Fewer than 5 books or a
slower-than-quarterly cadence and the survey data says the realistic
outcome is a fraction of the target. Achievable — romance is the one
genre where this playbook demonstrably works — but it is a top-quartile
outcome, not a typical one. (~80% of authors with 1–3 books earn under
$100/month; the $100k+ authors average 30 books in catalog.)

### Marketing stack and budget

Deploy roughly in this order: ARC team (BookFunnel/BookSirens) →
newsletter building + swaps with a reader-magnet novella → promo-site
stacking on Book 1 discounts ($25–150/placement) → Amazon Ads from
Book 2 onward (romance CPCs $0.30–0.80; Book 1 ads can run at apparent
breakeven because read-through multiplies reader value) → Facebook ads
once read-through is proven → BookBub Featured Deal in year two once
Book 1 has ~150–500 reviews. Treat organic BookTok as lottery upside,
not a plan. Budget **$300–800/month average**, weighted toward months
6–24 (**$8–15k total**).

### Costs — gross vs. net, stated plainly

Per 70k-word book, lean-but-professional (genre-standard cover +
copyedit, beta readers instead of paid dev edit): **~$1,000–3,000**.
Production for 6–10 books: **$8–25k**. Plus marketing $8–15k.

So the $50k-gross plan plausibly **nets $15,000–30,000** over two years
for ~500,000 written words plus publisher-level operations work. That's
a part-time salary, and the honest baseline to decide against.

## Recommended position (to brainstorm from)

**Demon/monster paranormal romance in Kindle Unlimited**, structured as
**interconnected standalones** (one couple per book, shared world,
HEA every book), ~50–70k words per book, high steam, first-person dual
POV, photographic/moody covers, trope-forward packaging, with a series
brand stronger than the pen name. Closest execution model: Scenario C
(two linked trilogies, banked before launch, box sets after each).

Why this fits: proven at every scale with named comps, still growing,
tolerant of dark *and* comedic registers, KU-native binge readership,
and the short-novel norm suits a rapid cadence.

**Avoid:** launching into hockey at the top of its cycle; broad
unlabeled contemporary; a 120k-word Book 1 in any KU niche;
cliffhanging the central couple; clean packaging on a steamy shelf or
vice versa.

## Open questions before a series bible exists

- [TK: steam level the author is actually willing to write and sustain
  for 6–10 books — this decides the niche more than anything else]
- [TK: register — dark (Laroux comp) vs. cozy-funny (Lemming comp);
  both are proven, they are different readerships and cover languages]
- [TK: the world hook — the one high-concept engine that generates a
  couple per book (a bargain, a curse, a bride-lottery, a matchmaking
  agency for the damned)]
- [TK: pen name — romance readers expect genre-dedicated pen names;
  this work should be firewalled from the middle-grade titles]
- [TK: production capacity — honest words/week, which decides between
  Scenarios A, B, and C]
- [TK: budget ceiling for production + ads over 24 months]
