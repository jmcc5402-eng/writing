# The curves — a timing spec for every arc, not just the plot

`hauge.md` gives the outer and inner journey their percentages. This
file does the same for the other five curves a book in this series
runs: the romance, each lead's arc, the antagonist, the quartet
position, and the emotional register.

**Diagnostic, not dogma** — the same caveat Hauge carries. A romance
midpoint at 62% is not a rule violation. It is a question: *is the
middle stalling?* The percentages exist so the question gets asked at
chapter 8 instead of at the box set.

`story-matrix.py` measures the book against these and reports the gap.

---

## 1. The romance curve

The genre's own structure (Gwen Hayes, *Romancing the Beat*), which
these books already follow by instinct — Book 1.1 kissed at 53%,
Book 1.2 at 50%.

| Position | Beat | What has to be true |
|---|---|---|
| 0–10% | **No mate in sight** | Each lead's life shown working, or failing, without the other |
| ~10% | **Meet / re-meet** | They are in the same frame and something is noticed |
| 10–25% | **No way** | The reason it cannot happen, said plainly on the page |
| ~25% | **Adhesion** | The thing that forces them together repeatedly. Not a choice — a structure |
| 25–50% | **Deepening desire** | The ladder climbs a rung at a time; the want is named to the reader |
| **~50%** | **Midpoint of love** | *Maybe this could work.* The kiss, or its equivalent. **The single most load-bearing mark on this chart** |
| 50–62% | **Inkling of doubt** | The cost of the thing arrives |
| 62–75% | **Deepening doubt** | The outside world presses; the terms strain |
| **~75%** | **Retreat** | They separate, and it is each lead's *wound* that separates them, not a misunderstanding |
| 75–88% | **Dark night** | Apart, and each sees what the other gave them |
| ~90% | **Grand gesture** | Public, costly, and in character |
| 95–100% | **Whole-hearted yes** | HEA. Standard 7 — never cliffhang the couple |

**House amendments.** Closed door (standard 9) moves the *heat* ladder,
never the *beat* ladder — the midpoint is still the midpoint whether
the scene cuts at the garment or not. And the retreat at 75% must be
caused by a wound, never by a misunderstanding a text would fix; that
is a standing author ruling and a one-star generator.

### The closeness ladder, against the same clock

The rungs (STYLE, "the closeness ladder") should climb roughly with
the romance beats. One rung per chapter maximum, and the high-water
mark never falls.

| Rung | What it is | Lands by |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | Noticing; the other in absence | 15% |
| 3–4 | Charge in company; the want named to the reader | 30% |
| 5 | Contact with cover — a cup, a plate, a sleeve | 40% |
| 6 | Deliberate touch with no errand in it | 48% |
| 7 | Mouth | ~50% |
| 8 | The approach; the garment line in sight | 70–85% |

## 2. Each lead's arc (the inner journey)

Hauge's identity → essence, given a clock. Run this **separately for
each lead** — the two curves should not be in phase, or the book has
one character in two bodies.

| Position | Beat | What has to be true |
|---|---|---|
| 0–10% | **Identity established** | The armour is visible and *working*. It is why they have survived |
| ~15% | **The wound glimpsed** | Not explained. Glimpsed — one line, in behaviour |
| ~25% | **First crack** | The armour costs them something on the page |
| ~50% | **Essence glimpsed** | They act from the true self once, and it is seen by the other lead |
| 50–75% | **Oscillation** | Back and forth; the armour is chosen again at least once |
| ~75% | **Retreat to identity** | Under maximum pressure they put the armour back on. This is what causes the romance's retreat |
| ~90% | **Essence chosen at cost** | The armour comes off in public and it costs them the thing it protected |

**The rooting-for ladder runs underneath it.** Every chapter, each lead
does one thing for somebody else, at a cost, unrewarded and mostly
unseen. That is not an arc beat — it is the floor that makes the arc
land. Ungated, a reader admires both leads and roots for neither, which
is the note the author gave on 2026-09-08.

## 3. The antagonist's pressure curve

Rex Boyd is the model: institutional, lawful, never a villain in his
own head.

| Position | Beat | What has to be true |
|---|---|---|
| ~10% | **Sighted** | His *interest* is on the page before he is |
| ~25% | **First cost** | Something is taken, legitimately, and it hurts |
| ~50% | **The shape is understood** | A lead sees what he actually wants; the reader is ahead of them |
| **~75%** | **Maximum pressure** | He causes the major setback. The romance's retreat and his peak are the same event, or the book has two climaxes |
| ~90% | **Beaten** | And never by luck. Standard: no coincidence, no cavalry. He loses to something a lead built |
| 95–100% | **The residue** | What he leaves behind, which the next book can use |

**The series rule.** A quartet antagonist may lose a book and survive
the series. What may never happen is a book where his pressure peaks
anywhere but at 75%, because that is the setback the couple's retreat
hangs on.

## 4. The quartet position

Where a book sits in the four. Percentages here are of *the quartet*,
not of the book.

| Book | Its job | Owes the set |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Establish the town, the bench, the calling card | Seed book 2's couple by its 75%; end with the town intact |
| **2** | Widen the town; the register shifts one click | Cameo book 1's couple by its 25%; seed book 3's by 75% |
| **3** | The darkest register of the four | The bench pays; a book-1 plant returns |
| **4** | The reunion — the whole town on the page | Every prior couple sighted; the quartet's own HEA |

**Standing rules.** Every book cameos at least one prior couple in its
first quarter, and seeds the next couple by its last quarter. Adjacent
books never move a dial more than one click (DIALS, the adjacency
rule). Each book stands alone: a reader entering at book 3 is never
lost.

## 5. The emotional register wave

The author's own, per quartet, on a 1–10 scale. **It resets every
quartet — it is never a series-long ramp.**

| Book | Season | Register | What that means |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | fall | ~4 | Warm, funny, low stakes on the surface |
| 2 | winter | ~9 | The heavy one; the individual's book |
| 3 | spring | ~6 | Recovery, with the bench doing the work |
| 4 | summer | ~5 | Celebration; the town's book |

**The 2026-09-11 amendment:** the *winter* book is the warm,
family-shaped one built to carry Christmas, and the individual's story
goes in another season. Assign each book's register with its season
*before* casting the couples.

## 6. The small dials, per book

Not curves — constants that should hold across a whole book, and drift
when nobody is counting.

| Dial | Target | Where it is set |
|---|---|---|
| Dialogue share | 15% floor, ~25% target | `studio/tools/dialogue-lint.py` |
| Chapters | 30 | outline |
| Words | 55–70k | standard 10 |
| POV | dual, alternating, never 3 in a row | standard 11 |
| Chapters apart | never two in a row | the closeness ladder |
| Endings | at most half on a wry button; ≥2 on plain information | the endings budget |
| Ending register | across any four chapters, ≥2 end on the couple | STYLE, "the ending register" |
| Romance beats | ≥3 per chapter, ≥2 kinds, one in the first third and one in the last | standard 26 |
| Body beats | ≥1 per chapter where the leads share the page | standard 27 |
| Wine night | once per book, interrupted at the crucial syllable | the calling card |
| Side romances | 2–3 side-POV scenes per book | author ruling, 2026-09-07 |
| One-per-book spends | "somebody's ___", each named phrase | `RECENT.md` ledger |

## How to use this

**At the outline gate.** Every beat above gets a chapter number in the
outline. The gate checks that the declared chapter's position matches
the target percentage within tolerance.

**During drafting.** `story-matrix.py` computes each chapter's actual
position and reports the gap. A midpoint kiss that has slid to 62% is a
finding at the moment it slides, not at the retrospective.

**EARLY and LATE are not equally interesting.** Getting the obstacle,
the adhesion or the wound onto the page sooner than target is usually a
gift. A beat arriving LATE is the one that signals a stall — the middle
that is not moving, the lead the reader cannot root for yet. Both are
reported; only LATE is ranked first.

**The tolerance is ±7%** of the book, which at 30 chapters is about two
chapters either side. Tighter than that is false precision; looser and
the chart stops meaning anything.

**And the standing caveat, which matters more than any row above:** a
book that hits every mark and is boring has failed, and a book that
misses three and is wonderful has not. These percentages catch the
*stall* — the middle that is not moving, the antagonist who peaks in
the wrong place, the couple who are never in the same frame. They have
no opinion about whether the prose is any good. That is still the
panel, the superfan, and the author.
