# Michael Hauge — the framework, as this studio uses it

*Author-adopted 2026-07-28. Source: Hauge's Story Mastery material — the
six-stage structure, the identity/essence inner journey, and the four
structural devices (his Avatar analysis). This is the studio's distillation,
not a reproduction; agents cite this file, not the web.*

The frame braids three things: plot, the timing of events, and the
internal/external emotional life of the characters. A story succeeds when
the outer journey and the inner journey turn at the same moments.

## The two journeys

- **Outer journey:** a visible goal with a clear finish line the audience
  can picture (win, stop, reach, save). If you can't name the image of it
  achieved, it isn't a goal yet.
- **Inner journey:** from **identity** to **essence**. The wound creates a
  protective persona (identity); the story forces choices where the persona
  fails; essence is who the character is when the armor comes off. The
  climax should require essence — the outer goal must be unwinnable from
  inside the identity.

## Six stages, five turning points — the timing spec

| Position | Turning point | Enters stage |
|---|---|---|
| ~10% | **Opportunity** | New Situation |
| ~25% | **Change of Plans** (outer goal locks in) | Progress |
| ~50% | **Point of No Return** (full commitment) | Complications |
| ~75% | **Major Setback** (all is lost) | Final Push |
| ~90–99% | **Climax** | Aftermath |

The percentages are diagnostic, not dogma: a Point of No Return at 70%
means the middle is stalling; a goal locked at 10% means the setup hasn't
earned pursuit yet. Both journeys should turn at these points — the outer
event and the inner cost arriving together.

## The four devices

- **Foreshadowing:** plant early what pays later, so the payoff feels
  inevitable instead of convenient. (Studio machinery: THREADS.md
  plants and `OWED:` markers.)
- **Echoing:** repeat an image, line, or object with its meaning changed
  by everything between. An echo is foreshadowing's receipt.
- **Superior position:** the reader knows something the characters don't.
  Suspense lives in the gap. (Studio machinery: knowledge threads; the
  Spytwins spy-wink S01 is this device as a series engine.)
- **Ticking clock:** a fixed deadline visible to the reader, compressing
  every choice. (Studio machinery: on-page day ledgers; the clock must be
  *seen* early, not revealed late.)

## How agents apply it

- `plot-architect` outlines to the timing spec and names both journeys
  before chapter beats exist.
- `developmental-editor` audits against it: journey braid, turning-point
  depth, superior-position and echo inventories.
- `drafting-assistant` plays it at scene level: external action carrying
  internal state; echoes planted small.
- Non-fiction (MYBYB): the reader is the hero — identity is who they are
  on page 1, essence is who the book argues they can be; the "turning
  points" are the argument's escalations.
