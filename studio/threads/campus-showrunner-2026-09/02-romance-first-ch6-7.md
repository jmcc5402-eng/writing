**Why it was missed, in one sentence:** every instrument we added this week counts plot mechanics, and the one romance instrument, the ROMANCE meter, is a score, not a gate. A chapter could carry a perfect plot, one glance, and pass everything.

**The wall, in one sentence:** every chapter carries at least three romance beats of at least two kinds, one in the first third and one in the last, whether or not the leads share the page, and a chapter under that floor comes back before it is staged.

**What makes it fundamental rather than another line item:**
- **Romance before plot in every brief.** The first line of a brief is now THE ROMANCE MOVE: what this chapter does to the feeling between them. The plot's argument comes second. A drafter who cannot say the romance move has not been briefed.
- **Eight kinds a beat can be,** so "tiny" has a shape: proximity, noticing, wanting, the other in absence, between them, the town ships them, the want against its wall, the private admission. One sentence counts.
- **Apart chapters owe the same three.** Apartness is the harder case, not an exemption. The absent lead has to be in the POV's head as a person, never only as an obstacle.
- **The panel's blind count stands.** It inventories beats before judging anything else, and a WALL FAILURE returns the chapter regardless of how good the plot is. Only you can waive it, per chapter.
- **It joins HEA, consent and the safety rules** in the campus standards as the things no better-looking chapter may trade away.

**Checked against the pages you have:** ch 5 as accepted has five beats of four kinds and ch 6 has six of four, so both pass. Ch 7 as it was first shipped, with Dan absent from the chapter, would have failed on kinds. That is the wall doing what you asked before it existed.
