- **The last two sections.** You were right and it was the variance card's fault: "open every scene mid-motion" produced scenes that never said where we are. Both now open with a plain sentence locating her. And it's a rule now: every scene break owes the reader where, when, and what's in her hand inside three lines.

**The likability note.** I think you're right, and I think it's the most important note you've given on this book. Here's my read on why it's happening. Both leads are built on wounds and restraint. She asks for nothing; he manages the verdict. That makes them admirable and hard to root for, because rooting needs to see them want things for other people, not just hold the line. Chapter 12 and 13 have him asking and her teaching, which is the start, but twelve chapters is late.

My proposal, for the chat:

1. **A rooting-for pass over chapters 1 through 13.** A few sentences per chapter, as you said, not a rewrite. Each is one of the leads doing something generous that costs them and that nobody sees or thanks them for. She covers for the trainer's mistake. He drives the walk-on home without saying why. Small, unrewarded, in character.
2. **A standing line in every card and brief:** "What does each lead do this chapter that makes a reader root for them?" If the answer is nothing, the brief isn't done.
3. **A panel question:** "Would you have a beer with her? With him?" answered per chapter, so a chapter can pass on romance and fail on likability.

I've written none of this yet. Tell me if that's the shape you want, or if you have specific moments in mind, and I'll run the pass before chapter 14's card.
