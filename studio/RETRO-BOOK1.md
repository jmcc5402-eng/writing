# Book 1 retrospective — design + draft best practices

Showrunner draft, 2026-08-29 (pre-#99-merge; the retro proper
fires when the finale is accepted). Two halves: (I) the
retrospective instruments — each built on something AI can do
that a human room cannot; (II) the best-practices list going
into Book 1.2, drafted NOW from lived evidence, to be
validated/amended by (I) and then ratified by the author as a
PR before the 1.2 kickoff.

## I. The retrospective (instruments, post-merge)

What makes an AI retro different: total recall (every draft,
loser, verdict, and timestamp still exists), self-testing (we
hold answer keys to our own instruments), and the losers as a
corpus (60 losing candidates nobody would ever ask a human team
to re-read).

1. **Defect escape analysis** (the software-postmortem move).
   Mine every defect found LATE (sweep mandatories on accepted
   text, fuzz hits, line-pass residue) and assign each the gate
   that SHOULD have caught it and why it didn't. We already
   know the #1 escape class — BRIEF REFRACTION (three waves
   running: "twelve winters," the eleven slides, the
   dance-floor smile): gloss lines in decision docs hardening
   into three-way prose convergences. The output is gate
   redesign, not blame.
2. **Winner-pattern mining.** All 30 accepted chapters vs all
   ~60 losers, plus every panel's "deciding line": what did
   winners share? Which variance cards over/under-performed
   (the LOG has every draw)? Did any slot (A/B/C) win beyond
   chance (B's late streak needs testing, not anecdote)?
   Output: drafting-brief upgrades and deck rebalancing for
   1.2.
3. **Instrument calibration audit.** For each instrument, its
   measured hit rate: panel fixes ratified vs struck by the
   author (nearly all ratified — quantify), sweep findings
   confirmed vs overturned, the dating test's 50% (already on
   file — instruments confabulate under confidence). Output: a
   trust level per instrument, and thresholds tuned from data
   instead of judgment.
4. **The losers' estate sale.** One pass over all losing
   candidates harvesting unbanked crown-jewel lines and beats
   into the energy bank, tagged by slot (ch 28's grief slot,
   1.2 openings). Humans cannot re-read 60 dead drafts; an
   agent can, once, and the bank compounds.
5. **Production telemetry.** Chapters/day by wave, agent-runs
   per accepted chapter, defects per 1,000 words by wave (the
   learning curve the scrub law and Closeness law should show),
   PR queue latency. Output: the 1.2 wave plan sized from
   measured throughput, not vibes.
6. **The parked questions, answered with data.**
   notes/book1-retrospective-seeds.md gets its hearing — the
   slow-churn question now has instrument evidence (browse
   9/10, warmth mean 7.3, dating seamlessness) rather than
   opinion.

## II. Draft best practices → Book 1.2 (each with its incident)

1. **Briefs quote canon verbatim with file:line — never
   paraphrase.** Every paraphrase is a refraction risk (the
   escape class of waves 6–8). NEW GATE: a pre-flight brief
   audit — continuity-keeper reads the BRIEFS against canon
   before any drafter fires. Auditing 2–4 briefs is cheaper
   than repairing 12 drafts.
2. **A condition that lives only in a ledger reaches no
   drafter** (T06). Conditions travel inside briefs, verbatim.
3. **The sweep outranks the panels, and sweeps the GRAFT
   PACKAGES too** — wave 8's sweep caught two contradictions
   the panel's own graft list would have imported.
4. **After any calendar ruling, grep every relative-time phrase
   book-wide** ("weeks gone," "Tuesdays ago") — SR-11's slide
   left ch 19's badge arithmetic stranded for three waves; the
   fuzz, not the slide's own checklist, caught it.
5. **Keep the three-candidate blind field.** It won every wave;
   the panels' deciding-line record shows the winner was rarely
   the orchestrator's guess. (Retro item 2 tests slot bias
   before we trust the streak.)
6. **Pleasure instruments outrank checkbox instruments** —
   now evidence, not policy: warmth 7.3, browse 9/10 on a book
   that shipped through mechanical gates ranked second.
7. **Every sampling instrument gets its action threshold set
   BEFORE it runs.** The dating test's 50%-accurate critic
   wrote a fluent seam report that would have cost a week; the
   pre-set threshold was the only thing that stopped it.
8. **Distant-pair fuzz joins the standing gates** at fixed
   milestones (ch 10/20/30), keyed to SHARED FURNITURE (both
   chapters touch the drawer, the clock, the wall) — the
   fuzz's own meta-finding.
9. **Keep a protected-signature registry** (grep-able sacred
   lines: "still winning," the terms' wording, the door-smile
   figure). The squirrel nearly spent T25's line; the ledger
   caught it by luck of remit. Make it a list, not a memory.
10. **Trade-diction discipline is the voice fingerprint** — the
    dating test's one verified finding: it never drifts. Keep
    the rule in every 1.2 brief.
11. **Bank withholds with explicit spend-slots and severable
    lines.** M1 sat banked for fourteen chapters and paid at
    the dance because the ledger named its slot and preserved
    its severable text. Do it from ch 1 next time.
12. **One decision per PR, the edit inside the diff.** #100/
    #101 merged from a phone in minutes with zero bookkeeping
    debt. The queue IS the to-do list; keep it mergeable.
13. **The climax cooldown law** (wave 7): fast chapters get the
    same reread as quiet ones; all wave-7 line residue
    clustered in the one chapter drafted hot.
14. **The repo is the brain; STATE.md carries checkmarks.** Two
    handovers and one lost container cost nothing because the
    checklist lived in the repo, updated at every step.
15. **Record-or-soften, never fill** — the [TK] discipline held
    through 30 chapters (zero unresolved markers in the staged
    finale) and is the reason the fuzz found six seams and not
    sixty.
