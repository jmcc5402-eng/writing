# Shared Canon (cross-series universe furniture)

Facts that more than one series uses. A series may cite these; none
may quietly redefine them — changes come through a PR here, and each
entry carries LOCKED/PROPOSED status like any canon.

## The invented social platform

The parent/community message-board platform (campus-series rule R7;
city-quartets' board culture). One invented platform serves every
romance series — same app, different local groups — so board lore,
UI verbs, and running jokes carry across series for free.

- Name: **Grapevine** — LOCKED (author, PR #89 comment,
  2026-08-21): "I like grapevine. One reason is that the word
  hearsay is never written down so it's actually kind of hard to
  read as a human." Groups live "on Grapevine"; the verb is "post
  it to the Grapevine" or plain "it's on Grapevine." Same
  recognition comedy — heard-it-through-the-grapevine is the joke
  every citation retells — and it scales to all four towns
  unchanged. Runners-up, retired: Hearsay (rejected as above),
  Porchlight (spends porch imagery the campus series owns).
- Register: lovingly unhinged, recognition comedy, never contempt
- All posts invented; never lifted or near-paraphrased from real
  posts or identifiable people

## The Grapevine epigraph (series device — PROPOSED 2026-09-02)

**Author directive, 2026-09-02:** *"At the beginning of each chapter
we put a quote from the grapevine. This could be something we do all
series long. We'd want the quotes to be a mix of related to the story,
just funny, or some unrelated but kinda related posts."*

Every chapter of every book opens with a short Grapevine excerpt,
above the first line of prose.

### The frame line (fixed, per book)

One frame line, identical in every chapter of a given book, so a
listener learns it as a chime by chapter three and stops needing it
explained. It names the ROOM, and the room rotates per book — each
cast brings its native room (`campus-series/town-ashford.md`, the
platform model):

| Book | Frame line | Room |
|---|---|---|
| Campus 1.1 | `On Grapevine, Ashford County —` | the town room |
| Campus 1.2 | `On Grapevine, the Ashford parents' board —` | the parents' board |

Never change a book's frame line mid-book.

### Form

```
> **On Grapevine, Ashford County —**
>
> **DeeAnn Whitlow:** Reminder that the fairground gate is not a
> road. It has never been a road. It was not a road last year
> either.
```

- Blockquote, bold frame, bold poster name, colon, post.
- **Hard cap 35 words**, one to three posts. Length discipline is the
  whole ballgame: 30 chapters of delayed openings is the risk this
  device runs, and brevity is the only thing that pays it.
- **No timestamps.** They pin the calendar and invite continuity bugs.
  (Exception must be ruled per instance, never as a default.)
- Ordinary citizens post under REAL NAMES. Only bad-faith accounts use
  handles — that contrast is what makes a sock puppet legible on sight,
  and it is doing plot work for free.

### The mix (the author's three flavors)

Roughly a third each, never a fixed rotation:
1. **Story-related** — the town's oblique angle on this chapter's event.
2. **Just funny** — recognition comedy, no plot content whatsoever.
3. **Unrelated but kinda related** — a lost retainer, a rail going on
   backwards; adjacent by accident, resonant by placement.

### Guardrails (binding)

1. **No epigraph carries a load-bearing beat.** Texture and dramatic
   irony only — never information the reader NEEDS, never a
   confirmation of something the chapter is withholding. Epigraphs are
   texture beats, so they stay slant (studio/STYLE.md, the
   explicitness dial).
2. **The realism rulebook governs the register**
   (`campus-series/book2/notes/grapevine-realism-2026-08-29.md`):
   grievances run through a child; no plain-text speculation about any
   adult's love life under real names (vagueposting and the
   asking-for-a-friend dodge only); pile-ons small, organized,
   epistolary. Lovingly unhinged, recognition comedy, never contempt.
3. **All posts invented.** Never lifted or near-paraphrased from real
   posts or identifiable people.
4. **Licensed exception — the bad-faith drumbeat.** A book's antagonist
   accounts MAY recur in the epigraphs, because a campaign the reader
   watches assemble across chapter heads is decoded by the time the
   plot needs it. It never replaces the in-scene decode; it makes the
   decode land. (Campus 1.1: Concerned4Ashford and AshfordSafetyMom,
   ch 7 forward, six appearances.)
5. **The epigraph is part of the listening file.** It ships in the .md
   the author hears, above the chapter.
