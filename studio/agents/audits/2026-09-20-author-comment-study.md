## The themes, in the author's own shapes

Every comment has one of six shapes. The shape is the thing to build
for, because the shape is what the author will say again.

**1. "We describe the mental side, not the physical side."** The
body and the ache. Nine of thirty-five: the ch 17 ending ("he was
hers till the lamp went off"); her feeling about Boyd (19); the lamp
on because it was his (19); her history — all she wanted was home
(19); romance "two or three out of ten" on a chapter the count passed
(20); the touch has a body (21); the ache beside the arithmetic (21);
the cab — one line of the arm going warm (21 reread). The pattern
under it: the drafts carry the LOGIC of a feeling (three weeks; she
could hold; the hands touched) and not the feeling. The instruments
measured PRESENCE — a body sentence, yes or no; twenty beats, nine
kinds — and presence passed while the amount was one line.

**2. "I can't tell who they are or what they want."** Ten of
thirty-five, the biggest: the portal's other face said in her head
(19); why Dan reads the board (20); Missy and Denny — good guys or bad
guys (21); who is Sonny (22); like or hate Denny and Boyd (22); Missy
the zombie, the pull (21 reread); and the four stakes comments — every
character has a stake, darker, the trainer's debt, Dan's second time
(chat, 22). The pattern: minor characters walk on with choreography
and no want, no pull, no stance the reader is told to take; a habit is
shown without its why; a returning character is not re-introduced.

**3. "It's confusing."** Six: who sent the chart (17); "I have no
idea if it's snowing or sleeting" (21); "he" for two men (22); the
timeline in the annex (21 reread); "is 'the copies' the letter?"
(22); who is Sonny (22, again). The pattern: the page knows a thing
the reader was never told — the clock, the antecedent, the referent.

**4. "Too on the nose — remove that concept."** Three: the drinks
explained in dialogue (20); place-stamped speech (21); the Verna
sentence unchained (18). The pattern: a character or the narrator
names the device. A tic. The author's word for it is "on the nose."

**5. "Let's have a paragraph about the storm."** Five: describe the
storm (21, twice); the dinner looser — the wine, the jelly jar, her
feet on the rung (18); "cool from the glass" (18); the boxes' work
reason (19). The pattern: the world is on the page as consequence
(the ice on the car, the post) and not as a thing with weather,
props and texture.

**6. "We said earlier…" / "set it up first."** Five: seed that they
bumped into each other before (17); Cal and Marisol are not married
(17); the film room set up the night before (18); the epigraph for
the reader who does not care about football (19); the letter is small
at a big football school (22). The pattern: the page contradicts or
skips what the book already established, or treats a small thing as
big.

Shapes 1 and 2 are two thirds of the comments. They are also the two
the current instruments are weakest at, because both are questions of
AMOUNT and FEELING, and every check we have asks yes-or-no.

(Two rows sit in two classes.) Every MISS and WEAK is now an
instrument — that is the lesson loop working after the fact. The
point of this study is the column before: NEW is the only class that
should exist, because NEW is the author adding taste, and every other
class is the room forgetting taste it already had.

One more finding, from 234: an instrument CAUSED a comment. The
August scrub ban rode in every campus brief for a month and set the
body budget at one line, against taste 20. The instrument-auditor
checks agents against the law; it did not check RECENT.md against the
taste sheet. That is a fixture to add (the audit's F-list).

## What "before the chapter is written" can mean

Three places to stand, earliest first:

1. **On the brief.** The brief is the last document before prose.
   Today it carries the matrix row, the addendum, the board items,
   STAKES ON THE PAGE. It does not carry the six questions above,
   asked of each scene, answered in one line each. A drafter given
   "scene 3, the cab: FEELING — her body across the ride, four beats
   at least, the confusion said plain; WHO — the trainer wants out of
   the truck, the reader is to feel his witness; CONFUSING — the
   clock: back inside 4:10, lights 5:45" writes that scene. A drafter
   given "the cab, three across, the touch" writes one line of warm.
2. **On the draft, before the panel.** A reader who reads AS THE
   AUTHOR — with nothing but the author's own comments, verbatim, as
   the brief — and writes the comments the author would write, in the
   author's shapes. Today the panel reads as the buyer, the keeper as
   canon, the red team as the acquiring editor. Nobody reads as the
   author. The superfan is closest and judges promise-keeping, not
   these six shapes.
3. **On the numbers.** The tests return PASS/FINDING. They should
   return counts — body sentences at the highest touch: 1; ache
   sentences in the apart section: 0; named characters with a want
   said: 2 of 4 — so that "one line" is visible as a number before
   anyone reads it, and the matrix can carry a floor for it.

## Proposals, in build order

**P1 — the author's questions on the brief (a skill and a gate).**
`studio/AUTHOR-QUESTIONS.md`: the six shapes as six questions, in the
author's words. Every chapter brief gets a section THE AUTHOR'S READ:
a table, one row per scene from the card, six cells, one line each
(or "—" with a reason). `brief-gate.py` refuses a chapter brief
without the section or with a scene from the card missing from it
(mechanical). A skill `/author-read plots/brief-chNN.md` drafts the
table from the card, the matrix row, STAKES and THREADS, for the
showrunner to correct. Cost: twenty minutes a chapter, before the
drafter. This is the one that acts BEFORE the page exists.

**P2 — the author-proxy read (an agent).** `author-proxy`: reads a
draft with AUTHOR-NOTES rows 158–234 and AUTHOR-TASTE as its only
brief, and writes comments in the six shapes — "we just have one line
of…", "I can't tell whether…", "this is on the nose" — with line
numbers. Runs BEFORE the panel; its comments are fixed before the PR.
Machine line: `AUTHOR-PROXY: more N · who N · confusing N · nose N ·
point N · senses N` — the accept gate reads it from ch 23. Its KPI is
the miss rate: every real comment on the PR is afterwards classed
"the proxy said it" or "the proxy missed it," and a miss is a lesson
row against the proxy's brief.

**P3 — the comment census (a tool on every fold PR).**
`comment-census.py`: from AUTHOR-NOTES, comments per chapter and each
one's class (MISS / WEAK / GAP / NEW, from a new column), as a table;
`pr-lint.py` prints it on every `[FOLD]` PR so the trend is in front
of both of us. The target is a falling MISS+WEAK+GAP line, not a
falling total — NEW is the author writing the book.

**P4 — counts, not verdicts (the panel and the lints).** The panel's
TESTS line carries numbers beside each result (touch 1, ache 0,
stance 2/4); chapter-lint gains an ACHE SPAN beside TOUCH SPAN (body
words in the apart section); a `cast-check.py` counts, per chapter,
the named characters and flags one returning after three chapters
away with no re-introducing clause, and one with three or more lines
and no STAKES row. All mechanical; all cheap.

**P5 — the amount in the matrix.** Two columns on TARGETS: Body (the
sentences of her body at the chapter's highest touch, 0–6) and Ache
(in the apart section, 0–4). Planned on the row, counted by the
lints, gated like Romance. The author's "two or three out of ten" and
"one line" both become a number said before the draft.

**P6 — the auditor reads RECENT.md against the taste sheet.** One
fixture on the instrument audit: every standing ban or cap in RECENT
and the scrub list is checked against AUTHOR-TASTE; a cap that
contradicts an entry is a finding. Ten minutes; prevents the 234
class.

## What this will not do

A third of the comments are NEW: the gossip room, the darker stakes,
STAKE × LEVER. No instrument pre-empts taste the author has not said.
Those comments should keep coming, and they should be the only kind.
The measure of success is not fewer comments on ch 23; it is that
none of ch 23's comments is a repeat of a shape above.

## Recommendation

Build P1 and P6 tonight (both are a file and a gate check; no new
agent). Run P1 on ch 23's brief. Build P2 for ch 23's draft and score
it against the real ch 23 comments — that is the experiment. P3–P5
after ch 23, with the data from it.
