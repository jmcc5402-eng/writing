# Spytwins — The Story Engine (Series Bible)

_This is the reusable machine for writing any Spytwins book. It formalizes the
author's "Book template notes" into a working checklist. Use it to outline every
new book and to pressure-test that a draft "feels like a Spytwins book."_

---

## 1. The must-have checklist

Every book should contain **all** of these. (They're what make the series feel
consistent from book to book.)

- [ ] **Location details** — a real sense of the new city/culture/geography.
- [ ] **A light secondary plot** — something small running underneath the mystery.
- [ ] **2–3 atmospherics** — see the menu in §4.
- [ ] **A lesson learned** by one of the twins.
- [ ] **A new/harder spy skill** — the twins level up each book.
- [ ] **At least one family dinner** (Dad usually grills; Mom wishes he'd cook
      more fish).
- [ ] **At least one surprise call from Aunt Jenny & Uncle Jacob** — always at
      the worst possible moment.
- [ ] **At least one "parenting" moment** — chores, homework, bedtime, laundry
      before the trip.
- [ ] **At least one "practice time"** — Amanda's soccer / Andrew's lacrosse or
      another skill they're working on.
- [ ] **A pet mention** — Blazer the dog and/or Moonbeam the cat.
- [ ] **Describe the dog in Chapter 1.** (Blazer is basically a series mascot.)
- [ ] **Every few books, the pets are a KEY feature** of the plot (e.g. in Book
      1, Blazer's nose cracks the case).
- [ ] **A signature gadget** — increasingly important; becomes one-per-book in
      Arc 3. Gadgets are gifts from the parents' work trips (or from Uncle Jacob).
- [ ] **A hint of the next destination** near the end.

## 2. The story structure

The author's notes lean on three classic tools. Use them together:

### The Snowflake progression (how to develop a book)
1. **One sentence** — the whole book in a line.
2. **One paragraph** — setup, three disasters, resolution.
3. **Four paragraphs** — expand each sentence of the paragraph.
4. **Break paragraphs into scene/sequel beats** → chapter outline.

### The beat spine (what has to happen)
Adapted from the author's "PreSnowflake template" + Michael Hauge's 6-stage
structure (`archive/reference/` has the chart):

1. **Intro / setup** — an interesting hook based on location, a pet, a character,
   plus 1–3 atmospherics and 1–3 details of the new place.
2. **The problem appears** — arising from a main character or a local character.
3. **3–4 obstacles** that make the problem hard to solve (see §5).
4. **A technical tool** enters (a gift from the parents' conferences).
5. **3–4 clues** that lead toward the answer.
6. **2–4 red herrings** — false leads (e.g. a suspicious character who turns out
   innocent).
7. **3–4 risks** the twins must take to get past the obstacles.
8. **The point of no return** — usually the biggest risk.
9. **The major disaster** — the moment it looks like the case can't be solved.
10. **Final risk + resolution.**
11. **The secondary plot** resolves too.

### The J.K. Rowling technique (how to open scenes)
Start each section/chapter with **one small thing that leads to the point of the
section** — and let the first section plant a clue for a later one.

## 3. The spy-skills ladder

The twins learn harder skills as the series goes. Menu to draw from (the author's
"Uncle pays for the kids to learn cool skills" list, plus Book 1 additions):

- Observation & pattern recognition (Amanda's specialty) — established in Book 1
- Photography / documenting evidence (Andrew's tablet) — established in Book 1
- Drone piloting — established in Book 1 (via Kekoa)
- Time-lapse / night-vision / infrared imaging
- Following an electrical or scent trail
- Orienteering, GPS, and map-reading
- Basic hacking / working around security systems
- Lock-picking
- Motorbike & go-kart driving (jump-starting a car)
- Judo / Krav Maga (self-defense)
- Parachuting
- Scuba diving

**Rule of thumb:** the skill that solves the case should be *set up earlier in
the same book* (or an earlier book), never pulled out of nowhere.

## 4. Atmospherics menu (pick 2–3 per book)

- Sickness (someone catches a cold at the wrong time)
- Weather (a storm rolls in fast — used well in Book 1)
- A holiday
- A recent local event
- A sports event
- A new movie coming out
- A "first time" for something (excitement or fear)
- A parents' decision (buying a new car, a new rule)
- A new toy or gadget
- A pet event (dog is sick, bath day, the cat goes missing)

---

## 5. Obstacles menu (pick 3–4 per book)

- **Weather / natural event** — rain, snow, flooding, tsunami, (rarely) earthquake
- **A mistake by a main character** — forgot something, lost their temper, broke
  something
- **Something goes missing** — stolen, lost, or left behind
- **A pet or animal interferes** — the dog eats the paper with the answer; bees
  outside the window
- **A physical block** — locked door, fence, moat, a big open lawn to cross
- **A parents' decision** — can't go out at night, early bedtime, stay home and read
- **"We don't even know who?"** — the identity of the culprit is itself the puzzle

---

## 6. Plot-type menu (pick one core mystery per book)

- Animals being released or stolen (zoo/aquarium) — _basis of an early "Missing
  Turtles" concept; see archive_
- Missing artifacts / cultural theft — **used in Book 1**
- School or teacher supplies going missing (perhaps in a poorer community)
- The local water has gone bad — maybe no villain at all (natural or animal cause)
- A sports team loses its star player (lost while camping?)
- A missing person (a lost child)
- Extortion — someone shaking down a local business; no one will testify
- Jealousy — a character covets what someone else has
- Fraud / framing — something is destroyed and the wrong person is blamed
- Hacking — as a stand-alone plot or the method behind another crime
- A sports mascot goes missing _(flagged by the author as possibly too cliché)_

## 7. Secondary-plot menu (always run one)

- The dog won't eat
- Learning to do the laundry
- Trouble with homework
- A favorite sports team loses
- Making (or missing) a new friend in a place they'll soon leave
