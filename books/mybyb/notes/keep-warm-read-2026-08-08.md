## Part 1 — Critique

What this is: a gift-book permission slip with jokes — bought as a gag
by someone who survived their first decade, read for real by someone
starting theirs. That matches CONCEPT.md's own logline, so no gap at
the pitch level; the gap is procedural, and it's the whole story.

**Verdict: this book is not stalled on craft. It is stalled on a
signature.** The rebuilt Part I (`drafts/2026-07-26/`) is
publishable-grade gift-book prose that has sat thirteen days at a
single author gate — DRAFT-NOTES says plainly the old `reboot/01` is
"still authoritative until the author approves this rebuild." Every
downstream job (Part II briefs, lexicon rows, the review's three
fixes) queues behind that approval. Per VISION, MYBYB should be kept
warm, not forced; but "warm" still means the gate gets named, and this
is it.

The voice-first read (card C3) confirms the rebuild's biggest gain is
exactly where a spoken book lives: the narrator now has a *character*.
The original reboot ch. 1 had jokes; the v2 chapters have a person —
one who confesses ("So have I. Mine had *three* follow-up bullets,"
ch02:164–165), coaches ("One light per room. You're an operator, not a
lighthouse," ch01:134–135), and coins on demand: "It's a timestamp
with feelings" (ch02:49); "Calibration — a word that sounds like
science and works like weather" (ch03:62–63); "Hope is what patience
calls itself when nothing is scheduled" (ch03:78–79). Against
SUPERCONCEPTS: SC1 intact, SC2 planted and cashed ("It's free — and
that's the other secret in this book," ch01:100, paid off as ch. 4's
thesis), SC3 repaired (the group-chat retarget), SC4 measurably
better — all four chapters carry a box and a pull-quote.

Verifying the colleague's 2026-07-26 review against the page: findings
1–4 all hold. The trailer-voice endings are real and n=4/4 ("That's
chapter two. Bring a calculator," ch01:166); the ch. 1 mic-drop
regression is real (fifteen lines of scoreboard tease after "The final
boss has a health bar"); "Nobody's auditing" is spent in the Donut box
and twice more in ch. 4 before its pull-quote moment. One mild
dissent: of the two flagged "ghostwriter flavor" jokes, I'd keep the
wine critic ("the confidence of a wine critic who can't taste,"
ch01:159) — it's an office-adjacent status joke in the book's own
register — and kill only Escher (ch02:89), which is reference-humor
from a different shelf. And one **new problem** the review missed: the
narrator's smug-pause beat runs three times in four chapters — "Take a
second with it" (ch01:31), "I'll wait" (ch03:17), "Take your time.
I'll be here" (ch04:45). Each lands alone; at n=3 it's a tic. Ration
it like the "just…" ellipsis.

What's stalled beyond the gate: canon is now lagging the manuscript.
`LEXICON.md`'s Bell Curve row still reads "90% of what you see people
do is the wrong move" while ch. 4 drafts on the opposite — "not a
ranking of ability. It's a heat map of fear" (ch04:67–68). That is a
live draft-vs-canon conflict; house rule 1 says the doc wins until the
author updates it, which means the book's best structural save is
technically non-canon today. Same lag: the tax co-thesis has no
CONCEPT.md paragraph, SUPERCONCEPTS is still PROPOSED, the title is
still TBD, and the hottest brand assets ("Nobody's auditing," Cheap
Power, the tax) have no lexicon rows — THREADS S01 calls it right:
"the brand core is lagging the manuscript."

The honest gap between ambition and pages: CONCEPT promises 13–15
chapters; roughly six exist in any form, and the four best are
unadopted. Part II — the moves, the part ch. 4's menu explicitly
prices — has zero drafted words, and it's where the tax thesis must
prove it powers a whole Part rather than one chapter. Part III's two
existing chapters predate the voice spec: R-09 is the thinnest comedy
in the project ("Rockets don't launch without a second stage" is a
poster, not a joke), closes on a second "take the wheel" ("Then take
the wheel — or grab the rope"), and carries no release valve. The
flip-test ambition (SC4, ~100% of spreads) is now met in Part I and
unmeetable in the back half, because the back half isn't there to
flip to.
