## 2. The two refusals — both upheld

### K12 — CORRECT, on stronger evidence than the architect cited

The claim under test: ch04:132–133 scopes the price tag to the tax.

> Different aisles. / **One price, printed on every tag: zero,** /
> **plus the tax you are no longer paying.** / They'll look like
> separate skills. / They're the same purchase, repeated.

Ch. 4 denominates its whole economy in one currency and only one — the
tax (ch04:17–18, ch04:24, ch04:150–159). Effort is never priced
anywhere in it. The tag names exactly one cost and is silent on work,
and **silence is not a promise.**

The decisive citation sits a rung above the drafts and the architect
didn't use it. **CONCEPT.md:27–28 (LOCKED)** glosses the purchase
itself: *"Every move in Part II is the same purchase repeated: **stop
paying the tax**, pick the free power up off the floor."* Canon defines
the repeated purchase *as* the tax transaction. That settles the scope
question above the level of the draft.

Does the new line retcon anything? No. *"Chapter four promised that the
tax comes off"* is true (ch04:133, ch04:33). *"It never promised the
thing you pick up is light"* denies a promise never made. And the tag
is left standing rather than reinterpreted — *"The tag still reads
zero"* — which is the actual difference from revision 1, whose *"true
about nerve and not about effort"* WAS a re-reading. The escalation now
runs on a second axis (what you carry out) instead of reinterpreting
the first. D1's three-way pricing contradiction resolves as a side
effect.

**The original K12 was wrong.** It conflated "ch. 4 puts no scope on
the tag" with "ch. 4 claims the work is free." Ch. 4 claims neither.
