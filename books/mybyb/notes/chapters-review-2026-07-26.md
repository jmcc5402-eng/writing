# MYBYB — Review of the Rebuilt Part I (ch. 1–4)

**Date:** 2026-07-26
**Scope:** the four drafts in `drafts/2026-07-26/`, judged against
`docs/CONCEPT.md`, my 2026-07-26 critique, the snowflake brief, the
drafter's notes, and the `manuscript/reboot/` originals. Line numbers
cite the draft files.

## Verdict

Yes — better, and not narrowly. The original reboot was one excellent
chapter and three orphans; this is a four-chapter argument where each
chapter closes an escape route. Chapter 2 is the best new writing in
the project — the green-dot section (ch02:25–48) plays every scene it
names, and "It's a timestamp with feelings" (ch02:49) is a line the
original manuscript would be proud of. Chapter 3's mirror one-on-one
(ch03:19–45) does the hardest thing the concept demands — turns the
lens on the reader without scolding — and chapter 4 finally puts the
tax where I argued it belonged: load-bearing, with both canonical
quotes landed verbatim (ch04:21–22, 36–37). In absolute terms these
are publishable-grade gift-book chapters. One regression, named below.

## Voice: preserved, with sheen at the seams

Ch. 1 is conservative — the improv-scene bit, both tells, and the
vacuum run survive word-for-word, so there is no drift where it
mattered. The retargeted 90% line is actually an upgrade in voice:

- Old: "the 90% who just complain about this in the group chat"
  (reboot/01:35)
- New: "the 90% who saw exactly what you just saw — and took it back
  to the group chat, because standing out feels expensive" (ch01:93–95)

Same image, contempt swapped for recognition. That is the author's
trick, executed correctly.

The sheen lives in the *additions*, and the drafter knew it — the
notes flag Escher and the wine critic themselves (DRAFT-NOTES:183–185).
"Escher used to sketch structures like this. He at least knew they
couldn't hold weight" (ch02:89–90) and "the confidence of a wine
critic who can't taste" (ch01:159) are reference-humor; the native
register is office props ("a scarecrow for middle management,"
ch02:73 — perfectly in voice). "Like a shipwreck full of gold"
(ch02:63) is pretty in a way this book never was. Three lines, one
redline pass. Not a crisis, but real.

## Did it deliver the recommendations? Verified:

- **Tax promoted to co-thesis (rec 1):** yes. Planted in ch. 1
  ("It's free — and that's the other secret in this book," ch01:100)
  and cashed as ch. 4's thesis. The CONCEPT.md/LEXICON.md recordings
  remain open [TK]s — correctly left to the author.
- **Bell Curve reframe (rec 4):** yes, and it's the draft's best
  structural save. "The fat middle of the curve is not a ranking of
  ability. It's a heat map of fear" (ch04:67–68); no wrong-90%
  arithmetic anywhere. Note the standing canon conflict: LEXICON.md's
  row still reads "90% … the WRONG action" — flagged, not fixed.
- **Cheap Power in-line, list killed (rec 2):** four boxes placed
  in-line; the Pre-Break Pee rewrite obeys one image, one turn, one
  line out (ch04:188–202). But only two boxes are *new* — the six I
  asked for still owe four to Parts II–III.
- **Hope conflict (rec 5):** resolved by ruling, and it works —
  ch. 3 never re-argues the boss, it re-aims the tell (ch03:13–16).
- **Endings varied (rec 6):** yes — ledger, mirror, floor.

## New problems the rewrite introduced

1. **The trailer-voice ending.** All four chapters now close by
   advertising the next one: "That's chapter two. Bring a calculator"
   (ch01:166), "That word is about to become a problem" (ch02:184),
   "Next chapter, we do the math in public" (ch03:180). The
   escalation contract asked for a handoff, not a Netflix bumper. At
   n=4 it is already a template — the exact disease rec 6 treated,
   reinfected at the other end of the chapter.
2. **Ch. 1's ending is worse than the original.** The original ends
   on its best line — "The final boss has a health bar. Aim for it."
   (reboot/01:53). The rewrite appends fifteen lines of scoreboard
   tease *after* the mic-drop (ch01:151–166). Better chapter, worse
   last page.
3. **"Nobody's auditing" is spent before ch. 4 uses it.** It appears
   in the Donut box (ch03:210), then twice in ch. 4 (ch04:52, 79).
   The pull-quote debuts as an echo.
4. **Self-narration creep.** Ch. 4 opens with a three-line recap of
   chapters 1–3 (ch04:9–12); ch. 3 cross-references both prior
   chapters repeatedly. A gift book gets opened mid-book; these pages
   now read like episode four of a serial.

## Three highest-leverage next moves

1. **Cap every handoff at two lines, and end ch. 1 on the health
   bar.** Move the scoreboard tease above "The point" or cut to one
   sentence. Cheapest fix; protects the flip-open test the boxes won.
2. **Author redline session on the flagged seams** — Escher, wine
   critic, "Bring a calculator" — plus approval of the blocking
   group-chat retarget, and recording the co-thesis and Bell Curve
   reframe into CONCEPT.md/LEXICON.md before drift sets in.
3. **Write the remaining Cheap Power boxes now, and name the
   feature.** The Front Row (ch02:196–209) proves the formula is
   repeatable. Four to six more, Parts II–III placement, one sitting,
   while the form is hot.
