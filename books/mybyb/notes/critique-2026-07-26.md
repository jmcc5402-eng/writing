# MYBYB — Outside Read

**Date:** 2026-07-26
**Scope:** the reboot material (`manuscript/reboot/`, all four files),
judged against `docs/CONCEPT.md`. The 2014–2018 master was skimmed only,
as raw ore.

---

## Part 1 — Critique

### What this actually is, and who buys it

Not a self-help book. A permission slip with jokes. Ninety minutes, ~10k
words, and the buyer is almost never the reader — it's a 32-year-old who
survived their own first decade buying it for a 23-year-old, or one
coworker sliding it across a desk after a bad meeting. Two jobs, in
sequence: survive a four-second flip-open, then be good enough that the
recipient photographs a page and texts it. Everything below is judged
against those.

### Is the thesis load-bearing?

The Peter Principle open is a strong hook — but it's a *diagnosis*, not
a method. It explains Chapter 1, licenses part of Part II, then runs
out. "Hijack the Meeting," "Bump It Up," "Volunteer for the Grenade"
work identically under a good boss; none of them need the boss to be
faking anything. And the book walks away from its own premise twice, on
purpose: Chapter 9 opens "Not every boss is a loser," and the closer
suggests the real problem may be "the *game.*" Those are the two best
structural decisions in the project — and they leave the announced
thesis holding only the front third.

Meanwhile the actual load-bearing idea is sitting in a sidebar: *"the
fear of standing out is a tax the average pay every single day, and you
can just… not pay it"* (`cheap-power-moves.md`). That sentence covers
the vacuum move, the donut, the pre-break pee, the wakeboard bet, and
the boat. That's a spine. Faking-it is the hook; the tax is the thesis.
The hierarchy is currently inverted.

### Is the tone rule held?

Mostly, and when it holds it's excellent: "a person doing an improv
scene called 'In Charge,' and they are hoping nobody asks a follow-up
question." That line *is* the method — precise, aimed at behavior, zero
grievance. Same trick in "a faker just… hopes at you, from a distance,
and then acts betrayed when hoping doesn't ship the project." And "it
will make you insufferable at brunch. Worth it" is the release valve
that stops confidence curdling into smugness. There is nowhere near
enough of that valve.

Where it breaks: **"the 90% who just complain about this in the group
chat"** (ch. 01). That's aimed at the reader's peers, not at
incompetence — exactly what CONCEPT.md bans in writing. The bigger
version is inherited and still scheduled: the Difference Bell Curve runs
on "the bottom 75%" and "90% of the actions you see people taking are
likely the WRONG action." That framework is queued for Part II and is on
a collision course with the book's one guardrail. Note the
inconsistency — a joke was already pulled from `cheap-power-moves.md`
under this exact rule, while the framework that file calls its "blood
relative" goes untouched.

### Funny — and how consistently?

In bursts, and frontloaded. Chapter 1 is the funniest thing here and
would sell the book by itself. Chapter 9 has almost no jokes; "Rockets
don't launch without a second stage" is a motivational poster, not a
laugh. The closer runs on sincerity with one real laugh ("post a
screenshot of your resignation, and set your savings on fire"). A book
bought as a gag that turns earnest in its last third stops paying the
rent it charged at the register.

### Cheap Power as a device

The best invention in the project and the least developed. The Donut
Move is the strongest writing in the manuscript, and "The donut is just
the receipt" is its best line. The Pre-Break Pee is over-narrated —
three numbered points and a moral. An interstitial should be one image,
one turn, one line out; the moment it has a numbered list it's a
chapter. And two of these is a pilot, not a feature.

### As an object

Flip-open test: Chapter 1 passes in two sentences, and the Cheap Power
boxes sell themselves instantly. Chapter 9 mid-section gives you "a
deliberate, aggressive strategy that happens to look like being a great
teammate" — true, useful, indistinguishable from LinkedIn. Call it 40%
of pages that announce a funny book. For a four-second buy that needs to
be near 100%.

### Comps

The doc's are aging out: *The 4-Hour Workweek* is nineteen years old,
*Go the F\* to Sleep* fifteen. Both signal the author's shelf, not the
reader's. Honest current set: ***The Unspoken Rules*** (Ng, 2021), the
real substance competitor — and it's earnest, which is the gap; ***Ask a
Manager*** (Green), where this audience already lives; ***Adulting***
(Brown), the funny-gift-to-a-young-person lane; and ***Bullshit Jobs***
(Graeber), the intellectual cousin and sharpest contrast — Graeber says
the absurdity is crushing you, MYBYB says the absurdity is your opening.

### Verdict

Chapter 1 is real, and a book that opens like that gets read. The
problem isn't quality — it's hierarchy. The funniest, most ownable idea
is a sidebar; the announced thesis can't carry Parts II and III; and the
comedy thins exactly where the reader has to decide whether they're
taking it seriously. Fix the order of operations and this sells. Leave
it and it's an excellent first chapter followed by good career advice,
which is a different and much less buyable book.

## Part 2 — Recommendations

Highest leverage first. Cost is rough working time, not calendar.

### 1. Promote the tax to co-thesis. Keep faking-it as the hook.

**Change:** add "the fear of standing out is a tax you can stop paying"
to `CONCEPT.md` as the *retention* thesis, with "your boss is probably
faking it" named explicitly as the *acquisition* thesis. Plant it once
in ch. 01, right after "That gap is the whole opportunity."
**Why:** it's the only stated idea that covers all three Parts and the
closer. It converts Part II from "good advice" into "the same argument,
applied," and it retro-justifies Chapter 9 and the boat.
**Cost:** one doc paragraph, one manuscript paragraph. An hour.

### 2. Write six more Cheap Power moves and place them in-line.

**Change:** six new boxes, ~150 words each, dropped between chapters —
not collected in a separate file with commentary about where they go.
Move the "where these live" and backlog notes out of the manuscript
file. Rewrite the Pre-Break Pee without the numbered list.
**Why:** highest ratio of effect to effort in the project. It fixes the
flip-open test, creates the screenshot/social asset, adds ~1,000 words
of the most re-readable material in the book, and doubles as the
cheapest voice-lock exercise going — the boxes are pure voice with
nothing to hide behind.
**Cost:** one focused sitting, and the most fun work available.
**Rule to adopt:** one image, one turn, one line out. Never a list.
`[TK — do the Cheap Power boxes get a branded name on the cover?]`

### 3. Land the title. It is blocking, not deferrable.

Cover drives the gag-buy, and the title drives the cover, so this gates
the acquisition engine. Four directions, with candidates:

- **A — the game frame.** *The Final Boss Has a Health Bar* / *Your Boss
  Has a Health Bar.* Owns the freshest metaphor in the draft and is
  2026-native. Risk: cute rather than edgy; may not gag-buy.
- **B — the thesis, stated flat and rude.** *Your Boss Is Faking It* /
  *Everyone Above You Is Making It Up.* Great spine copy, reads as
  exposé, low gag energy.
- **C — the profane imperative** (the old title's energy, none of its
  baggage). *Take the F\*cking Wheel.*
- **D — the tax idea.** ***Nobody Is Auditing*** — lifted straight from
  the donut bit, funny, ownable, and it is the entire philosophy in two
  words. My front-runner to test.

**Recommendation:** shortlist two, mock them as real covers, show them
to a dozen 24-year-olds. Subtitle carries the useful promise, per the
surprise-and-delight model. `[TK — subtitle; author decision]`
**Cost:** an afternoon plus a poll.

### 4. Fix the Bell Curve's guardrail collision before drafting it

**Change:** reframe from "90% of people take the wrong action" to "90%
of people are scared." Same math, opposite target. Fix the group-chat
line in ch. 01 the same way. Note the decision in `LEXICON.md`.
**Why:** contempt for coworkers isn't funny, it's the one thing
CONCEPT.md forbids, and it kills giftability upward. Recognition is
funnier than contempt — the Cheap Power framing already proves the
author can do it.
**Cost:** under an hour. But it has to land before the chapter is
drafted, not after.

### 5. Draft the rest of Part I — after settling one canon conflict

**Finding:** CONCEPT.md specifies "Hope Is a Loser's Strategy" as
*helpful-not-hopeful, aimed at the reader.* Chapter 1 has already spent
Hope as one of its two faker tells, aimed at the boss. Chapter 3 will
either repeat itself or contradict the doc.
**Change:** pick one — cut Hope from ch. 01's tells and let ch. 01 run
on Things alone, or keep it and hard-pivot ch. 03 to hope as the
*reader's* failure mode. The doc points at the second; the draft
currently blocks it.
**Cost:** one paragraph cut, or one outline decision. Then draft both
chapters back to back in a single sitting so the voice sets.

### 6. Vary the endings, and make Part III pay comedy rent.

**Change:** three of four drafts close on a punch paragraph plus a
Monday move, and two land on a variant of "take the wheel." At n=4
that's a motif; at n=14 it's a template. Also set a floor for Part III —
no spread without a laugh — and audit ch. 09 against it.
**Why:** the back half is where a gift book gets abandoned, and it's
where this draft is most sincere and least funny.
**Cost:** trivial per chapter if decided now, expensive as a retrofit.

### 7. Refresh comps in `POSITIONING.md`

Swap in the four titles above, keep the 2014-predates-Manson note (a
real asset), and demote *4-Hour Workweek* to a one-line "energy of"
reference rather than a comp.
**Cost:** thirty minutes, pure pitch credibility.
