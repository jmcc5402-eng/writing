## 2. Canon conflict (a): Hope — ch01 tell vs. ch03 chapter

**Verified on the page.** Both halves of the critique's claim are accurate:

- CONCEPT.md spine: "Hope Is a Loser's Strategy *(helpful-not-hopeful,
  aimed at the reader)*" — the chapter's target is the READER.
- Reboot ch01 spends Hope as a boss-facing faker tell: "**They love
  Hope.**" … "Hope is what people do instead of managing."

**But this is not a doc-vs-draft contradiction — both uses are canon.**
LEXICON.md lists **Things vs. Hope** as a named framework whose home is
Ch. 1 ("Two crutches bosses lean on instead of solving problems …
Drafted (Ch. 1)"), and CONCEPT.md's "Reusable assets carried forward"
names "Things-vs-Hope" explicitly. So ch01 using Hope as a boss tell is
doing exactly what the canon assigns it. The conflict only exists if
ch03 *re-runs* boss-hope — which CONCEPT.md already forecloses by
aiming ch03 at the reader.

**Resolution (see Ruling 1):** keep both, differentiate targets. The
critique's option 1 (cut Hope from ch01, run on Things alone) would
break the LEXICON — Things vs. Hope is a two-legged framework and a
carried-forward asset; amputating Hope from it to protect ch03 is
backwards. The critique's option 2 is the one the docs point at, and it
is also structurally better: ch03 becomes the mirror chapter — *the
tell you learned to spot in your boss is also in you* (hoping to be
noticed, hoping the boss changes, hoping the promotion arrives — which
is where CONCEPT.md's "quiet quitting is the average person's trap"
thread lands naturally). Ch03 then flips the signature phrase: quote
"Bosses need to be helpful, not hopeful," then turn it — so do you, to
yourself. Ch01 needs zero changes for this; ch03 needs the pivot built
into its outline before drafting.

## 3. Canon conflict (b): peer-bite ban vs. the group-chat line

**Verified on the page.** CONCEPT.md (Tone, twice): "❌ Punching down at
people, coworkers, or any group" and "the bite always points at
*incompetence and broken systems* — never at people or identities."
Reboot ch01: "Here's the part that separates you from the 90% who just
complain about this in the group chat."

The line targets the reader's *peers* for their character ("just
complain") — coworkers, not incompetence, not a broken system. That is
inside the ban as written. The project has already enforced this exact
rule against its own material once: the cheap-power file's author note
pulled the "hit on the waitress/waiter" ending because it "trips the
one guardrail we set (the bite points at incompetence, never at
people)." The group-chat line trips the same wire; enforcement should
be consistent.

**Resolution (see Ruling 2):** retarget, don't delete. The 90% stat and
the status-comedy both survive if the target moves from contempt
(peers are complainers) to recognition (peers are scared — the fear of
standing out is the tax). Proposed shape, not final copy:

> the 90% who saw the same thing you just saw — and went back to the
> group chat about it, because standing out feels expensive. It isn't.
> It's free. That's the secret.

Same fix, same reason, applies to the **Bell Curve before ch04 is
drafted**: the inherited framing (master: "the lower 25% are crappy
performers"; "90% of the actions you see people taking are likely the
WRONG action") reads as contempt for coworkers. Reframe: 90% of people
are frozen by the standing-out tax, so the visible consensus move is
usually the scared move — do the opposite. Same math, target moved
from people to fear. This must be locked before drafting, and the
decision belongs in LEXICON.md's Bell Curve row (author edit — this
analysis touches no canon files).

## 4. Cheap Power interstitials — placement + two new moves

`cheap-power-moves.md` establishes the format: boxed, zero-cost,
deadpan, "genuinely doable Monday," each proving in miniature that
"Everyone is terrified of standing out. So standing out is free power.
Pick it up off the floor." The file's own placement note: both existing
moves are "blood relatives" of the Bell Curve chapter — "Use them as
interstitials near that chapter, and sprinkle more throughout."

### The two new moves (proposed copy, in the established format)

**The 5:01 Walk**

It's 5:01. You're done. Everyone is doing the thing where they stay
glued to a screen they've stopped reading, because the first person to
stand up loses — nobody knows what, but definitely something.

Stand up. Say goodnight. Use words. Walk out at a normal human speed.

Nothing happens. That's the punchline — there was never a prize for
sitting there, and everyone still seated is paying for a raffle with no
ticket. Do it three days running and watch the room recalibrate around
you, because you just told everyone the exits work. You didn't leave
early. You left. There's a difference, and only one of them was ever on
your badge.

**The Front Row**

All-hands, training, town hall: forty chairs, and the back two rows
fill first, like the front of the room is served with subpoenas.

Sit in the front row.

That's it. That's the move. It costs nothing, nobody stops you, and to
every person presenting — including the higher-ups — you are now the
one visibly awake face in a sea of laptop lids. They will remember you
and they will not know why. The back rows aren't lazy, they're scared;
the front row scares no one who's already decided not to pay the fear
tax. Best seat in the house, permanently unreserved.

*(Both drafts: bite aimed at fear and the room's frozen physics, never
at the seated people — recognition, not contempt. Guardrail-checked
against CONCEPT.md tone rules and the cheap-power author-note
precedent. "The 5:01 Walk" also plants the closer's "logs off at 5:01"
image early — free callback.)*

## Rulings

1. **Hope conflict: keep both, split the targets (critique option 2).**
   Ch01 keeps Hope as a boss-facing faker tell — that IS the canonical
   Things vs. Hope framework in its LEXICON-assigned home, carried
   forward by name in CONCEPT.md. Ch03 must not re-run boss-hope: per
   the CONCEPT.md spine ("helpful-not-hopeful, aimed at the reader") it
   pivots to the reader's own hope (waiting to be noticed, hoping the
   boss changes — the quiet-quitting trap), opens with a callback to
   ch01's tell, and spends the signature phrase "Bosses need to be
   helpful, not hopeful." verbatim before flipping it at the reader.
   Ch01 requires no edit for this. Do NOT cut Hope from ch01 (critique
   option 1) — it would amputate a two-legged canon framework.

2. **Group-chat line: violation confirmed; retarget, don't delete.**
   "the 90% who just complain about this in the group chat" (ch01) aims
   the bite at coworkers, which CONCEPT.md bans in writing, and which
   the project already enforced once (cheap-power author note pulling
   the waitress joke). Keep the 90% and the status-comedy; move the
   target from contempt to fear ("scared to stand out," not
   "complainers"). Apply the identical reframe to the Difference Bell
   Curve BEFORE ch04 is drafted: 90% frozen by the standing-out tax,
   not "90% crappy/wrong." Author to record the reframe in LEXICON.md's
   Bell Curve row.

3. **Lexicon placement for ch1–4 is fixed as follows.** Ch01: Things
   vs. Hope (done) + land "You rise to your highest level of
   incompetence" quotably, attributed. Ch02: no mandated term; Slack
   performance-theater is the native thread; may foreshadow but never
   name/define BIU (reserved for its Part II chapter). Ch03: "helpful,
   not hopeful" verbatim + flip. Ch04: Difference Bell Curve (reframed
   per Ruling 2) + "To change, you must do things differently."

4. **Reserved terms stay out of ch1–4:** CFC, BLUF, NR (home: Manage
   Up), BIU (home: Bump It Up chapter), 80/20 intangibles (home:
   Grenade), Stagecoach/Olympic Rings/Compass (Parts II–III). One
   framework, one home — the LEXICON's value is that each term has a
   single canonical definition site.
