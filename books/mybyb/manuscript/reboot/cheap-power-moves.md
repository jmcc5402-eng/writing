# Cheap Power (recurring feature)

Little zero-cost power moves, scattered between chapters as boxed interstitials. Each one proves the book's core mechanic in miniature:

> **Everyone is terrified of standing out. So standing out is free power. Pick it up off the floor.**

These are the screenshot-bait — the bits people text each other, which is the whole gag-gift / word-of-mouth engine. Keep them short, deadpan, and genuinely doable Monday.

---

### The Donut Move

There's a box of donuts at the meeting. Nobody's touching it. Everyone's waiting for some invisible permission that is never coming.

Be the first to take one.

Congratulations — you are now the most relaxed, highest-status person in the room, and you achieved it with a pastry. Everyone silently thanks you, because taking the first donut gives *them* permission too. You didn't just grab a snack; you unfroze the whole table.

And the donut isn't even the point. If you're gluten-free, take one anyway and stuff it in your laptop bag. Nobody's auditing. The move is being the person who goes first. The donut is just the receipt.

---

### The Pre-Break Pee

At a long meeting or an all-day conference, stand up and leave about **five minutes before** the scheduled break.

Three things happen, all good:

1. **If you have to pee, you pee.** Revolutionary. Wildly underrated. Your bladder is not a team player and pretending otherwise helps no one.
2. **Everyone watches you get up and leave — and is quietly impressed.** Not because leaving is impressive, but because they are all terrified of standing out, and you just did it like it was nothing. You made a choice in a room full of people waiting for permission.
3. **When the real break hits, you're already free.** Your body's business is handled, so you get the full fifteen minutes to do something that actually matters: work the room, knock out real work, eat something, or call your mom.

You didn't break a rule. There is no rule. That's the joke — and the point.

The move still lands hard without it. Easy to restore or reshape if you disagree — your call.

## Where these live in the book

Both are blood relatives of **"Do the Exact Opposite of Everyone"** (the Bell Curve chapter) and the broader thesis that *the fear of standing out is a tax the average pay every single day, and you can just… not pay it.* Use them as interstitials near that chapter, and sprinkle more throughout.
