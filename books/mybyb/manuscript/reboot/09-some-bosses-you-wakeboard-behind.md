# Some Bosses You Beat. Some You Wakeboard Behind.

*Part III — Play the Decade*

Everything up to now has been about the beatable boss — the faker, the hoper, the one whose health bar is sitting on the floor. So here's the asterisk, and it's a big one:

**Not every boss is a loser. Some bosses are rockets. And you do not race a rocket — you strap in.**

If you spend your whole first decade treating *every* boss as an obstacle to overcome, you'll miss the single fastest way to climb that exists: getting towed.

## The wakeboard

You don't beat the boat. That's not how wakeboarding works. The boat has the engine. You grab the rope, let it pull you across the water, and — this is the part people forget — *you're the one doing the tricks.* The boat does the grunt work. You get the ride, the air, and the highlight reel.

A good boss is the boat.

If your boss is (1) tolerable, (2) genuinely ambitious and clearly *going* somewhere, and (3) sitting on top of a job you actually like — then being their indispensable number two is not settling. It's the smartest bet on the table. Their ambition is coming out of their ears? Good. Let it pull you across three levels of water you could not have crossed alone, or at least not this fast.

## How to ride the wave (on purpose)

Riding the wave is not passive. It's a deliberate, aggressive strategy that happens to look like being a great teammate:

- **Make them succeed on purpose.** Anticipate the thing they're about to drop. Cover the blind spot before it costs them. Hand them the win already gift-wrapped.
- **Become the person they can't get promoted without.** When the org tries to move your boss up, the first question is "who backfills them?" The correct and delicious answer is *you.* Rockets don't launch without a second stage.
- **Make them look good in the room.** Not with flattery — with competence they get to take upstairs. Their rise is your elevator.

In the faker chapter, you walked into the vacuum to expose a boss who couldn't fill it. Same move, opposite goal: here you fill the vacuum *for* a boss who's worth it — and get towed up the wake while you do.

## Gas pedal vs. ride the wave

This is the actual skill. Not "beat your boss." Not "serve your boss." **Read which situation you're in, then pick the move.**

- **Hit the gas** when your boss is an anchor — a faker going nowhere. Overcome, route around, outshine. (That's most of this book.)
- **Ride the wave** when your boss is a boat — competent, ambitious, climbing. Amplify, backfill, get towed.

Get it backwards and you lose either way. Flooring the gas against a rocket just annoys a winner and burns you out. Grabbing the rope behind an anchor ties you to something that's sinking — and it'll pull you under when it goes.

## This is not bootlicking

Let's be crystal clear, because the difference is everything:

- **Bootlicking** is fear-driven, aimed at a loser, and it costs you your spine for nothing. The boss goes nowhere; you went there with them.
- **Riding the wave** is confidence-driven, aimed at a winner, and it's a cold investment with an exit. You're not scared of them. You picked them. And the second the wave flattens — they plateau, they stall, they turn out to be a fraud after all — you let go of the rope and swim to a faster boat. No loyalty owed. It was a bet, not a marriage.

## The point

Your boss is a video game boss *or* your boss is a jet ski. Your first job, before any move, is to figure out which.

Then take the wheel — or grab the rope.

Are they getting handed bigger things, or quietly smaller ones? Rocket or anchor. Once you know, you know which pedal you're on.
