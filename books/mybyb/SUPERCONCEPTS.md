# Super Concepts — MYBYB

The 3–4 concepts that make this book win. Not the outline — the reasons a
stranger buys it, gifts it, and quotes it. Every chapter must *value* them:
not necessarily incorporate, never cheapen. Editorial agents judge chapters
against this file.

Status: **PROPOSED** — extracted from `docs/CONCEPT.md` and the 2026-07
critique/review evidence. The author locks each; wording is the author's.

---

## SC1 — The liberating diagnosis

"Your boss is probably faking it." The acquisition hook: it names what the
reader suspects and turns dread into comedy in one move. It's why the book
gets picked up, gifted, and read aloud in the first ten seconds.

**Chapter test:** does this chapter keep the diagnosis liberating — funny,
freeing, actionable — rather than letting it curdle into grievance?
**Today:** honored; ch09 and the closer complicating it ("not every boss is
a loser") is the concept maturing, not breaking.

## SC2 — The standing-out tax

The fear of standing out is a tax the average pay every day — and you can
just stop paying it. The retention thesis: the idea that spans all three
Parts and the one readers act on Monday morning.

**Chapter test:** does the chapter's advice reduce to "stop paying the tax"
— and does it say so in the book's language?
**Today:** promoted to co-thesis in the v2 draft (ch01 plant, ch04 payoff);
still owed its `LEXICON.md` row and its `CONCEPT.md` paragraph.

## SC3 — Amused confidence, never anger

The tone is the product. The reader looks *down* at a comically beatable
obstacle, never up at an oppressor; bite points at incompetence and systems,
never people, never the reader, never their peers. This is what makes it
giftable — including upward, to a boss with a sense of humor.

**Chapter test:** could the reader's actual boss read this page and laugh?
Is anyone punched who didn't earn it structurally?
**Today:** one canon violation caught and repaired (the group-chat line);
the Bell Curve must be built on "scared, not wrong" or it breaks this.

## SC4 — Wins the flip-test

A gift book is bought open at a random page. Any spread must land a laugh
or a screenshot-worthy line within ten seconds — Cheap Power boxes,
pull-quotes, and "Nobody's auditing" are this concept's machinery.

**Chapter test:** open this chapter anywhere: is something on that page
funny or quotable on its own, out of context?
**Today:** measured at ~40% of pages pre-rewrite; v2 draft improves it;
six more Cheap Power moves remain the cheapest lift in the project.
