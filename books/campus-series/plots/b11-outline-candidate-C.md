## Snowflake spine

### One sentence

When the booster bankrolling half of Ashford schedules the old
Hartwell House for demolition, the sunshine admin of twelve thousand
football parents launches a petition that lands on the desk — and
against the heart — of the one grieving man whose signature can
condemn the House or save it.

### One paragraph

Marisol Vega [CHECK: surname [TK] — "Vega" proposed, used only here]
moves her son into the dorms, opens her phone to twelve thousand
parents, and finds a photo of orange survey stakes in the Hartwell
House garden — so she starts a petition, and discovers the man
pulling those stakes up with his bare hands is the facilities
director whose crew the expansion feeds and whose late wife the House
remembers. **Disaster 1 (25%):** she learns her petition isn't aimed
at Rex Boyd at all — it's aimed at Walt, because only his signature
can condemn the House, and signing is the price of his crew's
payroll. **Disaster 2 (50%):** the night Boyd's sock accounts seize
her board with a "neutrality" vote, Walt's name surfaces on the
preliminary assessment — and when she asks him at the town hall, on
the record, whether he'll sign, he can't answer. **Disaster 3
(75%):** the assessment leaks as if it were the condemnation; the
pledge drive freezes, the town mourns a building that isn't dead, and
Boyd books the trustees for rivalry Friday. **Resolution:** found,
not wished — her receipts break the astroturf and convert the
petition into a funded restoration ledger; his thirty years of
maintenance logs prove the House repairable; and the signature
everyone fought over is finally given — to the other document. The
House wins a stay, the crew wins eighteen months of skilled work
[TK: duration], and Boyd, publicly gracious, turns to the winter
survey. The fight is worth having now. So are they.

### The four quarters

(No locked Q1–Q4 text exists for 1.1; these are proposed.)

**Q1 (Ch 1–8) — The clipboard and the stamp.** A woman with an empty
minivan and a full phone starts a petition at a barbecue joint's cash
register; a man with a dead wife's memorial to maintain and forty
paychecks to protect starts pulling survey stakes out of a garden at
dawn. They keep colliding — the market, the boiler room, the bar his
crew drinks at — until he finally says the thing out loud: *your
petition isn't aimed at Boyd. It's aimed at me.* Maps: Opportunity
Ch 3, Change of Plans Ch 8.

**Q2 (Ch 9–15) — Allies in public, more in private.** Petition
season: Fifth-Sunday dinners, a homecoming gala where somebody swaps
two seating cards, five thousand names on butcher paper, a first kiss
that neither of them budgets for. Then October does what Boyd paid it
to: the assessment lands in Walt's drawer, and the same night the
sock accounts vote Marisol's board "neutral," a screenshot puts
Walt's name on the House's death warrant. Maps: midpoint coup Ch 15.

**Q3 (Ch 16–23) — On the record.** She asks him in front of the whole
town whether he'll sign, and his silence costs them both everything
public. So they go to work in private — she hunts the sock network
with receipts, he reads thirty years of logbooks that say the House
is repairable — until Boyd leaks the draft as a done deal and the
town gives up on a building that was never condemned. Her lowest
2 a.m. ends on his own rule: *paper isn't true till it's stamped.*
Maps: PONR Ch 16, Major Setback Ch 22–23.

**Q4 (Ch 24–30) — The other document.** The payroll side and the
petition side meet in the same barroom and discover they're the same
side: restoration is work, and work is jobs. On rivalry Friday the
trustees walk the House with two papers on the table — Boyd's
condemnation, unsigned, and a rehabilitation scope with a pledge
ledger behind it — and Walt spends the signature Boyd needed on the
one that keeps the lights on. A stay, not a victory; a beginning, not
a wedding. Boyd smiles for the cameras and reschedules. Maps: Climax
Ch 27–28.

### Ch 1 — Stakes in the Garden (Marisol)
Move-in Saturday: minivan empty, phone full — twelve thousand parents
and one photo that stops her cold: orange survey stakes in the
Hartwell House garden. Marisol wants one hour of peace before the
empty house gets loud; instead she walks up Millrow to see for
herself and finds a big quiet man in a facilities shirt yanking the
stakes out like weeds while a speckled dog supervises. He won't give
his name, his reasons, or an inch — but he hands her a stake like
evidence. Something in town has already started without asking
anybody. MEM: the facilities director pulling survey stakes bare-
handed at dawn. SPINE: Boyd's September survey line, met on page one.

### Ch 2 — The Gateway (Walt)
At the booster kickoff [TK venue: proposed — the fairgrounds
exhibition hall], Boyd unveils the Gateway to a roomful of applause,
and Walt does the math nobody claps for: the render's footprint eats
the House his wife's fellowship called home, and the capital plan
under it funds his crew's payroll. He wants to sit down with Boyd
like colleagues; what he gets is a shoulder squeeze and "big fall
coming, Walt." At the motor-pool yard after, Gasket won't come out
from under the truck while Boyd's PR man is on the lot — the dog
grades him accurately. Walt keys the House boiler for fall like every
year. This year it feels like defiance. MEM: Gasket refusing to
surface for the man in pressed khakis. SPINE: the expansion's money
and Walt's payroll are the same water.

### Ch 3 — The Clipboard (Marisol)
The board wants somewhere to put its feelings, and Marisol builds it

sock account — *AshfordDad4Safety*, day-old, oddly fluent — posting
"safety first, sentimentality second" under her announcement. She
screenshots it. Habit. The habit will matter. MEM: the petition
clipboard beside the register like a second guest book. SPINE:
