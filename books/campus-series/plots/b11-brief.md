## Hard requirements (all three candidates)

1. **Shape:** chapter-by-chapter, 28–32 chapters (55–70k words at
   ~2k/chapter). Each entry: one short paragraph written FOR A
   STRANGER — momentum and stakes, never a beat inventory — naming
   POV (dual, alternating; Marisol and Walt), what the POV character
   wants, what blocks them, what changes.
2. **Standard 24 made visible:** each chapter entry ends with two
   tagged lines — `MEM:` the chapter's memorable thing in one line,
   and `SPINE:` where the chapter touches the main story. A chapter
   that can't fill both lines doesn't belong in the outline.
3. **The strand budget, declared up front:** one side romance (the
   sweep proposes the flower-truck owner × the stadium
   groundskeeper [TK names — propose them]); one subplot (the
   B-story: the admin war — Boyd's sock accounts vs. Marisol's
   moderator seat); one or two red herrings (invent them; every one
   goes in the fair-play table WITH the honest signal that clears
   it); one running totem (the facilities motor-pool shop dog
   [TK name — propose it; NOT the late wife's dog]). Side-romance
   beats happen inside main-story scenes — two town rooms meeting —
   never in detours.
4. **Apparatus below the prose, in labeled blocks:** Hauge
   turning-point map (10/25/50/75/90); the fair-play table (every
   plant → its payoff chapter, red herrings included); STATE IN/OUT
   chain (day/weather/who-knows-what/where the report physically
   is); the heat beats marked `[HEAT — per TK steam ladder]` at
   placement only.
5. **The town does real work:** the petition clipboard at Delmar's,
   the Liars' Table, WDSS, the Curb Market, the Blind Mule (where
   Walt's crew drinks — the payroll side of the fight), the
   Fifth-Sunday circuit. SC1's chapter test applies to every entry.
6. **Canon guards:** Rules v1 (no students on stage; kids via
   texts/calls only); the bench seed (Dan and Aisha argue at the
   town hall — one scene, plot-relevant); the matchmaker touch
   (one deniable beat: the seating-card swap [identity stays TK]);
   grief with weight in a bright register (Walt's widowhood: few
   scenes, real weight, never pervasive gloom); the
   petition-targets-his-signature power dynamic named on the page.
7. **Resolution discipline:** found, not wished. The payroll answer
   (restoration contract employs the crew) must be planted early
   enough to be fair and arrive by work, not luck. Boyd is NOT
   beaten in this book — the set finale (1.4) does that; 1.1 wins
   the House a stay and the town a fight worth having.
8. **[TK]s flagged, never filled.** Names, dates, and facts not in
   the sources appear as `[TK ...]`.

## What the judge will score (1–5 each)

1. Register: bright book that still lands grief with weight
2. The couple: obstacle survives the five-minute honest talk all
   book (his payroll vs. her petition — structural to the end)
3. Standard 24: memorable-per-chapter AND simple spine (the MEM/
   SPINE lines audited, not taken on faith)
4. The town as character (SC1 test across the outline)
5. Fair play: every payoff planted; red herrings declared and
   honestly cleared
6. Hauge/beat structure: the ratified midpoint/PONR/dark/resolution
   delivered and earned
7. Strand discipline: side romance and subplot tethered; no detours
8. Write-for-a-stranger prose: the outline reads like a story
