## What you are writing

Chapter 2 of a contemporary small-town romance for readers 35–45.
POV: **Cal Sutter**, third person limited, past tense. Length:
**1,800–2,300 words**. Monday after the first home game.

## Open from wanting

Cal wants a quiet season: mowers running, dorms watertight, WDSS on
the shop radio, and nobody needing him to attend anything. The
chapter is what happens to that want.

## The shape (ratified outline — the law of the chapter)

1. **His kingdom, established with pleasure.** The motor pool on a
   Monday: the crew, the radio, and Ratchet — the shop dog with her
   name stenciled on her own parking spot — grading everyone who
   crosses the yard. Cal's competence and the town's reliance on him
   should be a joy to watch (the reader should want him by watching
   him work).
2. **The block.** A calendar invite he never accepted: a "condition
   survey of Millrow structures." Cal's position on meetings is
   roughly Ratchet's position on bath day — right up until he reads
   which structures, and his stomach drops on the same line both
   times.
3. **The turn, and the secret.** His evening round at Hartwell House
   — boiler, gutters, and a staircase he doesn't look at directly —
   tells the READER what no one in town knows: he tends this
   building off the clock, and not for the university. WHY is not
   revealed, not hinted at in words, not named. The staircase beat
   stays unexplained. This is the book's held secret.

## Register (author rulings — quote-level authority)

- **Gilmore calibration:** upbeat and light; banter-forward; grief
  and loneliness may appear only as a brief chord, never the
  chapter's weather — a laugh lives near every ache.
- **Modern calibration:** modern small-town texture (group texts,
  a delivery app, playlists) without anything obviously-2026: no
  brands-of-the-moment, no politics, no phone models.
- **Cal reads WANTED.** Description canon (author-ruled): light
  brown hair the sun has been working on, the slightest touch of
  gray just arriving at the temples. Never "big and grumpy" — his
  grumpiness lives in behavior and dialogue only; the description
  is Robert-Redford-golden: capable, quiet, unhurried, funnier than
  he lets on.
- **College words:** salt ONE or TWO of these naturally through
  adult mouths (never a student on stage): syllabus week, drop/add
  deadline, work orders from the dorms, move-in aftermath,
  parents' weekend (upcoming), the bursar. A seasoning, not a list.
- **No heat beats in this chapter.** Attraction texture is not in
  this chapter's shape; his want is quiet and his Monday is full.
