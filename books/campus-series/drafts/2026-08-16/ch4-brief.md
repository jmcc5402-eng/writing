## What you are writing

Chapter 4 of a contemporary small-town romance for readers 35–45.
POV: **Cal Sutter**, third person limited, past tense. Length:
**1,800–2,300 words**. The Monday after chapter 3. The antagonist's
first scene — it must be FUN to read, not procedural: a charm
offensive in a motor pool, watched by a dog who isn't buying it.

## Open from wanting

What Cal wants this chapter is small and entirely his: Boyd off his
yard before the crew inside hears twelve names discussed like line
items — and one wrong-size crescent wrench off his bench, which he
has been meaning to return for two weeks and keeps not returning, a
fact he declines to examine.

## The shape (ratified outline — the law of the chapter)

1. **Boyd arrives in person.** Rex Boyd, the booster behind the
   stadium expansion: charming, reasonable, expensively casual, a
   PR man in tow who is built entirely of greens powder and
   optimism and whose offered jerky Ratchet refuses on principle.
2. **The arithmetic, laid out kindly — which is the worst way.**
   The expansion gift plugs the winter hole in the facilities
   budget that otherwise becomes twelve names on a layoff list —
   and Cal would hold the pen on that list too.
3. **The vise (get this exactly right).** The House isn't the
   university's to condemn — Millrow belongs to the trust — but no
   trust sells the town's porch without cover, and "unsalvageable,"
   over the facilities director's signature on a university
   condition survey, is the cover Boyd's gift is waiting on. The
   finding will want Cal's name before the trustees meet in
   November. None of this is delivered as a lecture: Boyd makes it
   sound like a favor between practical men.
4. **Cal's line, and the block.** Cal wants the House left out of
   it. What blocks him is math he can't unsee — twelve names, real
   people, his pen either way.
5. **The exit line.** Boyd leaves it on the yard like a business
   card: the House is "deferred maintenance with a porch."
6. **The button.** Alone afterward, Cal picks the wrench up, and
   puts it back down.

## Register (author rulings — quote-level authority)

- **Gilmore calibration:** upbeat and light even here — the menace
  arrives wearing perfect manners, and the comedy (the PR man,
  Ratchet's verdicts, the crew's eavesdropping) lives in the same
  scene as the squeeze. A laugh lives near every ache.
- **Modern calibration:** modern texture (the PR man's supplements
  evangelism, a tablet full of renderings) — never obviously-2026.
- **Cal reads WANTED.** Description canon: light brown hair, the
  slightest touch of gray at the temples; grumpiness in behavior
  and dialogue only; funnier than he lets on.
- **Villain rule:** Boyd is charming and never stupid; his offer
  would tempt a good man, and the sentence he wants is not even
  quite a lie. No mustache-twirling.
- **College words:** salt ONE or TWO naturally (adult mouths only):
  the athletic department, boosters, parents' weekend, work-study
  crews, the bursar. A seasoning, not a list.
- **No heat beats.** Marisol appears only as the wrench and the
  not-examining.
