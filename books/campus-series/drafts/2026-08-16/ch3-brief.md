## What you are writing

Chapter 3 of a contemporary small-town romance for readers 35–45.
POV: **Marisol**, third person limited, past tense. Length:
**1,800–2,300 words**. The Saturday after chapter one (an away-game
weekend — the town at its own scale).

## Open from wanting

Marisol wants nothing more complicated than a Saturday errand run —
and spends it discovering that watching one particular man cross a
market is apparently a town-wide hobby she has now joined.

## The shape (ratified outline — the law of the chapter)

1. **The market, and the man who believes he is invisible.** Curb
   Market Saturday: Cal Sutter crosses the shed carrying Verna
   Poteat's bushel basket through the boiled-peanut steam, steadies
   the Dillards' wobbling table on his way past, tips two fingers
   at somebody's baby, and pays for his tomatoes like a man who
   believes he is invisible — while Marisol stands at the zinnia
   buckets being one of the half of the market that watches.
   "Been like that for years," says Earlene, unasked. (No
   conversation between the leads this chapter — the watching IS
   the beat, and Marisol watching should be confident and amused,
   not moony.)
2. **The picture.** Then it arrives, phone to phone: a tidy
   architect's lawn where the House currently stands, captioned
   "pending trustee approval at the November board meeting."
   November lands on the whole market at once.
3. **The clipboard.** By dusk a clipboard sits beside Delmar's
   register — pickle jar of pens, one pen zip-tied — and the town
   is already calling it the House's second guest book. Ray signs
   it without being asked.
4. **The quiet mistake (play it light — it must not read as a
   mistake).** Between errands, being helpful, Marisol waves four
   hundred shiny new member requests through her parents'-group
   queue — which will matter more than anything else she does
   today. The chapter must NOT wink at this; it is one busy-thumbs
   beat inside a warm day.

## Register (author rulings — quote-level authority)

- **Gilmore calibration:** upbeat and light; banter-forward town
  chatter; a laugh lives near every ache.
- **Modern calibration:** modern small-town texture — the group
  chat going off, a screenshot traveling phone to phone, playlists,
  seltzers — never obviously-2026: no brands-of-the-moment, no
  politics, no phone models.
- **Marisol is SEXY.** Confident, quick, a woman who knows the room
  watches her too and enjoys it. Desirable first, den-mother never.
  Her watching Cal is appetite with a raised eyebrow, not longing.
- **Cal from her eyes:** light brown hair, the slightest touch of
  gray at the temples; capable, unhurried, quietly watched by half
  the market. Never an unattractive adjective.
- **College words:** salt ONE or TWO of these naturally through
  adult mouths (never a student on stage): parents' weekend
  (coming), FAFSA night, drop/add, the walk-on's travel squad, the
  board's thread titles. A seasoning, not a list.
- **No heat beats.** Charged watching only; the leads do not speak
  to each other this chapter.
