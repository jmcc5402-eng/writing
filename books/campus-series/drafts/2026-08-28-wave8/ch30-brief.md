4. **Evening, the porch, the mug.** The RV city folds itself up

   Marisol is here. **He hands her a mug of something hot
   without being asked** — and NOBODY comments the echo; the
   reader carries it alone. Let them have five quiet lines of
   ordinary evening first; the town's noise below is the
   band's warm-up, not the subject.
5. **THE THIRD STEP (the book's last withheld thing).** He sets
   the mug down, takes her hand, and walks her through the
   scaffolding to the staircase — to the third step, worn pale
   by a century of other people's nerve, where nobody in this
   book has stood two at a time. **Nobody kneels.** He stands
   on it WITH her and says the clumsy sentence he has been
   leveling in his head since August — **the word itself, out
   loud — "love," in his own construction — no podium, no town,
   just her.** Keep the sentence clumsy the way a careful man's
   only speech is clumsy; one sentence, maybe two; the book's
   restraint everywhere else is what this buys.
   **His third full smile — the door swung wide on the lit
   room — fires HERE, on or beside the step, for her alone: the
   last one in the book.**
6. **Her answer (M1's echo — the book's final beat).** She
   smiled at the whole town once, from a dance floor; this one
   is **for him alone** — the echo of the first, private where
   that one was public. And she says it back **"without one
   clumsy thing about it"** — her interiority may carry (in
   HER register refracted through his POV: what he can see) the

   over, and somewhere between the smoke and this step she
   stopped wanting time to move at all. (Cal's POV holds —
   stage what he can see and hear; her exact interior belongs
   to the page only as far as his eyes earn it.)
