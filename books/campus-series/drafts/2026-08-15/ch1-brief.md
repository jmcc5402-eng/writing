# Chapter 1 drafting brief — Book 1.1, "Third-Largest City"

Blind competition, register pilot. You are one of several independent
drafters; you will not see the other candidates, and you must not
read any repo file other than this brief. Everything you may use is
in this document.

## What you are writing

Chapter 1 of a contemporary small-town romance for readers 35–45.
POV: **Marisol**, third person limited, past tense. Length: **1,800–
2,300 words**. This is the first page of a sixteen-book series; the
chapter's job is to make a reader feel, by page five, *this book is
already paying*.

## Open from wanting

Marisol wants one Saturday that belongs to her and not to the year
she just survived. Everything else — the town, the man, the series —
arrives through that want.

## The shape (ratified outline — the law of the chapter)

1. **The montage opener (2–3 pages).** The book opens with a montage
   of the first home game day: the camera jumping cut to cut as
   Ashford wakes and triples — the bars filling, the RV city grown
   overnight on the fairgrounds, charcoal lit by eight, drumline
   under everything. Woven inside the montage, one **steam flash**:
   a couple, any couple, charged and kissing — the roving camera
   never claims them as anyone, because the TOWN is the couple on
   page one. Heat calibration is the series' closed-door line:
   charged, clothed, cut. The montage's final cut lands on Marisol
   in the middle of her tent — and the collision below.
2. **The collision.** Marisol is running the parents' welcome tent —
   three hundred name tags, a sheet cake in team colors, and one
   canopy that will not stay up. The third time it folds, a man in a
   green facilities polo plants one boot, resets the frame in two
   motions, and turns to vanish — except this time she's faster.
   The first exchange **crackles**: he keeps her wrench, she keeps
   his gaze a beat too long, and neither of them wins. Attraction is
   on the page in the first exchange or the chapter fails.
3. **The warm beat, mid-chapter.** At the north gate: Birdie Pham's
   flower truck rolls in, and Eli Fountain arrives with two coffees
   and the worst small talk in town. A couple in the making the
   reader gets to keep.
4. **The name.** "That's Cal Sutter," Earlene reports, like weather.
5. **The close.** Marisol spends the whole second half scanning
   ninety thousand people for one green polo, and drives home with
   the windows down for the first time since the divorce.

## Register (author rulings — quote-level authority)

- **Gilmore calibration:** upbeat and light; banter-forward,
  quick-witted town chatter; grief and loneliness may appear as a
  brief chord, never the chapter's weather — a laugh lives near
  every ache.
- **Modern calibration:** 80s-college-movie *energy*, modern
  *texture* — seltzers in a cooler, a group chat going off, a
  delivery driver defeated by the RV city, playlists. Never
  obviously-2026: no brands-of-the-month, no politics, no phone
  models.
- **Marisol is SEXY.** Confident, quick, a woman who knows the room
  watches her and enjoys it; the banter bites back; wanting is on
  the page. Desirable first, den-mother never. No parkas, no
  casseroles, no frazzled-mom comedy at her expense.
- **Cal is a Robert Redford type.** Weathered-golden, capable, quiet
  charisma. Grumpy is fine; unattractive never.
- **Closed door.** Nothing past the first piece of clothing on the
  ground — and in chapter 1, nothing close: the montage steam flash
  is charged, clothed, cut.

## Fact manifest (the ONLY facts you may state)

- Marisol: 43; divorced about a year; two kids just left for
  college; the house went loud with quiet; she runs Ashford's
  twelve-thousand-member online parents' group and the game-day
  welcome tent. **No surname on the page** (unresolved canon).
- Cal Sutter: 47; the university's facilities director; green
  facilities polo; widowed (NOT revealed in this chapter — nothing
  about his past may appear here beyond the town treating him as a
  fixture).
- Earlene: market/tent chorus, reports facts "like weather."
- Birdie Pham: owns the flower truck. Eli Fountain: stadium
  groundskeeper. Not yet a couple.
- Ashford: a Southern college town; on game days it triples into
  the third-largest city in the state; ninety-thousand-seat
  stadium; Delmar's barbecue (hickory smoke); fairgrounds RV city;
  drumline and cicadas; August heat you wear.
- Do NOT name the university, the team, the mascot, or team colors
  ("team colors" as a phrase is fine). Do not mention Hartwell
  House, boosters, petitions, or any plot machinery — none of it
  has happened yet.
- A fact not on this list may not appear except as `[TK ...]`, and
  never inside dialogue or at an emotional peak.

## Hard walls (series safety rules)

- No student is named, speaks, or is focalized; students exist only
  in aggregate (crowds, a swarm, a drumline). The steam-flash couple
  must read unmistakably ADULT (thirties at youngest), and the beat
  is staged in adults-only geography (a porch, a tailgate of
  gray-hairs, a bar doorway).
- No named student. No kids of the leads on the page.
