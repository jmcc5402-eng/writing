## The law (frozen beats — each an EVENT someone causes)

1. **The summons: the empty stadium.** Boyd brings Cal to the
   bowl on a weekday — wind in the girders, ninety thousand
   seats of nobody, the two of them small in it. Boyd at volume
   one, gracious as ever, makes the real offer: **a bronze
   plaque with Cal's late wife's name, prominent, in the new
   plaza; the fund doubled; sign by Friday.** His grief handed
   back to him as a decorating option. THE NAME IS NEVER SPOKEN
   OR WRITTEN — "her name, in bronze" is as close as any
   sentence gets; if Boyd's dialogue needs it, he gestures at it
   ("her name — I'd put it where ninety thousand people read it
   every Saturday"). **Cal refuses on his feet, flat and final**
   — no speech, no anger Boyd can use; and **the price goes up
   before he reaches the tunnel** (Boyd names the raise at his
   back, volume unchanged).
2. **The stamp (SR-22 — the reveal).** The unnamed engineer from
   the yard fence comes back into the story and turns out to be
   exactly what he read as: **nobody's man.** Independent. He
   has seen the counter-report's numbers (stage HOW with a light
   touch — the drawings, a meeting at the shop, the bar-board
   math below; your choice, one scene) and he is willing to put
   his stamp behind work that earns it: "real drawings, real
   numbers" got made, and a stamp will stand behind them. The
   payoff of "Cal never did learn whose man he was" is the
   answer itself — he was never anybody's. Keep him UNNAMED
   (the unnamed engineer is his identity); one dry line in his
   established register (the ladder man) is worth more than a
   speech.
3. **The Mule, closed to the public.** Peanut Kyzer shuts the
   Blind Mule for a night and the two Ashfords meet over the
   pool table — coveralls on one side, clipboard people on the
   other, **Checkerboard pie as neutral tender** (the appetite
   beat; somebody brought it, everybody reaches). **Cal chalks
   the winter math on the bar board:** Boyd's list, and under it
   the same trades, the same paychecks, the House's scaffolding
   instead of Boyd's decks, contract through March. The crew
   argues it out loud — real objections, payroll fears, twelve
   kitchens — **and lands where payroll people land: on the
   math.** The jobs-pledge letter comes off the corkboard (stage
   the taking-down; it has hung there saying "pending" for
   weeks — let its removal be the vote).
4. **Friday passes. Nothing gets signed.** The deadline dies
   quietly, on the page, without a scene Boyd gets to play in —
   the shop working, the day ending, the paper going nowhere.
