## The law (frozen beats — each an EVENT someone causes)

1. **The House, at night, holding everything they built.** She
   and Cal, the work spread out — drawings, ledger, folder — the
   last night of preparation. Let the room be warm before it
   gets cold.
2. **Nine o'clock: Boyd's last card arrives** (message or paper,
   volume one either way): **the gift made contingent — full
   footprint or the shortfall stands.** The trustees will be
   told tomorrow that half a gift is no gift.
3. **The worse arithmetic, said between them:** the report reads
   like his girlfriend's campaign; the pledge drive reads like
   the price of his signature; their public joy is the caption
   under everything. Everything true about them is the weapon.
   ZERO misunderstanding — neither doubts the other for one
   second; the enemy is the shape of the story, not each other.
4. **THE OFFERS — the bravest, worst thing two careful people
   can do: each offers to let the other go.** He'll stand down
   and keep his name off her clean fight; she'll stay out of the
   room so no one can say he was bought. The offers are REAL,
   structural, priced in named costs — not noble poses, not
   tests. Nothing is settled. **She leaves alone.**
5. **T33 — HER EXCAVATION (the chapter's center; D14).** Somewhere
   inside beats 3–4, in her own voice, as ADMISSION — never
   flashback, never marriage post-mortem, never a backstory
   block — she finally says (to Cal, or to herself on the page)
   the whole of what the reader has only glimpsed since ch 1:
   what the quiet house cost her (the house that went loud with
   quiet; a year of driving sealed up silent; a toolbox built
   out of principle and spite); **what she swore about her own
   Saturdays** (that they would belong to HER); and what it
   would cost to be the reason Cal loses his — his Saturdays,
   his shop, his House. The excavation is the reason her offer
   is real: she knows exactly what losing a life you built
   costs, because she has paid it once. Keep it inside the
   scene, in motion, at most ~a page of the chapter, and let one
   laugh live near it (Gilmore law holds even at the bottom).
6. **The exit and the lamps.** Down the hill, Delmar's pit is
   already smoking for tomorrow's crowd; the Checkerboard's sign
   buzzes its stubborn OPEN — the town keeping its lamps lit
   around two people in the dark. The work lights go off one by
   one behind her. **The chapter ends on the image: the mug he
   poured her, going cold on the rail.** The camera stays with
   the mug — NOT with her transit (do not end on her walking or
   driving away).
