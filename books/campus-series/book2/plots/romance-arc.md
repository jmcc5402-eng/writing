# Book 1.2 — The romance's own arc (THE ARC GATE, third arc)

**PROPOSED 2026-09-05 for the author's approval. Nothing below is
canon until this PR merges. Written in plain sentences.**

**The author's ask (2026-09-05):** *"Much like the Michael Hauge
concept of a character having an internal and external change
throughout the book, I think we need one for the romance overall to
show how early on the romance is hidden and just shows some signs of
life, but over the book it grows and turns external. The pattern in
book one made more sense."* And, on ch 8: *"Have we earned the right
for Dan to think that 'he wants her'? Has there been enough
interaction and banter or fighting previously in the book? It almost
seems to come out of nowhere."*

**What went wrong, in one sentence.** Aisha and Dan each got an arc
and the romance did not, so the two leads changed on schedule while
the thing between them jumped from strangers-at-work to "she wanted
him" with no steps a reader could point to.

---

## 1. The romance has two journeys, like a character

- **The inside journey** is what each of them feels about the other.
  It goes from *the answer I did not want* to *I want this* to *I
  need this and I will pay for it*.
- **The outside journey** is what the two of them DO together and
  what the town can SEE. It goes from *two professionals who argue*
  to *two people who enjoy arguing* to *two people who touch* to *a
  couple the county names*.

The rule that follows: **the inside may run ahead of the outside,
but never by more than one stage.** A character may not name a
want inside until the reader has watched, on the page, the two of
them enjoy each other at least twice. A kiss may not happen until
help has been accepted. A public claim may not happen until a
private repair has. The reader must always be able to point to the
scene where the next step started.

## 3. The ladder, chapter by chapter, with the holes marked

Book One's ladder for comparison, from its kiss chart: meet (1) →
terms shaken in front of witnesses (8) → first touch, his hand at
her elbow (11) → the plate taken with both hands (13) → the failed
phone call (14) → the kiss (16) → the two glances (17) → the cup
crossing (19) → the sleeve (21) → the smile withheld twenty-two
chapters (22) → the public claim (25) → the pen (28) → the square
(29). Twelve rungs before the public claim, every one an action.

| 7 | The memo over the box; "let me ask around" | 2 | — |

**The two adds this arc requires, both in accepted chapters:**
1. **Ch 2, the Friday check, becomes the fight the book keeps
   promising.** Two pages, not one. She reads the sheet; he pushes
   on a call; she wins; he tries the next one; she wins that too;
   and somewhere in it he is enjoying losing and she can tell. The
   reader leaves knowing what "since June" looks like. His POV, dry,
   no tenderness. This is the rung Book One had at ch 8 (terms in
   front of witnesses) and 1.2 never had.
2. **Ch 4, the lounge, gets one more exchange where they like each
   other** — already ruled once (B2-D03, "one more 'You're looming'
   exchange"); the page has it thinly. One beat more.

Nothing else in 1–8 moves. Ch 5 and ch 8 stay as written; they were
never the problem, the missing floor under them was.

## 4. What this changes from here

- **Every brief's ROMANCE MOVE names the stage** ("Stage 3: shown
  between them") and the rung it climbs. A brief that spends a line
  above its stage is returned at the audit.
- **The build check joins the audit.** Before a want, a kiss, or a
  public claim is briefed, the audit lists the on-page scenes that
  earn it. Fewer than two, the brief is blocked. This is the check
  the romance floor could not do: the floor counts beats inside a
  chapter; this counts steps across the book.
- **The wave-3 directive (ch 9–12) is written to stage 3:** apart but
  carrying each other, and the first question instead of a fight at
  ch 13 has to be set up by ch 12.

## 5. Why this is the pattern that "made more sense"

Book One's romance was a slow stair of things two people did. Book
1.2's was two people thinking. The characters' arcs were built and
the romance's was not, so the leads changed on schedule and the
thing between them did not have a schedule at all. This document is
that schedule.
