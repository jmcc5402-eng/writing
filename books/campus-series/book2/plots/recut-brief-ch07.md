## WHAT CROSSES THE SEAM FROM CH 6

Ch 6 was accepted on a bare merge (#118, B2-D07), so **nothing
crosses as a correction** — only as fact. What is true entering ch 7:

- The **December letter** exists, is unsent, and moves to the athletic
  director after Christmas **without Dan's name**. It is Denny's own
  draft. It never names the walk-on; Dan named him.
- The corner on Delmar's porch heard Dan refuse, and will repeat it.
  The board carries **Denny's sentence** but not Dan's no.
- **Aisha does not know Dan refused.** She was at the quiet end of the
  rail, a whole crowd away. *The drafter may not write a line that
  assumes she knows, and may not have anyone tell her in this chapter.*
  If the board is read on the page, it carries Denny's sentence only.
- Dan lost to the athletic director in writing on Tuesday (ch 5) and
  told nobody. That is two weeks of held losses entering this chapter.

## THE ARGUMENT, SAID

The university has put the athletics annex on Boyd's survey list and
given Aisha until February to have four rehab rooms and thirty years
of records inventoried and boxed, with nobody assigned to help her do
it. Dan hears the same day and tells her he will see what he can find
out, which is the soft version, and she knows it when she hears it and
thanks him for it anyway.

## REVERSAL — who loses what

**Aisha loses her building** — the one room in this county she
actually unpacked — and the deadline in her life stops being the
quarterback's and becomes hers.

**Dan loses something the reader can see and he cannot:** the first
chance he has had to spend anything on her. He declines it himself,
in the same breath he offers it, because the wrecking money is Boyd's
and Boyd's money is the performance center he still believes his
program needs. He walks away thinking that went well.

## ARC BEAT — Aisha

She tells nobody, asks for nothing, and adds it to the list. The false
belief rendered as one action: **a woman who has never been kept does
not request help, she packs.** She has already charted the building
before she has told a single person it is condemned. *(Arc doc:
"unpacking is a request and she does not make requests.")*

## ARC BEAT — Dan

**The soft version, out loud — the same move he caught himself making
in the hospital lounge in ch 4 and stopped. Here he does not stop.**
This is the chapter where the reader should be angry at him, and it is
what makes ch 15 and ch 19 worth anything. He is not being cowardly
and the page must not present it as cowardice: he is managing, which
is the thing he is good at and the thing that is wrong with him.

## COUPLE LINE

They share the scene where he hedges. **Ladder rung 4, new sense:
hands near, no skin.** He hands the folded memo back across the top of
a box and, as he reaches, she sees the shoulders of the man that coat
was cut for — his, though she does not know it and the page does not
say so. **B2-T02 stays sealed: nothing he does or says may claim the
coat, and he may not react to seeing his own name stitched on her
chest.** One flicker at most, unglossed, and she does not catch it.

High-water mark stays **5** (ch 5). Ch 7 plays at 4. It does not climb.

## END REGISTER

**DOWN**, arrived at quietly — the two are not in conflict. It opens
with a coat on a chair, a gift; it ends with a survey on the building
and a woman sitting on a made bed inside a coat that is not hers,
having answered nobody. The staged ending image (the sleeves rolled
twice, holding) is PROTECTED and still lands; what changes is
everything the reader now knows is underneath it.

## WHAT TO ADD

### 1. The memo gets its number — but the number is FEBRUARY, not thirty days.

**AMENDMENT, and the audit must rule on it.** The original brief said
"thirty days ... and the date lands before Christmas." **That
contradicts ratified canon and may not be drafted.** The outline's own
ch 7 entry (`plots/b12-outline.md:417–418`) says "her rehab rooms and
thirty years of records to be boxed by **February**," the accepted
memo says it twice (ch07:226, :246), and February is load-bearing
elsewhere in the book — the lease she signs on the first warm Friday
of February (:58), the February-inert signing day of ch 28 (:791),
Verna not charging February rent (:740). Moving the deadline to
December would break four chapters to buy one.

**What the original brief actually wanted was for the deadline to stop
being weather and start being arithmetic,** and February can carry
that. So:

- The memo names **a date**, not a month — a specific day in February,
  and it is the drafter's to place (`[TK: the day]` is acceptable;
  the audit may fix one). "By February" is an atmosphere. "By the
  sixth" is a deadline.
- **She does the arithmetic on the page, because she is the kind of
  person who does.** The count of weeks, or days, said once, flat, in
  her register. That number is the new object in the chapter.
- **Say what it means for her in a complete sentence, once**, before
  any of the good oblique material starts: four rehab rooms, four
  cabinets, thirty years of other people's handwriting, one X-ray
  shelf no machine on this campus can read — and no one assigned to
  move any of it. The accepted page circles this beautifully and never
  lands it. Land it, then let the circling do its work.
- **Protect the payoff:** ch07:352, "Every one of them had a February
  in it somewhere," is the chapter's best line and it only works if
  February is still the month. Do not touch it.

### 2. Dan is not in this chapter, and B2-D06 says he must be.

**This is the largest add and the audit should treat it as the
chapter's real work.** B2-D06 item 7 (author-ruled): *"The leads share
a scene in ch 6, 7, 8, 10 and 12."* On the accepted page **Dan does
not appear in ch 7 at all** — not in body, not in dialogue. The
chapter currently violates a merged ruling.

- **Placement: the annex, late afternoon, after the memo and before
  the Magnolia Court section** (i.e. inside the block that now runs
  ch07:216–285, at its end, before the `***` at :286). She has already
  read it twice, already walked the building, already charted it. He
  arrives into a woman who has finished absorbing it alone. That is
  the point.
- **Why he is there, and it may not be a coincidence** — every setup
  pays off, and men do not wander into the annex. Give him an errand
  that is his: he heard it in a building meeting, or the same envelope
  went to the fieldhouse, or he came about the quarterback's ladder
  and finds her boxing. The drafter picks one and it must be a reason
  the reader can name afterward.
- **The exchange itself, and its shape:** he reads the memo. He says
  the managerial thing — he will see what he can find out, he will
  ask, he knows the people who would know. **He may not promise
  anything he can be held to.** She does not accuse him. She says
  thank you, and means nothing by it, and the difference between those
  two things is the whole beat. **She does not call him on it. The
  silence is the beat.** Then he goes and she goes back to the
  cabinets.
- **The hand-back is the proximity beat** (see COUPLE LINE above).
- **One line naming why he will not push**, and per the brief it is
  hers to read off him rather than his to admit: the survey is the
  performance center's first step, and he has been nodding along in
  that film room. She sees him decide not to say so. *(In her POV,
  this is a reading of a man, not access to him — keep it to what a
  sharp woman can see on a face.)*

### 3. Give the reader one sentence of what the annex is FOR.

Related to the say-it test and the author's ch 6 note about Selection
Sunday being undecodable: a reader who has never been in a college
athletics building does not know what "four rehab rooms and a records
room" means to a working doctor, so the loss reads as real estate.
**One plain sentence, in her POV, saying what she does in that
building and what happens to the people in it if it goes** — before
the elegiac walk-through. Then the KNEES, 1994 carton and the
unreadable X-ray sleeves hit like they should.

### 4. Cut the "somebody" count.

**Seven in this chapter.** Two are the named B2-D06 fixes: ch07:55
"typed three letters into somebody's chart" → "into a chart"; ch07:61
"the rolls rode inside somebody's coat" → "inside a coat." A third is
the same family and should go: ch07:275 "somebody else's name on the
deed." The remaining four (:236 bolted true, :256 Somebody had already
drawn, :324 Verna's dialogue, :335 like somebody who had) are
different constructions and :324 is speech, which does not count —
**but four is still a lot of one word in one chapter and the author's
note was about the tic, not the grammar.** Bring the total to four or
fewer. Do not replace them with "someone."

### 5. Bans, live for this chapter.

- **"Never once" is BANNED outright.** The book-wide cap of one is
  already blown — two sit on accepted pages (ch05:330, ch06:21). Do
  not add a third in any form. Plain "never" is fine.
- **"Somebody's ___"** — see item 4; the once-per-book spend went at
  ch 1.
- **The beverage register (B2-D03):** the Table's *breakfast* coffee
  is protected and correct — the rule is nights and bars, and this is
  a lunch counter at noon. Nothing changes here.
- **The quarterback:** "7" from the rail, the board and Tick; "Trey"
  only from his parents and his doctor. **She is his doctor** — in her
  POV and in her mouth he can be Trey. The accepted page's "The
  seven's next evaluation" (ch07:101) is narration in her POV and
  should be reconsidered by the drafter against the naming list.
- No new epigraph — the folded one stands.
- Modernity binds props (DIALS 5). No casseroles, no foil.
