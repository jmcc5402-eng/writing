## THE ARGUMENT, SAID

The university has put the athletics annex on Boyd's survey list and
given Aisha until a date in February to have four rehab rooms and
thirty years of records inventoried and boxed, with nobody assigned to
help her do it. Dan comes by that afternoon on an ordinary errand,
reads the notice cold in front of her, and tells her he will see what
he can find out — which is the soft version, and she knows it when she
hears it, and thanks him for it anyway.

## REVERSAL — who loses what

**Aisha loses her building** — the one room in this county she
actually unpacked — and the deadline in her life stops being the
quarterback's and becomes hers.

**Dan loses the first chance he has had to spend anything on her, and
he declines it himself in the same breath he offers it.** He walks
away thinking that went well.

**How the reader gets Dan's half without Dan knowing it** *(audit
ruling E; v1 delivered this through item 5, which is struck)*: the
page has already handed the reader the rendering, in her voice, sixty
lines above where he will stand — "Somebody had already drawn the
building that wanted the ground… she had not found them yet"
(`ch07:256-260`). **The reader supplies Boyd. Dan does not, and may
not, connect the survey to the rendering in this chapter.** She reads
*managing* off him, not money: a man deciding what to do about a thing
rather than what he thinks of it. Superior position does the rest, at
no cost to ch 10.

## ARC BEAT — Aisha

She tells nobody, asks for nothing, and adds it to the list. The false
belief rendered as one action: **a woman who has never been kept does
not request help, she packs.** She has charted the building before she
has told a single person it is condemned. *(Arc doc: "unpacking is a
request and she does not make requests.")*

## ARC BEAT — Dan

**The soft version, out loud — the same move he caught himself making
in the hospital lounge in ch 4 and stopped. Here he does not stop.**
This is the chapter where the reader should be angry at him, and it is
what makes ch 15 and ch 19 worth anything. He is not being cowardly
and the page must not present it as cowardice: he is managing, which
is the thing he is good at and the thing that is wrong with him.

### What Dan may and may not do with his face — verbatim from the audit

**He MAY:** keep moving (SR-B2-15, `DECISIONS.md:132-135` — "moves
constantly — paces, claps"; the cap turned in his hands) · read the
memo twice the way he reads a call sheet and hand it back · look at
her while she says the number and hold it a beat past comfortable
(canon, and it is about *her*) · go flat in the mouth the way a coach
goes flat when he has already decided, **provided what she reads is
management, not guilt**.

**He MAY NOT:** **go still** — SR-B2-15 makes stillness his designated
tell, and a stillness here is a confession the reader has been trained
to read · give "one flicker at most, unglossed" (**v1's line, struck**
— unwriteable in third limited hers, and an unnamed flicker is the
drafter's cheat that flags the secret) · look at the stitched name or
away from it · touch the coat, adjust it, roll a cuff, or put a hand
near the patch · say the word "coat," ask where it came from, or
mention the order sheet, the equipment cage, or having been in the
fieldhouse at six that morning · **be asked** about the coat — an
on-page lie from Dan makes ch 29 the exposure of a lie rather than the
landing of a gift, and `outline:244` assigns the lie to the equipment
manager.

**Why his silence is not conspicuous:** every other character has
remarked on the coat (`:176-198`, `:302-311`, `:320-325`). **Put the
memo in his hands within the first few lines of his arrival.** A
survey notice outranks a parka, and the scene never has room to notice
what he did not say.
