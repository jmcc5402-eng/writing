## THE ROMANCE MOVE (first line)

**Stage 1 — Hidden.** The romance is not admitted by either of them.
The argument IS the flirt, and this is the first time the reader
watches a full round of it instead of being told it happens every
Friday. Rung on the ladder: **2** (standing distance; he reads her
hands because her face gives him nothing). No new high — ch 3's tent
owns rung 2's high-water mark and ch 4 climbs to 4–5. Nothing here
may be a want, a stated feeling, or a touch. What the reader takes
away: these two enjoy fighting each other, and he loses.

**The build.** Ch 8's "he wanted her" is earned by TWO scenes the
reader can point to: THIS one, and ch 4's lounge. Ch 8 already
carries the payoff line — "Being caught by her had never felt much
like being caught. It felt like the good part of a Friday." — so
this scene is where the reader learns what the good part of a Friday
is. Plant it so that line lands in ch 8 without explanation.

## THE ARGUMENT, SAID

He wants a yes on a boy today. She has already given him every yes
the medicine allows and will not give him one more. He knows that
before he asks, and asks anyway, because losing to her on the merits
is the best half hour of his week and he would not say so under oath.

## THE FIGHT — two rounds, both lost, both enjoyed

Shape (the drafter chooses the words; the facts below are canon):
- **Round 1 — fifty-six.** Stinger Tuesday night; symptom-free at
  forty-eight hours; full painless range this morning; strength
  equal side to side; PROBABLE; no contact today. Dan pushes for
  more than probable — he wants the word for tomorrow now. She does
  not move: he plays tomorrow if he is the same at noon, not before
  noon, and she says why in one plain sentence a stranger could
  repeat. He loses on the merits.
- **Round 2 — twenty-two.** Low ankle sprain, grade one, taped and
  braced, FULL-GO, on the sheet. He pushes here too — and she has
  already said yes. He is arguing with a yes. She catches it, and
  says so, dry. That is the moment he is enjoying this and she can
  tell. He loses again, this time to his own habit.
- **Witness.** At least one other person is in earshot — a trainer,
  a manager, fifty-six himself at the far wall — so the coach loses
  to the doctor where a kid can see it. (Book One's rung at ch 8 was
  terms in front of witnesses. This is that rung.)
- **His enjoyment is on the page, plainly, as behavior** the reader
  can see — he asks the next question when the sheet has already
  answered it; he keeps the exchange going one line past where the
  paper needs it. Narration may say he liked this part. Narration
  may NOT say he liked her, wanted her, or noticed her as a woman —
  that is ch 5's ("he had never once looked at her and seen a
  woman") and ch 8's. Stage 1 stays hidden on the inside.
- **She can tell.** One line or one look from her that shows she
  knows he is enjoying losing — and she does not spare him for it.
  Aisha does not soften; she is not gentle and not angry; she is
  right and amused about it under the surface. Her face still tells
  him nothing; the tell is in what she SAYS.

## REVERSAL — who loses what

Dan loses twice, on the merits, in front of a kid, and the second
time he loses to himself. The cost is small and real: the program's
head coach is seen asking the doctor for a word and not getting it,
the week before the biggest game the county has had in years. He
does not mind, and that is the reversal's second edge — the reader
sees he does not mind before he does.
