## 0. The constraint that shapes all of this

**Ashford LOSES the game** (ch 16, by a field goal, Trey in street
clothes — outline, canon). So the pledge is never CONDITIONED on the
win; if it were, Boyd's arc would end in December and the price he
names at ch 20 would have nothing behind it. **The win is Boyd's
STAGE, not his condition.** The loss at 16 is what puts Boyd in
motion at 20–24, and now the reader knows why.

## 1. The four stakes (RULED)

### Stake 1 — Boyd's announcement needs a win (the stage)

The performance center is to be ANNOUNCED on the field after a
playoff win on the nineteenth: the rendering on the video board at
the final whistle, the donors in the suite, the university's people
with a microphone. Lose in the first round with the quarterback
benched by the doctor and there is no stage — and the man who paid
for the stage knows who cost him it. **Boyd never says "play him."**
He says the true thing sideways: *"We've got people in the suite on
the nineteenth."* The pressure on Dan is the announcement, never a
condition on the money.

### Stake 2 — Dan's extension, priced (the athletic director folded in)

The spring extension (outline; arc-docs §5) is on the FEBRUARY board
agenda. A first playoff win in program history makes it a formality.
A first-round exit with the star sitting makes it, in the athletic
director's word, *"a review."* That is Dan's job on the clock beside
hers. **The athletic director stays institutional weather, not a
villain (premise) — but frightened weather:** he hired Dan, he
greenlit Boyd's building, and his own standing rides on the gift
landing. One line to Dan is his whole stake: *"I've got a board
meeting in February and a donor who counts doors."* His fear is why
he signs her suspension at 24 without a fight. He is not a separate
stake; he is the voice of stake 2.

### Stake 4 — The town's money

A home playoff game is a second week of the town's whole year. The
Checkerboard makes half its year in the fall (town file, canon); the
hotel block is full (ch 11 epigraph); the Magnolia Court's rooms; the
RV city; the Mule. The county's pressure on her is ECONOMIC before it
is sentimental — modern, and honest (grapevine rule 5: status leaks
through money). Somebody says what a second home game is worth to
this town, in dollars or in weeks. (Numbered 4 to match the chat
list; stake 3 was folded into 2.)

### Stake 5 — Her double bind, said once

If she signs and he takes a second hit, her career is over. If she
does not sign and they lose, she is the reason. Both are already true
on the page; nobody has said it out loud. It gets said ONCE, whole,
and then never explained again.

## 3. Walls

- The pledge is NEVER conditioned on the win, on the page or in a
  post. The word is "announce," never "fund."
- Boyd never says "play him" and never says "her job."
- The athletic director stays weather: no scheme, no villainy; one
  frightened line and a signature.
- The loss at 16 stands as outlined; the stakes make it cost more,
  not change its result.
- Stake 5 is said once, whole, then never explained again.
- No dollar figure for the pledge or the extension on any page
  (numbers are the town's — weeks, rooms, a December).
