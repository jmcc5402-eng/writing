## 1. Who he is, in one sentence

Rex Boyd is the money, and when he says "Ashford" he means the
stadium.

## 2. What he wants

The full footprint: the performance center on the annex's ground,
cleared by February eleventh, with a program that never embarrasses
the gift and a coach whose name is on the pledge. Under that, the
thing Peanut says at the Mule: he does not buy buildings, he buys
the men who sign for them.

## 3. What he believes, and what it makes him do

Everything is a list and every person is an entry. A name can be
passed along. A price does not need to be said to be paid. So he
never threatens, never signs a letter, never raises his voice, and
never has to: his staff does the talking (the consultant, the
laminated boards, "proactive risk posture"), the town does the
turning, and he leaves a wide silence for other people to fill with
reasons. Book One's Cal did not fill it. Book 1.2's Dan does, for
half the book.

## 4. What he is NOT (the walls)

He never threatens on the page. He is never a cartoon; the comedy
lives in his consultant, not in him. He is never proven: the wire
from the complaint to Boyd is said out loud once (27) and never
shown. He never loses on the page in this book; his defeat is Book
1.4's zoning vote (the town file). He is never punished. He exits
smiling.

## 5. His arc is escalation, not change

The leads change; Boyd does the same thing louder each time, and
the reader's reading of him changes from "generous" to "the
weather." That is the arc: what the town calls him.

## 6. What he gives the leads

The price. Dan's essence act (27) costs his season only because
Boyd set the price at 20; Aisha's staying (29) is a choice only
because Boyd made leaving the easy road at 19. The annex is a
building until Boyd wants its ground; then it is the town's memory
of its fevers.

## 7. The reader's superior position

The reader can match the consultant's phrase (10) to the letter
(23) before anyone on the page does (27). The reader sees the survey
greenlight land the same week as the suspension (24) and hears only
the Table say so.

## 8. Register (binding on every brief that stages him)

Volume one. Already smiling. Shoes shined for a meeting. No folder,
no phone. A wide silence he leaves for others. The wind moves his
coat and nothing else. He says "your quarterback" and "the boy,"
never "Trey." He calls her "the doctor." He never has a punchline.
