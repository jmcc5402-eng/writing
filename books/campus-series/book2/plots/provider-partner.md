# Book 1.2 — The provider partner (the annex is her JOB, not a building)

**PROPOSED 2026-09-07 for the author's approval.** The author, on
the ch 10 listening file: *"I'm a little confused exactly why he's
thinking about [the annex]. Is he just thinking about her? Does not
want the annex to go away? Is he worried she'd leave if the new
building was built? (which could be a good twist, the consultants
bring in a big corporate medical firm??)"* And: *"I don't really
want there to be nostalgia for the annex, there should be nostalgia
for her job if she's losing it. I like this idea of Boyd bringing in

about."*

## 1. The idea, in one paragraph

Boyd's pledge has a partner. A regional sports-medicine group
(`[TK name]`; on the page "the group" / "the partner" until named)
runs the performance center's recovery floor and provides team
physician services under contract. That is how the pledge pencils:
the university stops carrying its own sports-medicine line — her
salary, her rooms, her records. The new building's "office with a
window" is the group's medical director's. The consultant's phrase,
"proactive risk posture," is the group's own language (it is on
their materials), which is why it turns up word for word in the
complaint letter at 23. Nobody ever says "her job" out loud. Dan
understands it in the film room at 10. She does not know the partner
exists until Boyd walks her rooms at 19; what she knows now is that
the memo said "relocation in a subsequent communication," and a
doctor reads that as "we have not decided whether you exist."

## 2. What it fixes

- **Her stake is her job.** Not a building she unpacked. The annex
  is where her job lives; the drawing has no place for her in it.
- **Dan's silence has weight.** He is not withholding a floor plan.
  He knows she is being replaced and has not said so.
- **The film room reads plainly.** He is thinking she will be gone.
  A woman with two suitcases leaves when the job goes.
- **Boyd stays the unproven hand.** A provider partnership is lawful
  and ordinary in Division I. He never says her name. The walls in
  `boyd-arc.md` hold.
- **The offer out west (19) stops being random.** A graceful exit
  for a doctor the partner will replace anyway.
- **The letter's phrase (23 → 27) traces cleaner:** vendor language
  in eleven families' mouths.
- **The ending does not move.** The pledge is re-sequenced at 27, so
  the partner deal stalls; her program survives into spring (the
  clearance on her letterhead, 30); the annex's fate and the
  partner's ride into 1.3–1.4 as designed.

## 5. The walls (so this does not break the book)

- Boyd never says "her job," "the doctor," or "replace" in the same
  sentence. The consultant says "provider partnership" and "the
  university stops carrying that line." The reader does the sum.
- The group is real, lawful, and ordinary. Nobody on the page calls
  it corrupt. The wire to the letter stays unproven (27).
- Her medical judgment never bends for the job. The job is the
  price she is willing to pay from ch 5 on; the partner makes the
  price visible.
- The town does not know until 24. The board cannot know at 12.
