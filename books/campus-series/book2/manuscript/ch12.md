> **On Grapevine, the Ashford parents' board —**
>
> **PieBeforeKickoff:** Seven days. Stream link for the nineteenth is
> the same one as always, for the grandparents asking. Bookmark it now
> so I'm not fielding calls at kickoff.


Dan Merritt's phone lit face down on the Checkerboard's counter
at eight forty on a Saturday morning, beside his coffee,
and he turned it over.
It was from his offensive coordinator,
and it had nothing on it but a screenshot. No words.
Saturday was the roster's off day, so the turf was empty,
and the five fifteen check did not run again until Monday.
The game was a week out.
Trey Gault had a ladder to climb before he could play in it,
and the next rung was Aisha Cole's to call, and she had not called it.
Dan read the screenshot before he read anything else.

He had the stool nearest the register.
The sign over the pass-through still had the Coach's Slice on it,
a quarter of a whole pie cut as one piece, four dollars,
and the one cook had set one in front of him without being asked,
because it had been his order since his first fall in this town.
The coffee was self-serve.
The Liars' Table was seated at the front window,
Tick Moran, Tunk Ferrell, Earlene Tatum,
and Sonny Dillard on his feet at the end of it, not staying,
with the paper in sections among them.

The screenshot was mostly a picture.
A crew cab at a curb under a streetlight, at night, four doors, dark.
The top of the chat was cropped off, names and all.
Under the picture there were two bubbles.
The first one said,
*so SOMEBODY left the Mule in a coach's truck last night.*
The second one was three dots and nothing else.
He looked at the truck long enough to see it was not his,
and then he did not look at it again.
He knew what it was before he knew one other thing about it,
because he had been on the wrong end of one of these
ten years ago, two states east,
and the shape of them had not changed.

He typed one line back.

*Who types a pause?*

The answer came before he had the phone back on the counter.

*Somebody who was listening.*

Neither of them typed anything else.
The coordinator had sent it because by nine o'clock
the county was going to read *a coach's truck* as Coach,
and he wanted Dan to have seen it first.

Dan had been at home last night.
He had eaten standing up, read the board once, and gone to bed.
It was not his truck,
and none of that would matter to anybody by nine.

Earlene came up the counter with the pot,
filled his cup without asking, and did not go back down.

"There's a picture going around," she said.
"I haven't seen it. I've heard there is one."

"There's one."

She held the pot a second.

"A screenshot is a knife somebody else gets to hold, Coach.
That's all it is.
The one who took it never gets a say in who it's for."

She did not ask him whether he was in it,
which was how he knew she had heard the rest of it.
She went back down to the Table with the pot.

Then the carillon struck nine up the hill,
and Tick's phone lit on the table by his plate,
and Tick held it out at arm's length with his glasses down his nose
and read it to the window.

"'Interesting to see who gets rides home from the Mule these days.
Not naming names. Just interesting.'"

"Who's that," Tunk said.

"DeeAnn Prewitt."

"She sits in that car at practice every day with the heat on," Tunk said.

Nobody said anything to that.

"Keep going," Earlene said.

Tick thumbed down.
"Two under it already. Somebody about the coat.
And Rhonda Sipes, being Rhonda."

"Whose truck?" Tunk said.

"Doesn't say."

"Half this county drives a crew cab," Sonny said,
and picked up his keys.

Dan had the board open before Tick was done reading.
It was up at nine, in daylight, under a real name,
and it had two replies already.

> **DeeAnn Prewitt** Interesting to see who gets rides home from the
> Mule these days. Not naming names. Just interesting.
>
> **DossCoDadx3** She's been in a staff coat since the freeze.
> Everybody in the practice lot has seen it. Nobody said a word.
>
> **Rhonda Sipes** If it's who I think it is, this explains a LOT
> about the hold.

He read it as weather.
A front, in from the west, here by nine.
Nobody had typed a name. Nobody had needed to.
The county had watched a woman wear a staff coat for ten days
and wanted it to be a story since the first morning,
and this morning a cropped picture had handed it one.
It put the doctor in a truck and put him at the wheel.
He knew the second half of that was wrong. He had been at home.

He had known the coat would do this.
He had known it with the tags in his hand,
and he had asked for her name over the patch anyway,
and he had liked seeing it on her in front of that lot every night since.
That was not a thing he was going to say anywhere.

By the time he had his card out at the register,
somebody had typed *Coach* under Rhonda,
and somebody under that had typed *Dr. Cole*,
and the board had its story.
The story was him and her.
He paid. He had eaten half the slice.
The Table watched him to the door and waited for it to shut.

***

Saturday staff film ran at ten in the Fieldhouse,
staff only, the roster off, the coordinator on the clicker
and next Saturday's opponent on the screen.
Ty Beaumont was in the staff room when Dan came through it,
on the end of the couch with the game notes on his knee,
and his phone was not in his hand.
Dan noticed that, because he noticed phones now.

"Morning, Coach."

"Ty. Where's your phone?"

"Truck."

"Good."

"Their slot's hurt," Ty said. "Sixteen. He didn't finish last week."

"I saw. Put it in the notes."

"It's in."

A receivers coach on the Saturday after signing week,
leaving the phone in the truck and having a quiet day.
Dan had not managed that in ten years.
He envied it some, and went on into film.

The coordinator ran the opponent's base front for an hour,
and Dan watched feet,
and neither of them said a word about a screenshot,
because the film room's door was open, same as every door in the building.

At the break he went to the window.
The annex sat across the lot, low, the cold end dark,
and the RAV4 was nosed in at the warm end by the door.
She was boxing.
She had told him in her office she would do it herself, and she was.

The coordinator looked at the same window and back at the screen.

"Going over there?"

"Sheet comes Monday."

"That's Monday."

"I'll be back for the second half."

***

The annex corridor was cold end to end.
The boxes started at the records room
and ran down the wall past the whirlpool room,
past the fountain that ran cold when it ran,
stacked two high, taped shut, numbered in black marker.
He had asked her one Wednesday to wait before she carried anything.
She had not waited.
He walked past all of it and did not count.

Her office door was open.
Aisha was at the desk in the parka with the cuffs pinned back,
cardboard dust on both sleeves,
and Missy Gault's seven o'clock page was up on the tablet,
a photograph of a notebook page in Missy's hand.
Trey was at home. The page was the only piece of him on campus today.
The whiteboard stood level on the card he had folded under its wheel,
the ladder on it, and his date circled in red at the top.

"Held," she said, before he had said anything.
"Seven o'clock page. Slept eight hours.
Headache zero, second morning in a row.
Trey read for twenty minutes and stopped on his own.
That's five nights."

"So it holds."

"It holds. Step two is Monday."

"Monday."

"A bike, in a quiet room, heart rate capped, twenty minutes,
nobody talking scheme at him.
If the headache comes back, we step down and the clock starts over.
If it doesn't, Tuesday is three."

She said it looking at him.
Her eyes were brown, and on a thing like this
they came straight at a man and did not blink
for as long as the sentence lasted,
and he had stood across a sheet from her every Friday all fall
and had never been looked at with them from this close.

He did the count on her board without touching it.

"Monday two. Tuesday three. Wednesday four. Thursday five.
Friday it holds overnight, and Saturday's the game."

"That's the count."

"There's no day in it, Doc.
One headache Wednesday and he's out.
Move it to Sunday.
Sunday two, Monday three, Tuesday four, Wednesday five,
and I've got a day in the week to lose."

"You've got a day. Trey doesn't. The brain gets the weekend."

"He's held five nights."

"He's held five nights at step one.
Step two is the first time anybody asks his brain to do anything,
and I'm not asking it a day early because a bracket wants Saturday.
If the count only works when every day goes right,
then the count is telling you something, Coach,
and what it's telling you isn't Sunday."

He looked at the circle. It was his date.

"Then let me be in the room."

"No."

"I'll stand in the back. I won't say a word."

"You'd be scheme, standing there.
He'd ride for you instead of for me.
A quiet room means quiet, and you aren't.
You can have the sheet at five fifteen Monday, printed, same as always.
It'll say what happened."

"Who's in the room?"

"Me. The trainer at the door.
His mother drives him and waits in the hall."

"Not me."

"Not you."

He had come across the lot to argue a day out of her,
and she had not given him the day,
and she had not given him the room,
and she had done it without raising her voice
or taking her eyes off him.

Her phone was on the desk by the tablet, face down.
He looked at it. She saw him look.
She read that board and had never posted a word on it,
and he had read it every night he had lived in this county,
and this morning it had the two of them on it,
and the sentence that said so was one line long.
*They've got us in a truck.*
He did not say it. She did not say it.
She picked up the tablet. He put his cap on.

"Doc."

"Coach. Monday."

He went back down the corridor past the boxes.
Two things in that office had gone unsaid.
One she knew as well as he did, and it was on her phone.
The other was on a laminated board in his film room
and started at the door he was walking past,
and it was still his to tell her.
He was getting good at not saying things,
and it was not a thing to be good at.

***

The coordinator left at two.
Dan ran the opponent's third-down package alone
until the projector fan was the only sound left in the Fieldhouse,
and at some point the phone was in his hand and the board was open,
and he had not decided to do that.

The vaguepost was still up.
The coat was still up. Rhonda was still up.
Under Rhonda there were two gray lines where replies had been,
*removed by the moderator*,
and the two words that had been typed there at five past nine,
*Coach* and *Dr. Cole*, were gone.
Marisol Pruett had taken the names and left the weather.
She had not posted a word from her own account.

Under the gray lines, Tim Brasher had.

> **Tim Brasher** Just so everybody has the whole picture. The mod was
> in the back booth at the Mule with the doctor last night. Two
> replies are gone this morning. I'm not drawing a conclusion. I'm
> saying who's deleting what.

Dan read the thread a second time for what Marisol had left in,
and understood she had left it in on purpose.
She had cut the two names and left the sentence that pointed at them,
because a mod who cut the sentence was a mod with something to hide.
So the sentence stayed up, and she took the flak for not hiding it.

He had learned this ten years ago.
A leak doesn't arrive anywhere.
It's already everywhere it was ever going to be, and then you find out.

Further down, under a name he did not know, from out of state,
there was a post that was not about her at all.
Anybody who wanted on the letter could message the poster.
They were keeping it "respectful and factual."
"11 families so far."
He knew which letter.
Denny Gault had read him a piece of it off his phone
and called it some of the dads.
It had a number now,
and somebody in another time zone was keeping the count.

He closed the board.
He ran the third-down package again, nine plays,
and did not see one of them.
He opened it again.
He had not decided to do that either.
Out the window the RAV4 was where it had been at noon.
He put the phone face down on the marker tray
and put the next play up.

***

At the house, at midnight, the keys went on the hook,
and the coat was still under them, the one he had kept.
He did not open a beer.
He sat on the edge of the bed and read the board.

Three times today.
He counted it, and he had not counted before.
Nobody had made him read it at the counter;
the coordinator had sent a picture, not an order.
Nobody had made him open it in the film room.
Nobody was making him now.
Monday night he had read it once, for her name,
and her name had not been on it, and he had caught himself looking.
Today her name had been on it since nine in the morning,
in a coat and a truck and a booth and a hold,
and nobody had typed it.
He had read it three times anyway, at a counter and at a window and here,
for a story about the two of them that was wrong in every fact it had,
and at no reading of the three had he wanted it taken down.

Near the bottom of the thread, twenty minutes old, was a local name.

> **B. Hollis** Late one for whoever's still up. Showed Earlene Tatum
> the picture this evening. She says that is a crew cab, four doors,
> a Silverado, and Coach drives an F-150 with two doors and has since
> he got here. She doesn't type, so I'm typing it. Y'all can argue
> with her at the Checkerboard.

Earlene had seen it. She had counted the doors.
By breakfast the Table would have the whole thing adjourned,
and the town with it,
and the board, three states wide, had run it from nine to midnight
on a coat and a truck with the wrong number of doors.
He read the post twice.
It took the two of them out of one truck
and put them back where they had been, across a sheet,
and it put him back in a two-door pickup with nobody in the other seat.

He got up and took the phone across the room to the charger.
He set it down face up, and left it that way.
