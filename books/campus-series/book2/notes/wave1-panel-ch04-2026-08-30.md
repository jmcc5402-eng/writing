## 1. WARMTH FIRST

**CANDIDATE X**

Does she fall for Dan? Yes, and hardest of the three, because X lets her catch him being better than his own reflexes: "his mouth was already loading the soft version. A range. A 'most guys.' A 'we'll know more Tuesday.' ... and he shut his mouth and let the silence run a full lap of the room." The 10 p.m. reader: "He didn't even get credit for it. That's the whole appeal. Nobody saw him do the right thing except me." That's romance-hero manufacturing done in his own POV without a drop of self-congratulation.

The two-hander: this is the flutter she paid for. It has *duration* — "Sit down, Coach. You're looming," the consultation where she asks him for "straight, not loyal" and types his answers, then "Neither of them got up." Nothing is named. Two professionals sit in bad chairs and don't leave, and the buyer-of-four-books said out loud: "the minute ran long, and neither of them called it — okay, I'm in for the whole book now." The doorway close — "Good win," and "Six months of her voice, and that was the first call in it he didn't want to challenge" — is the exact unnamed-want note the outline ordered.

Lean in: from "The door opened at eleven-ten and it wasn't a nurse" straight through to "was gone down the hall" — no skims inside the two-hander. Mild coast: the middle of the Mule section ("He shook what needed shaking") reads as summary, but it's short and it's *supposed* to be Dan on autopilot. Nobody put it down.

Binge answer: **yes.** She turns the page to find out what these two are at eleven p.m. on a *normal* night. The board button ("His last look at the board, unread, pocketed") reads as a man changing — she wants to watch the rest of the change.
