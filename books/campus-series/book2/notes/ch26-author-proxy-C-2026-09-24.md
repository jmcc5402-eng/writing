**1.** l.64–70: "I'd have played him." … "the breath went out of him
with nothing in it." The moment the chapter is for — the reader furious
in 25 goes "oh, he didn't mean it that way." But we never hear the rest
of his answer: not here, not at the AD's desk ("there had ever been a
second sentence," l.93), not in the truck ("finished his answer,"
l.449). If I have to guess, I can't forgive him yet. Put the half he
meant in his head, in plain words. The thinking about her before the
question is only about six lines (l.37–52) — I want him drowning in her
in that too-warm room, so the old coach's answer reads as his head on
her, not a slip; the reviewer turning a page (l.69) doesn't stop a man
finishing a sentence — his head should. Also "the navy coat on the hook
in his hall… the mate to hers" (l.49–51) made me stop. [MORE]

**2.** l.305–313: "Aisha was going. Nobody had told him so." And then
nothing. Her last thought in 25 was "He had not asked," and 29 is him
asking — the climax of the whole book. So here I need to feel he wants
to ask her to stay and doesn't, and why he thinks he can't. She tells
him "I'd rather be gone than be that" (l.431) — the reason is right
there. Right now the truck's fear (l.456–459) is her believing the
radio: it sets up the hearing, but nothing sets up the ask. He must NOT
ask — let the reader watch him not ask, the way he watched her not ask
at the car window in 24. [MORE]

**3.** l.128–143: "So he would keep quiet through the hearing's week."
The plain "the job you've wanted your whole life" only comes out of
Aisha's mouth at l.378; the decision happens in one "so" right after
he hears she's leaving. Same as 24: too easy. The job, his guys and her
pulling against each other at that desk, with feeling. The Table does
it well ("He also knew it was the easy thing to believe tonight,"
l.279); the desk needs it too. [WHO]

**4.** l.447–456: "That was the price, and he counted it to the dollar.
What scared him was not the price." The price listed in one sentence
and the next line says it doesn't scare him — that makes it cheap. It's
not enough to say the stakes; they have to be high. Let him feel it:
the job, the men on his staff, Ty and his truck payment. And WHY does
speaking at the hearing kill his extension — the AD? the trustees?
because it makes the review about the two of them? One plain line of
who does it to him and why. [POINT]

**5.** l.347–350: "I'm not the one who wrote the first half down. If
there's a second half, you know whose it is." I don't understand this.
This is the line that sends him to the hearing in 27 — it has to be
clear what she's telling him to do. The card's "say it to them" was
plainer. [CONFUSING]

**6.** l.452–453 and l.466: "He thought of Whit's girl too… and did not
know why." If he doesn't, I don't. Is Whit's job tied to Dan's? And the
last line — "He put the turn signal on, away from home." Where at nine
on a Thursday? At least a clause of what that turn means before the
ticking. [CONFUSING]

**7.** l.106–108: "from a man who would not sit down. Neither of them
said where it came from." Is this Boyd? Let Dan think his name. Same at
l.245–253: Delmar's, "a Table," Tick, Ray — a clause each. And Verna at
l.303 just looks out the window; one line of what she thinks of the
coach's truck in her gravel the week of the hearing. [WHO]

**8.** l.102 and l.251: "a class that signed on Wednesday" — it's
Thursday, so I read yesterday; l.260–261 says two sign on the third.
Say which Wednesday; signing day and the findings are the same day. And
l.328, "together in the season" — to a normal reader the season ended
in December; say the rule runs to signing day. [CONFUSING]
