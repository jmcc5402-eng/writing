## FINDINGS A–I

**A. The argument, said.** Cover the page: *Dan agreed to twenty minutes to her face, asked his boss for Thursday with film on instead, and the boss said no and put it in writing with her copied.* The sentence is on the page at **356–358**: "He had agreed to twenty minutes to her face, / gone to the athletic director to get around her, / and the athletic director had told him no." A stranger can repeat it. It is a proper layered render — the email (image, 345–351), the gloss ("Projector off, he had said… Film on, to his boss," 353–354), the plain sentence, then the consequence (359–360). A listener knows all three facts by 358. Decode dependencies: "Projector off, he had said, standing at her board" restates line 277 inside the passage, so it is self-contained. "The thing she had not written down" (376) leans on 338, thirty-eight lines back — within a page, passes. **The one decode that leans too far for the ear is the cc itself, 340–343:** the word "email" never appears, and "her name under Merritt's on the tablet" asks a listener to assemble a To/CC line from a picture. Reader: "wait, is she reading his mail?" Editor: the prop is not named. Fix: graft 1.

**B. The reversal.** Who loses: Dan, in writing, to his boss; Aisha wins and pays — "So she had an angry head coach now, / and a no in writing she had not asked for" (359–360) is win and cost in one sentence, which is what the brief asked for. Does the reader *feel* Dan lose? Half. The loss arrives as information — a good email and a plain gloss — and the only feeling attached is hers: "She had not had him down as one of them" (363). What the passage never does is look at the thing he left in her room. His folded card is still under her caster (313, "The board stood level on his folded card and did not drift") and the passage reads an email about him going around her without glancing at it. The four-a-month reader: "the card is *right there*." Editor: the loss is reported, not staged; the one object that would stage it is on the floor of the scene, unused. Fix: graft 2. The second cost (the board, 390–395) lands, and lands better in audio than on the page: the listener heard "medical determination" in the email forty lines earlier and hears the board pick it up — the phrase leaking, exactly per addendum 7. Only the syntax at 391 trips the ear (see F).

**C. The arc beat.** Aisha: yes, it is an action watched, in her voice, with the why attached. "She did not call him. / There was nothing to say that his boss had not said" (367–368) — action and motive clause. "She told nobody. / She answered the athletic director alone, *Received, thank you,* / and sent nothing to Merritt. / Then she opened Trey's chart" (371–375). Then the belief, said once: "That was all she ever did with a win. / She wrote it down where it would keep" (387–388). That is the false belief — a record is a place where things keep — in eleven words a listener repeats. Dan, from her POV: "She had known men who agreed in the room / and went out the door to try the next one" (361–362) lands as *her* judgment, not the author's, because her wound is being reclassified from expert to problem by exactly such men (arc doc §1). It is close to the brief's wording ("agree in the room, escalate outside it") but it is the say-it line for what he is, and the listener needs it. What reads as the author's hand is the cap after it — "being wrong about a man was a thing she preferred / to find out early, in writing, at no cost to the patient" (364–365) — a triple arranged to be admired. Cut the cap, keep the judgment.

**D. The romance contract.** The email deepens the want-with-obstacle rather than writing him off, and the reason is one line: "She had not had him down as one of them" (363). That is the sentence of a woman who had him down as something better, and still does underneath. The third flat thing in the want passage was that he never saw a woman (329); the email adds that he didn't honor the colleague either, and the reader wants him more for it because she now needs him to *become* the man Aisha had him down as. That is the trope the package sells ("chain-of-command — inverted"). Is he made small? Not by the email — "You have asked… You have characterized…" is a boss's respectful paper trail, and he crouched to fix her floor sixty lines earlier. The only line that shrinks him is **369–370**: "calling a man to tell him he had lost / was for people who needed to hear it out loud. She didn't." That is a gloat in her mouth in the one beat where she should be disappointed, and it makes *her* smaller too. Reviewer-reader: "she's a little cold here, and not in the good way." Editor: reply-position quip in narration; the line before it already did the work. Fix: graft 3. Last page: she turns it. The chapter used to end on weather; it now ends on a question with his POV next.

**E. The ending.** Last five lines, 399–403: "Out the window the practice-field lights came on / across the lot, one bank at a time, / orange warming into white. / The whiteboard caught them and held them, / small and bright, just under the red circle, / while she worked." Still lands, and lands *better*, because 396 now sends her into it: "She put the phone down and picked the black marker back up." Black is the ladder's color from line 19; a listener who has lost that gets the literal sense anyway — she went back to work. The win sits under the lights unspent: no call, no reply-all, no line of satisfaction, just the county's calendar lit by the county's lights while her ladder gets worked. "back up" is a hair loose (she last held a marker at line 19, not in this scene), but it reads as "back to it" and I would not touch it. If graft 2 goes in, "held them" at 402 picks up a quiet second meaning — the board holding on his card — which costs nothing.

**F. Varnish and obliqueness.** Ruled line by line:
- "She had known men who agreed in the room / and went out the door to try the next one." (361–362) — **meant.** The say-it line for his pattern. Keep.
- "calling a man to tell him he had lost / was for people who needed to hear it out loud. She didn't." (369–370) — **varnish**, reply position. Cut.
- "and being wrong about a man was a thing she preferred / to find out early, in writing, at no cost to the patient." (364–365) — **half varnish.** The logic is hers; the cadence is the author's. Cut in favor of the card.
- "That was all she ever did with a win. / She wrote it down where it would keep." (387–388) — **meant.** Arc beat, said plainly then imaged once. Keep both lines; without "win" said, the second is just charting.
- "two words of him" (391) — **too oblique for the ear.** "Of him" is a grammatical sidestep a listener has to re-parse. Fix: graft 4.
- On-the-nose count: the same fact is told three times (email, gloss, chart). The chart is the brief's own requirement and is in a different voice, so I don't count it as a third stakes line — but it is why the passage feels long. No trim proposed there; see I.

**G. The email as prop.** Voice reads true: an administrator building a paper trail restates the request in neutral words before declining it, and cc's the person whose authority he is citing. "You have characterized the team physician's offer of twenty minutes / as insufficient to the purpose" (347–348) is the line that does the escalation work and it is exactly how an AD quotes a coach back to him. "This office is not in a position to grant it" (349) is the right no — it says the call was never his, which keeps the power line where the premise puts it. "your quarterback" is correct and slightly chilly, which suits. "the timeline under it is hers" is the keeper. Seven italic lines is right for the ear — long enough to be a document, short enough that the listener holds all of it. The only miss is that the narration never calls it an email (see A).

**H. The seam.** Resolved, and resolved well. 338 ("she wrote nothing down") is followed by *reading*, not writing, so there is no contradiction inside three lines. When the writing comes at 375–380 the distinction is stated in a sentence: "The thing she had not written down had been hers / and belonged in no record. / This had a patient's name on it, a date, / a request made and a request refused, / and things like that she wrote down." A listener hears both objects named and the rule that sorts them. Reader: "oh — the *want* was hers; this is the boy's." Nothing to fix.

**I. Length and momentum.** 52 lines against 25–40. In one sitting it drags at exactly two places, both where she stops acting and starts commenting: 364–365 and 369–370. Neither is the drafter's offered candidate. Ruling on those: "She had known men…" **keep** (it is the say-it line; cut it and the listener loses what Dan is). "She wrote it down where it would keep." **keep** (it is the arc beat's image and a swoon line). The grafts below take the passage to 49 lines. Still over the brief's number, and I'd ship it there: the binge reader doesn't count lines, she counts restatements, and after the grafts the passage says each thing once plus the chart.

**Superconcepts.** Nothing cheapened. SC4 is strengthened — the two of them were frank in the room and the conflict survived it, on the page. SC3 gets a quiet touch in "where it would keep" (the record stays; she never has). SC1's second test is met elsewhere in the chapter (the Checkerboard, the Blind Mule); the board is a chat room and doesn't count, per the file. SC2 untouched. Canon: athletic director unnamed, "your quarterback," no protocol title, no rung named, "Trey" only in the doctor's chart, no new double-dash, no new "never once," no paragraph ending on a colon or dash. "copied" is avoided rather than echoed; fine.

## VERDICT: SHIP WITH GRAFTS

All four grafts sit inside lines 340–397. Nothing outside them changes.

**Graft 1 — name the prop once (340–343).**
Before:
```
She was four lines into the athletic director's reply
before she understood why she had it at all,
her name under Merritt's on the tablet,
still lit from her four-o'clock charting.
```
After:
```
She was four lines into the athletic director's email
before she understood why she had it at all.
It was to Merritt. Her name was under his on the tablet,
still lit from her four-o'clock charting.
```

**Graft 2 — cut the cap, look at the card (361–365).**
Before:
```
She had known men who agreed in the room
and went out the door to try the next one.
She had not had him down as one of them,
and being wrong about a man was a thing she preferred
to find out early, in writing, at no cost to the patient.
```
After:
```
She had known men who agreed in the room
and went out the door to try the next one.
She had not had him down as one of them.
The board was still level on his card.
```
This is my proposal and my enjoyment is not permission: it looks at a protected object without moving it, adds no personification, and reads literally for a listener who lost the card. If the orchestrator would rather not risk it, the fallback is the same cut ending on "She had not had him down as one of them." — the passage still ships.

**Graft 3 — cut the reply-position quip (367–371).**
Before:
```
She did not call him.
There was nothing to say that his boss had not said,
and calling a man to tell him he had lost
was for people who needed to hear it out loud. She didn't.
She told nobody.
```
After:
```
She did not call him.
There was nothing to say that his boss had not said.
She told nobody.
```

**Graft 4 — fix the ear at the board (391–392).**
Before:
```
The athletic director was already on it, two words of him,
"medical determination" in quotation marks,
```
After:
```
Two of the athletic director's words were already on it,
"medical determination" in quotation marks,
```
("no name anywhere, not his" at 395 still resolves — the possessive keeps him in the sentence.)
