# Grapevine Realism Rulebook — 2026-08-29

Culture-researcher study, author-directed. Directive: "I don't want it to
become over the top… I'd rather keep Grapevine really in line with real
life chat rooms (which can be outrageous enough)." Mid-run course
correction (author, 2026-08-29): the story-engine group is a PARENTS'
BOARD — "more about the insecurities of the parents and less about the
town." The platform hosts multiple groups (Book 1 precedent: The Shed
Thread, ch03:48); the town-wide room is a second, separate space and is
NOT the drama engine.

## §1 Findings

### 1a. THE CORE: what sports/school parent groups are actually like

- **Playing time is the engine.** Coaching-communication and youth-sports
  reporting agree that playing time "dominates every sideline
  conversation" and is the root cause of parent conflict; parents
  "politick" to get into the coach's ear (Jersey Watch; I Love to Watch
  You Play; Youth Inc.). Grievance is about MY kid's minutes, captaincy,
  position — dressed up as concern for "the program."
- **Pile-ons are small and organized, not spontaneous mobs.** The
  documented Somers (NY) field hockey case: a U.S. Olympian coach ousted
  after "a small group of outspoken parents" complained about playing
  time and captain picks; one father's private text to the coach: "You're
  a cancer and embarrassment to this program… You're a dead man walking"
  (CBS New York). Pattern: a handful of aligned parents + email/text
  campaign to the AD, not 12,000 people raging in public.
- **The vicious sentence is written in PRIVATE and surfaces by leak.**
  Multiple coach-firing sagas turn on screenshots of texts surfacing
  after the fact (CBS NY Somers; Fox16 Arkansas; Athletic Business).
  The public board post is the polished version; the raw version travels
  as a screenshot.
- **Naming and shaming an adult in writing is legally dangerous, and
  parents half-know it.** A former high school coach was permitted to sue
  parents for defamation over their campaign against him (Athletic
  Business). This is a real brake on what parents put under their own
  names.
- **Performative concern is the accepted public register.** School
  WhatsApp-group reporting (Teachwire; Debrett's etiquette guide;
  Telegraph/AOL "scandals of the school WhatsApp group") documents groups
  turning into "gossip, hostility and mob rule," parents collectively
  turning on staff, and misinformation cascades — but the on-record
  idiom is "just worried about the kids," logistics questions with edges,
  and recruiting-other-parents-to-a-cause (explicitly listed as a
  don't in etiquette guides because it happens constantly). UK schools
  now issue conduct rules for parent chats and worry about liability for
  abuse of teachers in them (Daily Mail via PressReader; Teachwire).
- **Status and money anxieties are real and quantified.** Peer-reviewed
  surveys: high school baseball parents spent a median $3,000/yr, and
  35.9% believed their child would likely get a college scholarship
  while underestimating how few exist (~5 per D-I team is the real
  median they missed) (Sports Health / PMC6955882; same pattern for
  basketball parents, PubMed 34485584). The delusion gap between
  investment and odds is the documented root of parent insecurity.
- **Officials and medical staff absorb it.** Referee shortages driven by
  abusive sideline parents are widely reported (Patch/Block Talk; CBS
  Boston). Athletic-trainer research names "pressure from coaches,
  parents and students to return athletes to play" as a documented
  barrier in concussion management, with evidence athletes are cleared
  prematurely (PMC10242438; CDC HEADS UP return-to-play guidance).
  A parent board second-guessing a team physician's caution is squarely
  inside documented reality — the REALISTIC form is "why is she still
  holding him out, the state game is Friday," not accusations of
  malpractice under real names.

### 1b. Do these groups publicly gossip about identifiable people's

romances? (the author's core question)

- **On main boards: rarely, and the platforms formally forbid it.**
  Nextdoor's guidelines route personal disputes to private messages and
  prohibit shaming posts; "describing someone as 'dishonest' is never
  appropriate… even if you believe it to be true" (Nextdoor Moderator
  Academy / community standards). Searches for documented cases of
  neighbors publicly gossiping about a named neighbor's affair on
  Nextdoor/town groups come back essentially empty — the documented
  removals are political posts, feuds, and shaming, not romance threads.
  Treat the absence as the finding: named-neighbor romance gossip is not
  a documented mainstay of these boards. [CHECK: absence of evidence —
  no study directly measures it; hold this as "rare enough that no one
  reports on it," not "never happens."]
- **Gossip itself is constant — just not THERE.** Dunbar's line of
  research: ~65% of human conversation is social/personal topics
  (Dunbar 2004, Review of General Psychology; revisited in Language and
  Cognition, Cambridge). The gossip exists; it flows through face-to-face
  talk, DMs, and side chats — channels without receipts and audiences.
- **The public forms it does take are deniable.** Vaguebooking is the
  documented public register: deliberately ambiguous posts aimed at a
  specific person, associated in research with loneliness and
  participation in online gossip (Psychology Today 2024; HuffPost).
  "Asking for a friend" is a genuine, widely recognized real-world dodge.
  The other documented form: affectionate public teasing of a group's
  own well-known figures (see §3).
- **When staff romance IS discussed publicly, it's because it crossed
  into misconduct** — coach-student impropriety cases become news and
  investigations (SwimSwam; Athletic Business; CBS Miami/Gulliver), not
  recreational threads. Adults recreationally speculating in writing,
  under their own names, about a coach's or doctor's love life is the
  thing the defamation risk and the social cost both suppress. It
  happens in the parking lot and the side chat.
- **What happens to a gossiper who tries it on the main board:** mods
  remove it under no-drama/civility rules (Nextdoor guidelines; FB group
  moderation practice of 5–7 pinned rules, keyword filters), and/or the
  poster gets piled on for posting it — moderation reporting notes
  members tagging mods and mods stepping in when threads go personal
  (BuzzFeed News on neighborhood group moderation).

### 1c. Where the juicy version actually lives

- **The splinter space is documented by name.** "Croton Uncensored" —
  a Hudson Valley village's no-rules Facebook group founded Oct 2023
  specifically to counter the moderated village groups (The Croton
  Chronicle, two pieces). The moderated-main/unmoderated-splinter pair
  is a real, reported structure, and every moderated town group of any
  size tends to grow one.
- **Anonymous layers exist where there's a campus.** Yik Yak (shut down
  2017 "amid reports of rampant bullying," relaunched, acquired by
  Sidechat 2023), Fizz, Sidechat: the UNC System moved in 2024 to block
  all four named apps over cyberbullying and harassment (Inside Higher
  Ed; CBS News; FIRE objected on speech grounds). Campus-adjacent towns
  realistically have an anonymous student layer WAY nastier than any
  parents' board — useful as an offstage pressure source.
- **Group-chat sociology:** the real unit is concentric rings — the
  official team chat (coach can see), the parents-only chat, and the
  four-parent side chat where the real sentences get typed. The
  screenshot is the only bridge between rings, and screenshot leaks are
  the documented failure mode (see 1a).

### 1d. The town room (background register)

- **Topic mix, measured:** NYU's 2024 study of Nextdoor posts — services
  sought/offered first; then neighborhood logistics (lost pets,
  transportation, events); then pleasantries (holiday wishes, weather).
  Crime/suspicion posts were only ~6% of volume BUT drew the highest
  average comment counts (NYU News, May 2024). Roughly 1 in 14 posts is
  a pet post (Webbiquity's Nextdoor topic analysis). Surveys: "dogs,
  cats and parking" top the list (Miami Herald/AOL Nextdoor survey).
  So: mostly boring, punctuated by disproportionate flare-ups around
  suspicion and grievance. That ratio IS the realism.
- **Documented absurdity, for calibration:** the Best of Nextdoor
  account's origin post — a neighbor accusing others of rearranging her
  lawn gnomes at night, the complaint signed by every member of the
  gnome family, posted daily (AP interview with Jenn Takahashi, 2022).
  Gerald the turkey: five months menacing an Oakland rose garden, saga
  followed avidly online, captured by an expert disguised as his
  preferred victim, an older woman (CBC; NPR, 2020). Real absurdity is
  ANIMALS, OBJECTS, TERRITORY, PROCEDURE — almost never sex.
- **Mod drama is its own documented genre:** neighborhood-group admin
  life includes boycott threats and threats of physical assault over
  moderation calls (BuzzFeed News, "Running a neighbourhood Facebook
  group has become a seriously complicated job"); admins quitting en
  masse (KQED); Nextdoor's volunteer "Leads" wield broad power with no
  mechanism for residents to vote them out (CT Examiner; NPR). Book 1's
  moderator coup is genre-accurate.

## §2 THE GRAPEVINE RULES (for drafters)

Two on-page rooms plus the dark layer. The platform hosts many groups;
these are the ones the book visits.

### The Parents' Board (the engine — 12,000 members, Marisol moderates)

1. **DO run every grievance through a child.** The realistic public
   register is performative concern: "just worried about the kids,"
   "asking for clarity on the rotation," "is anyone else's son still
   waiting on an evaluation?" Real parent groups dress playing-time
   politics as program concern (Jersey Watch; Teachwire). A parent who
   posts naked self-interest is making a mistake other parents notice.
2. **DON'T let the board speculate in plain text about any adult's love
   life under real names.** No documented main-board precedent, platform
   rules forbid it, defamation is a live fear (Athletic Business suit).
   The permitted public forms: vagueposting ("Interesting to see who
   gets rides home from the facility these days"), the "asking for a
   friend" dodge, and affectionate lore about the board's OWN public
   figures (§3). Anything sharper gets typed in the side chat.
3. **DO make pile-ons small, organized, and epistolary.** Five parents,
   a shared grievance, a drafted "letter of concern," receipts folders,
   CC lines. Not a 400-comment flame war — a quiet whip count (Somers
   case). The board's public thread stays eerily polite while the
   campaign runs underneath it.
4. **DO give the physician dogpile its documented shape:** impatience
   with medical caution framed as devotion ("she's being so careful,
   which we all appreciate, but the state game is Friday and he FEELS
   fine"), second-hand expertise, comparison to what some other town's
   trainer allegedly cleared. Athletic trainers report exactly this
   pressure (PMC10242438). DON'T have parents publicly allege
   incompetence or misconduct by name — that jump is the story-event,
   not the baseline, and when it happens the board itself should flinch.
5. **DO let status leak through money and logistics.** $3,000 seasons,
   private hitting coaches, showcase-weekend humblebrags, the scholarship
   arithmetic a third of them privately believe (PMC6955882). Parents
   compare children by comparing expenditures and schedules — never by
   saying the child's name in a ranking.

### Board-artifact composition (showrunner condition, wave 1,
2026-08-30 — added at the wave-1 brief audit's direction, B8)

On-page board text reads as POSTS, not summary: short quoted
threads; usernames plausible for 35–45 parents; never more than
~6 posts per artifact. The vicious version is never on the main
board (rule 8).

### The Town Room (separate group — background texture, NOT the engine)

6. **DO keep the mix boring-with-flares:** services, lost pets (1 in 14),
   road work, events, weather pleasantries; suspicion posts rare but
   drawing triple the comments (NYU 2024). Its absurdity is animals,
   objects, territory, procedure — a gnome, a turkey, a parking spot,
   a casserole dish not returned — never romance (AP/Takahashi; CBC).
7. **DON'T cross the streams unprompted.** The town room does not care
   about the rotation; the parents' board does not care about the
   retainer in the waffle. When a story JUMPS rooms — a parents' board
   grievance surfacing in the town room — that's an escalation beat and
   should cost someone something.

### The dark layer (side chats, DMs, splinters — mostly offstage)

8. **The vicious version is always typed somewhere smaller, and travels
   only by screenshot.** The four-parent side chat (canon precedent:
   The Shed Thread) is where "dead man walking" sentences get written
   (CBS NY). On-page, we see the leak, not the feed: a screenshot shown
   across a diner table, a forward with the names cropped badly. If the
   author wants an uncensored splinter group, real precedent exists
   (Croton Uncensored) — but keep Grapevine's main rooms moderated and
   deniable. The campus's anonymous student app layer (Yik Yak/Fizz
   heritage) may be REFERRED to as a rumored worse place; keep it
   offstage per R8/audience rules.

## §3 Reconciliation with Book 1 canon (accepted pages — untouchable)

Honest assessment: Book 1 sits INSIDE realistic range, comfortably.

- **The ch 29 "kiss thread"** (the board inventing Marisol-and-Cal lore:
  high school sweethearts, porch in the rain, a first kiss somebody's
  cousin attended) is public romance talk — but of the precise kind
  reality permits: post-hoc, celebratory mythologizing of the group's
  own most public figure, by a community that adores her, after the
  relationship was effectively public. That is folklore-making about a
  local celebrity (the same communal energy documented around Gerald
  the turkey), not speculative exposure of a private person. Marisol
  reading it aloud, laughing, is exactly the difference.
- **"Asking for a friend"** is itself the real-world dodge (§1b), and
  the accepted ch 23 instance is Earlene SPOKEN ALOUD at the Liars'
  Table — oral small-town gossip, the most realistic channel there is.
- **Concerned4Ashford / AshfordSafetyMom** (ch 11, ch 13): coordinated
  sock-puppet astroturf with clockwork reply timing is well inside
  documented local-group behavior, and Marisol reading the timestamps
  is good moderator craft.
- **The moderator coup and badge restoration** (ch 25): mod drama is a
  reported genre — admins threatened, boycotted, deposed (BuzzFeed News;
  KQED; CT Examiner). Genre-accurate.
- **How 1.2 stays calibrated without retconning:** treat ch 29 as the
  board's one earned exception, not a license. The rule going forward:
  the board may lionize and tease its own public figures in daylight;
  it may NOT speculate about private adults' romances in plain text.
  The premise-seeds hookup beat ("Grapevine has it by 9 a.m.") should
  therefore arrive as a LEAK ARTIFACT — a screenshot escaping a side
  chat, a vaguepost the whole board can decode — not as a named
  thread on the main board. Same clock, realistic mechanism.

## §4 Ten real-flavor starters (composites from research; no real people)

Parents' board register (1–6), town room register (7–10):

1. "Not trying to start anything, just looking for CLARITY on how
   captains were selected this year. Several of us have questions."
2. "Reminder that we are all adults and this board is read by more
   people than you think. Deleting my earlier post. You know who you
   are."
3. "Can anyone recommend a private QB coach within an hour? Asking
   because SOME of us weren't handed the summer camp invite list."
   (grievance wearing a recommendation-request costume)
4. "Genuine question for the group, no agenda: is a 'precautionary
   hold' a real medical category or a coaching decision? My husband
   looked it up and couldn't find it."
5. "I've said my piece privately and I'll leave it there. All I'll say
   here is: interesting season. Interesting choices. Praying for the
   boys." (vaguepost, deniable, aimed)
6. "DM me if you'd like to be included on the letter. We're keeping it
   respectful and factual. 11 families so far."
7. "FOUND: retainer, in a waffle, at the pancake breakfast. This is a
   judgment-free post. Come get your retainer."
8. "The individual moving my planter six inches to the left every
   night: I have installed a camera. I am not angry. I am fascinated."
9. "Is the light on Millrow SUPPOSED to do that? Day 40. I have begun
   naming the pigeons that wait with me."
10. "To whoever returned my casserole dish full of a DIFFERENT
    casserole: this is not how the dish comes back, but I've prayed on
    it and honestly yours was better. Recipe?"
