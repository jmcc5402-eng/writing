# SUPERFAN — SIX RANDOM CHAPTERS (4, 6, 7, 15, 18, 19), 2026-09-15

Read as the woman who flips to the good parts. The promise I held it against: closed door "with its want said out loud," a doctor who is never proven wrong, a coach who pays for her call in public, and a parents' board written for me, not at me.

## The six, in the order I flipped to them

**Ch 18 — the week the town was gone. 5 stars.** I hit this one first and did not put the book down. He walks down the hill every night because his truck can't be seen; she takes his keys out of his pocket "so you'll know where they are at five." The five a.m. run past each other, one hand up, one hand up, "Walk faster, Dan. You'll lose a foot." The film room, "your mouth goes." The boy on her stool with her card behind him and her never saying so. Then the house: "You cleaned like the landlord was coming." "I cleaned like you were." The jelly jar that "came with jelly in it." Wine "because we're in a house, not a room." The sweater lands on the hall floor and the door shuts exactly where the package said it would, and the rinsed bottle in the sink the next morning finished me. Only skim: the replay of the week at the end, which I'd just read. Delivers taste 7, 8, 16, 18; the one holiday page is done right per 7.

**Ch 15 — the hold and the first kiss. 5 stars, with a grumble.** The grumble is the first paragraph: Wednesday two, today three, tomorrow four, Saturday five, a five has to sleep before it's a six. I skipped it. Then it got good and stayed good: Verna's "I rent nights. I know what one's worth," the trainer telling her what Coach said to the captains before she knew there was a bill, and her wanting him for it "with his trainer three feet off." Then he stands in her doorway and tells her the worst thing, and she gets up, shuts the door with him inside it, and says "I am not putting you out." "I've been carrying your name since a hospital lounge in November." His hands hold still first. The seven-week wait is explained on the page, so no "why aren't they together" rage. The pet-peeve reader will say she forgave "I nodded" too fast; I sided with her. Risks taste 1 (that opening); delivers 8, 12, 16.

**Ch 7 — the coat. 5 stars.** A parka in a men's XXL with A. COLE stitched on it, and she thinks "about the shape of the man it had been cut for" and nobody says whose it is. "She had put it on for the name as much as for the down." The equipment man with the heat packs and "count your fingers" is my favorite stranger in the book. The Liars' Table teasing her because "jokes were for fixtures." The paper hand to hand over the diploma box, "four inches of cardboard stood between them and nothing else did." The sleeves holding the roll at the end. I skimmed the lap through the records room; KNEES, 1994 made me come back. "What do you want out of me?" "Nothing" reads cold to some (taste 15 risk); delivers 1, 4, 11.

**Ch 19 — the portal. 4 stars.** Best opening post in the book: "Found his hoodie in my dryer. Feed him an egg for me." (Taste 19, delivered.) She hands a kid his records and a letter and he doesn't say thank you, and "nobody had handed her a thing at the door" got me. Boyd in her corridor with his shoes shined is genuinely creepy; "Son of a bitch," to the glass, is the most I've liked her. Then Dan with the boxes: "Where do you want me." "The boxes." I laughed out loud. Marker in his teeth. "Four weeks of Coach and Doc, and I've been in your building an hour." Where it sagged: she sits at the bar alone, then on the bed alone, and it goes a little lonely (taste 7 risk). And she doesn't tell him about Boyd's job offer. One day, fine; if that runs three chapters I will be in the comments.

**Ch 4 — the hospital lounge. 4 stars.** Flipped here cold and it's a dark room after a big win, which is a strange place to fall for people, and I did. He notices her hair "and then noticed that he had." "Sit down, Coach. You're looming." "Quit studying me like I'm film." "I'm studying the cup." "It's French vanilla, Coach. There's nothing on it." The minute that runs long and nobody calls it. The board thread at the end, "Clarity is not criticism," is every group I'm in. The whiskey at the bar I skimmed. Risks 7 (heavy); delivers 15 (the cup into Denny's hands), 4.

**Ch 6 — Selection Sunday. 3 stars.** A whole porch, a baby in earmuffs, brisket, the county's name on TV. Fun town, and Dan refusing the letter out loud is a good moment, and Delores's "pray for whoever it falls on to say no" is a great one. But they say six lines to each other and one of them, "I'm not on Millrow, Coach," I had to read three times (taste 18: too clever). He smells her hair as she passes. That's the chapter's romance. I skimmed most of it. Risks 8 and 5 (a lot of country); delivers 2 and 15.

## The section, predicted

**The superfan, five stars, long:** "I have read every closed-door small-town book on this app and this is the first one where the closing of the door is the hottest part. They walk to each other in the cold. They take each other's keys. He buys seltzer because 'I've seen what you drink.' She shuts her own office door on him and says she's keeping him. And she is RIGHT the whole book. Nobody makes her back down. He goes into his own building and pays for her call before she knows the bill exists. The town is real (the diner cook counting a kid's breakfasts, the motel lady with the hang tags) and the parents' board is my group chat with the names changed. Quotes on my wall already: 'I rent nights. I know what one's worth.' 'Then we're two people in a motel.' 'It came with jelly in it.' 'Lamp's on. Curtain's shut. It's still yours.'"

**The "I wanted to love this" three-star:** "The romance is wonderful when it's on the page. My one gripe: this author loves a clock. Ten past seven, five fifteen, ten to seven, a quarter to five, second stool from the register. Every scene starts by telling me what time it is and which stool, and I wanted her face instead. I skipped the top of nearly every chapter to get to the two of them."

**The two-star pet-peeve:** "He admits he sat in a room and nodded at a plan that cut her job, and thirty seconds later she kisses him? And then she sits on a job offer from the villain and tells the coach 'he liked my list'? Just TELL him. Also the chapters are all about a football bracket. I don't care about the bracket."

**DNF at chapter 6:** "A fifth of the way in and they have had one real conversation, in a hospital. I got a BBQ porch and a TV show instead. Closed it."

**The side-character reviewer, five stars:** "Verna Poteat. Give her a book. And the equipment manager who looks at her hands once and knows her glove size."

**The star math, as these six predict it:** 50% five ("the motel week, I stayed up"), 28% four ("the coat, wanted more of them together sooner"), 14% three ("too much clock, too much bracket, slow tops of chapters"), 8% one-two (steam readers who ignored the packaging, and the "she forgave him too fast" crowd). Promise check: closed door kept exactly at the line; her call never bends; he pays in public first. Kept. I can't rule on the "one steamy side scene" promise because chapter 11 wasn't drawn.

**The one-click test:** Yes. 11 p.m., the night she finishes, she preorders 1.3 for the town and for whoever gets Verna's next hang tag. What could cost that click: if the Boyd job offer becomes a secret she keeps for weeks.

VERDICT: 4.5/5 as these six predict it
