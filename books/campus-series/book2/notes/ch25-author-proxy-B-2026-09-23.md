# AUTHOR-PROXY — ch 25 candidate B (2026-09-23, C8, blind, before the panel)

author-proxy 1.1.1 (the third trial), card C8 (a reader who skips to the good parts). Filed by the orchestrator from the proxy's final message; the text is the proxy's.

**l.95–101: "Twelve days ago, on this same station, he had said her protocol."** This was the big moment of 22, and here it gets one paragraph and "like a thing in a coat pocket." I want to feel what those two words were to her on the end of that bed. It was maybe the first time a man stood up for her out loud, in front of the whole county. And the paragraph just before it says "the first time standing beside her had cost him anything, he had gone and stood on the far side." But twelve days ago he did stand beside her, on this same station. That is what should make this morning hurt worst: he gave it to her and now he's taken it back. We've sort of glossed over it again. [MORE]

**l.388–396: "So she was going."** She decides to leave the county and Dan in about six lines. They read like a debate with herself ("The brave thing was… They were not the same thing"), not like a woman leaving the man she's in love with. Where is he in that moment? His face, the truck in the gravel, the fact that she won't stand at the rail beside him again. The ache does come later with the parka, and that part is good. But the actual choice is the coldest paragraph in the chapter, and it's the biggest decision she has made in the book. [MORE]

**l.219–223: the trainer.** He stands in the door with a roll of tape, says "Doc," and "that was all either of them had." He's the man in the middle between the two of them and he owes ten thousand dollars. None of that is in this room. I can't tell if he's sorry she's going, scared for himself, or relieved. One line of what he stands to lose, or which way he leans, and this room gets tense instead of just quiet. [WHO]

**l.154–177: the offer.** Would a university really offer a director's job to a doctor who is on leave and under a review, eight days before the findings, by email, with no interview we ever saw? It lands out of nowhere. And "Rex Boyd had passed her name along so that she would go": how does she know that? If she knows it, she'd be furious about it right there on the bed. If she's guessing, the page should say so. Right now the thing that makes her leave town happened offstage. [POINT]

**l.264–309: the church.** I'm not sure what the point of this scene is. She counts forty-one boxes and they match. What would have happened if they didn't? Say it. Canaan Bottom and 1968 go by in one sentence and do nothing, the way the glass tower used to. Delores's line is the best thing in the scene, so keep that. But it's also the scene where I feel the least romance: one line of him saying "thirty-one." [POINT]

**l.103–105: "told her film was at four."** I still don't understand this line. I asked about it on 24, and it's back with no explanation. Same problem with l.73, "fourteen minutes and a hand going up under a hood". What does that mean to someone who has never stood on a sideline? And l.248, "the scouts' file on her tablet": what file? And l.118, "not one of the Table": which table? [CONFUSING]

**l.253–254, 318–321, 338–346, 365–370: the towels, the rent, the letter.** Why are Verna's towels at the clinic, and why twelve of fourteen? Then we spend a paragraph washing and folding them, and "She had not lost one." I don't know why that matters. Verna hands back the rent and says "Furnace in nine ran behind all January." Is Verna saying don't go, or letting her off because the heat was bad? I can't tell. Then the letter: who is Cordelia Hartwell, what does "Hartwell like the House" mean, and why is her old letter in Aisha's coat? A reader who skipped a week has no idea. And l.392, "Only one of them cost Dan nothing, and paying had always been hers to do": I read it three times. [CONFUSING]

**l.137–140: "The cook had heard it at seven, and that was why the plate… Both of them were the town."** The pie is great: the cook sets it down, turns his back, and nobody takes it away. l.29–30 already tells us what it means. Explaining it a second time takes the air out of it. Let the plate do it. [NOSE]
