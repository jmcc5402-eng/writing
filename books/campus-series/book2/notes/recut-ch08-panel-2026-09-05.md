## THE NUMBERED JUDGMENTS

**A. THE OPENING, SAID.** First paragraph alone (16–23): *a radio show from a BBQ porch is live in December for the first time, and the county wants the coach to say tonight that Trey Gault is safe to play in the playoff on the nineteenth.* A stranger can repeat that. No "one word." It makes the granddad land *better*: the reader is now listening for "safe" before the granddad says it at 69, so "Say that one word" (71) and "The hour wanted one word out of him" (81) become the county's phrasing rather than the narrator's. One ear-snag: "Tonight the show wore coats. / The county wanted to hear him say, tonight," — "tonight" twice in two lines. *Fix:* drop the second (graft 1).

**B. THE DIAGNOSIS.** The license clause works for a listener: she was on the porch, she heard him *not ask* what "precautionary hold" meant, and "A man who doesn't read that board asks the caller what she means" (321–322) is a deduction a listener can make with her. It reads as a doctor building a case from one sign, not the author explaining — four sentences is the outside edge but each does a step. "It was a finding." (324) is the tic, not her: it is narration naming her register a second time, twenty lines after 304 already did it in Dan's own words ("the flat clinical one she used on a man saying he felt fine"). Reader: "I got it the first time." It is the meaning-namer reflex RECENT.md warns about. *Fix:* cut it (graft 3). "He did not answer her." (330) lands — the shortest line after her longest speech, white space under it. And the ch 2 line pays exactly: ch 2 "He knew that was a weakness. He did it anyway." → ch 8 "She was right about the board. He opened it anyway." (416). Same shape, the private knowledge now public. The reader who remembers ch 2 gets the click; the one who does not gets it plain.

**C. THE HEAT.** "close enough that the heat off her came through two coats" (315) is exactly what this shelf buys: heat as a fact, in the cold, through clothes, nobody saying it. Not clinical. "Her hands were on the rail, bare in the cold" is the sexiest line in the chapter for this reader — she is watching him look at hands he wants to cover. The re-reader also gets "two coats" as ch 7's coat (the brief keeps it unnamed; right call — do not name it). "Neither of them stepped back." (317) against ch 5's "Neither of them stepped back to make room. / There was room." (ch05:247–248): reads as deliberate — her room then, his porch now, and the shorter, colder version. It is also rung 5's signature phrase in STYLE.md, so it is the rung being played, not a copy. Once more would be a tic; ledger it as spent twice. One real repeat inside the new lines: "She had not moved off." (312) then "Neither of them stepped back." (317) — same fact twice in six lines. *Fix:* cut 312 (graft 2).

**D. THE STAKES.** 406–413 is the on-the-nose beat, placed right: after the want, after "call by call," before he opens the phone. Four one-breath sentences, and the coda ("The hour had been the county trying to talk her out of the last one, / and he had sat in the guest chair and let it") turns the summary back into his fault, which is why it does not read as a recap. Not a third stakes line where one would do — it is the first time it is whole. "The nineteenth" is now four times in the chapter (23, 60, 268, 409); the ear does not want a count — the date is how a county talks — but do not add a fifth. "Healed" is his word, not hers; fine in his head.

**E. THE BOARD.** Decodable by ear: Tim's "'it can live there' — the board she means is this one. Us. The families." decodes her line for anyone who missed it; "Just my two cents" is a sign-off every 35–45 mother knows. Rhonda's "Coach stayed in his lane tonight. Thank you Coach." is the knife, and it cuts because "stay in my lane" is *his* phrase from the air (158) handed back as praise — the county rewarding the exact cowardice she just diagnosed. "Thank you Coach," no comma, is a real post. Kendra's flinch is a small one, and small is right: she defends the *answer*, not the woman — nobody defends Aisha, which is the loss. "He did not post." (438) carries it, because it is the fourth silence in a row (dead air 239, no answer 330, nothing to Boyd 345, no post 438) and the chapter has spent 400 lines teaching the reader to hear silence. Rulebook: real names, no handles, no medical allegation, no motive, no love life, "new in this county" without "six months," the flinch present. Clean. Two small notes: "Take the rest of it to your phones" (434–435) is decodable but "group chats" would be plainer by ear and matches the modernity dial — optional. And the epigraph uses "**Tim Brasher:**" with a colon while the in-scene posts have none [CHECK: whether epigraph-vs-in-scene convention is deliberate; the addendum specifies in-scene only].

**F. BOYD.** Villain, not furniture — and the reason is placement, not description. 120–122 puts him at the top of the steps "smiling at the porch, saying nothing to anybody," and Tick's "big news coming out of the Fieldhouse… I'm not told what" arrives four lines later; the reader does the arithmetic. The tease pre-spends nothing (no noun). "He said everything quiet." is three words of villain. "Spirit is expensive, Coach." prices her without naming what he would spend — a threat with no noun is the right kind; ch 10's plant is intact. "Boyd went down the steps she had gone down." — the reader feels the shadow follow her. One craft repeat: "He said it quiet. He said everything quiet." is the repeat-and-extend shape (RECENT.md: at most one per chapter), and the accepted text already has one at 152–153 ("He knew the answer. / He knew it cold, depth-chart cold."). *Fix:* cut the first sentence (graft 4). **G. VARNISH / OBLIQUENESS.** No "What he would never have said" scaffold — the private admission at 416 is two plain sentences; honored. "Thank you Coach" → "side by side where the two of them had left them" is the chapter's best irony, and the reader screenshots the last four lines. "He put the phone in his coat" (439), not pocket — ties to "two coats" without saying so.
