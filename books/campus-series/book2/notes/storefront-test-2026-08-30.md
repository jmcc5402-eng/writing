# Storefront test — 1.2 blurb, pre-prose baseline (2026-08-30)

**Instrument: RETRO NEW 20** (first run since ratification).
Blind panel reads ONLY the back-cover copy — no repo, no
outline, no chapter text — and states what book it believes it
is buying. Expectation-mismatch against the ratified outline's
actual contract is the score.

**Scope note.** NEW 20 specifies blurb + retail sample; no 1.2
prose exists yet, so this is the PRE-PROSE BASELINE on copy
alone (it gates the copy and the flag-13 title pass). The full
blurb+sample storefront test re-runs once accepted ch 1–2 exist
— that second run is the one that clears D17.5-class back-cover
copy for market.

**Panel & card.** romance-reader-panel under **P1 (binge)** —
the 1.2 P-deck deal (variance LOG 2026-08-30); four persona
reads: (a) the target reader (35–45 extremely-online mother,
DIALS row), (b) the carryover casserole reader (1.1 persona),
(c) a cold genre shopper browsing the shelf, (d) the two-star
risk (the reader most likely to feel falsely advertised).

**Action thresholds, set BEFORE the run** (calibration law):

1. Axes scored: SUBGENRE (what shelf), HEAT (what door), TONE
   (comedy/weight mix), AUDIENCE (who it's for), PROMISE (what
   the plot contracts).
2. **Blocking finding** — copy revision required before wave 1
   briefs quote it: ≥2 of 4 personas mismatch the ratified
   contract on the SAME axis, or ANY persona mismatches on
   HEAT (the two-star band is a heat band; the honesty clause
   exists for exactly this).
3. **Noted finding** — logged, copy stands: a single-persona
   mismatch on any non-heat axis.
4. Buy/pass verdicts are reported but NOT scored (selection
   data, not a gate — panels are never continuity gates and
   this test measures expectation, not appeal).

**Packet.** The blurb verbatim from plots/premise.md §1
(including the honesty-clause register line), passed inline;
title shown as untitled. Key: this file. Results below the
rule after the run.

## RESULTS (run complete, same day; agent report on file in the
run transcript; scored strictly against the thresholds above)

**Four persona verdicts:** target reader BUY (starts tonight;
Grapevine phrases load-bearing — "every parent with a login,"
"the comment section always open," "a heroine older than the
hero — sold"); cold shopper BUY narrowly (the mutual-checkmate
lines are "an actual conflict engine, not vibes"; respects the
honest heat label); carryover reader SAMPLE leaning buy on town
loyalty (passes the would-she-stick gate); two-star risk BUYS —
which is the problem (see blocking 1).

**BLOCKING (threshold rule 2 — heat, any persona): the
"door-ajar" coinage fails the one reader it exists for.** The
clean-reader persona equates closed door with clean, skims the
qualifier, and cannot decode "door-ajar" (not established
shorthand — closed door / fade to black / open door are); she
buys anyway and writes the exact "false advertising" two-star
the clause was built to prevent. The carryover reader also
stalled on the word ("Is that... am I going to see something?").
**FIX APPLIED:** package shorthand is now "closed door for the
leads, one steamy scene for a side couple" — blunt beats cute.
The scene's internal standard name (DOOR AJAR) and all fences
are unchanged (premise §7, HEAT map).

**BLOCKING (threshold rule 1 — tone, 2 of 4 on the same axis):
the copy signals zero warmth.** Carryover reader: "since when is
this town cruel? Last time everybody brought casseroles" —
reads 40/60 comedy-to-weight where she wanted 70/30; the
two-star persona expects wholesome and gets a dogpile. The BOOK
delivers the chorus warmth (promise ledger §2: the Liars' Table
translating, the meal-train grain joke) — the copy just never
shows it; the panel's own fix: "give the diner a kind line, not
just a comment section." **FIX APPLIED:** one sentence added to
blurb ¶3 — "The diner splits the question down the middle and
feeds both sides anyway." Honest to the dial shift (the edge
stays signaled); it adds the town's floor, not a softer book.

**NOTED, copy stands (single-persona, non-heat):**
1. Opening sentence "Ashford loved its new coach before it ever
   unpacked him" — lowest yield on the page; two personas
   reread it (garden path for strangers). Logged as input to
   the flag-13 title/copy pass (market-pitch), NOT changed now
   — the post-prose storefront rerun re-tests it.
2. Cold shopper's half-expectation of "a medical-ethics novel
   where two people also kiss" — the copy's climax paragraph
   spends more words on the signature than the falling. Same
   disposition; the A4 defusing beats (premise §8) already own
   this risk inside the book.
3. Panel deferral, on the record: if the book ever has her sign
   under romantic or communal pressure, no reader pleasure
   survives it — matches the ick-line guarantee (premise §3),
   which is already canon.

**PROTECTED PHRASES (panel-certified; the copy pass may not
lose them):** "Falling for the one person whose judgment he
can't overrule isn't a complication. It's the thing that can
end them both — and it is already, gloriously, too late."
(cited by 3 of 4 buyers — the sentence doing the most work);
"every parent with a login"; "the comment section always open";
"who is not, under any circumstances, going to apologize for
being right"; "She can bench his star. He can't overrule her."

**Verdict: copy CLEARED for wave-1 briefs with the two applied
fixes.** The full blurb+sample storefront test re-runs once
accepted ch 1–2 exist.
