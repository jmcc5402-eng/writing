## B. THE APART TEST

**Does she feel him as a person, not a problem?** Yes, in two places.

- Lines 89–97. "Dan Merritt had said it to a yard, looking at the yard." She saw where his eyes went when he said the true thing. That is a person. Then the plate: "One rib. One bite gone. It was a stupid thing to keep, and she kept it." Reader: *okay, she's in it too.* This is her half of ch 8's two plates, and it lands.
- Lines 398–401. "She would like to see him try." That is a woman wanting a man to come for her, and it is warm. Best line in the chapter.

**Where it goes cold for too long:** twice.

- Lines 99–231 (132 lines). The hook, the wall, the letter, the towels. No him. The letter earns some of that: it should be hers alone.
- Lines 238–384 (146 lines). The room-nine line, the Checkerboard, Earlene, the drive up Millrow, the letter into the wool coat. No him at all. For the reader who bought this book for the two of them, this is the stretch where she checks how many pages are left in the chapter.

The plan put the romance at the two ends and Verna's one line in the middle. Pleasure-wise, that is 80 lines of him, then 280 lines of building, then 20 lines of him. Two of those middle scenes have a cheap fix that is already sitting in the text.

**Fix 1 (the best one, free):** Lines 301–303, Earlene: "By yourself." / "By myself." That is ch 7 lines 384–386 word for word, and it was HIM who said it there. The binge reader hears him in Earlene's mouth; Aisha doesn't. One sentence after 303 in which Aisha notices she has now given that answer twice in three days, and to whom the first time, turns the dead middle into a kind-4 beat without him on the page. Editor translation: the echo is already written; let the POV hear it.

**Fix 2 (optional):** Ch 7 line 398, his: "Let me ask around before you start carrying anything. ... Give me a few days." She is carrying boxes on Saturday (lines 29–31) and the page never remembers he asked her not to. One clause in the opening — she was told to wait, she did not — puts him in paragraph one as a man who made a promise, and puts her false belief (ask nobody) on the page with a name attached. Watch that it reads as her not waiting, not as him failing; the brief wants him as a person here.
