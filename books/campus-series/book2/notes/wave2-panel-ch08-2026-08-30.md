## 1. WARMTH FIRST

**CANDIDATE X.**
His failure makes him MORE compelling, and here's why: he audits himself in real time. "His mouth had bought rooms in five states, / and it had just bought this one" — the reader panel gasped, because the chapter names the sin as a *skill*, which is worse and better than a mistake. It doesn't excuse him; it makes him pay in the exact currency he spent: "He heard every kitchen in Doss County hear him say nothing." The mic moment is a full stand-up-and-cheer — "It grew up on a message board, and it can live there" got an actual out-loud "OH." and "On a podium, that word is marketing. / When it comes out of my mouth, it's medicine" is the screenshot. The want-naming is the most *earned* of the three: he runs the tape like a coach grading film ("he had climbed out the back / and called it staying in his lane"), and only then — "He wanted her." One panel note: the brief's "inconveniently timed" reads thematic here, not situational — he names it alone, after everyone's gone. It's quieter than Z's version but it hurts more: "hold the line for a boy / who would never even know it got held." **Binge answer: yes, hard.** The plate "staged where he could find it in the dark later, / like everything he meant to come back to" is planted on page one and detonates on the last line. The 10 p.m. reader did not fall asleep; the reviewer already has her pull quotes.

**CANDIDATE Z.**
His failure is the ugliest and it's a real failure — "the fish-fry chuckle, the one that handled things" and "I quit refereeing words back in two-a-days" is a man actively demoting her work to vocabulary on the air. The chapter does not excuse it, and the reckoning line is the best self-indictment in the packet: "he'd been handed one clean turn to hold it with her, / and he had handed back vocabulary." Compelling — but colder. The four-a-month reader admired him; the 10 p.m. reader liked him less, because Z gives him almost no warmth to lose (the porch here is creditors: "every one of them had come to collect"). Mic moment: strong, mic-drop register — "Safe off a flatbed is a pep rally. / Safe on a schedule is a sales pitch" — though "He gets that one" made two panelists stop to work out whether "he" is the boy or the coach. Want-naming: the most literally inconvenient — named mid-dead-air, on a hot mic, "at the one hour of his week / when he could do exactly nothing about it / except lean into a microphone and talk about tickets" — the panel laughed, in the good way. **Binge answer: yes, mechanically** — "By nine tomorrow the board would have her forty seconds / word for word" is the only candidate that loads tomorrow's gun on the page. But the middle (bracket talk, allotment, ticket-office hours) is where the tired reader skimmed.

**CANDIDATE Y.**
His failure is the most *damning by design*: Y is the only candidate where he knows the answer before he ducks — "He knew it cold, depth-chart cold. / *It's real, it's medicine, it's hers, and I'm glad.*" — so the shrug is a choice, not a reflex. That's brave and it works; and the cost lands in the packet's single best community-pressure line: "The porch eased, and the six feet of rail around the doctor / got emptier without one person moving." I skipped nothing in the first half — the pre-show plate scene ("There's a salad bar." / "That's what I was afraid of.") is the swooniest domestic beat in the wave and retroactively makes the two-plates button a *shared* artifact, since he made both plates. Mic moment: the loudest cheer line anywhere in this packet — "Roberta, this is Aisha Cole. I'm the hold." Three of four panelists would text that to a friend. Want-naming: earned, good, slightly softer than the other two. **Binge answer: yes, but warm-yes, not need-yes** — the back half (kicking game, propane, folding tables) coasts, and the ending is gentle where the wave's hinge wants a held breath.

## 3. FAULTS

- **X — near clean.** One panelist wanted Aisha on the page (not just heard) before the mic moment; "He kept his eyes on Tick / and let his hearing do what it wanted about it" mostly covers it, but she is a sound effect for 100 lines. *Editor translation:* her physical presence is deferred a beat long. *Fix:* one glimpse of her at the rail mid-hour, three lines, no dialogue. Porch reads as **community**, fully — the refrigerator-clipping caller and the overdue casserole dish are people, not a chorus.
- **Z — coasting middle.** "The allotment's the allotment" through the ticket-office hours: tired reader skimmed, said "wake me at the fourth caller." *Fix:* cut or compress calls two and three into one.
