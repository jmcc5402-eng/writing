# Book 1.2 — conflict in the first half, and Dan's weakness as an arc

## The author's two problems, stated plainly

1. **The first eight chapters are activity without ups and downs.**
   Things happen — a bonfire, an injury, a hospital, a whiteboard, a
   selection show, a coat, a radio show — but nobody loses anything
   on the page. Every chapter ends roughly where it started. The
   plot's real events are all in the second half.
2. **Dan reading Grapevine every night makes him seem strange**,
   because the book never says why he does it, and he never says he
   knows it is a problem.

## Part 1 — Dan's weakness, named and cured

**What it is, in one sentence:** Dan reads the parents' board every
night because a town's opinion got him fired once, and he is
checking every night to see whether it is happening again.

**The arc the author asked for:** he knows it is a weakness; Aisha
is the one who tells him what it is doing to him; by the end of the
book he gives it up, and that is how the reader sees him change.

The outline already has the last step (ch 29: he shows her the
phone with the app deleted). It is missing the first step and the
middle. Here they are:

- **Ch 2 (his POV, on main).** After "He read to the bottom every
  night," add the reason and the self-knowledge, plainly: *He knew
  what it was. Ten years ago a town had decided what he was, and
  the board had known it a week before anyone said it to his face.
  So he read the board every night to see it coming. He knew that
  was a weakness. He did it anyway.*
- **Ch 8 (the porch, after the show).** Aisha names it, once, to his
  face: *"You read that board like it's a chart. It isn't. It's the
  weather. You can't treat weather, Coach, and you can't win it
  either."* He does not answer. That is the first time anyone has
  said it to him.
- **Ch 12 (the leak).** He reads the county guessing about his life
  and, for the first time, notices he has read it three times. The
  weakness is now visible to him as a habit, not a duty.
- **Ch 20 (the firewall).** He drafts a reply to the board at 1 a.m.
  and deletes it character by character. (Already in the outline.)
- **Ch 22 (Coach's Table, round two).** He defends her on air, and
  that night he does not open the board. First night in ten years.
  One line.
- **Ch 27 (the hearing).** He walks in without having read what the
  county thinks of him that morning. The book says so.
- **Ch 29.** He shows her the phone. The app is gone. (Outline.)

**What Aisha gives him:** not comfort — a diagnosis. She is a doctor;
she names things. Her line in ch 8 is the medicine, and his change is
that he takes it.

## Part 2 — one loss per chapter, ch 5–8

The rule this proposes for the fold and for every brief from wave 3
on: **every chapter names its reversal — who loses what, on the page,
and what it costs them.** A chapter without a loser is a chapter
without a plot.

The current shape of 5–8, in one line each, and the loss to add:

**Ch 5 — Two Calendars.** *Now:* Aisha puts the protocol on a
whiteboard, negotiates twenty minutes of meeting room with Dan, and
names her want. Nobody loses. *Add:* Dan does not accept the twenty
minutes. He goes over her head to the athletic director the same
afternoon and asks for the boy in the film room by Thursday. The AD
backs her, in an email Dan has to read with her copied on it. Dan
has lost in public for the first time this season, and she has won
in a way that costs her: the coach is now angry, and the AD's email
will be a screenshot on the board by supper. *Sentence a stranger
could repeat:* Dan tried to get around the doctor and got told no by
his own boss.

**Ch 6 — Selection Sunday.** *Now:* the town gets its playoff;
Denny asks Dan a vague favor; Dan says rosters get built in the
building. Nobody loses. *Add:* Denny's favor is specific and has a
threat in it: eleven dads have a letter drafted asking the program
to keep the scout-team kid and "review medical decision-making," and
Denny wants Dan's name on it before it goes to the AD. Dan says no,
on the porch, in front of the corner. Denny's face closes. The
letter goes without Dan's name, and that is the first move of the
campaign — in Q1, where the reader can watch it start, instead of
in ch 18. *Sentence:* Denny asked the coach to sign a letter against
the doctor, and the coach refused him in public.

**Ch 7 — The Coat.** *Now:* Dan gives her his coat and lies about
it; the vacate order arrives the same day. The vacate order is the
loss, and the chapter does not treat it as one. *Add:* the order has
a date on it, thirty days, and the date is HER deadline now, not the
quarterback's. She has to move thirty years of records before
Christmas with no help, and Dan hears about the order in the
Fieldhouse the same afternoon and does nothing — because the
building going down is Boyd's money, and Boyd's money is his
program. The reader watches him choose not to help. *Sentence:* The
university gave the doctor thirty days to get out of her building,
and the coach, who knew, said nothing.

**Ch 8 — Coach's Table.** *Now:* Aisha takes the mic and says the
protocol is medicine; Dan names his want; two plates on the rail.
She wins. *Add the cost:* the board turns on her that night for the
first time — not "asking for clarity" but *who does she think she
is* — and Dan reads it, all of it, to the bottom, and does not post
one word in her defense. Boyd is on the porch during the broadcast,
listening, and says one thing to Dan on the way out: that he liked
the doctor's spirit, and that spirit is expensive. *Sentence:* The
doctor stood up for herself on the radio, the town punished her for
it online that night, and the coach watched and stayed quiet.

That gives the first quarter four losses: Dan loses to the AD (5),
Denny loses Dan (6), Aisha loses her building (7), and Aisha loses
the town while Dan loses his nerve (8). The second half's conflict
is no longer being loaded; it has started.

## Part 3 — what this costs

- Ch 5–8 are staged on #107. These are fold-level re-cuts, not
  line edits: each adds a scene of 30–60 lines and changes the
  chapter's ending register. They should be drafted by the
  drafting-assistant from a brief written under the say-it test,
  then panel-read, not patched by the orchestrator.
- Ch 2's three lines are on main and are a line edit.
- The wave-3 directive gets one new mandatory slot per chapter:
  REVERSAL — who loses what, in a sentence.
- The outline's Q1 summary ("The one word she won't lend") stays
  true; it just stops being the only thing that happens.
