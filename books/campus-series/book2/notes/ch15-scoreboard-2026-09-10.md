**A wins.** Why, in one line: A is the one where the reader watches
her choose him twice before the kiss — the window going dark and the
count to a hundred and thirty, then "I'd hand it back if I knew how.
I don't. So I'm keeping it." — and Dan has a face at every turn.

The longer reason. All three kiss well; the chapter is decided
before the kiss. A opens the annex on the beat none of the others
found: his lit window across the black lot, the window going dark,
her counting to a hundred and thirty before the door. The reader
wants them together from that line and does not stop. A's arc turn
(she keeps what he spent, and says so) is the dossier's row 15 said
out loud in her voice, which is the explicit the author asks for.
A's partner disclosure has the cleanest prompt (her parking tag that
says BACK ROOM). A's Dan has a face at every turn and none of them
is a pointer. And A's after-terms talk — "You'd have lost." / "I'd
have liked losing it." and the window he will stand in every night
till February — is the hook that carries the reader into the loss.

B's "hand on my chest" line is the best single line of the three
and its door beat is the most direct; but B twice puts them "in the
truck" in ch 14, which the author, who just read 14, would catch on
the first pass, and it recites the trainer back to Dan. C's sound
conceit gives the most distinctive kiss and the funniest terms, and
"I wasn't going to start winning in a room you weren't in" is a
keeper; but before the kiss C's Dan is heard more than seen, with
four bare lines, and that is the author's twice-made note.

No grafting. A's drafter applies A's panel fixes. B and C are filed
at `notes/candidates/` with their reports; their best lines are
listed in the fold for the registry so no later chapter reinvents
them by accident: B's "It's a Fieldhouse rule. This isn't the
Fieldhouse." and "You wrote a rule at me with your hand on my
chest"; C's "wrong side of the lot," "start winning in a room you
weren't in," "a sound a man makes and would swear he had not," and
"one about coolers."
