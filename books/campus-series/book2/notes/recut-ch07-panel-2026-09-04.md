# Panel read — Book 1.2, ch 7 "The Coat" (re-cut, first draft)

**romance-reader-panel, card P1 (binge — the 1.2 book card, dealt at
kickoff; no draw). 2026-09-04.** Read straight off ch 6's porch, four
chapters into a sitting, judged as prose. Filed by the orchestrator;
the agent has no write tool.

**VERDICT: SHIPS WITH GRAFTS.** Six grafts, all inside new material,
none touching protected accepted prose, none changing plot. **All six
applied 2026-09-04.**

## THE THREE FIRST FINDINGS

### 1. Cover the page and say what happened — PASSES

The panel returned the whole chapter cold, from memory, in one
paragraph. **"What she lost: the one room in this county she actually
unpacked, and any certainty her clinic exists past February. What he
did about it: nothing. He offered to ask around."**

After the author's standing complaint this deserves saying plainly:
**the chapter is followable by ear.**

One qualification, which became finding 3: the panel could say what
she lost. It was less sure the chapter ever says, in a sentence a
stranger could repeat, **that they are taking the building.**

### 2. The COUPLE LINE — present, plays rung 4, climbs by the rule and plateaus in the body

Rung 4 by the letter of `STYLE.md:222-223` — object hand to hand,
hands near, no skin, and it measures a distance rather than a body,
which is what the audit bought. **"She stayed on her side" converts a
distance into a decision** and is the beat's best three words.

Three problems, two of them fixed by graft 1:

- **The box was not legible.** Frames leaned at floor level, "over the
  top of the box," then "four inches of cardboard" — four inches is a
  box's thickness, not its height. By ear: *box… four inches… of
  what?* The exact measurement the beat exists to deliver was the part
  that fogged.
- **"Four inches of cardboard, and the cold of the room in it" is a
  noun stack where a sentence should be** — the say-it failure shape
  at `STYLE.md:332-334`, sitting in the one beat the brief made
  load-bearing.
- **It climbs on the ladder, not in the body.** What lands is
  cardboard and the cold of the room. Nothing about *him* reaches her.
  Ch 6's rung 3 is a real sensory event ("close enough that he got her
  hair, cold and clean"). **The lower rung is warmer than the higher
  one.**

**And the chapter's real charge is not in the designated beat.** It is
at `:353` — "He looked up from the page when she said it, and back
down" — and in the silence. *"The couple line is at `:340`; the couple
feeling is at `:353` and `:403`."*

### 3. Does a reader outside college athletics know what she is losing?

**The one plain sentence works** and is not too much — it rescues the
KNEES, 1994 carton, which without it is just a funny box.

**What is still missing is not what the rooms are for. It is what is
happening to them.** "Survey" is never glossed and on the ear files as
paperwork. *"Okay, so somebody's coming to look at the building. Why
is she doing long division about it?"*

**The chapter answers it 130 lines later, in Dan's mouth** — "Does it
move you off this campus?" / "It doesn't say. It says subsequent
communication." / "That's not an answer." **That is the best decode in
the chapter and it belongs to the new scene.** The decode duty is met
late but it is met — and the plainness is carried by Dan, not by the
memo, so if the scene ever moves the decode goes with it.

## THE PARKA — the secret HELD

**The panel did not deduce that the coat is Dan's own spare.** Its
suspect list at chapter's end was the equipment manager, the head
trainer, or the department. **The stitched name is the concealment and
it works** — a name sewn on reads as *ordered for her*, which points
away from a personal gift. The mitigation the audit demanded worked:
the memo is in his hands within four lines and the panel never
wondered why the head coach didn't remark on a coat everyone else
remarked on.

**Four risks it flagged anyway, in order of danger — and the first two
live entirely in accepted prose, not in the re-cut:**

1. **The logic chain is already closed.** `:41-42` says the order
   sheet has one column and always has; `:144` says a second column
   was written this morning and lands in the spring. **Therefore the
   parka cannot have been ordered.** It is somebody's existing coat
   with her name put on it — and the tag says XXL and the hem lands
   below her knees. That is a specific very large man's coat, and the
   book has one very large man. The panel did not take the last step.
   A re-reader takes it instantly.
2. **Binge-specific, and the card doing its job:** in ch 2 Dan is
   alone in the equipment cage at night with the equipment manager,
   described in near-identical language (`ch02:296` "detergent and new
   leather" / `ch07:109` "dryer heat and new leather"), the same man
   doing the same action (socks into dozens), and the scene ends with
   Dan *"straighten[ing] a sleeve that did not need straightening."*
   **A spaced reader is safe; a binge reader is meaningfully less
   safe.**
3. `:302-303` — he arrives off a nineteen-degree field in a *jacket*
   while his staff have parkas. Too faint to be a tell on one pass;
   a gorgeous ch 29 payoff if deliberate. Somebody should decide which.
4. `:99-100` — the noticing beat is now two hundred lines closer to a
   man. It does not fire; nothing in the new scene invites the
   comparison. But the distance shortened.

## THE ANGER DEFICIT — the brief's target is missed

**"I am angry at the university. I am mildly disappointed in Dan."**
Four reasons, and they are good ones:

1. **He does more than ask around.** "A building doesn't get on a
   phase-two list by itself… I know the people who'd know how it
   moved." That is a man spotting a conspiracy in ten seconds and
   volunteering to investigate it. On the ear he sounds **sharp and
   protective**, not evasive.
2. **"Let me ask around before you start carrying anything" is a
   *care* sentence** — he doesn't want her hauling cabinets alone.
3. **The first half of the scene is him being good.** Forty lines of
   credit banked before the memo appears.
4. **The reader has no scale for what he is declining.** The chapter
   never shows what *spending* would look like. Contrast ch 6, where
   the reader watched him refuse in front of the corner and heard the
   porch go quiet. There, a scale. Here, none — so "he declined to
   spend anything" registers as a normal offer.

**Which means the anger is carried entirely by narration asserting
it.** The chapter's designated emotional outcome is stated rather than
produced.

**The one thing that nearly lands it, which nobody flagged:** `:407`,
*"Five fifteen tomorrow," he said. "It'll be printed."* He ends on the
small thing he *can* deliver, ninety seconds after a promise he can't.
**Unkeepable, then keepable and trivial** — that pairing says his whole
character with no narration at all. **Protect `:407`. It is doing the
brief's job better than the brief's own instrument.**

Structural; not grafted. **Goes to the author.**

## SWOON INVENTORY — four beats, named so nobody touches them

1. **`:297-299`** — *"He knocked twice on the frame. / The last time,
   he had saved the knock for the way out."* **The best romance beat
   in the chapter, and nobody flagged it.** In ch 5 he skipped the
   knock coming in and knocked on the way out. Here he knocks going
   in. **He changed a habit for her, she noticed, and neither of them
   says a word about it.** Protect absolutely.
2. **`:353`** — "He looked up from the page when she said it, and back
   down." Four words, more charge than the box.
3. **`:344`** — "She stayed on her side."
4. **`:403-405`** — the silence.

**Load-bearing: 1 and 4.** Cut either and the scene is two colleagues
doing paperwork. **The box itself is the least load-bearing of the
five and was absorbing all the design attention.**

## DOES HE SOUND LIKE HIMSELF?

**She does, whole.** He mostly does, and where he is best he is the
best he has been — **"I'm holding the meeting room at seven from here
on. / Projector unplugged, not off."** is called the best Dan line in
the book so far: the ch 5 callback escalated into a promise he can't
wriggle out of, characterization and payoff in six words.

**Where he wobbles, and this is the craft finding for a TTS-first
author:** twelve lines of alternating dialogue with one tag. In
ElevenReader that is one narrator voice ping-ponging with no anchor
for eleven exchanges, and the panel lost the thread once. **It does not
need tags; it needs beats of business inside the run.** Grafts 3 and 6.

## HEAT

**Restraint-charge, not body-charge**, and everything physical in the
scene is cold — the cold end of the corridor, the cold off his jacket,
the cold of the room in the beat. Meanwhile the chapter's whole warmth
budget is in the coat, the one object he may not touch or mention.
**The man walks into the warmest chapter in the book and lowers the
temperature by three degrees.** Structurally necessary; still true.

**The one safe channel for a notch of steam is his voice** — she never
registers it once, and voice touches neither the coat nor his size.
Graft 6.

**Two fixes a reviser would reach for that would be wrong:** do not
put her hands on her own cuffs in this scene (it dilutes the protected
ending); do not write his hand, reach, shoulders or size anywhere in
it (that is the XXL tell the audit blocked v1 for).

## RULE-1 DEFERRALS — ruled by the orchestrator

- **"Projector unplugged, not off"** and **"a man deciding what to do
  about a thing, / not what he thought of it"** were flagged as
  cousins of the banned negation pair. **RULED: they are not.** The
  ban (audit F28) targets *"did X and did not Y"* — a sentence saying
  someone did not do something. These are contrastive specifications,
  a different move. Both stay. The count is recorded here so the next
  audit sees it was ruled and not missed.
- **"The quiet went past comfortable"** — the meaning-namer
  construction. **Resolved by graft 5**, which makes the silence long
  instead of saying it was.
- **Three plain statements about the same loss in 120 lines** — the
  panel puts the chapter at the edge of plainness creep, not over.
  Agreed. `:355-357` is the one to sacrifice if the author wants one
  fewer; it is the only line in the new scene that repeats.

## THE GRAFTS — all six applied

1. **The couple line's geometry and running order.** Pacing before the
   handover so a listener hears it in order; frames "on their edge"
   makes four inches a legible distance; the noun stack becomes a
   sentence — "Four inches of cardboard stood between them and nothing
   else did. / She stayed on her side of it."
2. **The two narration blocks separated.** She reads management off
   him *before* he offers, which primes the offer instead of
   labelling it afterward; the scene no longer stops twice in eight
   lines to explain itself.
3. **One anchor inside the untagged run** — "He read the last two
   sentences over." Gives a TTS listener a reset, and earns "That's
   not an answer" by showing him check first.
4. **The rate math** — one number per clause instead of six in three
   lines; the rate stands as its own sentence.
5. **The silence, physical instead of labelled** — "He turned the cap
   over once in his hands, and then again. / Neither of them filled
   it."
6. **The steam notch, in the one safe channel** — "He said the next
   thing lower than the rest of it." The only sensory fact about him
   she registers all scene; touches neither the coat nor his size.
