### A. The February amendment

**STANDS. February is load-bearing — but not for the reasons the brief gives,
and its citation list must be replaced.**

All four cited lines check out exactly: `b12-outline.md:417-418` ("boxed by
**February**"), `ch07:226` and `ch07:246`, `outline:58` (the lease),
`outline:791` (ch 28), `outline:740` (Verna's rent). But three of the four are
not dependencies of the *deadline* — they are facts about the month existing in
the last act. Moving the annex deadline to December would not touch the lease,
signing day, or the rent by one word. The brief makes the weakest true argument
available, and an author could knock it over in a sentence without the amendment
being wrong.

The dependencies that actually break are worse than the brief claims:

- `outline:447-450` — ch 9 (wk3 Sat) is where she *begins* packing, four days
  after the memo. A December deadline gives her ten days for four rooms and four
  cabinets.
- `outline:535-537` — ch 14 (wk5) has Dan packing records beside her for two

- `outline:623-635` — ch 19 (Jan 2) has Boyd admiring her packing and Dan
  arriving with boxes. A "before Christmas" deadline makes the scene impossible.

  afternoon." Still not packed.

have emptied. The superseded instruction was also internally impossible:
`recut-briefs-ch05-08.md:326-328` asks for "thirty days ... and the date lands
before Christmas," but the memo arrives in the second week of December and

**Is "a specific day and she does the arithmetic" enough to deliver a deadline
that is hers, with a number on it? No — not as framed.** A date in a memo is the
institution's number; a countdown off it is the county's register, and the
county already owns a countdown (DEC 19 in red, `ch07:101`). Two countdowns
flatten the chapter's own best structural line, "The whiteboard held its two
calendars. / The mail had brought a third" (`ch07:262-264`).

What makes it hers is converting the institution's date into her working units
and spending against it. The accepted page already half-does this: room nine

baseline for a patient nobody had asked her to take" (`:280-283`). That is a
doctor taking a building's history. Missing: one flat **rate** line in her
register, at `:262-264` where the chapter already names the third calendar — not
"fifty-nine days," but workload per week with the university's Christmas closure
subtracted, said once, uncommented.

**The day: I license a bounded `[TK]`, plus a strikeable default — because the
book cannot currently tell you what weekday any February date is.** See F7.
December 19 and February 6 are forty-nine days apart, exactly seven weeks, so
they share a weekday; `outline:572` ("Wk5 **Sat**") and `outline:731-732`
("**Friday** the 6th") cannot both stand.

- Licensed marker: `[TK: the day — a date in the first half of February; no

- Strikeable default if a number is wanted now: **the eleventh.** From the

- In every case the memo names a date and **never a weekday**.

`ch07:352` is safe under all of this; the month survives in the memo either way.

### C. The proximity beat

**Yes, he can be in that room — but not doing what the brief and the standing

  was cut for …)" (`CHANGELOG.md:222-225`, under B2-D04 / `STATE.md:87`). The
  prescribed beat is a second noticing beat, and it is the *same* one.
