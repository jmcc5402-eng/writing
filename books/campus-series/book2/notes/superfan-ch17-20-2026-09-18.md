# SUPERFAN — THE STRETCH, ch 17–20 (2026-09-18; the cadence read, every four accepted chapters; card C10 "suspend attention to plot; report only what it felt like")

Read as her, Sunday afternoon, four chapters in a row. Her numbers were written before she opened `notes/romance-levels.md`; they are at the bottom.

**Ch 17 — The Gray Season.** Slow open: an empty parking lot, "It was the twenty-first," the February date, the review. I skimmed to the kid with the taped fingers, and that's where she leaned in: "Till January it's his." The diner with Cal and Marisol is pleasant and long; Christmas alone on the end of the bed is the first time this book made me sad and then glad in the same page, and it earned it. Then room nine. "I've never been that glad to see a window in my life." "It's a motel room, Dan. It's heard worse." The ice machine. "I want you, Aisha." Her palm on the back of his neck, the quarter-zip on the floor, "she let it lie there all night." She did not put this down. Steam against the packaging: exactly what the cover promised, closed at the first garment, and it still feels like a night. Star 5. Level 9.

**Ch 18 — The Week the Town Was Gone.** She takes his keys out of his pocket: "So you'll know where they are at five." The 5 a.m. hand up on the square. "You look like a man reading. Your mouth goes." The barber: "like a yard nobody waters." The pillow talk told from the barber chair, "Then we're two people in a motel," is the best-placed thing in four chapters. New Year's: "I cleaned like you were," the jelly jar, "Wine, because we're in a house, not a room," the hand on the cushion, "There it is." Skims: the staff-room truck-door joke (I had to read it twice to get who rode in whose truck), and the Friday-night replay of the whole week in quotes; she just read that week, she doesn't need the highlight reel. Star 5. Level 9.

**Ch 19 — The Portal.** Comedown, and the reader feels it in the first line: the stool, the date, the window explained. The kid's records are a kind thing done for a boy who doesn't say thank you; that's the Band-Aid at the bonfire again and it works. Boyd in the doorway is genuinely creepy. Then the pet peeve fires: he offers her a job out west, Dan walks in with boxes, asks "What did he say to you," and she says "He liked my list." One honest sentence. The book's rule is about her medical calls; a job offer isn't a call, and she knows that, and so does the reader. The tape gun turned handle-first is the kind of small thing my group screenshots. "Where do you want me." "I'd have walked them over if he'd been standing on the hood." Peanut and the can is a good bar. End: lamp on, curtain shut, alone. Star 4. Level 6.

**Ch 20 — Verbal Commitments.** The father's call, and Dan naming two other programs, is the best rooting-for beat in the stretch. But then Boyd is in a doorway again, hands in pockets, smiling, saying nothing, which is the same scene as Monday with the furniture swapped. The dinner's glad half is real: chin on her hand, "Your spoon's been in the air since Wednesday," his hand over hers. Then "Boyd came by," and now they're BOTH sitting on a Boyd visit across one table, and she gets to say nothing too. Then he asks about the bike, she shuts it, and the silence runs "the length of a play clock." Then she leaves at ten with a hand on his wrist. After a week in his bed, a Friday alone in his house with nobody watching, and no kiss, not even at the door. The deleted post letter by letter ("Dan Merrit. Dan Merri.") will split the room: half will cry, half will say "we get it." Star 3. Level 5.

**The stretch.** Week, then town home, then boxes, then dinner: the shape works because 19 says the ache out loud (a lamp on for nobody). A binge reader forgives 19. Twenty is where she checks how many pages are left, because the good half is good and the rest is a rule being kept in a kitchen. The one thing she'll tell her group: "They spent a week walking to a motel in the dark so nobody would count the truck, and she took his keys out of his pocket." The one gripe: two people who just told each other "I want you" are now keeping two separate secrets about the same man, and the book calls it the terms.

**Repetition she noticed.** Every chapter opens on the calendar and the stool (the twenty-first; the fourth; the eighth, six days left). "The first Wednesday in February" is in all four, several times each. Three of four chapters end with one of them alone at night under a lamp, thinking about the other. Boyd's silent doorway: twice in two chapters. "In plain words, because that was how she thought": three times. "Timed and short and finished": three times. Verna's light on till eleven and never a word: four times. Each is good once; by the fourth she's counting instead of feeling.

**THE LONG REVIEW (4 stars, "ReadsAtRedLights").** "I did not expect to stay up for a closed-door book and I stayed up. Chapter 17 is the one. He walks down a hill in the cold because her lamp is on, and she has the door open before he knocks. No sex on the page and I did not miss it, which I have never written in a review. The week after (18) is the happiest I've been in this series: she takes his keys, she sits in his film room and tells him his mouth moves when he reads, he drinks wine out of a jelly jar. Then the town comes home and it gets quiet. The boxes chapter is fine, and Boyd is a good villain, but she gets a JOB OFFER and tells him 'he liked my list.' Chapter 20 has a dinner I loved for a page and then a silence I sat through with them, and she goes home at ten. I get it. I still wanted the kiss at the door. Four stars because 17 and 18 are five and 20 is three."
