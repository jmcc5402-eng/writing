l.409–410: "Dan read the parents' board every night, every line of it." It still seems weird that this football coach reads a message board; we need the little bit of the why every time it's shown — a board knew first the last time, and he's scanning for football in normal talk. "Like it was a chart" is her joke, not his reason. [WHO]

l.415–416: "The county had written a line under him once, ten years ago, and that one had been true too." That's the hint, but it doesn't say what it costs him now. l.445–451: the last five lines. Her body is strong at 418–427, then it's twenty lines of the county and the bill, and we end on "her phone, on the blanket," "his phone too," and buildings. She looks at his lean one last time and we get an inventory of the picture. Where is she in the last five lines — her heart, her hands on the glass, wanting him? It ends on a thing. [MORE]

l.450–451: "It was the same picture on two phones in two buildings, and the county was in both of them." Now we're being too on the nose — the writing explaining its own picture. Better to have it open on her phone and on his and nothing be said about the county. It's a romance novel; it doesn't need the connected thing at the end. [NOSE]
