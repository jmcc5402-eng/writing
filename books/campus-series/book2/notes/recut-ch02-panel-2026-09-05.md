## B. THE BUILD QUESTION

**Yes.** "He wanted her" in ch 8 is now earned.

Why, in order:
- Ch 2, 218–231: he keeps arguing after the sheet says yes. "I read the sheet." / He kept reading it anyway. This is the first time the reader watches him lose and watches him not want to stop.
- Ch 2, 227 and 236: she catches him. "Coach. That was a yes." / "You're arguing with a yes." This is the exact thing ch 8:314 pays: "Being caught by her had never felt much like being caught. / It felt like the good part of a Friday." A reader now knows what the good part of a Friday is. She was there.
- Ch 2, 269–274: he names it to himself, fight-only: "He liked this part." Stage 1 holds; the woman is not named.
- Ch 4, 283–305: "Neither of them got up." / "In a minute," he said, / and the minute ran long, / and neither of them called it. That is the second enjoyed scene, and it moves from enjoying the fight to enjoying the person.
- Ch 4, 311–313: "Six months of her calls, / and that was the first one all season / he let stand without an argument." After ch 2 this is a step the reader can feel, because she watched him argue with a yes six chapters earlier. Before the rewrite it was a claim.
- Ch 8, 328: "That is the first thing you have agreed with me on since June." Now a three-rung ladder: argues with a yes (2) → lets one stand (4) → agrees (8).
- Ch 5, 324: "the argument had been the best part of her fall." Ch 2, 271: "the one half hour in his week." The two leads now say the same thing about the same half hour, one in ch 2 and one in ch 5, and neither knows the other said it. That is the Stage 1 pleasure the book was missing.

What the reader knows about "since June" now that she did not before: it is not a grudge and not a running joke told in one line. It is a standing appointment he tried to move once and was told no (151–154). He pushes on the merits and loses on the merits (179–202). He pushes when he has already won (213–231). She counts his arguments (238). She has a tell he has memorized (233–234). A trainer and a kid watch the head coach lose to the doctor the week of the biggest game in years (244–247). He typed the half hour into his own schedule (295–297). Before this rewrite "since June" was a phrase. Now it is a room she has stood in.
