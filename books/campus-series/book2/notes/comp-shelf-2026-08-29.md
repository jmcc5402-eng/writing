## 1. The comp shelf (who owns "small town but modern" now)

**1. Lucy Score — *Story of My Life* (Story Lake #1, Mar 2025).**
The lane leader's current series: heroine impulse-buys a house
that comes with a *town council seat*; gossip "spreads faster
than wildfire"; pitched as "Gilmore Girls meets Schitt's Creek."
Comps 1.2 on town-governance-as-plot and gossip machinery.
Readers praise the quirky town chorus; complaints: "cringe"
humor, an immature hero, an abrupt romance, and 528-page bloat
([Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/en/book/show/216233719-story-of-my-life),

**2. Elsie Silver — Rose Hill series (*Wild Love* → *Wild Side*,

**3. Sarah Adams — Rome, Kentucky / When in Rome series

**4. Annabel Monaghan — *Summer Romance* (2024), *It's a Love

**6. Abby Jimenez — *Say You'll Remember Me* (Apr 2025, #1 NYT).**
The cautionary comp: heavy adult weight (dementia caretaking,
trauma) drew mixed reviews — "less rom-com and more women's

**7. Liz Tomforde — *Play Along* (Windy City #4, 2024).** Heroine

**8. Stephanie Archer — *The Fake Out* (Vancouver Storm #2).**

Second data point that medical-staff-over-athlete is a live,

**9. Tarah DeWitt — Spunes, OR series (*Savor It* 2024, *Left of

**10. B.K. Borison — Lovelight series.** The crowded cozy floor
of the lane ("sweet and steamy," Inglewild)
([review](https://www.thatartsyreadergirl.com/2025/05/mixed-signals-by-b-k-borison-book-review/)).

**Shelf read:** romance print is up sharply (Circana reports
~51M units/12mo, +24% YTD —
[Circana](https://www.circana.com/post/another-year-of-romance-with-a-dark-twist-circana-bookscan-reports)),

and cozy-sweet (Adams/Borison/DeWitt). The modern-texture,
adult-clock middle is thinner than the shelf looks.

## 2. Heat positioning: closed door, frank want

Where it sits in 2025–26: **a real seam, not a crowd.** The
open-door pole dominates BookTok discovery; the closed-door pole
exists but splits into (a) sweet rom-com (Sarah Adams — younger,
gentler want) and (b) women's-fiction-romance (Monaghan, Center —
right age, right door, but quiet engines and no genre-romance
propulsion). Titles at exactly 1.2's heat: *Summer Romance*,
*It's a Love Story*, the Rome KY books, Center's recent three.
Nobody at that heat is running a forbidden-authority engine with
online-town texture. Risks of the seam: closed door gets less
BookTok lift (trend coverage skews darker/spicier —
[Author Ever After](https://www.authoreverafter.com/romance-market-trends-reader-expectations/)),
so the blurb must state the register plainly — this lane's
readers actively filter by door, and midlife-romance demand
pieces confirm the buyer exists
([Viva/vocal on midlife romance](https://vocal.media/viva/moms-mates-and-menopause-midlife-romance-is-the-newest-hottest-romance-trend),
[Goodreads over-40 list, 361 titles](https://www.goodreads.com/list/show/21311.Best_older_hero_AND_older_heroine_romance_books_the_main_couple_has_to_be_over_40_)).
"Frank want on the page, door closed" should be a stated feature,
not an apology.

## 3. The forbidden / authority angle

Post-2020s sensibility is genuinely wary of supervisor-downward
romance — the consent-under-power problem is now mainstream HR
discourse, not just reader discourse
([Forbes 2025](https://www.forbes.com/sites/kimelsesser/2025/02/12/heres-how-to-keep-your-workplace-romance-professional/),
[Harper Grey](https://www.harpergrey.com/workplace-law/workplace-romance-the-impact-of-power-imbalance-on-ability-to-consent/)).
What still lands, per the sports-romance comps:

- **The inversion is the asset.** Aisha holds the overrule power,
  and it is *domain-specific and safety-grounded* — she can bench
  his player, not fire him; he owns the locker room, not her
  license. Neither reports to the other. That's peer-conflict
  wearing authority's clothes — SC4-clean, and it dodges the
  boss/subordinate consent trap entirely.
- **Career jeopardy is the proven engine** (*Play Along*): what
  readers accept as the obstacle is "this costs her the job /
  him the season," not "this is naughty."
- **Ick triggers to avoid:** her medical call ever bending an
  inch for the relationship (readers will torch a heroine whose
  professional judgment softens for a man); him campaigning
  against her calls to the board; resolution via her resigning
  her authority. The genre-safe arc is his *conversion* — he
  defends her call publicly at cost to himself before anything
  is admitted privately. Competence-as-courtship, 1.1's own
  register, does the heavy lifting.
- **The board second-guessing a female physician online in real
  time** is extremely current texture — but per SC1's second
  test, Grapevine can't be the only adjudicator; the diner, the
  paper, the pub must carry rebuttal weight on the page.
- [CHECK] I found no clean 2024–26 comp doing coach × team
  physician at any heat — treat "first in category" claims
  cautiously, but the shape appears open.

## 4. Package signals (for the storefront test)

- **Covers:** illustrated still dominates contemporary romance
  but is maturing — less flat cartoon, more typography-forward
  design (oversized serif titles, text integrated with
  illustration); photographic is reclaiming darker/western
  subgenres only
  ([NovelPad 2025](https://novelpad.co/blog/bcf2b554-f08a-4806-b40f-0b56a888d582),
  [AIBookArt 2026 survey](https://www.aibookart.com/blog/book-cover-trends-2026),
  [Covers in Color](https://coversincolor.com/blogs/the-studio/the-2026-book-cover-aesthetic-trends-insights),
  [Book Riot on photo-cover pushback](https://bookriot.com/romance-books-with-photographic-covers/)).
  Target: illustrated/typographic, *adult* rendering (Monaghan/
  Center shelf cues), stadium-plus-town iconography, not
  chibi-cute couple clinch.
- **Titles:** the lane runs on idiom/phrase titles — *Story of
  My Life*, *When in Rome*, *Practice Makes Perfect*, *It's a
  Love Story*, Silver's two-word *Wild X*. A sports/medical
  idiom with a double meaning is on-pattern (return-to-play
  language is unmined). [TK — title candidates belong to the
  premise PR, not this note.]
- **Blurb language:** trope-forward and register-explicit —
  "grumpy sunshine," "closed door," "small town with big
  opinions." State the door. State the ages (over-40 leads are
  a *selling* line to this buyer, per §2 sources).

## 5. Five one-line positioning options

1. *Ted Lasso* meets Annabel Monaghan: the team physician whose
   word outranks the coach's — in a town with the comment
   section always open.
2. She can bench his star. He can't overrule her. And neither of
   them can do a thing about the other.
3. *Friday Night Lights* for the group-chat generation — closed
   door, open tabs.
4. Lucy Score's town chaos at Annabel Monaghan's heat: one
   return-to-play call, and every parent on Grapevine thinks
   they get a vote.
5. The only call Dr. Aisha Cole can't make by the book is the
   coach.
