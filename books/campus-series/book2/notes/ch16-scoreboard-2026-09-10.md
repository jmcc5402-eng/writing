**A wins.** Why, in one line: the not-looking is felt hardest and the
one look from forty yards is the chapter's line; the gap lands on
her refusing to ask; the town is in the stands; and Dan has the
fewest slips to fix.

The longer reason. The chapter's job is the town's night with the
two of them forty yards apart, and A is the one where both halves
are on the page at full strength: the county's hands on the
chain-link and its breath over the field, and inside it a man whose
neck does not turn. A's one look — "She was standing… and not on
Dan, / and that was the terms, held, forty yards apart" — is the
beat the reader rereads. A's annex has the tape held up "so he
could see the nothing," her "I have never asked you. I'm not asking
now" as the prompt, and the gap answered in one sentence with a nod
"she gave a right number." A's cage line — the name "where a winning
coach would have written a score" — is the button. And A's fixes
are the lightest: no continuity hole, no ch 15 template reused
whole.

B's coolers are the best single invention of the three and "I
stopped at the coolers" / "nothing in them was sorry about the
coolers" is a keeper; but B reuses two ch 15 lines as templates, its
loss ball goes back into Monday's rotation with the name on it, and
her last line sends him to the ball, which spends "nobody
watching." C's prompt — "Where does a man learn to walk somebody in
and not talk" — is the best question in the three, and Tick's song
into a coat pocket is the walk's best line; but C's game has too
little of the town in it, its frame is assembled across eighteen
lines, and its fixes include a paragraph the chapter does not have.

No grafting. A's drafter applies A's panel fixes. B and C are filed
at `notes/candidates/` with their reports; their best lines are
banked for the registry so no later chapter reinvents them: B's
coolers as the forty yards he will not walk, "the bowl gave him a
sound it had been saving all fall for somebody else," "sound and
grabby and had a kicker," "like two people who'd bought tickets,"
"I'd have come across that lot tonight if we'd won it by thirty";
C's "so I'd have something to turn off," "Where does a man learn to
walk somebody in and not talk," "the not asking was the thing she
gave him for it," "Tick played a song into her pocket," the name
turned to the wall.
