## 1. WARMTH FIRST

**CANDIDATE X.** The coat beat is warm and almost a swoon. Discovery is good restraint — "She hung it on the door and did the morning charting first" (l. 157) reads exactly like a woman refusing to let a coat mean anything, which means it means everything. The real swoon lands late and private: "she had put the coat on that morning / for the name as much as for the down" (ll. 293–294) — that's the line the 10 p.m. reader rereads. **But X spoils its own mystery.** Verna on the phone Tuesday night — "Somebody up at that school better see to that woman's coat" (l. 88) — comes BEFORE the parka appears, so when it appears, my four-books-a-month reader said "oh, Verna's phone tree did it." The gift's suspect field collapses toward the town and away from *him*, and in a book where her want is named and unacted, I wanted to be allowed to hope. The institution beat lands mostly as feeling ("the column running away from her like a fence," l. 45) but then gets narrated twice — "That was how a building told you who it had been expecting" (l. 71) is a thesis sentence wearing a nice coat. Binge answer: lean in at l. 6, coast lightly through the cold-hands meditation (ll. 20–37), lean in hard at the clipboard scene, never put it down, turn the page at the end — mostly for the vacate order, partly for the name.

**CANDIDATE Z.** The put-on beat is the best of the three: "There was a doctor in it somewhere" (l. 470) — screenshot, sent to a friend. And "It's warm... I'm keeping every inch" (l. 540), refusing Delores's hemming, is quiet swoon logic: you don't alter a gift you're in love with. But Z spends its interiority on the building, not the giver — the private confession (ll. 610–615) is "she liked the coat better than anything she owned that fit" and the annex better than glass, "neither preference was reasonable." That's theme, beautifully done, and my reviewer-reader admired it; my reader-who-wants-them-together noticed there's no romantic charge anywhere in it. "The institution doesn't fit her" lands as feeling once — "nobody had decided that" (l. 336) — then as ledger ("exposure, staff, unmitigated," l. 339). Z has the same telegraph problem as X: Verna's "Somebody had better see to that woman's coat" (l. 405) precedes the coat. Binge answer: lean in at the ceramic-drop opening (l. 313), **skim ll. 342–386** — the tape protocol, heater rotation, radio traffic, Merritt's periods; it's competent process with no want in it and my DNF-at-15% reader's eyes slid — lean back in at Verna, stay in through the records room, exit warm but not hungry.

**CANDIDATE Y.** This is the swoon delivered as built. The escalation is the trick: Tuesday she gets a *generic* wrong coat ("Men's large. Smallest in the whole shipment," l. 646) — so Wednesday's parka isn't just warm, it's *addressed*. "C. COLE" lands twice as hard because yesterday she was nobody's column. The institution beat never once gets narrated; it's all dialogue and object — "'Whose sizes are in the column?' / 'Not yours,' he said, / and handed her a cup instead of an answer" (ll. 657–659), then the paragraph my whole panel screenshotted: "The coat was wrong, / ordered by a column that had never imagined her. / The cups kept coming anyway, handed across by people who had" (ll. 693–695). That is "the institution doesn't fit her" as pure feeling. And Y is the only candidate that protects the mystery: she asks once, "The shrug stood" (l. 738), no pre-coat phone call pointing at the phone tree — the suspect field stays wide open, *including him*, which is where a romance reader wants it. The cuff is a runner — falls at the treatment log (l. 668), falls "by the sugar" (l. 796), Verna's seen sleeves "come back down all her life" (l. 932) — and the last line pays it: "The sleeves held the roll" (l. 956). Something in this county finally held. My tired reader closed the chapter and immediately wanted ch 8, because the coat is warm and unexplained and *his want is due*. Binge answer: lean in at l. 630, brief coast in the cold-protocol paragraph (ll. 673–684, rescued by tape rolls riding inside her coat), lean in and stay in from the cage scene to the end. No put-down point.

## 2. RANKING

**1st — Y.** The only candidate where every job in the brief arrives as experience instead of statement: institution-as-feeling via "Not yours," gift-mystery preserved via the standing shrug, the vacate order given its best gut-punch ("Room nine went into the truck in nine minutes. / She had timed it. / Nobody had ever timed the annex, / because the annex was the one she'd unpacked," ll. 861–864 — my reviewer-reader read that aloud to her husband), the diner funniest ("Nobody asked the doctor, and the doctor did not rule," l. 813), and the ending image *earned* by a chapter-long runner rather than assigned by the outline. She charts the building as a patient nobody asked her to take (l. 879) — character doing theme's work. **DECIDING LINE:** "The cups kept coming anyway, handed across by people who had" (l. 695).

**2nd — X.** The most romantic interior beat of the three and a clean, fast, voice-forward read — "principled shivering" (l. 217), the headset splice, the permanent-sunset rendering with no rehab rooms in it (ll. 280–283). Loses to Y on two counts: it narrates its thesis where Y dramatizes it, and Verna's pre-coat phone call hands the reader a culprit the night before the gift, trading romantic hope for cozy certainty. **DECIDING LINE:** "she had put the coat on that morning / for the name as much as for the down" (ll. 293–294).

**3rd — Z.** The best sentences per pound — and the least *wanting*. Its long Tuesday is process, its confession is about architecture, and its swoon assets (the doctor-in-it-somewhere beat, the cuff tip, "no line on a survey for warm in winter," l. 605) sit inside the slowest read of the three. Third by binge math, not by prose quality. **DECIDING LINE:** "There was a doctor in it somewhere" (l. 470) — the line proves Z *can* do the beat; the chapter around it keeps choosing theme over pulse.
