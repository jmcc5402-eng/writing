**1.** l.302–353: The fight at her door is too polite. We spent all of
25 making the reader furious at Dan, and she was furious too ("I knew
it"). Here one line says her eyes "were furious," she says "Don't," and
after that she is generous — the easy way out for him, then offering to
leave so he doesn't have to pay. She never says to his face what she
heard over her eggs: that he told the whole county she was wrong. The
first room they share in three chapters, and it goes from her fury to
the kiss in about fifty lines. Let her be angry out loud for a real
exchange — it can be awkward — then the two offers, then the kiss.
"Then we're both saying no." / "Then we are." is two one-liners where
the scene should slow down. [MORE]

**2.** l.406–418: "the extension was gone in February, and his staff's
jobs went with it. / That was not what he was afraid of." The cost he
has to weigh before 27, waved away in one sentence. At l.134–138 the
job is "the one thing he had ever wanted as much as he wanted her"; in
the truck it suddenly doesn't scare him. That's 24's "that seemed too
easy." For the testimony to feel like a sacrifice, watch him hold the
job in one hand and her in the other and have it hurt — the dream job,
his coaches' families, Ty's truck payment, the lawyer's don't. He can
decide she matters more, but he can't decide it's free. [WHO]

**3.** l.58–60, l.68–69, l.320: "The rest of the sentence was right
behind the first half, and it was the half that mattered." What is the
half? We never hear it — not in the interview room, not on the porch,
not at her door. The chapter's point is that the reader understands
him: "we know that she should not be mad at him because it was just an
honest mistake." I can't know that if I don't know what he meant. Put
the other half in his head plainly, once, in the interview room — that
this is exactly why it isn't his call. Then her stopping him at the
door hurts, because we know what she didn't let him say. 27 still says
it out loud. [POINT]

**4.** l.66–93: Monday to Thursday, and the moment Dan hears his own
words on 1340 Tuesday morning — and understands what he did to her — is
skipped. l.76–79 sets it up ("Something had gone wrong at that
table… and could not find the place"), then he finds it offstage. Give
it a few lines: where he is when it comes on, his body, his thumb over
her name, the lawyer's "don't call her." [MORE]

**5.** l.234–271: Who is Tick here, and what does he want? "There was
no opinion in Tick's voice at all." I can't tell if he's giving Dan a
chance because he's on her side, or baiting him for radio. And who is
Delmar, whose porch? A reader who skipped a week won't know the Coach's
Table is a radio show from a porch. One line for which way Tick leans;
one clause for what the Table is. [WHO]

**6.** l.365–366: "Neither of them had had a thing to drink, and both of

lot and not for him." Let her pull the curtain and leave it. [NOSE]
