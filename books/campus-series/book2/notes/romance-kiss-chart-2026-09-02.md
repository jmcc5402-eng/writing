## 4. WHAT THE CHART SHOWS

1. **Book Two hands its first physical beat to somebody else, exactly like
   Book One did.** Ch 1's anonymous porch couple → ch 11's sub-couple. Both
   books let the reader watch other people get there first.
2. **The kiss moved one chapter earlier and got a much bigger job.** Book
   One's ch 16 kiss follows a rupture. Book Two's ch 15 kiss IS the point of
   no return, and it comes with terms attached the same night — the firewall
   that ch 20 strains and ch 27 vindicates. **The kiss is load-bearing plot,
   not a milestone.**
3. **The dry stretch is at the other end.** Book One front-loaded its
   dryness (15 chapters, no touch). Book Two's dry run is 23–28 — six
   chapters of strain beats after the couple is established, which is a
   harder thing to hold. ○ EST. **This is where the romance reader is most
   at risk in 1.2**, and it's worth a couple-beat check at wave 6.
4. **Both leads' pasts are professional wounds, not romantic ones.** His is
   a scandal that cost a colleague her career; hers is having stopped
   expecting to be needed. Neither carries a dead spouse or an ex-husband
   the way Book One's leads did.
5. **The sub-couple is the thesis stated twice.** Their cost is exposure;
   the leads' cost is a rule. Running the sub-couple's arc backwards —
   bed first, date last — is the book arguing its own case about what
   actually damages people.
