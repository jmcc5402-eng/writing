**Does it breathe?** Mostly yes — the one-button-per-wave discipline shows, and the spine reads right from endings alone: cut at 11, kiss at 15 (dead center, same 50% mark the casserole reader is trained to), dark button at 26, warm run-out 28–30. Two clusters land wrong:

## READER ONE — THE TARGET (extremely-online 38-year-old mother, 10 p.m., three group chats, has watched a sideline mom drama happen)

**The blurb: she one-clicks.** "She can bench his star. He can't overrule her. And neither of them can do one thing about the other" is a finished piece of marketing — it does the trope, the power inversion, and the ages in three sentences. "Closed-door romance with its want said out loud" states the door plainly, which the comp-shelf note says this buyer filters by. Stating "she is older" on the package: keep it, but know that a handful of reviews will say "the 'older woman' selling point is three years, calm down." Costs nothing; noted.

**The want: on the map, not just claimed.** This was my first suspicion — "want said out loud" is easy to promise and easy to vaporize into longing-adjacent mush. It's actually slotted: hers named in interiority ch 5 ("files it under inadvisable" — that's her voice already), his ch 8 (inconveniently timed, at the exact moment she publicly out-argues him — correct), named-and-shelved at 13, spent both directions at 29 and 30. The dial's +1 is genuinely spent on frankness, not staging. Promise: kept on paper.

**The internet material: this is the book's five-star engine, and it reads TRUE.** I checked every board beat against the §2 rules like the pedant I am:
- The dogpile is eleven families and a drafted letter, not a flame war — rule 3, exactly, and it is the more frightening version. "DM me if you'd like to be included. 11 families so far" is a sentence this reader has RECEIVED.
- "She's being so careful, which we all appreciate, but the playoff is three weeks out and he FEELS fine" — rule 4 verbatim register. She will read that and make a noise.
- The leak arrives as a screenshot escaping ring by ring, never a named main-board thread (rule 2/8, honored at ch 12); the board decodes the vaguepost WRONG (RH1) — the board being confidently wrong is the truest thing in the outline.
- The daylight jump at 23 is staged as the story event the board itself flinches at — rule 4's exact "the jump is the event, not the baseline."
- The town room stays boring-with-flares and the one room-jump (24) costs its poster — rules 6–7.
- The meal train with Earlene's casserole inside it (grain rule, one joke) is precision-guided at this reader.

One graze, flagged even though I loved it: **the consultant trawling the board's own playoff album for the photo (23)** is thriller machinery grafted onto Facebook realism. It's defensible (F4's plant is honest — the album is named at 16, the over-documentation is love), but it is the single board beat with no citation in the rulebook, and the drafts should keep his trawl mundane (a contractor scrolling on a lunch break, not a forensics montage) or it will be the one place the internet stops reading true.

**The heat contract: one honesty problem.** The package says "closed-door" flat. Chapter 11 goes past the first garment. It's fenced, side-POV, non-explicit, cut before any act — and I personally think it's the smartest scene in the outline (the whole cost of exposure demonstrated on someone else, seven chapters before the leads need the lesson). But the metadata bright line is my line to enforce: strict closed-door readers count side couples, and "closed door" on the cover plus ch 11 on the page is where the two-star "false advertising" review comes from. This is a COPY problem, not a scene problem. The scene stays; the storefront must whisper it. See mandatory fix 1.

**The suspension arc: it holds the romance spine — by exactly one thread per chapter.** The A4 defusals are real: couple beat every chapter 15–30 with no gap over two (I counted; the thinnest link is 28, covered by 27's testimony and 29's repair — legal under NEW 19, but 28 is a one-beat margin), the bottom staged as want not procedure, the verdict delivered inside the signing-day joke (ch 28 is the best structural idea in the book — the biggest plot beat inside the fondest empty ceremony). Where the Jimenez drift actually threatens is NOT the hearing — it's **chapters 24–26**, where the couple beats are all strain beats AND the endings are all down (per C7) AND he is silent on advice. Three chapters where the romance's pulse is only ache is survivable; three chapters of ache with no ending-lift and a silent hero is where "less rom-com, more family drama" gets typed into a review box. The margin is thin and the drafts will decide it.

**The coat echo: buy it. Buy two.** Wrong-size at 7, worn anyway, folded like a flag at 25, "tags off his own at six a.m." at 29, two parkas one hook at 30. This is not too cute; this is the thing she screenshots for the group chat. The withheld reveal — she wore his coat all book without knowing — is a genre payload with a hundred-year service record. B2-T02's severable line is already mug-ready.

**Reader One verdict: this is her book.** She one-clicks, she stays up, she posts the coat line. Her risks are ch 24's silence (she will scream at Dan, which is fine, as long as the draft lets her see it costing him) and the January tunnel.

## 4. THE GRIPE, RANKED (by stars it costs; findings, not fixes)

1. **The January tunnel (ch 21–27).** Seven chapters of down-endings, a buttonless wave, strain-only couple beats, and a silent hero — the entire three-star band and most of the DNFs live here. Costs half a star off the book's average by itself. (C7-confirmed.)
