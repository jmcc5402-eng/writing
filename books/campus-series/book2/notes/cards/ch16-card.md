# Where we are — Chapter 16 of 30 (week 5 of 11)

The third set piece. Three drafters wrote it blind, the panel judged
blind, and you read only the winner.

## The plot

Everything since the hit in November has been counting down to the
nineteenth, and the nineteenth is today. Trey has been out since
Monday's bike test brought the headache back, Aisha signed the
paper on Thursday, and the county has had two days to decide whose
fault that is. It decided. Ashford plays its first playoff game at
home tonight, under the lights, with Trey in street clothes, and it
loses by a field goal. The backup gets them to the fifty and no
farther. The suite is full of the people who paid for a building
rendering that was supposed to go up on the video board at the
final whistle, and the board holds the score instead. The town got
the December Saturday it had never had, and spent it on a loss, and
walks home together talking, which is worse than shouting. By
Monday the word on Dan's extension will be "review."

Underneath the game there is one thing the reader sees and nobody
on the page does. At eleven in the morning, in the lots, a father
puts his phone up for his kid on an ice chest, and the sun comes off
the lid in the same instant Aisha laughs at Dan's shoulder. One
picture, for the parents' album, taken innocently. It surfaces in
chapter 23.

After the game the two of them get the one place nobody can see,
her office at the warm end of the annex. It is the firewall's first
night, and neither of them says a word about her call. What he
gives her instead is the thing he has never given anyone in this
county: the seven missing years, in one sentence, and then he
stops. Chapter 17 is the gray season after the exodus, Birmingham,
and the second heat scene.

## Aisha

She has spent this book learning that a perfect record does not
protect her and that needing someone is not how you get hurt. On
Thursday she signed the no-go, let Dan spend his locker room on
her call without handing it back, kissed him, and built the rule
that keeps the two things apart.

Tonight we see her from his sideline. She stands at her end of the
bench all four quarters with the tape on her wrist for a boy who is
not playing, and that morning she walked the stadium's emergency
plan the way she does before every home game, for the forty-odd
boys who are. By the fourth quarter the county has her name for the
loss. She does not leave the sideline. Nobody thanks her for any of
it, and the trainer beside her is the only one who sees.

## Dan

The town decided what he was ten years ago and the board knew
before he did, and he has managed the verdict ever since. This week
he stopped: he told her the decade as proof he could want nothing,
backed her call to his captains at a cost, and told her about the
partner before the kiss.

Tonight he loses the game he could have won with the boy on the
field, and he does not say so to anyone, and the county says it for
him all the way home. At the annex he hands her the seven missing
years in one sentence, the way the rule says he must, and the
board's theories about where he went stay standing, wrong. Then,
alone in the equipment cage, he takes the loss ball nobody keeps
and writes the backup's name on it where a winning coach would have
written a score. Nobody watching. Nothing back.

## The romance

The kiss was Thursday. The terms are two days old: nothing anybody
can see until the class closes in February, and no talk of her
calls anywhere but across a sheet at five fifteen. Tonight the
whole county is in the stands, so the two of them do not look at
each other all game, and the reader feels every time they don't.
One look, late, from forty yards, and she is not looking back,
which is the terms held from her side too.

The picture in the lots is the first time anyone has the two of
them in one frame. It moves the romance to the stage where the town
half-sees, without the town knowing yet.

Postgame, the annex. No new rung on the ladder; the kiss is the
ceiling until the spring. The intimacy tonight is the sentence
about the gap and her not asking for a second one. The chapter ends
down, on the ball in the cage, after chapter 15 ended up.

## Boyd

He is a shape in the suite's glass, seen once from the field and not
spoken to. His rendering was cued and never plays. The stage he
bought a season for is gone, two days after the no-go took it. In
chapter 19 he walks her rooms uninvited, and in 20 he names his
price to Dan.

## The town, and the clock

Tick Moran's second anchor is his radio sign-off, heard on a truck
window going up Millrow: win or lose, we walk home together in this
county, always have. Missy and Denny are on the walk; Denny says
nothing, and his December letter still goes to the athletic
director after Christmas. The album caption by midnight reads *Dr.
Cole at her end. Four quarters. Never sat.*

Saturday, December 19. Kickoff at six. The season ends at the
whistle. Seven weeks to the first Wednesday in February.
