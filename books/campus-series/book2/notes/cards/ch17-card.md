# Where we are — Chapter 17 of 30 (week 6 of 11)

A connecting chapter: one drafter. The first one written under the
new rule that the two of them get room.

## The plot

The season ended on the nineteenth and the town emptied the next
morning. Students gone, the RV city gone, the lots gone, and by
Monday you can park on the square again. The Checkerboard runs its
one winter cook for a counter of regulars, the Mule's TV stays dark,
and Boyd's survey crews are chalking the curbs on Millrow in the
cold. Christmas is loud for the married and long for everybody
else, and Aisha is the doctor who stayed in an empty town with the
clinic's holiday line forwarded to her own cell.

Two things move under the quiet. The Gaults drive to Birmingham
with a folder of hopes aimed against her, and when the release for
the records comes in, she sends the whole chart the same afternoon
to the Birmingham office with one line and no question about what
it is for. She knows what it is for. And the one lit room: the
motel has emptied, the VACANCY word is lit again for the first time
since the week of the game, and on the last night of the week the
two of them have the whole night in front of them, which they have
never had. This is the book's second heat scene. It
runs up to the first piece of clothing on the floor and cuts there,
and it takes its time getting there. Chapter 18 is Dan's, the week
the eleven families' letter arrives at the Fieldhouse.

## Aisha

She has spent this book learning that a perfect record does not
protect her and that needing someone is not how you get hurt. The
Thursday before the game she kissed him and built the rule that
keeps her calls out of any room with a shut door. Now the town is
gone and it is
Christmas, and she stopped expecting Christmas years before she
stopped wanting it.

This week she gets the thing she stopped expecting, in the dark, on
terms. She does not ask him to stay and he does not offer, and the
firewall holds without either of them touching it. Early in the
chapter, before any of that, she gives something away that has
nothing to do with the case: thirty-four is one of the kids who
could not fly home, and when he comes through the empty clinic with
something small and not his hamstring, she does the small thing,
walks him to the annex door, points him at the Checkerboard, and
tells him the second stool there is his till January and his
breakfasts are paid for. She has arranged it with the cook, not
with Earlene; the stool is hers only by habit. It is the one seat
she has in this town, in the week she is loneliest. Nobody thanks
her. Earlene finds him on it next morning and never mentions it.
And she sends the Birmingham office the chart that will be used
against her, unasked, the same afternoon.

## Dan

Seen from her side this week. The county's word for his contract is
"review" now, and by Monday it is the athletic director's word too,
though nobody has shown us him saying it. He is the one man working
in the Fieldhouse over Christmas break, and on the last night of the
week he walks down the hill to a lit room because she left it lit.
What it
costs him is the thing he told her in the records room: if anyone
sees, it is the same story as ten years ago. Verna is the one person
who could.

## The romance

The kiss was Thursday. The terms are five days old: nothing anybody
can see until the class closes in February, and no talk of her
calls behind a shut door. The window signal has no poles now, so it
has to be something else, and the chapter says what: her light,
this time, and he comes to it. The one lit room is the first time
they are alone with the whole night in front of them, and the page
gives it the room it needs: how they walk to
each other, what each of them looks at, the hands, the pauses, the
things two serious people say wrong when they are nervous. It cuts
at the first garment on the floor, and the reader is not cheated of
the approach. Nothing about the call is said. Ends up.

## Boyd

Off the page, and at work: his survey crews chalking the Millrow
curbs on a weekday morning in the empty town, seen from her car. He
does not need the stadium anymore. In 19 he walks her rooms.

## The town, and the clock

The chapter's one anchor is the Checkerboard's, said at the counter
about the meal train the board has organized for the Gaults:
Christmas is loud for the married and long for everybody else, so
eat. Whose mouth it goes in is a call for you (below). Cal and
Marisol are at the counter, the settled couple the reader of Book
One wants to see warm. Verna's motel is empty, the word lit, her
eyes open. The Gaults drive to Birmingham on the twenty-third and
come back quieter, in a way the town will notice before the reader
is told why.

Week 6, the twentieth through the twenty-sixth. Six weeks to the
first Wednesday in February. The January transfer window is the
next real clock.

## Open before the brief

1. **Where the lit room is.** The outline says the motel, emptied,
   with Verna in the office. Default: room nine on Saturday the
   twenty-sixth, and he walks down the hill from the Fieldhouse, no
   truck at her building; Verna sees the light and says nothing to
   anyone, ever. Say if you want it at the annex instead.
2. **Does he stay the night?** The outline's morning image (two
   coffee cups on a windowsill) is Book One's ending and the
   no-coffee-at-night rule both, so the cups are gone either way.
   Default: he does not stay; the chapter ends on the cut. Say if
   you want a morning.
3. **The anchor's mouth.** The outline gives the Christmas line to
   Earlene, but Earlene already has four of the book's anchors and
   the ledger says no fifth before chapter 20. My recommendation:
   give it to Cal at the counter, the settled man, which keeps
   Earlene at four and puts the warm couple to work. Say Earlene if
   you want her fifth.
4. **The stool for thirty-four** is the survey's proposal for her
   kindness this week. Default yes, paid for with the cook. Say if
   you'd rather it were Verna's office phone on Christmas Eve
   forwarding to room nine.
5. **Christmas Day itself.** Default: on the page in one paragraph,
   alone, the clinic line ringing once, and not the day the lit room
   happens. Say if you want them together on the day.
