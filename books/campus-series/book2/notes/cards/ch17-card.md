# Where we are — Chapter 17 of 30 (week 6 of 11)

A connecting chapter: one drafter. The first one written under the
new rule that the two of them get room.

## The plot

The season ended on the nineteenth and the town emptied the next
morning. Students gone, the RV city gone, the lots gone, and by the
twenty-second you can park on the square again. The Checkerboard is
down to one cook, the Mule's TV is dark, and Boyd's survey crews are
chalking the curbs on Millrow in the cold. Christmas is loud for the
married and long for everybody else, and Aisha is the doctor who
stayed in an empty town with a clinic line that now forwards to her
own cell.

Two things move under the quiet. The Gaults drive to Birmingham
with a folder of hopes aimed against her, and when the release for
the records comes in, she sends the whole chart the same afternoon
with one line and no question about what it is for. She knows what
it is for. And the one lit room: the motel has emptied, Verna's sign
is dark, and for the first time since the terms the two of them have
a door nobody is watching. This is the book's second heat scene. It
runs up to the first piece of clothing on the floor and cuts there,
and it takes its time getting there. Chapter 18 is Dan's, the week
the eleven families' letter arrives at the Fieldhouse.

## Aisha

She has spent this book learning that a perfect record does not
protect her and that needing someone is not how you get hurt. Two
days ago she kissed him and built the rule that keeps her calls out
of any room with a shut door. Now the town is gone and it is
Christmas, and she stopped expecting Christmas years before she
stopped wanting it.

This week she gets the thing she stopped expecting, in the dark, on
terms. She does not ask him to stay and he does not offer, and the
firewall holds without either of them touching it. Early in the
chapter, before any of that, she gives something away that has
nothing to do with the case: thirty-four is one of the kids who
could not fly home, and when he comes through the empty clinic with
something small and not his hamstring, she does the small thing,
walks him to the annex door, and tells him the second stool at the
Checkerboard is his till January and Earlene feeds anybody who sits
on it. It is the one seat she has earned in this town, in the week
she is loneliest. Nobody thanks her. Earlene finds him on the stool
next morning and never mentions it. And she sends Missy the chart
that will be used against her, unasked, the same afternoon.

## Dan

Seen from her side this week. The county's word for his contract is
"review" now, and the athletic director has said it to his face. He
is the one man left in a building over Christmas break, and he
crosses a dark town to a lit room because she left it lit. What it
costs him is the thing he told her in the records room: if anyone
sees, it is the same story as ten years ago. Verna is the one person
who could.

## The romance

The kiss was Thursday. The terms are five days old: nothing anybody
can see until the class closes in February, and no talk of her
calls behind a shut door. The window signal has no poles now, so it
has to be something else, and the chapter says what. The one lit
room is the first time they are alone with the whole night in front
of them, and the page gives it the room it needs: how they walk to
each other, what each of them looks at, the hands, the pauses, the
things two serious people say wrong when they are nervous. It cuts
at the first garment on the floor, and the reader is not cheated of
the approach. Nothing about the call is said. Ends up.

## Boyd

Off the page, and at work: his survey crews chalking the Millrow
curbs on a weekday morning in the empty town, seen from her car. He
does not need the stadium anymore. In 19 he walks her rooms.

## The town, and the clock

Earlene's fourth anchor, delivering a plate for the Gaults' meal
train and one for the doctor anyway: Christmas is loud for the
married and long for everybody else, so eat. Cal and Marisol at the
Checkerboard, the settled couple the reader of Book One wants to
see warm, at the counter with the one cook. Verna's motel empty,
her sign dark, her eyes open. Missy's posts go quiet after
Birmingham, which is the first sign the report said what Aisha
said.

Week 6, the twentieth through the twenty-sixth. Six weeks to the
first Wednesday in February. The January transfer window is the
next real clock.
