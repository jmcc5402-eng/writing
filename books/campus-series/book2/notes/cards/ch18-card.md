# Chapter 18 — Eleven Families

Dan, the week between Christmas and New Year's. One drafter.

**The plot.**

The letter finally exists. Eleven families, polite,
asking for "clarity on medical decision-making and roster
priorities," and the athletic director sends Dan a courtesy copy. It
is about her protocol and about the walk-on's spot, in one letter
now. Denny Gault's name is not on it. Dan does the quiet right thing
first: he watches the walk-on's December film alone at night, keeps
him on merit, and tells nobody. The portal opens in four days.

**Dan.**

He is sitting on a letter about the woman he was with on the
twenty-sixth, and the terms mean he cannot say a word of it to her.
He also ends the staff-room ribbing
about Ty without a word, by handing Ty the marker and the red-zone
install, so the name the room hears is Ty's and not Mackenzie's.

**Aisha.**

Seen from his side only. He walks into the Checkerboard one morning
and thirty-four is eating on her stool, and the cook says it's paid.
He understands what she did and never says so to her. Her texts read
like her charts: the time, the thing, done.

**The romance.**

Terms-legal and warm. They text about nothing: the furnace, the ice
machine, a window. Nothing about the letter. He can't.

**The town, Boyd, the clock.**

Odell cuts the week of beard off before the portal's cameras, and
gets the chapter's line: eleven families is still eleven out of
twelve thousand. Count both numbers. New Year's Eve is one line of
texture. Ends flat.

**Your calls.**

1. The walk-on gets a name here, or stays "Marisol Pruett's boy."
2. Mackenzie's own meeting: a short section in her head, or told through Ty.
3. The letter about her: he holds it till the athletic director tells her, or tells her it exists and nothing else.
4. Odell's line at the barbershop with the beard coming off, or at the Checkerboard.
5. Midnight on New Year's: a three-word text from her, or nothing.
