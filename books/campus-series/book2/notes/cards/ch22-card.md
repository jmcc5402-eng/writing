# Chapter 22 — The Whip Count

Dan, Thursday the fourteenth of January. Three blind drafters.

**Targets.**

Romance 5 · Heat 2 · Aisha 1 · Dan 2 · Wound 1 · Fun 1 · Town 3 · Menace 2 · Ends flat · Talk normal · Words 3200 · Pays Dan

**The plot.**

The storm's second day. The hill is still glass, her car is still
under ice at the annex. The portal window shuts tonight. On the board the letter
finishes: eleven families, a delivery day, a copy line, a folder of
receipts, all of it polite. Coach's Table goes on the air from Delmar's
porch anyway. A caller
asks for clarity. Dan gives it unasked: the program has the best in
the league and follows her protocol. He says everything but her name,
and the county hears both. Off the air he walks to the corner where
Denny Gault stands, asks after Trey by name, and says nothing about
any letter. Tick tells him what signing day is. That night, for the
first time in ten years, he does not open the board.

**Dan.**

Defends her on the air as process and stops one word short. Stands
at Denny's wall where nobody has stood since December. Loses the
board's next sentence and does not read it.

**Aisha.**

Seen once, across the square at dusk, walking. One look. Neither
lifts a hand. The county sees a woman walking.

**The romance.**

Apart, and it costs. He says why once: the terms, the post this
morning, three weeks less a day.

**The town, the clock.**

The letter goes to the athletic director Friday. Signing day is the
third of February. The extension sits on the same agenda. Boyd is on
the porch, smiling, saying nothing. Ends flat, with dread, at a
counter with people in the room.

**Your calls.**

1. Boyd stands at the top of the yard steps and says nothing, or he is not there and the county only feels him.
2. Her one look is across the square before the show, or she is at the quiet end of the rail and gone before the bulb lights.
3. The last section is the Checkerboard counter with the cook and Sonny, or Delmar's porch emptying with Tick.
