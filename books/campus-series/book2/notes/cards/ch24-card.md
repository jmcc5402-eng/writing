# Chapter 24 — Pending Review

Dan, Wednesday the twentieth of January. Three blind drafters.

**Targets.**

Romance 5 · Heat 2 · Aisha 2 · Dan 3 · Wound 3 · Fun 0 · Town 2 · Menace 3 · Ends down · Talk quiet · Words 3200 · Pays Dan

**The plot.**

Tuesday night the aldermen voted Boyd's ground through. Wednesday
morning the athletic director's email goes out: the team physician is
suspended pending an independent review of the December hold, findings
in two weeks. Polite, neutral, a letterhead on the dogpile. The
university's lawyer and the department's press woman sit Dan down
before eight. Say nothing. Any statement makes it worse for her. He
says nothing. Rhonda's post crosses into the town room under DeeAnn
Prewitt's name and the town room turns on her for it. At the annex
Aisha carries one box to her car and sits in it with the heat on. Dan
crosses the lot in daylight, taps the window, and says the one flat
thing a coach can say. She does not ask him to speak. He sees that she
does not, and what it is.

**Dan.**

The last, most plausible cowardice. He knows the statement he could
make and what it costs, and he keeps his job instead. The reader
should hate him a little. He knows they should.

**Aisha.**

Seen from his side. Suspended, with her name under his on an email.
She does not ask him for the one thing, and it is the wound itself.

**The romance.**

Apart, and it costs both. The conflict is real and between them: he
chooses the job in front of her, and she lets him. The window is the
closest they come. Nothing is touched.

**The town, the clock.**

Two weeks to the review. Fourteen days to signing day. The Table counts
the week: a woman suspended and a building approved in the same four
days, and nobody says the name that connects them. Earlene says it the
other way: they didn't fire her, they invited the county to do it.

**Your calls.**

1. DeeAnn carries Rhonda's post into the town room and pays for it, or a town-room regular reposts it and Rhonda pays twice.
2. At the window he says one flat line about film at four, or nothing at all.
3. It ends at his house with the statement unsent, or at the Fieldhouse window at dark watching her taillights take Millrow.
