# Chapter 20 — Verbal Commitments

Dan, Friday the eighth of January. One drafter.

**The plot.**

Six days left in the portal, and the class is wobbling. Two families
due to sign in February have stopped answering. The quarterback he
flipped asks, through his father, whether the doctor here holds
kids out. Dan tells the father the truth. Then Boyd stands in his
office door, hands in his pockets, and names the price. The pledge
and Dan's extension sit on the same February agenda, sequenced. If
the medical situation embarrasses the program before then, they get
re-sequenced. He never says her job and never says Trey. That
afternoon Dan tells his coordinator to get his own name out before
February, and not to wait on Dan's review.

**Dan.**

He carries Boyd's price home and does not put it on her. At his
table he gets half a question out about Trey's next check. She stops
him. He says that's fair, and it costs him. At one in the morning he
types his first reply ever to the board, a defense of her with his
name on it. Then he deletes it a letter at a time.

**Aisha.**

She walks two streets in the dark to his house, the first time
since the town came home. Her phone face down at his table. She
walls the question without raising her voice.

**The romance.**

A house with the town home outside it. Both chairs used. The one
thing they can't talk about between them, and dinner anyway. What
the rule costs, paid by both, and neither breaks it.

**The town, Boyd, the clock.**

Sonny at the register: a commit is a promise from a seventeen-year-
old; build accordingly. The board says ice by midweek next week.
Ends on the reply going away.

**Your calls.**

1. Dan asks Boyd to say it plain, and Boyd doesn't; or Dan gives him nothing, as at chapter 10.
2. He gets half the question out before she stops him; or she sees it coming and stops him first.
3. The coordinator gets a name from you, or stays "the coordinator."
