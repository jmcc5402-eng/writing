# Rooting-for survey — ch 1–13 (developmental-editor, card E6, 2026-09-08)

I have everything I need: the taste sheet, the arc docs, the ch 13 card, and all thirteen chapters. No further reads. Here is the survey.

## The shape I found

Thirteen chapters in, this is the story of two careful people — a doctor who asks for nothing and a coach who checks the verdict — holding a line about a boy's brain while a county counts down to the nineteenth, and falling for each other across a printed sheet. That matches the premise and the arc docs. What does not match the author's intent is the *temperature*: from chapter 5 onward the book's warmth flows almost entirely toward the leads (pie, coat, gloves, pins, towels, fries, seltzer, saltines, Earlene counting doors, Marisol leaving the knife) and almost never out of them toward anyone but each other. The chorus has become the book's heart and the leads its spine. That is the whole finding, and it is a finding about what is missing, not what is wrong (variance card E6, applied; no banned-moves list was handed to this run, so none applied).

What is genuinely working and must survive: chapters 1–4 are a model of how to do this — the Scout's thumb (`ch01:81–122`), the tackle's grandmother's service and "I dialed a phone" (`ch02:96–102`), the receiver kept back with no whistle and no audience (`ch02:132–138`), "No lights through town. He's had enough soundtrack." (`ch03:246–247`), the cup for Denny and the chair for Missy (`ch04:59–63`), "Tell him they won. That part's confirmed." (`ch04:198–199`). Every one of those is small, unrewarded, costs something, and sits beside the wound rather than against it. The book knows how to do this. It stopped doing it for third parties around the time the leads started doing it for each other.

The Hauge and build audits from my standing remit are out of this run's scope per the brief; I have kept the TASTE finding last as required.

## The pattern

Both leads' virtues are negative-space virtues: she doesn't ask, doesn't post, doesn't soften; he doesn't sell, doesn't post, doesn't check (once). The reader is asked to admire what they refrain from, and restraint is not warmth — it is the wound wearing its best clothes. The book front-loaded the outward acts (Scout, tackle, receiver, Missy's chair, the plate) and then, from ch 7 on, turned both leads into recipients: the county hands them coats and pie and pins, they hand each other sheets and plates, and neither hands anything to a third party who cannot pay it back. That is the right *shape* for her arc — she is learning to be given to — but it strips both of the beats a reader roots for, and it leaves the chorus doing the book's loving while the leads do its holding. The wounds do not forbid the fix: her wound forbids asking, not giving; his forbids being blindsided, not kindness. Giving is in fact the one direction each of them is free to move without the arc noticing, which is exactly why a handful of two-sentence acts can do it.

## The standing rule for chapters 14–30

**Once a chapter, before the first romance beat, the POV lead spends something small — time, credit, comfort, a win — on a named minor character who cannot pay it back, where nobody who matters is watching, and the brief names that beat beside the ARC BEAT.**
