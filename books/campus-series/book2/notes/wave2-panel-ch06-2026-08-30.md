## 1. WARMTH FIRST

**Candidate X — does the joy hurt later?** Yes, and it hurts *sideways*, which is the good kind. The joy is warm rather than huge — "The fight song started up in the yard / on no instruments at all" (l.89–90) is the peak, and it's lovely — but what X does better than anyone is make the joy *cost-aware in real time*: Denny's "I promised Missy / I wouldn't ask anybody a thing tonight. So I'm not asking" (l.155–156) had two of four panelists wet-eyed at the JOY chapter, which is exactly the dogpile-plant the brief wants. Town-love: up, mostly via Delores ("she fed the crowd in the order of how cold they looked," l.46). Dan: extremely likable — "handing a town its night / meant staying under the tin till the town was done having it" (l.209–210). Binge answer: yes, she reads on, because the last line — "the quarterback line on it still read OR" (l.277) — is a plot hook with teeth.

**Candidate Z — does the joy hurt later?** This is the biggest joy in the packet by a mile, and the panel is confident it will hurt the most later because it is the most *communal*: "the station's feed handed the porch its own noise back, / a breath behind and twice as wide, / the county cheering the sound of itself cheering" (l.429–431). The 4-books-a-month reader read the reveal sequence (l.421–460) out loud to the room. Town-love: way up — Ray writing the date "backwards, so it read right from Millrow" (l.442–443), hats coming down on the wrong heads and "nobody traded back" (l.444–445), Earlene handing a full thermos cup to a stranger: "It's a day" (l.454). Dan: the most charming version — refuses the front row, Delores rules "Keep your wall, then" (l.361), gives the county exactly six words on air ("December nineteenth... Bring your coats," l.479), and turns the Delores line into an action, walking a plate to Denny: "Tell her all of them" (l.537). Binge answer: strongest of the three — she turns the page *mid-yawn*, because the last image is Dan "doing subtraction" (l.585) under all that love.

**Candidate Y — does the joy hurt later?** The *plants* hurt the most — "was 7 in that tunnel in December... and he's the one gets to run out into it" (l.748–751) is the single most devastating love-plant in the packet — but the joy itself arrives latest and through the most upholstery. The 10 p.m. reader skimmed from the plumb-line sky (l.602–605) to Ray's first spoken line (l.623) and admitted it. Town-love: up, but she loves Y's *weather* more than Y's *people*. Dan: likable until the relief confession (l.816–821, gladness that the "no" will never fall to him) — honest, aching, and one panelist flinched: "so he's glad it lands on *her*?" It's a strong beat wearing no cushion. Binge answer: soft yes — "and lay there a while with the wind crossing the eaves" (l.874) is beautiful and completely un-propulsive.

## 2. RANKING

**1st — Z.** The only candidate where the reveal physically got the panel — hearing the name through the crowd ("He did not hear the television say the name. / He heard the front row hear it," l.421–422) is the correct instinct for a POV character managing everything, and the county-cheering-itself radio loop is the screenshot of the wave. Z also delivers the brief's machinery best *as pleasure*: the grievance lands pre-reveal with the sharpest read in the packet ("a man whose own boy could not be handed a date / had gone shopping for a problem with a lever on it," l.395–396), the Delores anchor line is aimed straight at Dan ("She never looked at him while she said it. / She didn't have to," l.509–510) and *causes* the two-person gesture instead of decorating it, and the romance thread has an actual pulse — see WANTING below. DECIDING LINE: **"Somewhere in the middle of all that sound was a laugh / he knew at one hearing."** (l.468–469)

**2nd — X.** The warmest hands in the packet and the best ending. X's night-tail montage ("The night had two or three natural ends and used none of them," l.190) made two panelists homesick for a town that doesn't exist, and X's board scene is the scariest in the wave precisely because it's quiet: "matching porch faces to usernames without trying to" (l.272). The panel's single favorite *couple-adjacent* beat in the whole packet is X's: Denny saying "That doctor talks the same way... You notice that?" (l.167–168) — the town noticing the leads share a language before either lead will. What costs X the win: the grievance is given to "The man at Denny's elbow" (l.172), a nobody, so the menace has no face; and the doctor only gets watched, never *felt*. DECIDING LINE: **"She was reading the date off the screen / like it was the bottom line of somebody's chart."** (l.102–103)

**3rd — Y.** Owns the two best single sentences in the wave and the panel wants them grafted, but as a chapter-length *experience* it's the one the tired reader put down at 10:04 p.m. and finished the next morning. The atmosphere is jeweled past load-bearing ("the cold coming through the wire anyway, fine as sifted flour, / sifting the whole afternoon," l.628–629 — the same jewel twice in one sentence), the Tuesday-whiteboard flashback lands *inside* the roar (l.720–729) and taxes the peak, and Denny's grievance becomes a podium speech (l.758–768) — politely written, but a speech, where Z's version is a lever and X's is a handshake that lasts a second too long. DECIDING LINE: **"As of a Sunday afternoon they pointed at the same date. / Only one of them had hands."** (l.728–729)

## 3. FAULTS

**Flat:**
- X, l.190–201: the long-tail montage is lovely but list-shaped ("The pans went out a third time... The cake got cut at nine"); the sleepy reader's eye slid. Editor translation: three of these six beats are load-bearing (cake, shift swaps, Tick staying off-air); cut or compress the rest.
- Y, l.600–636: thirty-six lines of rigging and weather before the first joke. Skim ledger entry: pure process/exposition, no want in it. Fix: open on Ray's "Plate first... Screen'll keep. Brisket won't" (l.623–625) and pay the weather out in change.

**Confusing:**
- X, l.172–179: "The man at Denny's elbow" airs the wave's most important grievance and has no name. Reader voice: "wait, who is this guy and why do I care what he thinks about rosters?" Fix: name him or give the line to a face we'll see again.
- Z, l.336 vs X, l.94: flag-keeper is Ray in Z, Odell Fountain in X. [CHECK: which is canon — not resolvable from the packet.]

**Machine-made:**
- Y's jewel cadence: "puddles cased over in window glass" (l.608), "breath keeping a half step ahead of him" (l.609), "the television turned lantern" (l.790–791) — each fine alone, fatiguing at three per page; the review-writing panelist called it "prose that keeps curtsying."
- Z, l.482–483: "the porch treated the sentence like Scripture anyway, / repeating it outward in rings" — one metaphor past earned; the sentence before it did the work.

**Off-audience:**
- Y, l.816–821: the relief confession, uncushioned, is the only beat in the packet that made a panelist like Dan *less* on a night the brief needs him loved. Fix is one clause of cost, not deletion — the ache is right, the aim (implicitly at her) needs softening or owning.
