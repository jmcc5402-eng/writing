## 4. Ch 4 "reads too much like a hospital drama" — feedback asked for

**The author asked: maybe this chapter should be a low point?**

Answer: **no — structurally it is doing the opposite job, and it
should keep it.** Ch 4 is the biggest win of Dan's career spent in a
hospital lounge; that inversion IS the book's thesis in one night, and
the lounge two-hander is the courtship's first rung (the panel scored
it; the ladder is built on it). The low point is ch 24–26 by ruling.
Making ch 4 a low point would spend the shape early and leave the
real bottom with nowhere lower to go.

**But the author's ear is right about the ratio.** The chapter is
~380 lines and the first ~190 are clinical delivery: the concussion
speech (long), the symptom list, Missy's deposition. The listener
hears a hospital drama because for half the chapter it is the exact
content of one. The two-hander that makes it a romance chapter is
buried at line 195. Three moves, none structural:

1. **Compress the medical delivery by a third.** Aisha's speech and
   the symptom list can each lose lines without losing a fact; the
   protocol is restated in ch 5 anyway.
2. **Warm the Mule.** Currently the room is curdled on purpose and
   Sonny's jello joke dies on silence. Deliberate — but it's the one
   scene in the chapter built for lightness and it's been denied it.
   Peanut sets a COFFEE in front of Dan at midnight (item 6). Make it
   whiskey and the scene's temperature changes by itself.
3. **Let the two-hander play.** It already has the right bones ("Go
   be had" / "In a minute"). Under item 2's reading — dry, not tender —
   it wants one more exchange of the "You're looming" kind and one
   fewer held silence.

Net: the chapter stays what it is; it just stops front-loading the
part that sounds like a hospital.

introduction; the grump's heat lives underneath his restraint.

**The fix has two parts and the first is the author's:**
1. **RULE flag 10** — a short slate for each lead (hair, build, one

   DECISION PR with proposed defaults is the studio's mechanism.
2. **The noticing pass, ch 1–8** — once the nouns exist, one beat per
   chapter IN POV: what she notices about him (ch 1, 3, 5, 7), what
   he notices about her (ch 2, 4, 6, 8). The author's own examples

   were… he noticed the way her hair curled."* Plain, on the nose by
