# Campus Series — Standards

Series-level constraints and standards. **Locked** items are author
decisions — canon, per hard rule 7. **Proposed** items are drafted for
approval and are not canon until the author locks them.

Last updated 2026-08-06.

---

## Locked (author, 2026-08-06)

1. **No students as active characters.** Students may be discussed,
   texted, worried over, and felt everywhere — but they do not drive
   plot on the page. The cast is the adult world of the college town.
2. **Target reader: 35–45.** Leads, stakes, humor, and life-stage
   texture are written for this reader, not for the New Adult shelf.
3. **The college town is the theme — not the student/parent dynamic.**
   The series is about the towns themselves: the whole ecosystem of
   adults who make a college town run — owners, staff, faculty,
   coaches, townies, alumni, and parents as one slice among them.
   The parent boards are one town institution and one thread of town
   life, not the premise or the spine. Each set's identity is its
   town, not its student body. (Author, 2026-08-06.)

## The offstage-student mechanic (proposed)

A testable rule, so every chapter can be checked against it:

- Students may appear **in aggregate** (a stadium crowd, a move-in day
  swarm, a graduating class crossing a quad) and **as artifacts** —
  texts, voicemails, parent-group screenshots, an empty chair at
  Thanksgiving, a jersey number on the field.
- **No named student carries on-page dialogue or point of view.**
- The emotional presence of students should be *huge* — that's the
  point. They are the offstage moon pulling every adult tide. (The
  inverse of the Peanuts adults: heard as trombone, felt as gravity.)
- *Chapter test:* if a student walked on stage and spoke, the scene
  fails. If you removed the students' offstage pull entirely, the
  scene should also fail — the setting stops being a college town.

## Proposed standards (for author approval)

### Cast and life stage

3. **Lead age band ~36–48.** Honest age math: parents of college kids
   average late 40s–50s; a 40-year-old with a college sophomore had
   them at ~20 — usable, even a story hook, but it can't be every
   lead's math. Mix parent-leads with non-parent adult leads (coach,
   professor, innkeeper, trainer, bar owner) so the band stays
   credible. [TK: lock the exact band]
4. **Adults with real ballast.** Mortgages, exes, custody schedules,
   aging parents, tenure clocks, small-business margins, career
   reinvention, widowhood, the empty nest approaching. Stakes of this
   life stage, not college stakes borrowed upward.
5. **Second-chance-rich trope mix.** The 35–45 shelf's power tropes:
   second chance, widow/widower, divorced-and-done, single parent
   (younger kids on page are fine — it's *college-age* kids who stay
   offstage), friends-to-lovers at forty, rivals with twenty years of
   history, marriage in trouble is out but marriage-of-convenience at
   45 is in. [TK: trope grid regeneration — see Implications]
6. **Age-authentic bodies and desire.** Reading glasses, bad knees,
   real bodies wanted anyway — without self-deprecation being the
   only joke, and without airbrushing the age away.

### Content lines (reader-trust rules for this shelf)

12. **No cheating between leads, and infidelity is never romantic.**
    Exes and divorces exist; affairs are backstory or antagonist
    material, not the romance.
13. **No instructor–student or any adult–student romance, full stop.**
    Mostly mooted by the no-students rule; stated anyway because the
    setting makes readers check.
14. **Grief and divorce are handled with weight,** not as quirky
    setup. This readership has lived them.
15. **Alcohol is dinner-party, porch, and pub** — adult texture, not
    party-scene fuel.

### Series mechanics

17. **The quartet structure stands.** Four sets, four campus worlds;
    each quartet complete and box-settable; Set 1 launches without
    obligating the rest.
18. **Every setup pays off** (house rule 5). The S## register and the
    evidence-stack discipline survive the pivot unchanged.
19. **Canon docs before drafting.** Bible, THREADS.md, SUPERCONCEPTS.md
    per the workspace process; decisions recorded, not remembered.
20. **Pen name firewalled** from the middle-grade titles. [TK: name]
21. **Superconcept candidate — "the town is the character."** Every
    book shows the town *working* — its economy, rituals, seasons,
    and rooms — beyond the campus gates and beyond the parent
    boards. *Chapter test:* could this scene happen in any town? If
    yes, it hasn't earned this one. Second test: does the chapter
    touch at least one town institution that is not the college and
    not a chat room (the diner, the inn, the pub, the paper, the
    sugarhouse, the surf shop, the market)?

## Implications of the pivot (flags, not fixes)

- **The BRAINSTORM.md couple grid (§3) is invalidated** — all sixteen
  couples were students. Flagged there; the grid needs regeneration
  with adult couples. The container, set-antagonist structure,
  mega-themes, S## thread mechanics, setting research, and the
  parent-group engine all survive.
- **The parent-group engine is promoted from garnish to a major
  engine — but subordinate to the town (locked 3).** The chat rooms
  become an arena the leads inhabit rather than a chorus about
  students, and the comedy is self-recognition comedy for the target
  reader. But the board is one town institution among several; the
  diner counter, the pub, the inn, the gala, and the market carry
  equal weight as the town's rooms.
- **Adult-cast candidates per world** (sketch, not canon): SEC — the
  coach, the athletic trainer, the booster's CFO, the BBQ owner, the
  compliance officer, the parent-group admin mom; New England — the
  innkeepers, a tenure-track professor, the sugarhouse owner, the
  town's plow-and-diner economy; London — the pub landlord, a program
  director, a visiting American academic, the developer's project
  lead; California — a surf-shop owner, a marine-bio PI, an athletic
  director, the volleyball star's *mother* (the star herself now
  offstage).
- **The Hartwell container still works and may work better:** trusts,
  trustees, program directors, house stewards, convocations, and a
  benefactor's unfinished love story are already an adult story. The
  slow-burn coordinator × trust counsel couple (S03/4.4) survives
  untouched — they were never students.
- **Market check worth one research pass later:** "college-town adult
  romance" sits nearer small-town romance than New Adult — likely a
  *better* fit for 35–45 buyers (small-town is a proven steady shelf)
  but the sports-romance-adjacent packaging changes. Re-aim comps
  before covers/blurbs. [TK: re-run market positioning at bible time]
