# Campus Series — Brainstorm

**Working title:** [TK — series name undecided; placeholders throughout]
**Status:** brainstorm only. Nothing here is canon. Every name — schools,
towns, people, the series title itself — is a placeholder to make options
concrete. Compiled 2026-08-06 from two writers'-room runs:
`plot-architect` (variance card A1, antagonist-first) and
`culture-researcher` (variance card A3, sensory inventory), plus
`studio/romance-market-study.md`.

**The brief (author's):** mid-steam college-campus romance. Sixteen
books, four sets of four, each set a different campus world — a big
Southeastern football school, a New England college town, London, and a
California coast campus. Tone: excitement, thrill, cozy.

---

## 1. Market-fit notes (from the study)

- Structure obeys the study's rules: interconnected standalones, one
  couple per book, HEA every book, next couple seeded early, dual
  first-person POV default, 55–70k words, steam-locked packaging,
  series brand over pen name.
- Sixteen books exceeds the study's 6–10-book $50k model. The fix is
  architectural: **each quartet must be a complete, box-settable,
  enterable-anywhere unit.** Set 1 alone is a viable Scenario-C launch;
  Sets 2–4 are options exercised when read-through data votes, not
  obligations.
- College romance shelves as New Adult / sports-adjacent contemporary.
  Sports romance is growing and **football is not hockey-saturated** —
  leading with the SEC set is the commercially correct opening.
- All leads 18+, mostly upperclassmen/grad students, no
  instructor–student power dynamics. Stated in the bible, not assumed.

## 2. The container — what connects four campuses

Five candidates; the antagonism was designed before the premise (A1).

**A. The endowed exchange ("The Hartwell Exchange").** An eccentric
alumna's 50-year-old trust funds a small, beloved exchange program
linking the four campuses — a historic program house on each campus, an
annual all-cohort convocation, cross-registration semesters.
*Conflict engine:* the trust is precarious by design; each set, a
different institutional force tries to kill it (booster expansion,
enrollment-crisis cuts, commodified study-abroad, the trust's own
sunset clause). And every exchange student is structurally temporary —
"I leave in May" is a real obstacle, not a manufactured one.
*Weakness:* administrative stakes can read dry; keep every threat
physical — a house to bulldoze, a festival to cancel, a pub priced out.

**B. The founding pub's four anchors.** An immigrant family's original
pub in Bloomsbury; four grandchildren each run an offshoot
coffeehouse-pub in a college town — the campus living room on each.
*Conflict engine:* a hospitality corp rolling up college-town real
estate; the family split on selling. *Weakness:* the obstacle texture
repeats (everything is the buyout) and the corporate villain risks
cartoon by book 16.

**C. The traveling circuit** (mock trial / a cappella / rowing across
all four schools). *Weakness:* UK team on a US circuit strains
plausibility, and constant travel undermines the cozy-campus promise.
Weakest fit.

**D. The scholarship cohort with strings.** A dead benefactor's will
funds sixteen scholars across four schools — with conditions, a hostile
executor, and a series-long mystery (what did she really want?).
*Weakness:* will-condition obstacles slide into contrivance unless few
and emotionally motivated.

**E. The docuseries / campus-media project.** A student crew covers a
year at each campus. *Weakness:* the same antagonism (exposure vs.
intimacy) four times; media cynicism fights the cozy register.

**Recommended: the A+D hybrid.** The exchange program *is* the
benefactor's trust — A supplies movement and cozy rooms, D supplies the
human antagonist (a hostile trustee) and the underground mystery (the
benefactor's own unfinished love story). It generates a different
species of antagonism per set while keeping one villain and one mystery
running underneath. The full grid below assumes it.

## 5. Mega-themes (SUPERCONCEPTS.md candidates, with chapter tests)

1. **Cozy is earned.** The warm room is always genuinely threatened and
   always saved by community effort, never luck. *Test: does the
   chapter treat the setting as something someone fights for, or as
   wallpaper?*
2. **Home is a person (and everyone here is built to leave).**
   Semesters end, visas expire; every couple must solve transience.
   *Test: does the chapter deepen or dodge the leaving/staying
   question?*
3. **Load-bearing obstacles.** No conflict a five-minute honest
   conversation would dissolve. *Test: if these two talked frankly,
   does the conflict survive?*
4. **Every love story finishes an older one.** Cordelia's unfinished
   romance shadows the series; each set pairs its couples with an
   elder's unresolved story. *Test: are the adults people with stakes,
   or props?*

## 8. The four worlds (setting research, condensed)

Full sensory detail is in the researcher's run; this is the working
core. All schools fictional composites; comps are for study.

### World 1 — Southeastern football campus
(comps: Athens, Oxford MS, Tuscaloosa, Baton Rouge)

- **Senses:** August air you wear; cut grass, magnolia, charcoal at
  8 a.m. Saturdays; cicada static and drumline at dusk; over-A/C'd
  lecture halls. October: bourbon-and-Coke stadium cups, smoker smoke
  through tent cities, 100,000 people exhaling at once audible blocks
  away. March: azaleas detonating, windshields filmed yellow-green
  with pollen (real small talk), crawfish-boil cayenne steam.
- **Calendar spine:** move-in and rush (Aug) → football Saturdays
  structure everything → homecoming → rivalry game (late Nov, natural
  climax) → quiet gray winter, formals → azaleas/spring game →
  graduation under live oaks.
- **Rituals to fictionalize:** tent city with chandeliers and a
  midnight tear-down rule (the Grove); ringing the chapel bell into
  the small hours after wins; rolling the oaks; Greek swaps; ring
  ceremonies; midnight biscuit runs.
- **Social physics:** Greek letters, football celebrity, and gameday
  real estate are status currency — but GDIs, band kids, townies, and
  grad students run parallel worlds; cross-world romance is native.
- **Romance-ready:** tent city at dawn before anyone arrives; the bell
  line at 1 a.m.; an empty stadium on a Tuesday; the dive-bar walk-in
  cooler mid-shift; a porch swing in a power-outage thunderstorm; the
  3 a.m. diner after a loss; truck bed at a field party; the band
  practice field in sprinkler mist.
- **Pitfalls:** one phonetic drawl loses Southern readers; the South
  isn't white/rural/Baptist by default — these towns are progressive
  islands with big Black student communities and indie music scenes;
  "y'all" is plural; students dress UP for games; Louisiana ≠ Alabama.
- **Feels-invented (real):** generator-powered chandeliers in tailgate
  tents; pollen thick enough to write in.

### World 2 — New England college town
(comps: Amherst, Middlebury, Hanover, Williamstown)

- **Senses:** woodsmoke on the first cold night (mid-September); cider
  doughnuts frying; bell tower across a quiet valley; flannel-at-8,
  t-shirt-at-2. Winter: sunset 4:15, libraries glowing like ships, wet
  wool on radiators, boot-squeak on dry-cold snow, ice storms turning
  trees to glass. Mud season (the secret fifth season): sugarhouses
  venting sweet steam, spring peepers arriving overnight. May: lilacs.
- **Calendar spine:** outdoor pre-orientation trips → foliage (peak
  Oct 1–15, bare by Halloween — get this right) → surprise-holiday
  mountain tradition → the long dark and the emptying → J-term →
  Winter Carnival → mud/sugaring season → lilacs and commencement.
- **Rituals to fictionalize:** the president cancels classes by
  surprise via dawn bells and the whole college hikes (Mountain Day);
  homecoming bonfire under administrative pressure (a tradition being
  tamed is itself a plot); a cappella auditions and serenades; the
  outing club's gear rooms and cabin overnights.
- **Social physics:** 2,000–6,000 students — village gossip physics,
  the cozy and thrill engine at once. Several comps abolished Greek
  life; hockey is the glamour sport; a car is social capital;
  town–gown friction and warmth both real.
- **Romance-ready:** the 24-hour library room at 3 a.m. in December; a
  sugarhouse at boil; the surprise-hike summit; the rink after hours
  (someone with rink keys is a whole trope); an outing-club cabin in a
  storm; the bell tower; the diner counter at 6 a.m. in mud season;
  first real snowfall on the quad at midnight.
- **Pitfalls:** not everyone is old-money preppy — write scholarship
  money-awkwardness specifically (who Venmos whom); "wicked" is
  eastern-Mass, not Vermont; Dunkin' outranks Starbucks; townies
  aren't rubes — many are priced out by the college they serve.
- **Feels-invented (real):** midyear-admit students ski down the
  college snow bowl in caps and gowns at their own graduation;
  quidditch was invented at Middlebury as a dorm joke.

### World 3 — London university
(comps: UCL/KCL/Edinburgh; partly through study-abroad eyes)

- **Senses:** rain on pavement, pub interiors (hops, old carpet,
  chips), the 6 p.m. Friday pub roar — all talk, no music; damp cold
  that beats New England's 15°F; dusk at 3:45 in December, light past
  10 in June (51°N — farther north than the continental US; always
  ambushes Americans). Spring: legal park drinking on the first warm
  day, urban foxes screaming at 2 a.m., summer-ball season.
- **Calendar spine:** Freshers' Week (bonds calcify fast) → autumn
  term, reading week → month-long Christmas scatter (long-distance
  yearning is structural) → grey grind term → back-loaded May–June
  exams worth most of the year → summer balls.
- **Structural differences that gut US assumptions:** it's *uni*, not
  college; you read a course of modules, no GPA — First/2:1/2:2/Third;
  three-year degrees; freshers live in halls then move to rented
  houses signed with people met eight weeks ago (a plot engine);
  single rooms standard (changes romance logistics entirely);
  drinking age 18 so the pub is the default room from day one; no
  Greek life, no varsity spectator culture — the sports-club social
  scene fills the frat-shaped hole; "sports Wednesday" nights out in
  fancy dress (= costumes); society committee elections are drama;
  London has no campus bubble — flatmates, not classmates, are family.
- **Romance-ready:** a pub snug in the rain; the night-bus top deck at
  3 a.m.; a Georgian-flat kitchen during pres; free museums on a
  Wednesday; a park hill at 9:45 p.m. midsummer; the overnight library
  floor in exam term; the club smoking area (where real conversations
  happen); a food market cheese-toastie Saturday; the summer ball's
  dawn survivors' photo; the sleeper train north.
- **Pitfalls:** one "sophomore," "GPA," or "campus quad" in a UK mouth
  breaks the spell; posh is one accent among many and often mocked;
  US dating formality doesn't transplant — more ambiguity, more
  group-mediation; it drizzles, rarely pours; Scotland ≠ England.
- **Feels-invented (real):** UCL displays philosopher Jeremy Bentham's
  preserved skeleton, dressed in his own clothes, in the student
  centre [CHECK current display]; a rugby team dressed as smurfs at a
  cash machine is an ordinary Wednesday.

### World 4 — California coast campus
(recommended blend: UCSB/Isla Vista + San Luis Obispo — ocean-town
texture contrasts hardest with the other three)

- **Senses:** eucalyptus (the defining smell), salt, sagebrush
  petrichor after first rain; waves audible from lecture; thousands of
  cruiser bikes; the Pacific is 55–60°F — wetsuits year-round. Winter
  is the green season (hills go emerald); rain on flat roofs is an
  event; whales visible from cliffs between classes. Spring: **May
  Gray / June Gloom** — the marine layer, the single most-missed
  authentic California detail; the 1 p.m. burn-off like a curtain.
  Santa Ana/sundowner winds and fire-season dread are real texture.
- **Calendar spine:** quarter system — three 10-week sprints, midterms
  in week 3, everything at double pace. "There are no slow burns on
  the quarter system" is practically a thesis. Fall = the secret best
  beach season; a giant unsanctioned spring street party (Deltopia
  comp) as chaos setpiece or thing-the-leads-escape; grey-sky June
  graduation and lease-turnover melancholy.
- **Rituals to fictionalize:** dawn patrol (surf before 8 a.m. class,
  buoy report as morning liturgy); balcony sunset call, with applause;
  a Thursday-night farmers market with open-pit tri-tip smoke (best
  recurring low-stakes date venue in American college towns); hiking
  the hillside letter at sunrise; bioluminescent waves (sporadic,
  real, magical).
- **Social physics:** oceanside balconies are the ultimate real
  estate; surf hierarchy quiet but real; Greek life shares power with
  surf/climb crews, co-op houses, and the DIY house-show scene; the
  bike-path collision is a genuine local meet-cute genre; housing
  precarity is authentic (six in a two-bedroom, a semi-open van
  dweller at the beach lot).
- **Romance-ready:** the cliff edge at sunset; the dawn-patrol lot at
  6:10 a.m. sharing coffee between wetsuit halves; a balcony during a
  house show; the bike roundabout at class change; a bioluminescence
  night — running into black water that lights up blue; the monarch
  grove in winter (thousands of butterflies, silent); tri-tip smoke
  and a shared strawberry basket; the letter-summit at 2 a.m.
- **Pitfalls:** not everyone is a blond surfer — these campuses are
  heavily first-gen, Latino, and Asian-American; June Gloom exists;
  the ocean is COLD; SoCal says "the 101," NorCal drops the "the";
  ignoring fire season and housing costs reads tourist.
- **Feels-invented (real):** natural offshore oil seeps leave tar on
  beach feet — students keep baby oil by the door; a downtown alley
  coated in decades of chewed gum is a canonical first-date dare.

### Quartet-level notes

- **Contrast architecture:** heat/ritual/crowd → cold/intimacy/village
  → city/anonymity/translation → light/water/precarity. Each set gets
  a dominant sense: smoke and crowd-roar; woodsmoke and snow-silence;
  rain and pub-warmth; salt and eucalyptus.
- **Calendar friction is a free plot device:** SEC semester (Aug
  start), New England semester + J-term, UK late-Sept terms with
  June-heavy exams, California quarters ending mid-June — characters
  crossing sets have structurally mismatched breaks.
- **Sensitivity notes:** the Southern book should engage at least
  glancingly with the region's racial history and present — an SEC
  campus with an all-white cast reads inaccurate and evasive; a
  Southern and/or Black Southern reader pass is worth it. Don't import
  charged real-school symbols as neutral decor. UK slang dates fast —
  a current UK-uni-age reader pass catches more than any list. The
  Isla Vista comp carries real community trauma; invent freely, don't
  echo specifics.
