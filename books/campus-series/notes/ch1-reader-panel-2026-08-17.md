## BOTTOM LINE

**The panel picked right, and this is the real register — not
competent prose wearing it.** The jokes are load-bearing, the wanting
escalates on five clean rungs, and there is no page where the reader
puts it down. A good front door for sixteen books.

**Two things stand between it and excellent, both cheap:**

1. **Cal wants her for exactly one line** (l.112–114).
2. **The only heat in the book's first half is a joke** (l.36–45).

Everything else in this report is trim.

## 1. First page

**It moves. There is no line where a browsing reader stops — and that
is the finding.**

l.9 does a first line's whole job: *"On the first home Saturday of the
season, Ashford woke up and tripled."* l.11 (*"It started in the
dark"*) buys the next paragraph outright. The three magnets land fast:
first laugh at l.34 (the delivery driver taking his hat off), the
closed-door flash at l.36–45, the heroine at l.52 — all inside two
manuscript pages.

Nearest thing to a stop is l.27–29, the third and fourth consecutive
texture beat with no agent in them. She *drifts* there, she does not
stop. The hat joke rescues it.

## 2. Pacing (card C1)

Two slack stretches, both diagnosable.

- **l.20–29.** Montage paragraphs run 1, 8, 10, 4, 10, 7 lines. Both
  ten-line paragraphs are long, but the second has a couple in it and
  the first has nobody — six sensory items in a row, no human agent,
  no consequence. **Fix:** cut l.27 and fold the heat lines into the
  preceding sentence. The paragraph drops to seven lines and the hat
  joke arrives a beat earlier.
- **l.173–181.** Nine lines from the collision to Birdie's boots: a
  recap sentence, a flower truck, and a description of the flower
  truck. The chapter's longest stretch with no want moving through it.
  **Fix:** graft A's shared grin here (§5, item 4).

**Do not touch l.68–94** — the three-fold canopy build. Fold one is a
shrug, fold two threatens the cake, fold three brings him. That
escalation is why the collision doesn't feel arranged.

## 3. The montage ruling — two of three parts

**Part 1, the montage: delivered.** l.9–53, ~45 lines, cut to cut,
about two pages.

**Part 2, the steam flash woven inside: delivered on the letter,
under-delivered as heat.** l.36–45 is a real kiss, unmistakably adult
(*"She was fifty if she was a day, glorious about it"*), in
certifiably adults-only geography (*"three lawn chairs' worth of
gray-haired neighbors whistled"*), clothed, cut. **Compliance is
perfect — better than either loser.**

But ask what it *does* to the reader. It is funny, communal, warm:
*"The town rolled on past the porch and left them to it."* That is a
joke about a town, not a promise about bodies. The one bodily line is
*"took two fistfuls of that pressed shirt."*

**It lands as charm, not heat** — and the frozen outline places HEAT
at ch 16 / 21 / 23 with the line *"No beat before the midpoint."* So
this flash is the **only heat in the book's first half.** If it reads
as a laugh, the first-half heat account opens at zero and stays there
for fifteen chapters.

**Fix, two lines, no compliance cost:** keep B's staging and the
whistling neighbours — that is the audit-proof geography *and* the
joke — and import C's bodily specificity (*"his hand at her jaw, her
fist closed in his collar"*). B stays funny and starts also being hot.

**Part 3, "the final cut lands on the mains' charged collision":
NOT delivered, in any of the three candidates.** B's montage ends on
Marisol alone at l.53; Cal's boot arrives at l.98 — a 45-line gap.
C's gap is 32, A's is 35. **Three blind drafters, three misses, same
direction** — which means it is not a drafting failure. All three
obeyed the brief, whose Shape section puts the want-block and canopy
build between montage and collision.

**The panel's honest verdict: the letter is missed and the spirit is
exceeded.** The want-block is where the reader got attached to her,
and the canopy build is the funniest sequence in the chapter; it would
not trade them for literal compliance. If the author wants the letter,
compress l.55–66 from twelve lines to six and let the first canopy
fold happen inside the montage's last cut — do not move the collision
earlier.

## 4. The collision — and the report's most consequential finding

It earns the chapter. *"'Leg's bent,' he said. To the canopy."*
(l.116) is the best character introduction in any of the three drafts
— six words and you know him. *"'It's the wrong wrench.' / 'It's my
wrong wrench.'"* Her crossed arms *"which she was aware did excellent
things, and watched him be aware of it too"* is the "Marisol is sexy"
directive executed in one clause.

**But Cal wants her for exactly one line.** l.112–114 — *"He glanced
at her — one glance, down and back up, quick but not quick enough, and
she watched him decide not to have done it."* Lovely, and the entire
inventory of his side. Everything else is her looking, her wanting,
her heart.

This is not new: the 2026-08-13 romance audit flagged it as F4 — *"his
on-card attraction interiority before ch 16 is limited to 6 and 8, so
the first kiss's charge is under-built from his side."* **The prose
inherited it. As the register pilot, this chapter is about to teach
fifteen more books that the hero's wanting is one glance.**

**Fix:** B already built the hook at l.145–148 (*"his hands were
perfectly at home, thumb riding the adjustment wheel, unhurried"*).
Let the stare be measured by that thumb going still. Two lines.

## 5. Grafts — the panel disagrees with the judging twice

**Priority order for a reviser:**

1. **C's held gaze, rewritten in B's thumb-on-the-wheel idiom** (§4).
2. **C's toolbox ending** — one slot empty, riding home on the
   passenger seat — replacing *"it was just parked."* **Promoted from
   the judging's third slot to second:** one object fixes the weak
   last five lines, kills the near-trailer line, and is the best
   available use of B's own D2 hands-and-objects card. *Caveat:* B
   established the wrench in the **glove box**, not a toolbox — needs
   one line of adjustment.
3. **C's bodily line into the porch kiss** (§3).
4. **A's shared grin** (*"shareholders in something that had just been
   founded"*). **Promoted on pacing grounds:** B ends the Birdie/Eli
   beat with Marisol turning away alone, then restarts with Earlene
   arriving separately. A's grin merges the two and removes a seam in
   the chapter's second-longest coast.
5. Author's choice on the exit button (see disagreement b).
6. Trim l.27, and one of the two RV jokes.

**Disagreement (a) — the judging conflated compliance with charge.**
It calls B's flash *"the only one that fully lands the brief"* and
*"the series heat contract in one image."* B lands the **letter**
best and is the **coolest** of the three; C's is hotter. Making that
distinction is precisely what this panel exists for.

**Disagreement (b) — graft 2 ("I liked the wrong one." / "I know.")
is not a free win.** Two unnamed costs. B's exit already has a button
— *"'That's not a return policy.' / 'That's a hostage situation.'"* —
which is funnier and more in B's register; taking the graft gives the
exit two buttons. And C's line needs Cal present, while B's Cal is
already walking and raises the wrench over his shoulder without
turning. **The graft requires re-blocking the exit, not swapping
words.** Take one button or the other, not both.

**Disagreement (c) — graft 4 should be a swap, not an addition.** The
judging says B *"has room for exactly one more beat there."* It
doesn't: l.109–114 is already three beats, and the third is the only
line of Cal's wanting in the chapter. A fourth appraisal joke flattens
it. If the author prefers A's line, **cut B's** *"improving the whole
time."*

**One beat the judging missed:** C's Earlene reports **unprompted**
(*"'I didn't ask,' Marisol said. / 'Sky's blue, too,' Earlene said,
and kept cutting. 'Nobody asks about that either.'"*), which is what
"reports like weather" actually means. B's waits to be asked. B's
*"That's my whole ministry"* is the better joke; C's is the better
character. Let B's Earlene volunteer the name first and keep the
ministry joke as the follow-up.

## 6. What to protect

**Swoon inventory** — five beats, and note that four of the five are
**hers**:

1. **l.160–162** — *"hand still half open, / holding exactly nothing.
   / The nothing was surprisingly warm."* The screenshot beat.
2. **l.288–291** — *"She pressed her thumb against it once, where his
   hands had been, / and then packed the frame away and didn't tell
   anyone that."* The reread beat; the secrecy is what makes it.
3. **l.112–114** — the glance he decides not to have had. Fragile and
   irreplaceable; do not bury it under a fourth appraisal joke.
4. **l.139–141** — the crossed arms.
5. **l.36–45** — the whistling porch kiss, as staging.

**Load-bearing charm** — cut these and the chapter loses something:
l.59 (*"you could hear the refrigerator think in that house now"* — an
ache and its laugh in one clause, the Gilmore ruling executed);
l.88–91 (the wrench *"assembled herself the first week alone, out of
principle and spite"* — which is *why* the theft lands); l.143; l.236;
l.255; and the delivery driver, who delivers the modern calibration's
own example without naming a brand.

**Charm doing no work:** l.27 (fourth texture beat in a row); l.211
(second RV callback, competing with "watered" eleven lines later);
l.191 (*"a man visiting his life's work on its wedding day"* — the one
line where the author is audible instead of Marisol, in a draft whose
whole advantage over C is that it isn't writerly).

**The wanting system, five rungs, untouchable:** l.160–162 → l.167–169
→ l.262–263 → l.279–283 (*"One cart slowed near the north gate and her
whole body / sat up before she could vote on it"* — the highest
wanting in the chapter, higher than the collision) → l.288–291.

## 7. The last five lines

They turn the page on a three-line delay, and the actual last line
closes down rather than forward. The page-turn is carried entirely by
**l.307**, *"which meant he owed her"* — a debt is an appointment, and
appointments turn pages.

The judging wants the whole line cut as trailer-adjacent. **Agreed on
the diagnosis, disagreed on the cut:** the trailer-ness is in *"it was
just parked"* only. Cut those four words; keep the debt.

**Convergence finding:** all three candidates close down (A: *"it had
been hers, all day, start to finish"*; C: *"hair entirely wrecked"*).
The cause is the brief's own close spec — *"drives home with the
windows down for the first time since the divorce"* — a closing-down
beat by specification. **If the author wants chapter endings that turn
pages, the brief has to change; all three drafters obeyed it
faithfully.**

## 8. Walls — clean

No violation found. **R1 / offstage students:** no named student, no
student dialogue, no student focalized; crowds are aggregate; the
toddlers at l.175–176 are pre-school children of alumni, which
STANDARDS 5 expressly permits. **R8 / party geography:** the flash
couple is silver-haired among gray-haired neighbours — the most
audit-proof of the three drafts. **Consent:** she initiates.
**Standard 9:** kiss only, clothed, cut. **SC1 chapter test:** passes
both halves.

**Three deferred flags.**

- **W1 — "The bars filled at breakfast" (l.20) against STANDARDS 15**
  (*alcohol is dinner-party, porch and pub — not party-scene fuel*).
  The panel reads it as **not** a breach: nobody is on the page in
  those bars, no student is named, and the author's own montage ruling
  names *"the bars filling"* as an intended cut. But **all three
  candidates wrote the same line**, which makes it a ruling-level
  question sitting on page one of sixteen books.
- **W2 — invented fact:** *"Been here longer than the clock tower"*
  (l.249). No campus clock tower in the fact manifest. It is a good
  line, so the cheap answer may be adding the tower to canon rather
  than cutting the joke.
- **W3 — a rung-contract problem, and the most important item after
  §4.** The brief presents the montage under the heading *"The shape
  (ratified outline — the law of the chapter)."* **It isn't.**
  `plots/b11-outline-rev2.md` is marked RATIFIED AND FROZEN, dated
  2026-08-14; its ch 1 entry contains **no montage**, the word
  "montage" appears **nowhere in the file**, and its HEAT placements
  line still reads **"No beat before the midpoint."** The montage
  ruling is dated **2026-08-15** — it postdates the freeze and was
  never back-propagated.

  So the manuscript and the frozen outline now disagree about page one
  of the series, and the disagreement includes a heat beat the outline
  says does not exist. `DRAFTING-PROTOCOL.md` is explicit: *"a hole
  found later is fixed at the rung that introduced it and re-derived
  downward — never patched downstream."* The montage entered at rung 2
  and was patched at rung 5. **Recommend rev2's ch 1 entry and its
  HEAT placements line be re-derived before this chapter is
  accepted.**

## 9. Correction to the briefing — the orchestrator's error

The brief quoted a prior panel finding: *"Before the midpoint there is
exactly one swoon run, ch 9–10–11, between two deserts: ch 1–8 (only
ch 5 qualifies)."*

**The panel could not find that sentence anywhere it could read, and
said so.** It was right to. The sentence is real — it is in
`notes/b11-reader-panel-2026-08-16.md` — but that file sits on the
**unmerged branch `campus/b11-panel-findings` (PR #70)**, so it is
invisible to any agent reading main. The brief cited it without
pointing at where it lived. **Orchestrator error, and the fourth of
this family in four shifts: material an agent was expected to use that
it had no way to reach.**

**And the panel's substantive challenge stands.** The ratified gate
scores (`notes/b11-rev2-gates-2026-08-14.md`) put ch 1–8 ROMANCE at
**6, 2, 5, 2, 6, 7, 3, 6** — not a desert with one qualifying chapter
— and the earlier romance audit found dead zones at **ch 2–5 and
11–15**, not 1–8 and 12–15. The underlying concern is sound and §3 and
§4 confirm it in prose, but **the specific chapter numbers should not
propagate into later briefs.** They would send revisers to the wrong
chapters.

**Two process consequences worth acting on:**

1. Instrument output parked on an unmerged branch is invisible to
   every later run. Panel and sweep reports should land on main
   promptly, independent of the decision PR they support.
2. `DECKS.md` assigns no deck to `romance-reader-panel` — the critic
   deck header names three other agents. C1 is untagged so it was in
   scope, but this agent's LRU draw is undefined and all ten critic
   cards are unused by it. Worth a deck assignment.
