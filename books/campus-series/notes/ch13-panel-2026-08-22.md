## 1. RANKING

### WINNER — Candidate C (D6)

The deciding line is the plate beat, and it's two lines: **"She'd passed a hundred plates that afternoon one-handed. / His she took with both."** (C:180–181). All four of us read that twice. That's the screenshot; that's the text-a-friend beat of the whole batch. The rung itself is everything the ladder ordered — "skin over knuckles, in front of God and the whole fifth Sunday, / and stayed a half second past anything cake requires... both of them looking anywhere else" (C:184–189) — public, mutual, unremarked, and unmistakably warmer than ch 11's elbow, and then the aftermath tell is perfect servers'-code: "the next dozen slices came out generous" (C:197). C also wins three of the four vision items outright. The empty chair is the only personal one in the batch that lands clean at Gilmore weight: "for a minute it was a particular chair... He'd have gone back for pudding twice. He'd have let Delores call him baby" (C:233–238), and then the town fills it — "'This seat spoken for?' / 'It is now,' Marisol said, and it was" (C:239–241). One reader teared up at the chair, not at Birdie. The crown restage is the POV-cleanest solve on the table: Marisol *steers*, so the banked line pays through her hands — "the crown came up through the wheel into her hands, / right where Eli had said it lived" (C:109–110) — and Birdie's tears stay hers: "whatever her face did next stayed between the two of them" (C:113). That's beat 3 earning the lump in the throat without stealing one minute of the leads' chapter. And the RH2 signal is pure Marisol thinking: "She didn't read them again. She read the clocks on them. / The first sat square in the middle of the early service" (C:302–304) — nobody points. Bonus the other two don't have: Cal going still during the porch-supper plan — "Still, on that man, was not rest... Whatever he was looking at was not on the table" (C:266–271) — the only candidate that lets the reader's ch-12 knowledge ache under the town's happiest scene. Reader verdict: lean in at the tow, lean in harder at the cake table, nobody skimmed, no DNF risk anywhere.

### SECOND — Candidate A (D7)

A is a clean, fun, compliant chapter that loses on wattage, not on error. Its deciding line is the rung's aftermath: **"Her pulse came up like she'd taken stairs. / The frosting was too sweet and she ate every bit of it."** (A:249–250) — the best single aftermath sentence in the batch, and the plate relay staging ("the plates traveled hand to hand down the tables, / because that was how everything traveled here," A:226–227) makes the touch feel inevitable rather than staged. A also has the strongest fair-play signal: the timestamp litany "Eight-fifty-one. Nine-twenty-two. Ten-oh-four. Ten-forty. / Eleven-eleven... filling every slot a Sunday morning has" (A:311–313), set against "handing an offering plate down the pews, hand over hand" (A:326) — which is a gorgeous deliberate rhyme with the cake plate. A's leads get the batch's only table banter ("You'd have hated it. People enjoyed themselves in public," A:176). Why it's second: the empty chair is somebody else's chair — "a mother Marisol knew off the group" (A:255) — the ache observed at one remove instead of felt, which is exactly the beat the 35–45 reader bought the book for; and "He said less than usual after that, even for him, / and she put it down to Sunday" (A:252–253) sits right after the touch, so Cal's dread reads as touch-flusteredness — the ch-12 irony goes missing. Ending is register-legal: "Who was left over when the whole county was in church?" (A:349) sits there being an actual question, though it's the most leading of the three.

### THIRD — Candidate B (D5)

B is the tear-jerker and the rule-bender, and the deciding line is the one that broke the spell: **"Mutual, public, remarked on by nobody."** (B:194). That is the directive's spec language ("mutual, in public, unremarked") surfacing in the prose — the author grading her own beat on the page. Reader translation: "I felt a checklist tick." The rung itself is otherwise fine ("skin on skin under somebody's coconut sheet cake," B:191–192) but it's the only version with zero interiority — no pulse, no too-sweet frosting, no generous slices — so the warmest beat in the chapter is also its coolest prose. Meanwhile Birdie and Eli walk off with the chapter: the churchyard crown ("she stood where he set her in her church shoes, / eyes shut, hat still, and the yard held its coffee and let her," B:220–221) is genuinely the biggest cry in the batch — one panelist said "I'd screenshot the church shoes" — but it comes stacked with the tow, the married-off tables, Cal's "He wouldn't let another man's hands near it" (B:172–173), and "the pump came with a ninety-day warranty" (B:181), which is Birdie publicly reciprocating. That's beats 3, 4, and arguably 5 of a five-beat strand spent in one chapter, in the leads' A7 chapter. To B's credit: it's the only candidate that honors both of Marisol's empty chairs — "one twenty minutes off and one at Bellamy" (B:141) — which is canon-true (manuscript/ch07.md:27; DECISIONS.md:122).
