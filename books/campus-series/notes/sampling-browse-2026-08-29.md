**B01 — pontoon auction / thumb on her name**

1. VERDICT: **KEEP READING.**
2. First ten seconds: charmed and slightly lost — "two anchors... one of them came with a steering wheel" is funny before she knows who anyone is. By "I'd go at low water" she's grinning.
3. Sells: a small-town comedy with a man quietly in trouble over a woman.
4. Best line: "He knew exactly how she'd take it. That was the trouble." (Runner-up she'd text a friend: "I'd describe the seller's afternoon the same way.") Worst: "doing inventory that could have waited" — the only line telling her what the scene already showed.
5. Lost-without-context (calibration, not findings): who Tick is, "everything on his desk," the booster who admired the fence. None of it stalls her; the page ends on a woman's name and a hesitating thumb, which is the exact right cliff for a browser.

Reader voice: "I don't know what he's about to tell her, but 'chin first' — I already like her and she hasn't appeared." Editor translation: voice carries a page with zero context. Fix: none needed.

**B02 — the banquet glance**

1. VERDICT: **KEEP READING.**
2. First ten seconds: pulled in by sound — brushes on a snare, the kitchen door keeping time. She relaxes; this writer is driving.
3. Sells: slow-burn small-town romance where the whole room is a witness.
4. Best line: "He went back. / A man finishes his coffee, he told himself. / He hadn't ordered coffee." Worst: "That finished the plan." — cold, she doesn't know the plan, and it's the first full sentence.

Reader voice: "The glance going 'one beat past what a table of neighbors allows, and then one more' — okay, now I'm in." And "You're a useful man at a party, Cal Sutter" is a screenshot line. Editor translation: this is a swoon-adjacent page; if the Look Inside could be steered, steer near this.

**THE TALLY: 9 KEEP READING / 0 PUT IT BACK / 1 COIN FLIP (B03).**

**Strongest page:** B08 ("Yours.") — a complete swoon legible with zero context, with B09 (the diner) a half-step behind as the pure browse-bait page.
**Weakest page:** B03 — the only page where plot machinery (leak, report, caption) outweighs voice for a cold reader; even it carries two genuinely tender lines.

**Browse-appeal, whole-book:** Yes — a random page of this book sells the book, which is rarer than it sounds. Nine of ten pages pass cold because the voice does context's job: the humor lands before the nouns are explained, the pining is carried in procedure (a held glance, a carried coffee, a tape measure said back) rather than in interiority that needs backstory, and nearly every page ends on a small human turn — a thumb on a name, a badge gone quiet, a pie set down — that forces the page-turn. The ensemble is a genuine asset in the aisle: two pages with no lead-couple content at all (B05, B07) still passed on their own charm, which means the book has no dead zones for a sampler to land in. The one calibration note: the sample skews toward set-piece pages; a packet with more connective-tissue pages like B03 would likely score lower, so I'd put true random-page appeal at "very high" rather than "near-perfect." One caution under my own rules: my enjoyment of B09's public claiming and B08's evidence handoff is not a ruling on whether those beats play fair with the book's plot or its locked concepts — that's the developmental editor's call, and I defer. But if the question is whether a woman flipping this open at page-anything keeps reading: she does, and after B08 or B09 she buys it.
