## 3. THE CH-19 SHAPE

Chapters that are (a) long, (b) high-warmth / high-ensemble, and (c) scoring ≤1 — where a reader who came for the romance is asked to spend a lot of time somewhere else. Ranked by risk.

**1. ch07 "Two Pies" — 443 lines (longest chapter in the book), score 1.**
The highest-risk chapter by a distance. Cal is not on the page at all. The warmth is enormous and it all belongs to other people: Earlene's two pies, the porch of women, Aisha's espresso martinis, and — the killer — Birdie's Birmingham dock story, drawn out deliberately over sixty lines to a hard interruption: *"and she swears his shirt came off over his head and hit those dock boards, and the second it landed she—"* and the truck horn takes it. That is the hottest beat in the first half of the book and it is about a cousin in Birmingham. The leads' only content is a reverie Marisol immediately disowns: *"It's not about him."* Risk: the chapter teaches a reader that this book's heat lives in the anecdotes.

**2. ch12 "First to See It" — 423 lines, score 1.**
Opens with Eli Fountain walking Birdie Pham onto the fifty-yard line at dawn and standing there holding her coffee while she turns a slow circle under the work lights — *"The cup was hers. It had been hers the entire way."* It is the most romantic image in the first half of the book and the leads aren't in it; Cal is watching through a gate. His own beat is a picture he *"put away twice."* Risk: the side couple out-swoons the leads at the exact midpoint.

**3. ch03 "The Second Guest Book" — 432 lines, score 1.**
Market ensemble at full strength, Eli's ninety-dollar mum, Verna on the sod, the rendering. Cal and Marisol are on the same page — he crosses the whole shed, rescues the honey table, hands Verna her basket — and **he never registers her once.** She gets *"the same consideration she gave the weather, which is to say constantly, and with plans."* One-directional by definition. Risk: the reader is asked to hold the meet-cute's charge for two more chapters on her attention alone.

**4. ch14 "The Drawer" — 376 lines, score 1.**
Warm crew comedy, the swap-shop pontoon hour, Ray's unrequested to-go box. Couple content is two aborted phone calls. Mitigated: the aborted calls are load-bearing — they set up ch16's *"I picked the phone up Monday night. Twice."* Lower risk because the wanting is doing plot work.

**5. ch04 "Gravel Is Honest" — 328 lines, score 1.** Below my length threshold, listed for completeness: high-ensemble, warm-hostile Boyd set piece, couple content is one wrench and one line to a dog. Lowest risk on the list because it is the book's engine chapter, not a warmth detour.

**Note on the shape's namesake:** ch19 itself scores **2**. It is long-ish (335), maximum-warmth, twelve-crew ensemble — and it earns its keep because the couple beat is threaded *through* the ensemble rather than parked beside it: *"his fingers crossed hers on the cardboard a half second longer than the handoff required, nothing any of the twelve could have named from any distance, and she felt it in the soles of her feet."* That is the shape solved. Chapters 3, 7 and 12 are the shape unsolved.

## 4. THE RECIPROCITY CHECK

**Plain verdict: this book does not have the problem.** Roughly 32 on-page wanting beats flow HER→HIM and roughly 32 flow HIM→HER, across an exactly even 15/15 POV split. Call it 50/50 ±5%. That is unusual and it is a strength.

But the balance is **front-loaded on her side and back-loaded on his**, and that asymmetry is the finding worth naming:

- **ch01–ch13:** her ≈ 19 beats, his ≈ 16 — and two of his first three POV chapters (ch02, ch04) contain wanting that is **100% object-mediated**. He never once pictures her. He looks at a wrench.
- **ch14–ch30:** flips. His ≈ 17, hers ≈ 13. From ch18 on, his noticing is the more specific, more escalating, and more expensive of the two.

**Exhibit A — his wanting is at first entirely displaced onto hardware (ch04, lines 309–322).** *"He picked it up. Wrong size. Light in the palm. Somebody else's... and he stood there holding it long enough for the compressor to cycle twice, and then he set it back down on the rag, square where it had been, and squared it again anyway. / 'Not one word,' he told the dog."* Charming, and it does work — but three chapters in, a reader has no evidence he has thought about *her*, only about the errand.

**Exhibit B — he catches up in one chapter, ch06 (lines 46–47, 181–188).** *"He knew maybe five things about Marisol. It bothered him that her laugh was one of them."* Then: *"He noticed her hands anyway — quick, certain, ringless — and the laugh, twice more, and the dress every time she stood, and once, unbudgeted, her face, which was having a better evening than the evening had earned, and he filed every bit of it under problems."* Six discrete beats in one chapter. This is where his side of the ledger opens for real.

**Exhibit C — by the back half his is the more costly wanting (ch22, lines 234–238).** *"He knew the flight path by heart. He had known the number for months, and he kept it where he kept the House key. Close. Out of sight."* Compare ch18, lines 224–228: *"wanting the laugh that went low through the middle with the break at the top, wanting the chin coming up, the mouth taking its time over whether he'd earned the sharp thing or the kind one, wanting the dark hair down out of its day knot by that hour, wherever her Thursday had her."* That is a hero doing his own noticing, itemized, at cost.

Structural conclusion: the ledger is balanced. The only exposure is that a reader's *first* two Cal chapters give her a man who wants a chore, not a woman — and if she DNFs, she DNFs before ch06 pays it off.

## 5. THREE CHEAPEST REPAIRS

Smallest addition, biggest gap closed. A few lines each, no new scenes. Not written here, and nothing applied — these are named for the author to accept or decline.

**1. ch03 — the middle of the only three-chapter dry run.**
*What's missing:* mutual awareness. Cal walks the entire length of the shed on the page — the basket, the honey table, the tomatoes, the change into the tip cup — and never once knows Marisol is there. This is the sole reason the chapter scores 1 instead of 2, and it is the reason ch02–ch04 reads as a three-chapter run rather than two singles.
*Kind of beat that would fix it:* two to four lines at his exit — one returned look, or one word to Verna that lands where Marisol can hear it. Converts 1→2, breaks the run into 02 / 04, and removes the book's only structural violation of the 1.2 law read backward.

**2. ch02 — the reader's first Cal chapter.**
*What's missing:* any evidence he wants *her*. The wrench sits under a note reading RETURN, and *"The wrench kept its own counsel, and Cal let it."* The reader's introduction to the hero's interiority gives her a man with an unfinished errand.
*Kind of beat that would fix it:* three lines inside the existing wrench paragraph in which the object calls up something specific of hers — the open palm, the "hostage situation" line, the laugh. No new scene, no new location. Converts 1→2, and more importantly gives the reader his appetite four chapters earlier than ch06 currently delivers it.

**3. ch07 — the book's longest chapter at zero couple content.**
*What's missing:* anything reciprocal. Her only beat is a reverie she then denies out loud (*"It's not about him"*), and the chapter's biggest heat belongs to a stranger on a dock in Birmingham.
*Kind of beat that would fix it:* one small received artifact — something that arrives from his side rather than being remembered from hers — inside the existing diner or porch scenes. It does not need him present. It needs the debt to become active rather than nostalgic. Converts 1→2 and stops the longest chapter in the book from being the one where the side story out-wants the leads.
