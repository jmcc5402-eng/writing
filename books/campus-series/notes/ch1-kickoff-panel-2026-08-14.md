VARIANT A — STRANGER STEAM (cold-open couple, never seen again)
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

1. Immediate payoff by page 5: technically yes — steam lands on the
   page. But it is payoff on an account she hasn't opened. P1: "I
   don't know these people. This is a trailer, not a scene." P3: "I
   read three pages of strangers hooking up and then got handed a
   whole new name. I checked how long the book was." Editor
   translation: steam pays interest on invested characters; page-one
   strangers hold zero of her investment.
2. Bait-and-switch risk: HIGHEST of the three, and it loses both
   ways. If she bonds, they vanish — P4: "The couple in chapter one
   isn't even the book's couple. Said so in my review, two stars off."
   If she doesn't bond, the scene was skippable — a skim entry on
   literal page one.
3. Steam contract: this opening promises open-door heat from page 1.
   A mid-steam book then reads as retreat for 29 chapters — the
   ratings-mismatch complaint ("first chapter promised heat that
   never came back") is the most preventable one-star on this shelf.
   Worse here: the hero is widowed and the House is his wife's
   memorial (ADULT-CAST-IDEAS.md 1.1); anonymous sex on page 1
   against a grief-register hero is a tonal contradiction the 35–45
   reader feels even if she can't name it.
4. Reread test: fails. Once she's finished, the cold open is a
   bolted-on ad — "may never lead a book" (the design's own words)
   guarantees it never becomes part of the story.
5. Verdict: P3 flags real DNF risk at the pivot; P1 and P4 skim the
   open on any reread; P2 alone is neutral ("I'd read it, then
   forget it"). SKIM / DNF RISK.

Rule-1 report (deferred, not ruled): a steamy scene "amid the
game-day chaos" sits adjacent to R1/R8 (STANDARDS.md safety rules —
students in aggregate fill that crowd; on-page party geography is
adults-only). Any version of A needs its geography engineered
adults-only. Our dislike of A is not the reason to kill it; but this
is a live safety-rule exposure, and we defer to it.

VARIANT B — THE CHARGED COLLISION (mains, heat through tension)
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

1. Immediate payoff by page 5: yes — IF the design mandates the
   collision by page 3–4. The one failure mode is the event eating
   the opening: five pages of hickory smoke and drumline before a
   lead appears is coasting (SC1 texture is wonderful and is not a
   want). P2: "Give me her in the crowd on page one, him by page
   three, and I'm awake." P1: "Chemistry on page one IS the payoff.
   'Oh no, it's him' is what I bought the book for."
2. Bait-and-switch risk: none. She bonds with the right two people
   in the first scene she reads.
3. Steam contract: promises exactly what mid steam can keep —
   tension, awareness, "a moment neither admits" — and lets the heat
   escalate for 29 chapters instead of apologizing for chapter one.
   Denial here is denial ON PURPOSE, the engine of wanting.
4. Reread test: fully part of the story. The first-meeting scene is
   the single most reread scene in the genre; B puts the reread
   magnet on page one.
5. Verdict: KEEP READING, four of four. P4's caveat: "If the
   'collision' is a polite meet-cute with no current, then no — the
   page-one promise has to crackle." Editor translation: B wins only
   if attraction is on the page in the first exchange, per the
   design's own spec.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
VARIANT C — THE CAMEO KICKOFF (persistent side couple, then mains)
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

1. Immediate payoff by page 5: yes — a warm, steamy-adjacent beat
   from a couple who exist. P2: "A flower truck and a groundskeeper
   on game-day morning? I'd stay in that scene." It also banks
   STANDARDS.md item 24's tether rule: the side romance's beats
   happening inside main-story scenes.
2. Bait-and-switch risk: low and self-correcting — she bonds a
   little with the wrong couple, then gets to KEEP them. P1: "Mild
   'wait, who's the book about?' until the mains collide; annoyance
   converts to a bonus." But honest finding: Book 1 is the weakest
   possible instance of the C pattern, because from Book 2 onward
   the slot holds the PREVIOUS couple — a beloved, already-invested
   pair (standard 8: "past couples cameo"). That version isn't bait;
   it's the series paying a dividend. Book 1 has no dividend to pay.
3. Steam contract: warm/steamy-adjacent promises mid steam
   accurately. No mismatch.
4. Reread test: passes IF the side romance genuinely pays through
   the book (item 24: a strand that only decorates is cut). As the
   series signature from Book 2 on, it rereads as tradition — the
   thing she checks the sample for.
5. Verdict: KEEP READING, but P3's warning stands: "If the mains
   don't collide until the chapter's last page, I spent chapter one
   with the B couple. Don't make me wait that long."

SWOON / FUN / CONTRACT NOTES (comparative)
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

- Swoon inventory: the only planned beat with screenshot potential is
  B's collision. Protect it in any hybrid; nothing in A is
  protectable ("may never lead a book" = unprotectable by design).
  C's beat is FUN inventory, not swoon — company she enjoys, per SC1.
- Fun inventory: game-day Ashford is the room she wants to stay in
  (census §7 fall is genuinely delicious) — but it is load-bearing
  charm only when a want moves through it. Event-as-wallpaper is
  coasting; event-as-collision-course is the register note delivered.
- The contract (from the docs): mid steam, HEA, energy from page one.
  B lands the romance half of the promise on page ~3; C lands the
  town half by page 2; A lands neither — it lands a different book's
  promise on page 1 and retracts it on page 5.
- Buy the next one: the series-signature that sells Book 2 is C's
  mechanism — "every book opens at a big event where last book's
  couple is happy and this book's couple collides" is a preorder
  engine and a sample-chapter brand. A as signature trains her to
  skip chapter one of every book in the series.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
RANKING, HYBRID, AND THE UNDERLYING QUESTION
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

RANKING: B > C > A. A is not third by taste; it is third because it
fails the reread test by design, mismatches an undefined-but-mid
steam contract, and carries the run's only safety-rule exposure.

THE HYBRID (the panel's honest answer is a combination):
Open on the event; the mains collide with genuine charge inside the
first 3–4 pages (B is the spine and the swoon); the signature
"kickoff slot" is a warm beat for the persistent couple mid-chapter
(C's mechanism) — in Book 1 the flower-truck × groundskeeper pair,
from Book 2 onward the previous book's couple, which converts the
slot from bait into the series' proof it pays its debts. A dies.
Order matters: mains first, cameo second. Chapter one belongs to the
couple on the cover.

THE AUTHOR'S UNDERLYING QUESTION — is he right that today's reader
needs a payoff in chapter one? The panel does not split on the
principle: YES, she needs to feel "this is a romance and it's
already paying" by page 5, and a slow civic opening would lose P3
outright. The split is on what counts as payoff — and there it is
4–0 against steam-from-strangers and 4–0 for charged-contact-
between-the-mains plus a warm couple already in love somewhere on
the page. Steam is a payoff only on characters she has invested in;
on strangers it is an ad, and this shelf (35–45, mid steam,
second-chance register) reads it as a mispackaged promise. The
"low-romance-rating time" he wants to buy is real — but B+C buys it
honestly: tension between the mains IS romance content, so the
meter never reads low.

PREREQUISITE FLAG: lock STANDARDS.md item 9 (the steam ladder,
currently [TK]) before this design enters the outline. "Steamy" in
a signature chapter one is a series-wide promise; it cannot be made
before it is defined.
