## IV. THE THREE ANSWERS

**1. Did defect rate per wave fall after the scrub law (enforced from wave 4) and the Closeness law (wave 7)?** Split answer, and the split is the finding. *Named-class* defects collapsed on contact: scrub-class residue went 1 C1 firing + quota borderlines (W4) → 1 brief-import (W5) → 1 banned opener (W6) → 0 (W7) → 0 (W8, "never once" watch honored 6/6, "first fully zero board" LP7:67); mechanical line-pass fixes ran 3 → 0 → 0 → wraps-only → 4-trivial. Closeness went ≈1.3 → ≈5.0 tagged moments/1,000 words in ONE wave, from briefs alone (LP6:121–130). But *total* sweep findings per candidate did not fall — W2 ≈2.9, W3 ≈1.4, W4 ≈2.7, W5 ≈1.9, W6 ≈1.75, W7 ≈1.9, W8 ≈2.2 — because the defects migrated upstream (candidate errors → brief refractions) as fast as laws closed the downstream classes. Caveat on the proxy: each wave's sweep checked more than the last, so early-wave counts are undercounts and the flat curve overstates non-improvement. Conclusion: laws suppress exactly the class they name, within one to two waves, permanently; they do not lower the total — they relocate it.

**2. Which gate earned its cost least?** The standalone mechanical lint/tic sweep at staging. Across eight waves its unique catches round to zero — every string it greps is re-grepped by the sweep's §II-c and again by the line pass — and the one defect in its exclusive jurisdiction that appeared (the banned dash form, ch22:302–305) passed it, because the sweep deferred "linter authoritative" while the linter had no such check (row 29). It isn't expensive, but it is the only gate whose removal (with its checks explicitly reassigned to sweep + line pass) would have changed no outcome in the book. Runner-up, with the defense on the record: the per-chapter blind panels caught essentially zero continuity defects (every sweep's bottom line says "dangers no single-chapter panel can see") and their graft lists repeatedly tried to *import* contradictions (W8 findings 3 and 5; the graft-hygiene strike lists of W5–W7) — but the panels are a selection instrument, not a defect gate, and RETRO §II.5's evidence is that the three-candidate field earned its cost on quality; they just should never be scored as a continuity gate.

**3. What class did NO gate catch until the distant-pair fuzz existed?** Long-gap restaging: collisions between two *accepted* pages (or an accepted page and the bible) separated by many waves — a wave-7 chapter restaging a routine a wave-3 page fixed to the minute (rows 14–15), a callback misdated against a distant anchor (16), a persistent object's container silently swapping across ten chapters (17), relative-time arithmetic stranded by a calendar ruling (18), asserted content on a distant wall (19), and two accepted chapters agreeing against the census (20). Sweeps read candidates against briefs and wave-mates; panels read one chapter; line passes read one wave. None of these collisions was ever on one desk until the fuzz held two distant accepted pages side by side — all seven of its hits were this class, all in accepted text, and five of seven surfaced *between* the sampled pairs by luck of adjacency, which is why the shared-furniture keying (P2) is the cheap version of the lesson.
