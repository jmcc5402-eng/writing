# Cross-batch repetition scan — wave 1 (ch 1–4), 2026-08-17

Mechanical n-gram scan (4/5/6-word phrases appearing in 2+ chapters)
across the accepted ch 1 and the three staged winners. This is the
batch-mode instrument's first production run: blind drafters cannot
see each other's habits, so this pass looks for convergence AFTER
judging, before the author reads. Findings are revision-pass items,
not blockers; none rises to the tic-epidemic level the instrument
exists to catch.

## Findings

1. **The mirrored opener — KEEP, budget spent.** Ch 1: "On the first
   home Saturday of the season, Ashford woke up and tripled." Ch 3:
   "On the first away Saturday of the season, Ashford woke up its
   own size." Reads as a deliberate home/away rhyme and the panel
   praised both. Ruling proposed: the pair stands; NO third "On the
   first X Saturday… Ashford woke" opener anywhere in the book.
2. **The wrench described identically twice — TRIM ONE.** Ch 2:
   "wrong size for the job it had been caught attempting." Ch 4:
   "Wrong size for the job it had been bought for" (adjacent to its
   own "wrong size… for any job in this building," so ch 4 carries
   the idea twice on its own). Proposed: ch 4 drops the duplicated
   clause in the graft pass; ch 2's funnier version stands.
3. **"a man who had never once …" — RATION.** Ch 1 (instruction
   manual), ch 4 (never been late). Two uses in four chapters of one
   characterization template; cap at current count, deck the
   linters' watch list.
4. **"of a man who …" characterization scaffold — WATCH.** Appears
   in ch 1, 3, 4 (hands / ease / crunch "of a man who…"). Legal and
   good each time; at once per chapter it becomes the room's tic.
   Added to RECENT.md watch list, not banned.
5. **Marisol's age-plus-confidence construction — TRIM ONE.** Ch 1:
   "Forty-three years old, and she'd spent a half of football…"
   Ch 3: "She was forty-three years old, she knew exactly what she
   looked like standing there." Same move, same number. Proposed:
   ch 3 keeps the confidence, drops the age (ch 1 established it).

Minor idiom overlaps ("both hands flat on the…", "down at the end,"
"every one of them") judged normal English, no action.
