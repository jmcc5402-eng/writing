## 1. RANKING

### WINNER: Candidate C (D4, minor-character competence)
`/home/user/writing/books/campus-series/drafts/2026-08-21/ch10-candidate-C.md`

The deciding line is Cal's, at the exact center of the sanctioned beat (ll. 189–191): *"His shoulders came down an inch. He felt them go, and he let them stay down. 'It's allowed,' he said. 'She'd have counted it as applause.'"* All four readers stopped there. The 10 p.m. reader read it twice; the reviewer said "that's the pull quote for the whole book." It is the Gilmore calibration made literal — the dead wife endorsing the living woman's laugh, spoken aloud, warm, six words. C also makes the watering an *answer* rather than a private tell — she sets it up (*"They'll want water." / "Then it's a good thing the House keeps a man," she said, to a point just past his ear*, ll. 238–242 — the past-the-ear motif from ch 1/ch 7, redeployed at Cal, M1 intact) — and the close (*"He'd been on that porch the better part of an hour, and he didn't count one minute of it against the ten,"* ll. 264–265) is the best last line of the batch: the grief ritual grown by her company without being displaced. Engagement map: lean-in at *"One phone in this county sounds like that"* (l. 131 — he knows her by her *phone's buzz*; the four-books-a-month reader called that "the noticing that makes me feel noticed"); mild coast in the Doss aisles (see Violations-adjacent note below); no skim, no DNF. Editor translation: warmest, funniest, and the ache lands in dialogue instead of interiority. Fixable flaws, protected core.

### SECOND: Candidate A (D2, hands and objects)
`/home/user/writing/books/campus-series/drafts/2026-08-21/ch10-candidate-A.md`

Worse, the ending is pre-spoiled at l. 121 (*"watered, lately, by somebody who kept that to himself"* — the mum): the reviewer said "you told me he secretly waters flowers on page 3, so page 9 was a rerun." Editor translation: highest ceiling, most defects; a graft mine.

## 4. GRAFT LIST from the runners-up

1. **A → C, the "still winning" exchange** (A ll. 258–269). Insert in C after *"'It's allowed,' he said. 'She'd have counted it as applause'"* (C l. 191), before *"The hand went back to the canes"* (l. 193): her *"Is it going to bloom late this year?" … "Then she's still winning."* — his *"Every year."* This is the escalation the slot deserves: his permission, then *her gift back* — and it hands Marisol the grief scene's best give, balancing lead warmth (ranking rule 3). Highest-value graft in the batch.
2. **A → C, the zinnia placement logic** (A ll. 295–298): *"The rail gets better light. She can keep an eye on them out here."* — "she" being the rose. Replace C's lighter *"The rail was looking underdressed"* (l. 236): it converts the flowers from decor into a gift to the wife's rose, and makes his watering afterward a three-way tenderness.
3. **B → C, the over-watering clause** (B ll. 310–311): fold *"till it nearly ran over, which cut zinnias did not strictly require, and got anyway"* into C's spigot beat (l. 249) — keep C's twice-told lie around it.
4. **A → C, the jute exchange** (A ll. 34–44): *"Jute rots." / "That's what I like about it."* into C's counter scene — this is mandatory-fix #1 wearing its best clothes: Cal wins a beat, and the season's-promise theme rides in under it. Optionally also A's burn-ban goodbye (A ll. 98–104: *"I'm tying roses, Sonny. Not lighting them." / "That's what everybody says right up until the brush pile"*) — Cal gets a second point on the board and Sonny still gets the last word.
5. **B → C, the notebook tether** (B ll. 268–273): *"Verna's expecting me at two… the good stories run you a pie."* into C's exit, before the "keeps a man" line. C currently touches the main story only by abstention (*"I have not said one word about pens,"* l. 199 — keep that too); this graft satisfies standard 24's tether test and pays the ch 7/8 pie-and-Verna texture forward.
6. **The green-garden-wire ruling (A ll. 157–160): a gift wrongly wrapped — hold it, don't graft it.** As texture it's the best T18-neighborhood object anyone has found: her ties, older than jute can reach, worked around every fall by a man whose whole system is ties that rot on purpose. But *as written* — "and did not price out the reason" — it signs a promissory note the frozen outline has no slot for (T18's payoff is `[TK]`; the wire is not T18's object, and hard rule 5 says every setup pays off). It also only means anything *with* A's jute-rots system attached; grafting the wire alone into C is half a machine. Ruling: **leave it in the graft bank, flagged for the ch 20 drafter's packet** (T18's chapter), where the skipped frame and the wedding come due and the wire can either join the payoff or be spent as its overture. If anyone insists on planting it now, the defused version is the wire *without* the flagging clause — end the sentence at "same as every fall" — so the noticing reader gets a gift and nobody gets an IOU.
