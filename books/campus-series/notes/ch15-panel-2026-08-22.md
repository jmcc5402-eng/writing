## 1. RANKING

### 1st — CANDIDATE B (`ch15-candidate-B.md`)

B wins on the three things that matter most, and its one weakness is fixable in a sentence.

**The 9:41 guts hardest here.** The seat is taken mid-kindness, and not a generic kindness — *the* kindness, the ch 1 tow number ("Four minutes. I timed it. We were on an off-ramp," ch01 ln 205–206): "She was halfway through typing the interstate towing number from memory, for a mother in the group stranded on an off-ramp two hours south, when the badge went out beside her name" (B ln 152–155). And then the beat that made two of our four readers put a hand over their mouth: "She finished the towing number anyway and sent it, and it went out naked, no badge on it, one voice out of twelve thousand. The number was still the number. That was the last thing that worked all night" (B ln 174–178). Reader voice: *they took her chair and she finished helping anyway.* Editor translation: the coup strips the seat, not the woman — exactly the tragedy-of-generosity read the brief demands. P14 lands clean and never tips into stupidity: "she had been standing at the door when they arrived... her own thumb, steady, being helpful. Approve. Approve. Approve. She had held the door open herself" (B ln 191–200).

**The weld is the best-engineered of the three because the rumor is the truth inverted.** "'They're saying he signed it Monday,' Earlene said, reading. 'They're saying that's why it came quiet'" (B ln 239–240). The reader — only the reader — knows what Monday actually was: the drawer, unsigned, told to no one. That one line runs the whole gap engine. Then the ch 11 callback that made our reviewer reader gasp: "the Blind Mule had convicted Tunk Ferrell on a voice vote and bought him a sympathy round in a flower vase... There was no round for Cal Sutter tonight" (B ln 263–268). The town's warm mob has gone cold, and "the difference between a list and a mob was nowhere in twelve thousand phones" (B ln 270–271) pays her own ch 11 notebook line. Crucially, no Cal-blame sticks: the red box "some helpful hand had drawn" (B ln 224–225) marks the artifact as handled, Verna stands for him on the page ("I have watched that man go up ladders nobody sent him up," B ln 247–248), and Marisol lands on *inability*, not verdict: "no way, at eleven o'clock on a Tuesday, to make those be the same man" (B ln 284–287).

**The thumb beat is the deciding line of the whole field:** "with the name covered it was a page about an old house, and she took her thumb away, and it wasn't" (B ln 300–304). She *tests* the name — covers it, uncovers it — and the page changes meaning both ways. Wanting and dread in one physical experiment. Our screenshot reader photographed this passage unprompted. And the drive is aimed at his ch 1 smile: "She had seen a smile go once like a door opening on a lit room and closing again, and she did not know which man was standing inside this one" (B ln 332–335) — the single swooniest-and-scariest sentence in three candidates.

**B's weakness:** the cut doesn't cut. "the bay light ahead of her, seen, and no closer yet" (B ln 339) is a composed, near-static landing — the car may be moving but the sentence has parked. It reads one notch too much like an image with a comment, when the dealt register is a sentence stopped mid-motion. Fixable — see MANDATORY FIXES.

### 2nd — CANDIDATE C (`ch15-candidate-C.md`)

C is the cleanest craft of the three and has two crown jewels of its own; it loses on the gut-punch being one size smaller. The interrupted kindness is Thanksgiving flight advice ("book the Tuesday, my love, the Wednesday flights are a casino," C ln 125–126) — charming, but it's tip-sharing, not the tow number. What C does better than anyone: **it shows the seat occupied.** "The pinned post was not hers anymore... the one at the top now wore a stock sunset and cursive, Concerned4Ashford under it" (C ln 161–166) and "Twelve thousand people, and not one door in the place her key still fit" (C ln 169–170). Reader voice: *that sunset graphic sitting in her spot made me angrier than the badge.* C also has the best fair-play beat in the field — she hunts for the ink herself: "She spread two fingers on the page looking for ink, a date, a signature, anything a hand had touched, and the picture went to gray blocks before it gave her any" (C ln 205–208). Her hostess ache is the chapter's quiet kill: "not one of them could have said anything was wrong, which was the last thing she still ran, so she ran it" (C ln 182–183). Its thumb beat inverts ch 14 most explicitly — "not typing, not calling, the screen's little heat coming up into it" (C ln 270–272), and she pulls the door shut "on the ninth one" of twelve strikes (C ln 278), which is dread with a clock in it. And **C owns the best cut in the field:** "she drove at it down the middle of the gravel, fifty yards out and closing" (C ln 292–293) — ignition running, distance collapsing, sentence stopped before arrival. Exactly the dealt register. If B didn't exist, C ships with almost no notes.

### 3rd — CANDIDATE A (`ch15-candidate-A.md`)

A is a good chapter that would read fine in isolation and third in this company. Its 9:41 is solid — the reply to a first-flight mother quits "under her thumbs" (A ln 147), and the timestamp mechanism is lovely: "She knew because she screenshotted the banner, which is what Sunday had trained her to do, and the screenshot wrote the hour in the corner for her, neat as a postmark" (A ln 185–188). Its weld has one very good communal beat — "'Minding it for the paperwork,' somebody said, and nobody stepped on it, and that was the sound of a name being welded to a thing" (A ln 233–235) — and Verna's crop-notice ("Whoever sent this trimmed it to fit the name on a screen," A ln 219–220, which "Nobody was listening to" per ln 222) is a smart fair-play plant. But the thumb beat dissolves into a memory litany of his hands (tent, twine, thermos, A ln 298–311) — our tired reader skimmed the middle of it; it *tells* the two men where B and C *test* them — and the closing "lit, not reached, and she kept driving" (A ln 345) narrates its own register instead of performing it. A also carries the field's only real canon smudge: Verna staging the proposal on "Second board from the top... Not the bottom step" (A ln 62–64) collides in the reader's ear with ch 5's locked kneeling-step lore — "Third step's the kneeling step... Second's too low" (ch05 ln 151–152). Our reviewer reader stopped and said *wait, Verna, it's the third step* — a canon flinch in the middle of the warm-up scene. A also runs over the notice-composition cap (two-plus hands/competence beats in the litany, at most one allowed) — a linter matter, noted and deferred to the editorial agents.

## 3. MANDATORY FIXES (for winner B)

1. **Fix the cut.** Replace or extend B's final sentence so it stops mid-motion, not mid-contemplation. Graft C's kinetics: end on the gravel with distance closing — "...the bay light ahead of her" then motion, something with the shape of C's "fifty yards out and closing" (C ln 293). Keep B's rear-view lights ("the lawn's half-hung lights still burning small in her mirror," B ln 338) — that image is doing the brief's exact job, both lit doors at once. Do NOT cut the "which man" sentence (B ln 332–335); it belongs *before* the final motion, not in it.
2. **Conform Verna.** Take C's phrasing — "the third step. Inside. The pale one" (C ln 64) — or drop "porch board" from B ln 71. One reader flinch in the warm scene is one too many the chapter before the midpoint.
3. **Patch the how-she-knows.** One sentence of A's logic before the drive: Earlene's ch 1 "Whole campus runs through his key ring" and "you'd be where your keys were" (A ln 320–326). Costs three lines, closes the only fair-play seam B has.
4. **Protect, do not touch:** the naked tow number (B ln 174–178), "signed it Monday" (B ln 239–240), the no-round-for-Cal paragraph (B ln 259–271), the cover/uncover thumb test (B ln 294–304), Verna's defense (B ln 246–249). These are load-bearing pleasures; revision that grazes any of them loses the chapter's reread value.

## 4. GRAFT LIST (from runners-up, with insertion points)

- **GRAFT (strong): C's occupied seat.** Insert C ln 161–170 ("The pinned post was not hers anymore... not one door in the place her key still fit") into B immediately after "The queue was gone. The pins were gone" (B ln 170). B currently shows the seat *emptied*; C shows it *taken*, which is what a coup is. One paragraph, no seams.
- **GRAFT (strong): C's ink hunt.** Insert C ln 205–208 (fingers spread, hunting a signature, pixels giving nothing) into B's crowd scene, right after "The picture stopped at the name" (B ln 229). It arms the reader's superior position with her own hands and sharpens ch 16's relief.
- **GRAFT (optional): A's screenshot postmark** (A ln 185–188) could replace B's bare "It was nine forty-one" (B ln 166) — it makes the timestamp *hers*, via the Sunday-logs habit. Panel split 2–2; take it only if the line count allows.

**The three thumb beats:** A = the width-of-the-word litany (A ln 292–313); B = cover/uncover (B ln 294–304); C = held through the tower strikes, "not typing, not calling," gone on the ninth bell (C ln 268–278). **Winner keeps B's — no swap; it is the field's best single beat.** Optional micro-graft: C's clause "not typing, not calling" would slot cleanly into B ln 302 and makes the ch 14 inversion audible to the reader who remembers his phone going down twice; the panel wants it but won't insist.
