## The register

- **Drinks:** the young crowd drinks cocktails, seltzers and shots;
  beer stays with the older chorus. The contrast is texture (261).
- **Weed:** allowed, including gummies; **nothing stronger, ever.**
  That it is illegal is stakes; the hemp/CBD grey zone may carry a
  plotline (261).
- **Consent:** when a scene turns intimate, both are clear-headed —
  buzzed yes, drunk or high no. If someone has had too much, an outside
  obstacle breaks it up ("the cops are here, everybody run") (257).
- **Banter:** crude intent yes ("damn, what I would do to that…");
  crude or demeaning language never (258).
- **Swearing:** unchanged — it earns its place, never for shock (258).
- **LOCKED:** anyone drinking, using or in a steamy scene is 21+; no
  reporting lines — no professor/student, coach/player, boss/assistant
  (263).

## The leads

Fake dating stands as ruled: **Priya, 33 × Marcus, 36**, Boyd's
"unity" campaign, Priya the anonymous leak
(`ADULT-CAST-IDEAS.md`, 1.3).

- **Priya** lost her twenties to work and is meeting this world late
  (262). Part of her arc is the edge itself: what she missed, what it
  costs, what she wants from it.
- **Marcus** — divorced, two young kids. His friends still want to
  tailgate and take shots; he is always making tradeoffs. Custody
  weekends set his calendar, so **the ex-wife becomes a character**.
  He carries **a quiet sports-betting debt**, which is what lets Boyd
  stage-manage him into the fake relationship — the vice is the
  fake-dating engine (262).
- **Gambling is never romanticized** (262). The debt is never solved by
  a winning bet — hard rule 5, no solutions by luck. He gets out by
  paying for it: telling someone, losing something, asking for help.
