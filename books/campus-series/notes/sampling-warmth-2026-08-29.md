## 3. Bottom five — what's missing, smallest warm-up

**K12** — "The drawer was locked. The shop was dark." Ten words, deliberately cold; there is no person on the page to want anything, so there is nothing for me to feel except dread. Smallest fix: one sensory trace of a person who was just there — a mug still warm, the dog waiting — so the cold registers as absence of someone instead of absence of everything. (If this window is a chapter-ending sting, it may be doing exactly its job; I only report that read blind, it scores cold.)

**K17** — the hardware store. Sonny is delightful and "Good bones, bad wiring. Same as the rest of us" is a keeper, but for 300 words nobody on the page wants a person — it's commerce and thread pitch, and the target reader is idling. Smallest fix: let one item in the basket be secretly for her, or let Sonny's House gossip make Cal think of her for one sentence.

**K21** — the board vote. Victory, menace, procedure — my pulse is plot, not warmth; the leads never touch, look, or ache, and Boyd's exit owns the temperature. Smallest fix: one beat where Cal finds Marisol in the room — or deliberately can't, per their terms — so the win costs or feeds the want.

**K10** — the bar caucus. The pie knife "crossing the border all night" is lovely town-warmth, but this is a budget meeting with beer; Marisol is placed "at the corner pocket where the two sides met" and then never felt again. Smallest fix: one glance across the chalk math — she watches him write, or he writes better because she's watching.

**K16** — the shop reassembling. The last paragraph (the wrong-size wrench, "squared it again anyway") is a genuinely warm 9-line beat buried under 250 words of machines restarting. Nothing is wrong; what's missing is her, sooner. Smallest fix: get to the wrench faster, or let one crew line brush against her name so the longing has a runway.

## 5. The pattern

The warm windows all run on the same engine: one person performing a small, specific, unrequested act of care — the white mug, the ladder feet kicked square, the thermos lid filled without a question, the pen already uncapped and walking up the path — while the want itself is routed through objects and bodies rather than declarations, and any denial on the page is chosen (the terms, the agreement, the stripe of sun stepped back across) so the ache reads as discipline, not accident. Even the fights (K02, K13, K29) score warm because the anger is the shape love takes under pressure. The cold windows are the ones where nobody on the page wants anything from a person: meetings, commerce, procedure, machinery — rooms full of competence with the leads absent, apart, or present only as job titles. The town's charm (pie, banter, Earlene) reliably buys a 6 on its own, but it never buys more than a 6; only want between people gets this packet above 7. Plainly: this book is warmest when someone's hands are doing the loving and the mouth is saying something else, and coldest whenever the plot convenes without a body in the room that misses another body.
