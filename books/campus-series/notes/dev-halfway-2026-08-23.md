## 4. The smile (M1): recommendation

**Fire it at ch 22, at the ask — and against the gate default (HOLD), which I recommend the author strike.**

The reasoning, as someone who has watched withholds curdle:

- **A withhold pays at the moment of maximum meaning, not maximum delay.** Ch 8 defined what this tell means: she smiles dead-level at strangers; only Cal gets the past-the-ear. So it isn't general armor — it marks him as the one person whose gaze is dangerous to her. The smile therefore means: *you are no longer the thing I guard against.* The single moment in the book where that is the truth being enacted is ch 22 — he crosses the floor and claims her in front of the town. His essence act (public claiming) answered by hers (the guard dropped) is the Hauge braid firing in one beat. Ch 23's gate kiss doesn't need it and shouldn't carry it; ch 28/30 can't use it well (see below).
- **The camera argues for 22.** Ch 22 is Cal POV; ch 23 is Marisol's. The smile ledger has been kept in *his* head since ch 8 ("he had not counted that a loss until right now"). The payoff must land in the POV of the man who kept the count — in her POV it becomes a decision to smile, which is a lesser event.
- **The stinginess threshold sits exactly at this weekend.** Through ch 20 the withhold reads as character, because secrecy justifies it. After 22–23 — public, kissed, co-laboring, grief shared — every in-character justification is gone, and a still-withheld smile flips from *her* guardedness to *the author's*. This book's own house law (SC4) bans obstacles that survive on nothing; a tell surviving on nothing is the gestural version. The author's instinct ("I don't want to remove any satisfaction") is exactly right and points at 22, not away from it: the meter says the romance peaks 22–23, and withholding the tell *past its own peak* is what removes satisfaction. Holding to 28/30 also stacks the smile under the podium sentence and the word on the step — a garnish on the book's fullest plates, where it will be barely tasted.
- **Firing at 22 buys a paid echo at 24.** The photo that runs beside the leak is the dance photo. If her first smile at him is *in that photo*, then ch 24 weaponizes the truest thing she ever gave him — the withhold's meaning changes once more, at the setback, for free. Holding forfeits that.
- **What it costs elsewhere, both ways.** Fire at 22: ch 23 loses nothing (the gate kiss is already its own first — the cap, the unhurried kiss), and 28/30 lose only the *virgin* payoff, fully compensated by an echo on the third step where the narration can name that nothing goes past his ear anymore — an echo, not a spend, and echoes are free. Hold to 28/30: the peak weekend reads a degree cold at its warmest point — which, per the author's own law in VISION.md, is the one failure this studio has agreed to treat as disqualifying.

Stage direction worth putting in the brief: even fired in public, the smile should be aimed at him alone — private inside public. That keeps both candidate slots' virtues in one beat.

## What is working and must survive revision

- The payment discipline. Every payoff in 1–20 lands verbatim against its plant (the wrench's "I said I'd bring you the right one," the frame, the rose). Do not let back-half compression loosen this — it is the book's trust contract and its rereadability.

- The town-as-jury pattern (the Mule's two verdicts, the moat at the supper, the Liars' Table as press release). Ch 24–25's public reversal should run through these same rooms, because the reader now knows how this town rules.
- The couple's economy of gesture — half-second cup crossings, one pencil, nine-second replies. When the dam finally breaks at 22, the break is worth exactly what this restraint saved up; don't let post-22 chapters spend couple-warmth loosely and deflate what the front half banked.
