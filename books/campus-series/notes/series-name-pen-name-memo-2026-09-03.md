## 1. Series name

**The tests, in the order A5 demands.** (d) Does it fit 1.2 (a
physician and a coach, winter, the annex) and 1.3 (a fake courtship,
gala season, leaked invoices) with no reference to a porch fire?
(a) The argument. (b) The sweet-shelf neighbor. (c) Thumbnail: the
Amazon series line renders as "Book 1 of 4: NAME" under a ~100px-wide
cover; one word of eight letters or fewer survives, three words do not
[ESTIMATE — from the 2560x1600 upload spec and Amazon's grid layout].
Collision searches run 2026-09-03; Amazon's own pages were unreachable
from this run (egress block), so every "clear" below is web-level only.

### Ranked

**1. MILLROW** ("A Millrow Romance" / "The Millrow Books").
(a) The street is the quartet's spine, not Book 1's: the House sits at
its high end, Delmar's and the Blind Mule on it, the stadium gates at
its top — and 1.4's decisive vote is literally whether to vacate two
blocks of Millrow (town-ashford §5). Invented word: the series owns
its own search term from day one. (b) Sits beside the invented-place
series that lead the lane — Dream Harbor (Gilmore), Story Lake
(Score), Rose Hill (Silver) [VERIFIED, comp-shelf 2026-08-29; search
2026-09-03]. (c) Seven letters, one word — the best thumbnail on this
list. (d) A5 PASS: 1.2's survey crews chalk Millrow's curbs; 1.3's
rezoning signs go up on Millrow frontage; neither book needs the porch.
Collision: no "Millrow" title or series found [VERIFIED, search
2026-09-03]. Near-miss: *Mill River* (Darcie Chan, PRH, 3 books, sweet
Vermont small-town) — one letter apart aurally, same shelf. Not fatal;
flag for the human check. Weakness: says nothing regional; the
subtitle must carry "small-town" and the cover must carry the South.

**2. ASHFORD** ("Welcome to Ashford" / "An Ashford Romance").
(a) The shelf's dominant convention is town-as-brand (Virgin River,
Cedar Cove, Dream Harbor), and the shared town/college name is SC1's
whole argument. (b) Same neighbors as above. (c) Seven letters, fine;
"Welcome to Ashford" is not. (d) A5 PASS by construction — every Set 1
book is Ashford. Collision: BAD. *Town of Ashford* (MM romance series,
Ashford PA); Lilly Ashford (author, sweet small-town SC); *Ashford
Academy* (Piper Lawson, college-set romance); the Ashford family in
*Maine Mornings*; and Ashford University — a 35,000-student online
school absorbed into University of Arizona Global Campus in 2020–23,
still the top search association for "Ashford" + college [all
VERIFIED, search 2026-09-03]. R9 makes the last one a live risk.
[CHECK: the epigraph frame line says "Ashford County"; town-ashford.md
says Doss County. Canon conflict — flag, do not resolve here.]

**3. PORCH LIGHT** ("The Porch Light Books").
(a) The Hartwell light — kissed under it, you stay — is Set 1's
romance engine and SC3 as an object, and it belongs to every Ashford
couple, not one. In-house it is already reserved: SHARED-CANON retired
"Porchlight" as the platform name because the campus series owns porch
imagery. (b) Reads sweet-shelf on sight; illustrated-cover gift. (c)
Two short words; workable. (d) A5 PARTIAL: 1.2 lives in Cordelia's
room nine and finds her letter, so the light is present — but the
light going out and coming back IS 1.1's climax, and 1.3 (gala,
invoices) touches it only as texture. Names Book 1's plot more than
the middle. Collision: no series found [VERIFIED, search 2026-09-03],
but "porch light" is generic blurb vocabulary across the lane — the
series would never own the search term.

**4. TOWN AND GOWN** — the umbrella, not the Set 1 name.
(a) The theme in three words, and the only candidate that fits all
four planned towns. (b) No romance series uses it; the neighbors are
literary novels (Jan English Leary's *Town and Gown*; Thomas Carver's,
which is explicit MM kink — an also-bought hazard for a sweet series)
and nonfiction [VERIFIED, search 2026-09-03]. (c) Three words — fails
thumbnail. (d) A5 PASS for every book of sixteen. Verdict: reserve it
for the super-series line if Set 2 ever ships (the Melissa Foster
model — "Love in Bloom" over town-named sub-series, verified at
melissafoster.com 2026-09-03). Do not spend it on Set 1.

**5. HOME GAME(S).**
(a) Football-town idiom; Book 1's five Saturdays. (b) Sits beside
sports romance, which is the wrong shelf — *Home Town Advantage* /
Fourth Quarter Fever (AK Landow, 2025) and the football lists [VERIFIED,
search 2026-09-03]. Launch-mechanics §1 warns the packaging mismatch is
the most predictable way to tank the launch; this name invites it.
(c) Fine. (d) A5 FAIL: 1.2 is the gray season, 1.4 is the summer with
no games. Reject on positioning.

**6. THE LIARS' TABLE.**
(a) The Checkerboard's six-a.m. senate is in every book. (b) Reads
cozy-mystery, not romance. (d) A5 PASS. Collision: FATAL — *Liars'
Table: A Novel (Small Towns | Big Lives)*, D.K. Wall, 2021, same
small-town shelf [VERIFIED, search 2026-09-03]. Keep the phrase for
the newsletter name; reject as series name.

**7. HARTWELL HOUSE.**
(a) The set's contested object. (d) A5 PARTIAL — 1.2's annex is
Hartwell-adjacent, 1.3 barely. Collision: FATAL — *Hartwell After
Dark* (P. Harlow: Southern small-town romance set in Hartwell,
Louisiana), plus Hartwell Hearts, Hartwell Brothers, Hartwell Academy
[VERIFIED, search 2026-09-03]. Reject.

**Considered and dropped without ranking:** *Off Campus* (Elle Kennedy,
five books, Prime Video adaptation May 2026 [VERIFIED, search
2026-09-03] — and it says students); *Ashford State* (names the
college; R9); *Seven Saturdays* (fails 1.2 and 1.4 outright).

## 2. Pen name

### Framework

1. **Gender signalling.** Romance readership is ~82% female
   [VERIFIED, RWA figure via sapiens.org, retrieved 2026-09-03]. Every
   author the sweet/small-town bestseller searches surfaced today is a
   female-signalling full name — Mary Ellen Taylor, Carolyn Brown,
   Courtney Walsh, Katie Winters, Jodie Bailey, Susan May Warren,
   Maggie Tate, Emma St. Clair, Laurie Gilmore, Lucy Score, Lilly
   Ashford [VERIFIED, search 2026-09-03]; the 1.2 comp shelf adds
   Sarah Adams, Annabel Monaghan, Katherine Center, Abby Jimenez, Elsie
   Silver, one initials name (B.K. Borison). Zero male names.
   [ESTIMATE: the shelf's visible convention is a warm diminutive or
   short first name plus a two-syllable surname — Jenny, Katie, Lucy,
   Abby, Maggie, Lilly. A candidate should match that shape.]
2. **Initials vs. full name.** Initials are the honest way to signal
   nothing (J.D. Robb is the documented case [VERIFIED, sapiens.org])
   — but on THIS shelf initials read romantic-suspense or steamy (K.A.
   Tucker, L.J. Shen) [ESTIMATE, from shelf pattern]. Use initials only
   if the author wants a neutral signal; otherwise a full name.
3. **Nothing false.** A pen name is a label, not a persona. The bio
   says nothing about gender, family, or region that is not true. This
   memo does not know and does not state the author's real name or
   biography; nothing here should be read as either.
4. **Firewall.** Must share no word, initial pattern, or surname with
   the middle-grade or nonfiction names (STANDARDS 20; market study
   open questions). [CHECK by the author — those names are not in this
   memo on purpose.]
5. **Searchability.** Two words, each three syllables or fewer,
   spelled as it sounds, no famous namesake, no existing romance author
   within one letter, no name in the Ashford cast or Grapevine
   registry.
6. **Domain and handles** are a CHECK item per candidate, never
   asserted.

## 3. Recommendation

**Series: Millrow. Pen name: Annie Farrow.** Millrow is the only
candidate that passes the A5 test cleanly, thumbnails at seven
letters, and owns its own search term — the vote that ends the quartet
is about that street, so the name is truest in Book 4 and never needs
Book 1's porch to explain itself. Ashford is the more conventional
choice and the worse one: five existing "Ashford" romance properties
plus a defunct online university sitting on exactly the college
association R9 says to keep out of metadata. Annie Farrow matches the
sweet shelf's visible author-name shape, collides with no author, and
asserts nothing about who the author is; Jenna Rutledge is the
runner-up if the Southern signal is wanted in the byline. Reserve
*Town and Gown* as the super-series line for the day Set 2 exists.
