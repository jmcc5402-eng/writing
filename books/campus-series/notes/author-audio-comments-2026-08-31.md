## Application research — how these become law/practice

1. **The explicitness dial, operationalized:** each chapter
   brief deals its LOAD-BEARING BEATS (the 1–2 things every
   reader must hold leaving the chapter — usually the plant or
   the turn). For each: the drafter owes ONE plain decode
   within a page of the beat (anchor-line law generalized).
   The sweep checks decode presence; the panels' distracted-
   reader/audio posture (P3) checks decode SUFFICIENCY ("did
   you understand X without rereading?").
2. **The layered render** (item 2's pattern) is the licensed
   form for making a beat explicit without going flat: image →
   gloss → consequence, once per load-bearing beat, never for
   texture beats (texture stays slant; that's the mix).
3. **Audio is the reference reader for decoding.** The
   listener can't flip back — if a beat's decode depends on
   text 60+ lines away, it fails the audio test (the ch 11
   Tunk-trial fix is the precedent; items 2–3 here are the
   same class).
4. **1.1 docket (author-gated, unapplied):** ch 14 kitchens
   layered render · ch 13 sock-puppet decode line · ch 16 kiss
   slow-down beats. Each is a small diff on request.
5. **1.2 forward application:** wave-3+ briefs name each
   chapter's load-bearing beats + decode duty; the ch 9
   Cordelia letter and ch 11–12 leak mechanics are exactly the
