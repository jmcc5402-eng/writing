**2. THE REVIEWS**

FIVE STARS — "Table nine owns me now." *I bought this because the cover said small-town and closed-door and forty-something, and for once the book IS the cover. She's 43, he's 47, nobody's pretending to be 28. He steals her wrench in chapter one and I have been emotionally held hostage ever since. The line that finished me: "Every smile Cal had ever gotten off that woman had gone on past his ear, and he had not counted that a loss until right now." Sir. SIR. And Birdie's cousin-in-Birmingham story with the truck horn going off right at the shirt — I laughed so hard the dog left the room. Also the third step being pale from a hundred years of proposal knees? I need this house to be real so I can go stand on it. Where is book two.*

FOUR STARS — "I wanted to love this, and mostly I did." *Everything about this book is warm and I trust it completely — the argument on the barbecue porch might be the best fight I've read all year, because they're both RIGHT and neither one is stupid about it. My one gripe, and it's real: eight chapters in and the scoreboard reads two handshakes and a place card in a jacket pocket. I know it's a slow burn, the package says closed door, fine, I signed up for that — but closed door means the door eventually gets APPROACHED. He promised her the right wrench in chapter one. It's been two weeks of book time and that wrench is still sitting on a rag in his shop. If chapter nine isn't him walking up her street with it, I will simply pass away. Docking one star as a hostage negotiation tactic.*

TWO STARS (the harshest plausible, with a DNF cousin inside it) — "Not what the blurb sold me." *Chapter one was cute — the tent, the wrench, the crossed arms, I was in. Then chapter two is an entire day of a man fixing a boiler and grading his employees by the sound of their leaf blowers. I flipped ahead: chapter three is a farmers market, chapter four is a business meeting at a garage, and the love interests don't share a room again until chapter five. I DNF'd on page 2 of chapter two, at the forty-one work orders, and came back only because my group made me. It got better — the gala is genuinely great — but be honest in the marketing: this is a book about a TOWN and a HOUSE, and the romance is the side dish for the first hundred pages. Also they never kiss. Not once. In eight chapters. I don't care what "swoony" means to you, that's a long time to hold my drink.*

That two-star is survivable and priced in — she's the reader who wanted a different shelf. She is not a defect; she's the cost of the promise the package correctly makes.

**5. PET PEEVES LIVE, AND WHAT CH 9–12 OWES HER**

Live now (all quibble-grade):
- **The wrench is 8 chapters overdue.** As a running bit it's gold — RETURN note, the clean rag, "Not one word." But it was a promise out of his own mouth in ch 1 ("I'll bring you the right one"), and the review section keeps books. Every chapter it stays on the rag past ch 9 raises the interest rate.
- **Ch 2 is where the DNF lives.** For the town-first reader it's the best chapter in the wave; for the romance-first reader it's forty-one work orders between her and the hero's heart. Nothing to change — but know that's the page number in the two-star.
- **Two withheld things are stacking on Cal:** the frame near the top of the wall he tours right past, and whatever the third step's creak means to him. Right now these read as delicious. But the series sold "no secret a conversation would fix" — so when these come out, they'd better come out of his mouth to her, for a structural reason, and not be something she discovers sideways in a way that makes him look like he lied by omission. That's the one spot where this book could accidentally commit the sin its own back cover swears off.
- **Zero physical contact beyond two handshakes at the quarter pole.** Fine for the shelf, but the burn-rate meter is running and the four-stars are already saying so.

**The single biggest thing ch 9–12 owes the woman who preordered book 2 off chapter 8:** the wrench walks. The right one, hand-delivered, off campus, no porch full of witnesses — the first scene where these two aren't performing for a town, and the moment converts from the best argument she's read all year into something she can screenshot at 1 a.m. with no caption. Chapter 8 ends with him wanting to pay money to keep the loud going; chapter 9–12's job is to make him pay it.
