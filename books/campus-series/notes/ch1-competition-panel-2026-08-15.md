CANDIDATE A — "Game Day" (card D6)
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

(a) Page-one test. Awake by paragraph three, yes — the lost delivery driver
("a man lost in a city that had not existed on Thursday") is the first laugh
and it comes fast. P3: "The 'Cut to' thing made me feel like I was reading a
treatment, not a book. Cute once. By the third 'Cut to' I noticed the device
instead of the town." Editor translation: the scaffold is visible; the camera
should rove without announcing itself. Fix: delete the three "Cut to" labels;
the paragraphs already jump fine on their own.

(b) The collision. It crackles, and it crackles twice. The held look is real
— "He looked at her with a slow, exact attention, an estimate being made and
revised upward, and she looked right back and let him feel it" — and the exit
is the best button in the candidate: "'That was my grandmother's wrench,' she
called after him, which was a lie, and 'I know,' he called back, which was
somehow worse." P1: "Okay, now I'm in. He called her bluff without turning
around. That's the guy." One debit, P4: "'an estimate being made and revised
upward' — I felt the author being clever where I wanted to feel her stomach
drop." Editor translation: the standoff is staged in appraisal-language, not
body-language. Fix: one physical beat inside the stare (A already has "A bead
of sweat ran down between her shoulder blades" — move it to the peak).

(c) Register. Gilmore: strong — the grief chord is brief and gets its laugh
("Tonight she thought she might just be tired instead, which was an enormous
promotion"). Modern: seltzers, group chat, playlists — compliant, no
year-stamps. Marisol sexy: yes, and by her own hand — "a sundress she had
bought specifically because it was not a sensible choice for manual labor"
and "she was forty-three and had stopped apologizing for enjoying it." Cal
Redford-golden: "weathered gold, gray coming in at the temples like it had
been invited... wearing every year of it well, which she found frankly rude
of him" — P2: "Screenshot. That's the assignment."

(d) The montage. Energy, mostly, but the steam flash pulls its punch: "his
mouth an inch from hers and closing" — the brief says "charged and kissing,"
and A cuts an inch before the kiss. P1: "You teased ME on page one? The
steam flash is the sample's promise. Land the kiss." Editor translation:
under-delivery on the one beat that was allowed to deliver. Fix: let them be
mid-kiss when the camera finds them; cut on the flag snap.

(e) Skim ledger. One coasting patch, quoted: "The tent's work thinned.
Marisol restocked, broke down boxes, sent the volunteers off in shifts" —
process with no want in it, rescued two lines later by the polo census
("twice the certainty turned around holding a trash bag," which P2 laughed
at). No DNF point; the panel is confident because every dip is under four
lines long.

(f) Compliance. Steam flash: adult ("neither of them a day under forty"),
porch geography, clothed, cut — pass. No named students; crowds only — pass.
No surname for Marisol, no Cal backstory, no plot machinery — pass. No
banned moves detected.

CANDIDATE B — "Third-Largest City" (card D2, hands and objects)
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

(a) Page-one test. Awake by paragraph two. The delivery driver beat is the
best of the three tellings: "checked his screen, looked down the rows,
checked his screen again, and slowly, respectfully, took his hat off." P3:
"That's where I decided the book was funny on purpose. I stopped auditioning
it and started reading it."

(b) The collision. Crackles — via comedy and hands rather than the staring
match. "'Leg's bent,' he said. To the canopy." got a laugh from all four
personas; "'It's the wrong wrench.' / 'It's my wrong wrench.'" is the
exchange P2 read out loud to her husband. Marisol wields the moment: "she
crossed her arms, which she was aware did excellent things, and watched him
be aware of it too." And the after-beat is the single best swoon on the
table: "hand still half open, holding exactly nothing. The nothing was
surprisingly warm." Honest finding, P4: "He barely holds her gaze. One
down-and-up glance and he's gone. The brief promised me 'she keeps his gaze
a beat too long, and neither of them wins' — B gives me banter where A and C
give me the stare." Editor translation: B under-delivers the brief's one
specified standoff beat; it's a graft-sized hole, not a structural one. Fix:
see GRAFTS.

(c) Register. Gilmore: the strongest of the three by laugh count, and every
laugh does work — the ache gets its joke in the same breath ("a house that
had gone loud with quiet the week both kids left — you could hear the
refrigerator think in that house now"). Marisol sexy: "*pinned post, my
loves*" is confidence as texture; the toolbox "assembled herself the first
week alone, out of principle and spite" makes the wrench mean something
before he steals it. Cal: "Silver-gold hair the August sun had clearly been
working on for years and improving the whole time" — Redford, delivered.
Modern: seltzers "rattling in ice like applause," screens, playlists — pass.

(d) The montage. Pure energy, zero delay, and the steam flash is the only
one of the three that fully lands the brief: an actual kiss, unmistakably
adult ("She was fifty if she was a day, glorious about it"), adults-only
geography certified on the page ("until three lawn chairs' worth of
gray-haired neighbors whistled"). P1: "The whistling neighbors ARE the
closed-door promise. Charged, clothed, public, funny. That's the series
heat contract in one image."

(e) Skim ledger. Nearly clean. One transitional inhale, quoted: "By ten
thirty she'd handed out two hundred name tags, recited the parking answer
enough times to set it to music" — recap-flavored, but one sentence and it
carries a joke. P4 flags one near-miss on a banned move: "which meant he
owed her, which meant Saturday wasn't over, it was just parked" — trailer
adjacent. The chapter's true last line lands in-chapter ("that was between
her and the third-largest city in the state"), so the panel scores it legal
but trims it in revision. No DNF point; P3 never reached for her phone.
[CHECK: "Been here longer than the clock tower" — a campus clock tower is
not in the fact manifest; likely harmless furniture, but it is an invented
fact and should be cleared or cut.]

(f) Compliance. Steam flash adult and staged among gray-hairs — the most
audit-proof of the three. No named students. Fathers and toddlers at the
tent are unnamed aggregate — pass. Closed door: kiss only, clothed, cut.

CANDIDATE C — "Third-Largest City" (card D7)
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

(a) Page-one test. Awake by paragraph three — "two silver beards at the rail
relitigated a game older than the ice machine" is the first grin, and the
drumline/cicadas "trading fours" is the prettiest sentence in any candidate.
P2: "Gorgeous. I also felt myself admiring it instead of laughing, which at
10 p.m. is a warning light."

(b) The collision. The sharpest dialogue on the table, and the only
candidate that delivers the brief's standoff verbatim-faithful: "She kept
her eyes on his while she didn't get it back, one beat past polite, two
beats past casual... Neither of them won." The exchange escalates cleanly —
"'The leg and I had an understanding. You've interfered.' / 'I noticed the
understanding. From the road.' / 'And you couldn't stand it.' / 'I couldn't
watch a cake die.'" — and the exit is the single most screenshotted beat of
the whole run: "'I liked the wrong one.' / 'I know.'" P1: "That's the line I
text my group chat. Two words and he's the whole love interest." The smell
beat is the second: "cut grass and clean sweat and the inside of a toolbox,
which should not have worked on her, and did."

(c) Register. Marisol sexy: yes — "she had worn the good sunglasses and the
better jeans, and half the tent had noticed, and she had noticed them
noticing." Cal: "forearms that had clearly won every argument they'd ever
been in" — pass. Gilmore: the ache-with-a-laugh is there ("a year past the
divorce, give or take a lawyer" is the best grief-chord phrasing of the
three) — but the overall temperature runs a shade cooler and more lyric
than B; P2: "I admired C. I *enjoyed* B." Editor translation: C is the most
writerly candidate; as a 16-book calibration document, writerly drifts
heavy by Book 4.

(d) The montage. Tightest splice of the three — the final cut lands
mid-catastrophe ("both hands over her head and a hundred pounds of folding
aluminum coming down to meet them"), so the collision arrives almost
instantly. Cost of that choice: the next paragraph rewinds — "The first
time, the canopy had waited until she'd alphabetized three hundred name
tags" — and P3 flagged it: "You just showed me the ceiling falling and then
paused it to tell me history. Small stall, but I felt it." Steam flash:
kiss lands, adult geography ("a porch of gray heads"), and "Nobody's
business. Everybody's town." is the montage's thesis said out loud — P4
loved it; P1 thought it a half-step precious.

(e) Skim ledger. The fold-flashback (quoted above) — recap. And one process
patch: "Teardown went fast... The tables folded, the coolers lightened" —
saved by Earlene leaving "with the cake knife and a report on tomorrow's
humidity." No DNF point. Earlene overall is C's secret weapon — "'Sky's
blue, too,' Earlene said, and kept cutting. 'Nobody asks about that
either.'" — and C is the only candidate where she reports unprompted, which
is what "like weather" actually means.

(f) Compliance. Steam flash adult, doorway-behind-the-gray-heads geography,
"the screen door swallowed them" — charged, clothed, cut — pass. No named
students. Earlene stating "Forty-seven" is manifest fact, legal. No banned
moves.

RANKING
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

**B > C > A.** Split: P1, P2, P3 for B; P4 for C, conceding to B on reread.

Deciding reasons:

1. **The FUN meter is the primary gauge by the author's own ruling, and B
   reads highest on it** — the most laughs per page, and every laugh is
   load-bearing (the refrigerator, the hat, "watered," "I've watched him
   not drink it," "in this heat that's practically a courtship"). C's
   pleasures skew admired; A's skew composed. B's skew *felt*.
2. **B's Marisol is sexiest by the author's own definition.** A enjoys being
   looked at; C notices being noticed; B *deploys* it — "which she was
   aware did excellent things, and watched him be aware of it too." That is
   the register-note directive in one clause, and it is the calibration the
   next fifteen books need.
3. **B's steam flash is the only one that fully keeps the brief** — a real
   kiss, certifiably adult, certifiably adults-only, and funny. As the
   series-signature beat, it is the sample-chapter heat contract done right.
4. **B's swoon travels through objects** (the warm nothing in her open hand;
   the thumb pressed to the cam lock "where his hands had been... and
   didn't tell anyone that") — beats that reread, screenshot, and scale
   across a series without inflating the heat level.

B's debits — the soft gaze-standoff, the clock-tower [CHECK], the "just
parked" near-trailer — are all line-sized and all fixable by graft or trim.
C loses on temperature, not quality; it is the best-written and the
second-best-*felt*. A loses on the visible "Cut to" scaffold, the pulled
kiss, and a thinner Birdie/Eli beat — but donates freely (below).

Superconcept note (rule 1: reported, deferred): all three value SC1 —
Delmar's, the fairgrounds, the bars carry the town beyond the campus gates —
and none cheapens SC2–SC4 (no plot machinery on the page, per the brief).
No safety-wall violation anywhere. Our ranking is pleasure only; it
authorizes nothing.
