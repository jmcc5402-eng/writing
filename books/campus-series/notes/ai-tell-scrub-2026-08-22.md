# AI-Tell Scrub — Campus Book 1.1 (deep sample ch01/05/08/11/12; structural scan all 12)

## 1. The Verdict

The target reader — the small-town-romance buyer, the person the hand-seller pitches this to — will not clock it. Page by page this prose is warmer, more specific, and more object-driven than anything a skeptic's mental model of "AI slop" predicts; the wrench, the slides, the guest book are real novelist moves. But the pro will feel something at *book* scale that no single page shows, and the C2 question ("would the hand-seller clock the machine?") is exactly where the risk lives: a bookseller skims chapter endings and jacket-adjacent pages, and this book's endings are twelve consecutive perfect landings in the same two shapes. The 200-book/year Goodreads reader won't say "AI wrote this" — they'll say "this author has three moves and never misses," which in 2026 is the polite form of the same suspicion. The deepest tell is convergence: chapters credited to different blind drafters share the same nine scaffolds down to exact idiom ("before he could vote on it" in ch01, ch06, ch08, ch09 — four "different hands," one phrase). One mind with a suspiciously consistent metabolism. The town is also frictionlessly witty: in five chapters read closely, no named character ever says a line that merely fails. A generated town.

## 4. Ready-to-paste additions for STYLE.md ("AI drafting tics" section)

```markdown
- **Endings budget.** At most half the chapters in a book may end on a
  wry button or antithesis couplet ("Nobody X. Everybody Y."; "wanted
  A / settled for B"). At least two chapters per book end unresolved,
  mid-gesture, or on plain information — a sentence that sits there
  being true. Twelve composed landings in a row is itself the tell.
- **No feelings in the filing cabinet outside Cal's POV.** The ledger
  family (filed, logged, unbudgeted, itemized, under advisement,
  paperwork, on the record) applied to emotion is Cal's
  characterization, max twice per chapter, and never in a chapter's
  final ten lines. In any other POV or the narrator, cut it.
- **Body-autonomy scaffold, once per chapter.** A limb or impulse may
  outrun its owner once. The exact idioms "before s/he could vote on
  it" and "before s/he could dress it up/down" are spent — banned.
- **Banned fingerprint words:** "unhurried" (2 per book, total);
  "declined to" + mental verb (Cal only, once per book); "That was the
  whole [X] of it" (spent); "and meant it" (spent); sentence-initial
  vague "Somewhere..." (2 per chapter).
- **Precision-timed feelings: one gauge per chapter.** "for exactly N
  seconds / one second longer / N entire seconds" is one move, not
  three. ("One beat past" remains separately budgeted — it is at five
  uses and closed.)
- **Personification ration: three per chapter.** The town/house/objects
  may act three times per chapter, memorably. Everything else gets a
  plain verb.
- **Someone's joke must die.** Per chapter with three or more named
  speakers, at least one line of dialogue lands flat, boring, or wrong
  — and stays uncommented. A town where every line lands is a
  generated town.
- **Homeward-coda cap.** The alone-in-transit-home closing (windows,
  radio, porch lights) is spent for Book 1.1; wave 4+ chapters may not
  end in a vehicle or on a solitary walk home.
```
