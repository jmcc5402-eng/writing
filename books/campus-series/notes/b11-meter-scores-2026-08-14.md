## 5. Findings

F1 [HIGH] — Romance peak and dip are transposed. The target wants
the couple at their most magnetic at 75% and the bond dipping at
90%; the outline delivers the mirror image — ch 22–23 is the
lovers' lowest stretch (apart, forbidden to call, absence only)
and ch 27 is a heat peak. In reader terms: the reader is never
afraid of losing the couple — the all-is-lost costs the campaign
and his name, never the bond — and the night-before chapter plays
as reward, so the climax arrives with no relationship question
left open.

F2 [HIGH] — The romance line flatlines twice in the first half.
Ch 2–4 sit at 1 after a single hostile meeting, and ch 11–15 run
1/1/4/1/3 between the ch 10 vulnerability high and the ch 16
kiss. The target's early waves have a floor of 4; a romance
reader gets three- and five-chapter stretches where the love
story is simply not on the page.

F3 [MEDIUM] — The middle does not climb; it spikes. Ch 17–26
averages about 4, with islands at 16, 20, and 24 and troughs of 1
at 18 and 26. The "climbing through the middle" the target
declares reads instead as two great scenes on a low tide.

F4 [MEDIUM] — FUN floor breach walking into the midpoint. Ch
14–15 both score 2 — two consecutive grim chapters carry the
reader into coup night with no relief; the first laugh back is
the dog's boot vote in ch 16. The floor rule allows one dark
chapter, not two.

F5 [MEDIUM] — Ashford vanishes in four door-closed chapters. Ch
12, 14, 16, and 24 score TOWN 3. The outline's own SC1 audit
flags 12/14/16 as the price of the drawer trap; ch 24 (truck,
drawings, office phone) is unflagged and has no town in it at
all.

F6 [LOW] — Ch 30 is a game-day chapter at FUN 7 against the 8+
event standard. The register is valedictory — warm rather than
grinning. Miss by one; likely intentional.

F7 [LOW] — Ending lands 9 against "skyrockets (10)." The HEA is
doubled and earned but quiet. Whether ▇ versus █ matters here is
an author call, not an instrument call.
