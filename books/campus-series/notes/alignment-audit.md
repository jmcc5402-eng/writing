# Alignment Audit — 2026-08-06

Developmental-editor run (variance card E6: weight what's missing over
what's wrong) auditing STANDARDS.md, BRAINSTORM.md, and
ADULT-CAST-IDEAS.md against the three locked standards and the
workspace hard rules. All findings are proposals; nothing is decided.

**The shape found:** as regenerated, this is a sixteen-book series
about adults defending a beloved exchange program from four flavors of
institutional money, with romance obstacles now genuinely structural
and the parent boards demoted to one engine among several. That is
*almost* the book the standards describe — but locked standard 3 says
the series is about **the towns**, and the regenerated grid is still,
book by book, mostly about the campus, the trust, and the boards. The
towns do not yet exist in any document. That is the audit's first and
largest finding.

**What is working (protect in revision):** the pivot's core trade is
real — payrolls, visas, custody math, and medical authority survive
the five-minute-honest-talk test where the student grid's obstacles
didn't. The B-story "earns its pages" discipline is the best
structural hygiene in the docs. 3.1 (visa marriage × community buyout,
Home Office interview as clock) and 4.3 (her debt vs. their archive)
are fully load-bearing as written. The ripple table is honest about
its own damage.

## A. Locked 1 — offstage students under drafting pressure

   students, on page. *Fix: stage Aisha's calls in the medical tent /
   film room / paperwork — the reader sees the argument about the
   call, never the call; write the staging rule into the bible.*
2. **(Significant)** The 1.2 B-story's resolution belongs to an
   offstage character (the son's roster fate is his choice). *Fix:
   resolve on the adults' side — the campaign is defeated regardless
   of the roster outcome; the son's choice arrives as one voicemail.*
3. **(Significant, blocker-adjacent)** The docs draw the student line
   in two places: locked 1 says "students"; proposed standard 5 says
   "college-age kids." Pete's continuing-ed students and Lucía's
   nontraditional scholars are 30–50 — the target reader. *Fix: rule
   once that the mechanic covers enrolled undergrads (~under 23) of
   the four colleges, and write it into locked 1.*
4. **(Significant)** 4.1 hinges on the daughter's consent, which
   can't be staged. *Fix: make her position load-bearing as artifact
   from chapter one (a drafted-but-unsent text Renata finds), so the
   ending turns on Renata's choice about what she already knows.*
5. **(Minor)** Student-facing day jobs (Nora's classroom, Gil's
   counter, Dana's ops desk) will brush the line constantly. *Fix:
   bible-listed default "adult rooms" per lead; keep the
   does-a-quoted-text-count question at the top of the must-lock
   list.*

10. **(BLOCKER)** **The Set 1 climax is a vote with no body to hold

    fight is decided by a city council or planning commission; no

    civic institutions to the town census.*
11. **(Significant)** Set 3 has no answer to what "the town" is in a
    metropolis. *Fix: one bible line — "in Set 3, the town is the

12. **(Significant)** The towns exist only as open questions; no
    recurring townsperson exists who is not a lead, a parent, or

13. **(Minor)** The off-season is silent — no doc shows these towns

## Verdict

**Not ready to graduate to a bible — but close, and nothing argues for
re-architecting.** What blocks graduation is absence, not error, in
five clusters, in order:

1. **The finale mechanism** (15, 16) — constrains plants in every
   earlier book; nothing else can lock first.
2. **The series timeline + age/family spreadsheet** (24, 6, 17).
3. **The towns** (8, 9, 10, 11, 12) — locked 3 is an aspiration with
   no artifact yet.
4. **The 2.4 HEA mechanics** (21) — the one place the architecture
   breaks a locked genre promise.
5. **The student-definition ruling** (3, 5) — one paragraph deciding
   whether 2.1 and 4.2 are draftable as premised.

Everything else resolves inside bible drafting. Protect untouched: the
B-story discipline, the no-repeat trope contract, the honest-cost
framing, and premises 3.1 and 4.3 as written.
