## The dialogue question — the most useful finding here

All three cleared the floor. **The percentage is measuring the wrong
thing, and this field proves it.**

**How much of the talk is owned** — speaker turns with no named or
characterized speaker behind them:

| | Dialogue % | Anonymous turns |
|---|---:|---:|
| A | 21.4% | ~15% |
| **B** | **23.6%** | **~26%** |
| C | 22.5% | **~6%** |

**B has the most dialogue in the field and the least owned dialogue in
the field, and those are the same fact.** A quarter of B's talk is one
seventeen-line block of unnamed townspeople reading a picture back to
the reader. That block is where B bought its number.

**How much of the talk causes something** — a line is load-bearing if
the scene would differ without it: **A four, B two, C six.** C's talk
is almost entirely people with names getting things done.

**Recommendation for the protocol:** add a second cheap check beside
the percentage — **what share of dialogue turns have a named or
trade-identified speaker? Set it at 80%.** That one number separates
this field correctly and would have caught B without anyone reading a
word.

*Also worth knowing: all three drafters independently wrote `with
dignity` on Eli. Three blind writers, same joke-attribution. It is the
right joke; it cannot be the room's only one.*

## What C does that the others don't

- **Opens mid-negotiation.** Line 1 is a woman winning an argument.
  No DNF point; the chapter never goes nine lines without someone
  speaking or twenty without a laugh.
- **The best ending in the field by a distance.** It pays off Eli's
  absurd mums purchase *inside the same chapter*, gives the House a
  clock in four words (*"entirely uninformed about November"*), ends
  on Marisol acting, and puts dread and delight in one breath.
- **The side romance carries the genre promise.** In a chapter where
  the leads may not speak, A advances Birdie/Eli as *plot* and B as a
  *joke*; **C advances it as feeling** — *"neither of them stepped
  apart"* — and pays it off in the closing image.
- **It demonstrates SC4 on the page.** *"Whose board? Whose
  trustees?"* answered with *"It's a courthouse question, and the
  courthouse is closed till Monday"* — the obstacle shown to be
  structural, not a misunderstanding.

**Protect in revision:** the peach-man haggle (it buys a character who
returns to be the first voice on the rendering); *"You have confident
arms"* into Marisol's wrench reply; *"neither of them stepped apart"*;
*"Things near him simply began behaving"*; Verna's *"I have buried a
husband, and I have never yet had to bury a house. I don't aim to
start in November"*; and *"Ray, my Saturdays are the best thing I
own"* — a ch 1 callback no other candidate found.

## The field's shared weakness

**Cal is off the page for the chapter's biggest scene in all three.**
The brief said "compressed"; all three read that as "exit the
chapter." The rendering detonates and the man she has been watching
for two chapters is nowhere near it, so the romance thread and the
plot thread never touch.

**Highest-value revision available to the winner:** put Cal at the
edge of the shed when the picture lands, wordless, reading his own
screen, and let her be the only person positioned to see what it does
to him. No dialogue, no rule broken, and it converts watching into
intimacy at the moment the stakes arrive.

## Three things the panel would not decide

1. **The queue motive.** C gives Marisol a *reason* for waving the
   membership flood through — *"a bigger room was a louder room, and a
   louder room was exactly what November required."* The brief's one
   explicit prohibition is that this beat carry no wink. **It converts
   her from careless to strategic**, and if the flood later goes
   wrong, she is complicit rather than unlucky. Arguably *more*
   fair-play compliant; also telegraphs. **Character culpability with
   downstream consequences — rule it on purpose.** The panel's taste,
   deferred: better with the motive cut.
2. **"One year in Ashford."** All three say it, because
   `ch3-brief-v2.md` handed it to them as a stateable fact. **A
   drafting brief is currently ruling canon through the back door.**
   It is consistent with ch 1, so nothing collides — but ratify or
   overrule it deliberately. Two riders: *"A year in town, and…"* is
   now a tic in all three; and C positions her as a market insider,
   which leans against ch 1's *"for the first time in a year she felt
   like part of the weather instead of somebody standing out in it."*
3. **T06, Marisol's tell.** **All three drop it. Second field running.
   Six blind drafters, zero occurrences.** Concrete slot in C, and a
   good one: *"Buying flowers first. Don't cry where the customers can
   see you"* is already the ch 1 shape — a line delivered to a man
   while pointedly not looking at him. Give her that line **smiling at
   a point somewhere past his ear** and the tell survives as a
   *habit*, which is what a tell is. **A THREADS OWED item, not a
   panel preference.**

## Does the earlier panel's calibration still hold?

**Half of it. The half that doesn't is worth reading twice, because it
is this instrument criticizing itself.**

**Reuse:** the no-wink test at the queue (still discriminating —
broken by exactly one candidate per field); the laugh-inside-the-ache
test at the picture beat; the tic warnings. And record this as
evidence the mechanism works: tightening the manifest in the rerun
brief produced **zero manifest inventions** this field, against two in
the last.

**Retire axis (a), "the watching."** The old panel graded *watching
quality* and rewarded the most beautiful passivity available. Read its
own protected list cold and it is **a list of moments where nothing
happens.** The author's rejection — *"nothing really happens"* — is
the note that instrument could not have generated, because its top
axis was built to reward the opposite.

Said plainly, since it is our own instrument: **the panel measured
pleasure correctly inside a plan that could not deliver the book, and
did not say so.** A reader panel reporting "no DNF point, four of four
keep reading tonight" on a field the author then rejects entirely has
found a true thing about the pages and missed the true thing about the
book.

**Replacement axis: not "how well does she watch" but "what does she
cause, and where."** Applied to this field it ranks C first on the
first pass — which is also where the fun, the swoon, the ending and
the shelf placement land it.
