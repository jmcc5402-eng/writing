CANDIDATE A — "The Second Guest Book" (declared card D5)
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

(a) The watching. Confident, never moony — "one eyebrow enjoying itself, the
frank appraisal of a woman who had spent a year remembering she was allowed
to look," and "she awarded the maneuver full marks" is exactly the assigned
posture. The town-as-instrument staging is A's best idea: "Nobody stopped
talking. Nothing so obvious as that. The shed simply developed a direction."
P1: "That's the sentence the whole beat is built on." Debit, P4: "She grades
him like a judge at a fair. I believe she's amused; I don't quite believe
she's hungry." Editor translation: appraisal reads slightly cooler than
appetite. Fix: one body-beat inside the count (B and C both have one to
lend).

(b) Fun. Solid but the lowest laugh density of the three. The keepers: the
away game "seen off that morning like a relative, proudly and with food";
the flyer post "FAFSA night and a gospel sing and a tractor, runs good"; the
father "posting the same photo of the same stadium from four angles, all
four captioned 'we're here'"; Earlene's "From who?" / "Sugar... From
everybody." P2: "I smiled a lot. I laughed out loud twice. In B and C I lost
count."

(c) The picture and the queue. The picture beat has real weight — "Marisol
looked at the lawn and could not place it, because there was nothing left on
it to place" is the smartest single observation of the rendering in any
candidate — and A alone gives the leads a wordless thread across it: "if his
shoulders carried a different set going out than they had coming in, Marisol
was possibly the only one positioned to see it. // She saw it." P1: "That's
the swoon of this candidate. She's already reading him at a distance nobody
else can." One heaviness flag, P2: "'as if the whole market had become a
sickroom' — that word is a hospice word; it tipped past weight for me,"
though the chain-yourself-to-the-porch-rail / "if it could wait until after
supper" laugh arrives on schedule and releases it. The queue: plays light,
mostly invisible — "she did her good deed for the day," "approve, approve,
approve," and the sunglasses-spammer gag keeps it human. No wink detected.
P4's only squint: "feeling like a woman with her town's whole Saturday
riding in her lap" leans a half-step meaningful; survivable.

(d) Voice-match. Clean continuation, no image theft — but the register runs
a shade quieter than chapter one throughout, more elegy than energy on an
away Saturday. P4: "Chapter one was a party. This is the morning after the
party, written by someone still a little tired from it."

(e) Skim/put-down, quoted. Coasting at the open: "What stayed home was the
radio... You just heard it handed along ahead of you, house to house" —
P2: "Pretty. I read it with my eyes half shut and lost nothing." Coasting
again at "The market took the news and went on being a market... Marisol
paid for her zinnias and let the list carry her around the square" —
process, no want in it. No skim-outs, no DNF point; every dip is short and
a joke is never far.

(f) Compliance. **One manifest violation:** "Her son had not made the
travel squad" — the travel-squad outcome is not in the fact manifest, and
Candidate C states the opposite, which proves both invented it. Everything
else clean: no named students (texts and waffles are artifacts), no years,
leads never speak, no surname on the page (she signs "the rest of it"
without the page saying it — nicely done), "Nobody sent it. Everybody
forwarded it" honors the unattributed-sender rule and is funny doing it. No
banned moves.

CANDIDATE B — "Away Saturday" (declared card D6)
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

(a) The watching. The sexiest Marisol on the table, by the author's own
definition — she deploys it: "She was forty-three years old, she knew
exactly what she looked like standing at a flower bucket in this dress, and
she gave the view her full professional attention. Fair, she figured, was
fair." And then she says the register note out loud in character: "I'm not
watching. I'm appraising. There's a difference, and the difference flatters
him." P1: "Screenshot. That's the assignment in dialogue form." The
town-hobby choreography is the funniest telling — sun hats angled for sight
lines, the peanut man stirring "facing the wrong direction, which is to say
the right one," deacons "with the studied blankness of men judging
livestock."

(b) Fun. Highest banter score of the run, and Earlene is at series-best:
"Half this county's been watching that man cross rooms since before your
kids could vote. Pace yourself. It's a distance event." and "Man thinks a
room goes quiet because he oiled the door." P2 read both aloud to her
husband. The zinnia button — "Both oranges. It seemed wrong to make them
compete." — is pure chapter-one DNA without borrowing a single image.

(c) The picture and the queue. Here B stumbles twice, and both stumbles are
on judged axes. The weight is real — "three other voices said *mine too* so
fast it sounded rehearsed, and wasn't, and that was somehow the worst part"
is the single most affecting line about the House in any candidate — but
then B holds the note past the Gilmore line: "The quiet went on past the
point where market quiet usually knows to quit, on and on... buzzing, and
buzzing, and buzzing." No laugh lives inside this ache; the nearest release
("outlasted tougher boards than that one") is a full scene away. P2: "It's
good. It's also the one place in three candidates where the book felt heavy,
and the author's note says that's the one thing it must never feel." Second
stumble: **the queue beat winks, three times** — "shiny profiles wanting in
on the day the town needed all its people" (ties the surge to the crisis),
"It was less deliberation than she'd given the zinnias" (points the camera
straight at the carelessness), "the day's one unambiguous good deed"
(ironizing adjective; the reader hears the other shoe). P3: "I literally
thought 'this will go badly' — which means the chapter told me to think it."
The brief forbids exactly this. All three are line trims, but they are the
brief's one explicit prohibition.

(d) Voice-match. The closest match to chapter one's comic rhythm — "the
swept, satisfied calm of a house the morning after a very good party," mums
"ready to become somebody's entire porch personality," tomatoes "graded, as
far as she could tell, by pride." B is also the only candidate to deliver
Cal's ruled look verbatim-faithful: "Light brown hair the sun was still
working on, a little gray at the temples, none of it looking accidental."
P4: "That last clause is the Redford directive in six words."

(e) Skim/put-down, quoted. Nearly clean. One inhale at "Pinned threads,
patient answers, *FAFSA Night (New Date)*..." — inventory-flavored, rescued
by the bake-sale joke ("the House could be saved one pound cake at a time").
No DNF point; P3 never reached for her phone. Note: "the same as water
declining to argue with a dock post" and "like plants doing something about
the light" skate near the banned simile scaffold without using it — legal,
twice-noticed.

(f) Compliance. The cleanest of the three. Manifest kept exactly (B alone
declines to invent the travel-squad outcome). College words salted right
(FAFSA Night, Parents' Weekend hotel blocks, the board's thread titles). No
students, no years, leads never speak, "Let November come read the sheet"
lands in-chapter, ending is present-tense-of-the-day, not trailer. The only
brief breach is the triple wink in (c) — an execution breach, not a wall
breach.

CANDIDATE C — "The Second Guest Book" (declared card D2, hands and objects)
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

(a) The watching. Appetite, arrived at through hands — "She knew the hands
before she placed the man" is the earliest and most physical wanting in any
candidate, and it pays the chapter-one wrench forward before Cal even
appears: the glove box with "the clean empty spot where a certain crescent
wrench had lived since the divorce. The sunglasses were sitting in it like a
subletter." P1: "That's how you carry a crush in an object. I grinned at a
glove box." The crossing itself is the best-executed version of the
brief's beat: the bushel "flat on one forearm... a grip that made a bushel
of anything look like a hatbox"; "he ran exactly at the speed of Verna,
which no man that size had any business doing gracefully"; the newspaper
doubled twice under the Dillards' short leg, pressed "to hear its new
opinion." P2: "Him walking at the old lady's speed is the single hottest
thing in all three files, and he doesn't say a word. That's the 35-to-45
swoon, right there." Marisol's frame is confident, funny, zero moon:
"watching a capable man cross a room was one of life's few reliable free
amenities. Apparently in Ashford it was municipal."

(b) Fun. Dense and effortless — "the square had all its parking back and
seemed embarrassed about it"; exclamation points "she was not prepared to
defend in court"; tomatoes "in order of self-esteem"; zinnias "in colors
nobody had authorized"; Earlene's "It's been discussed at FAFSA night"
(college word deployed as a punchline — best salt of the run); the name on
the clipboard "in what appeared to be eyeliner."

(c) The picture and the queue — the deciding axis, and C wins it. The
picture's arrival is staged as a physical object: "the phone itself went
walking — held out at arm's length to the next stall, passed across a card
table, angled, tilted, shaded... and where it had already been, people stood
differently. Sacks got set down. Arms got crossed." P3: "I watched the bad
news walk toward her. Best paragraph in the stack." November lands with
full weight — "It had no beginning. It was simply everywhere at once, and
November landed on the whole market in one piece" — and then the Gilmore
release arrives INSIDE the ache, on Verna: "'A lawn,' said Verna Poteat...
'I have a lawn. Nobody ever drove fifty miles to get down on one knee in
it.'" followed by the exactly-calibrated "The laugh that went around the
shed was real, and it did not fix anything, and everyone knew both things."
That is "a laugh lives near every ache" performed to spec. And the queue is
the most invisible of the three: surge attributed to the game weekend
("game weekends did that"), "thumb, thumb, thumb, approve and approve and
welcome aboard... It took four minutes. She felt brisk and useful about it."
No irony, no shadow, no wink. P4: "On reread, knowing what it is, it's
perfect. On first read it's nothing. That's the assignment."

(d) Voice-match. Very close; a half-shade more writerly than chapter one
("the Earlene experience entire," "innocent as a church picnic") but the
laugh-to-lyric ratio stays on the Gilmore side. The signature beat is
Marisol-sexy in B's league: "the full signature, the one with the swagger in
the M that she'd been perfecting since ninth grade and had never once wasted
on a tax form." The clipboard close observes the town through objects the
way chapter one did through hands: "Somebody in this town had stood at this
counter this morning and planned for the moment the jar ran dry."

(e) Skim/put-down, quoted. One coasting stretch: the market inventory ("The
Doss County Curb Market ran Saturday mornings under a long open shed...") —
third telling of the same shed across the stack, though "order of
self-esteem" saves it. P3 flagged the crossing's length — "four seconds at
the Dillards' table got four sentences" — then withdrew the flag: "I didn't
skip any of them, so never mind." No DNF point; the beat everyone came for
starts by the second page and the chapter is never more than a few lines
from a laugh. P4 flags the final clause — "tied by a short string to a town
that had a use for her name" — as a half-step toward announced meaning; the
list-crossing beat above it ("Four for four, a full and honorable
Saturday") already lands the ending.

(f) Compliance. **One manifest violation:** the duffel-bag photo "which in
the native language of walk-ons meant *I made the travel squad*" — outcome
not in the manifest, and Candidate A rules the opposite. **One register
flag:** "the round-shouldered ease of a man who genuinely believed he was
invisible" — "round-shouldered" is the run's only arguably unattractive
adjective on Cal; the sentence survives without it. Otherwise clean: no
students, no years ("ninth grade" is a grade, not a year number), leads
never speak, sender unattributed, ending lands in-chapter.

RANKING
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

**C > B > A.** Split: P1, P2, P3 for C; P4 for B, conceding to C once the
queue wink was read back to her.

Deciding reasons:

1. **The chapter's centerpiece is beat 2, and C is the only candidate that
   lands November with weight AND its Gilmore release in the same breath.**
   Verna's lawn line inside the ache is the register note executed; B holds
   the heavy note alone ("buzzing, and buzzing, and buzzing") and A's
   "sickroom" tips before its joke arrives.
2. **The brief's one explicit prohibition — do not wink at the queue — is
   kept perfectly by C, kept by A, and broken three times by B** ("less
   deliberation than she'd given the zinnias," "unambiguous good deed,"
   "the day the town needed all its people"). On a judged axis, that
   outranks B's otherwise best-in-run compliance.
3. **C's watching is the most rereadable version of "appetite with a raised
   eyebrow"** — it runs through hands and objects (the glove-box subletter,
   the hatbox forearm, Verna's walking speed), which is the same channel
   chapter one's swoon ran through (the cam-lock thumb press), so it
   continues the accepted voice without touching its images.
4. B loses on two line-level breaches, not on quality — its Earlene, its
   Marisol dialogue, and its Cal description are the best of the run and
   graft cleanly (below). A loses on temperature: composed where the series
   weather is energetic; its shoulders-beat is its one irreplaceable gift.

Rule-1 note (reported, deferred): both A and C invent the walk-on's
travel-squad outcome, in opposite directions — proof neither had canon.
The son's season status is plot-adjacent and must be ruled by the author
and written into the bible before any candidate's version ships; our
enjoyment of C's duffel-bag beat is not permission to keep it. Also noted
for the record: B and C independently open Earlene's beat with the
identical "I didn't say anything," and all three reuse chapter one's
Earlene humidity/road-closure register — it is becoming the room's tic;
ration it going forward. This ranking measures pleasure only; it
authorizes nothing.

GRAFTS INTO THE WINNER (C)
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

From B — priority grafts:
1. **The appraising exchange.** C's Earlene beat ends early; extend it with
   B's "I'm not watching. I'm appraising. There's a difference, and the
   difference flatters him," and Earlene's "Pace yourself. It's a distance
   event." It is the sexiest Marisol dialogue of the run and C has the
   exact slot (after "He still has my wrench").
2. **Cal's ruled look.** C never states the canon description; graft B's
   "Light brown hair the sun was still working on, a little gray at the
   temples, none of it looking accidental" into C's crossing — it also
   replaces the "round-shouldered" clause that must go anyway.

From A — priority graft:
3. **The shoulders beat.** Cal is offstage when C's picture lands; insert
   A's beat — Cal at the shed's edge reading the screen, the basket at his
   feet, "if his shoulders carried a different set going out... She saw
   it." It is the chapter's only lead-to-lead thread, it needs no dialogue,
   and it converts C's watching from hobby to intimacy at the exact moment
   the stakes arrive.

From A — optional:
4. **"Nobody sent it. Everybody forwarded it."** — as Earlene dialogue, it
   is a funnier delivery of C's narrated "It had no beginning"; keep one,
   not both.
