## 1. SLOT BIAS

**Wins by slot: A 6 · B 15 · C 10** (base expectation ~10.3 each).

Two eras, two different biases:
- **Ch1–ch16 (17 judgings): C won 9, B 4, A 4.** P(C ≥ 9 of 17 at 1/3) ≈ 7% — noticeable, plausible under chance.
- **Waves 5–8, ch17–ch30 (14 judgings): B won 11** (A took ch19–20, C took ch26). P(B ≥ 11 of 14 at 1/3) ≈ **7 × 10⁻⁴ — about 1 in 1,400.** The tightest run: ch21–ch30, B won 9 of 10 (≈ 4 × 10⁻⁴). Even discounting for the post-hoc window choice and three slots to pick from (multiply by ~6–10), you're still under 1%. **This is not plausibly chance.**

The drafters are blind and identically briefed, so a real effect implicates the harness, and the panels' own notes supply corroborating slot-style signatures no blind process should have:
- **A-slot signature: "quotes the assignment."** Brief-gloss import was the named fault against the A candidate in waves 5, 6, 7, and 8 — ch17 ("imports the brief's own gloss nearly verbatim"), ch22 ("Where B writes the smile, A quotes the assignment"), ch24, ch25, ch28 ("the same fault this panel dinged A-slots for in waves 5 and 6"). The panel itself started citing its own prior A-slot dings — an anchoring loop.
- **C-slot signature (late): lyric jewel-forge that loses** (see §4).
- **B-slot signature (late): "best staged," wins.**

Three correlated slot-styles from supposedly independent blind runs means either (a) launch-order context differences in the harness (the LOG shows drafters always launched A→B→C within a chapter; if the orchestrating session's own context grows between launches, later launches aren't identical), or (b) panel presentation-order effects — the panel always reads A first with the directive freshest (so brief-language is most salient in A) and forms its winner impression mid-sequence. Note ch21 and ch27 both felt obliged to declare the repeating letter-order a coincidence — the panels noticed the pattern themselves.

**Test for 1.2:** the orchestrator randomly permutes candidate labels before staging into the panel folder (permutation logged sealed, unmasked only at retro), and rotates the panel's reading order. If the win-bias and the "quotes the assignment" fault follow the *presented position*, it's a judging artifact; if they follow the *launch order* after shuffling, it's a drafting-harness artifact. One book of shuffled labels settles it.

## 3. THE DECIDING-LINE TAXONOMY — what the panels actually reward

Every stated winner-reason, classified; primary class per judging (n = 31):

1. **Staging of the mandated beat — ~12 of 31.** The single biggest class by far. Panels don't reward *having* the ordered peak; they reward who staged it best: delayed, in motion, in the POV body, arrived-at rather than teleported-to (ch2, 7, 8, 11, 12, 13, 15, 19, 22, 25, 26, 27). The recurring phrasing is literal: "best staged, best delayed, and best landed."
2. **Warmth / felt-not-admired register — ~7 of 31** (ch1, 3, 5, 10, 17, 23, 24). "I admired C. I *enjoyed* B" (ch1) is the archetype; ch24's "warmth can't be grafted in; a crop can" is the doctrine. This is the author's 2026-08-21 pleasure-outranks-checkbox ruling working exactly as ordered.
3. **Series-ledger payoff / echo discipline — ~5 of 31** (ch4, 16, 18, 20, 30). Winners cash prior chapters' plants — and get punished for *reusing images* instead of growing motifs (ch21's "the nothing grown up and gone heavy" won; C's verbatim windows-down echo was dinged in the ch3 rerun).
4. **Wanting/heat escalation — ~4 of 31** (ch6, 9, 14, 21). When the chapter's job is the burn, the hottest compliant version wins, full stop.
5. **POV closeness (worst-scene scoring) — ~3 of 31, but 100% of wave 7–8 verdicts it applied to** (ch26, 28, 29). Once the closeness law arrived, "never leaves the lead" became the tiebreak that decided waves.

And the mirror finding — the most consistent *losing* reason across waves 5–8 is **brief-gloss import**: the assignment's own language surfacing in the prose at the peak. Named as the deciding or near-deciding fault at least eight times (ch17-A, ch22-A, ch24-A, ch25-A, ch27-A and C, ch28-A and B, ch29-C, ch30-B). Panels reward the brief performed; they punish the brief quoted.

## 4. THE REPEAT LOSSES — the graft-donor pattern is real

The suspect is confirmed, but it's the **slot**, not a single card. C-slot, waves 6–8 (ch21–30): **1 win, 7 seconds, 2 thirds**, with the panels explicitly certifying the losses as jewel-rich: ch25 — "produces more graftable sentences than any loser this series has fielded"; ch27 — "loses the chapter and wins the excavation's best lines; the graft package below exists mostly to move them"; ch29 — "owns the deepest single beats of the wave, which is why the graft package is heavy"; ch22 — "beaten, not bad — wins four individual beats and I want them grafted." C carried different cards through that run (D1, D5, D3, D7, D2, D6, D5, D3), so the forge behavior isn't card-caused — which is itself more slot-effect evidence for §1.

At the card level, **D5 is the closest thing to a natural jewel-forge**: 2 wins, 7 seconds, and the "beautiful but cooler" panel profile. Every panel already runs a formal GRAFTS section, and winners routinely shipped with 3–5 transplanted organs (ch1's held-gaze standoff from C, ch2's pocket key from B, ch9's four grafts from B). The pipeline has been quietly *depending* on losers' lines all book. In 1.2, make it deliberate: one slot per chapter briefed and judged as the forge — graft yield is its score, not the win. (The retro-estate-sale run filed alongside this one is mining exactly that inventory.)

4. **Designate a jewel-forge slot in 1.2: one candidate per chapter briefed lyric-dense (D5-family), scored on graft yield, not rank.** ← §4: D5 podium 9/13 with 2 wins; C-slot waves 6–8 at 1-7-2 with four panels certifying the losses graft-rich; every accepted chapter this book already carries 3–5 loser grafts. 1. 31 blind judgings: slot B won 15, including 11 of the last 14 — odds under chance ≈ 1 in 1,400; with A's recurring "quotes the assignment" fault and C's late lyric-loser signature, the harness (launch order or panel reading order) is implicated, not the drafters.
