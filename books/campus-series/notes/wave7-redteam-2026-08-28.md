## Ranked objections

### 1. Ch 28: Boyd's red line dissolves and nobody on the page notices — the win is narrative gravity
`/home/user/writing/books/campus-series/manuscript/ch28.md`, lines 255–296.

Boyd's ultimatum is stated twice as absolute: ch27:82–84 ("The full footprint, or the gift is withdrawn **and the shortfall stands**. Half a gift is no gift.") and again minutes before the vote, ch28:86–87 ("Whole footprint or none of it, and I mean that kindly"). Then an anonymous trustee moves to "accept the gift shy of the Millrow phase," it carries in two lines of procedure with the microphones off — and Boyd *congratulates the board* and announces "The towers go up regardless." His own stated condition is violated on the page and he folds it in the same breath, unremarked by any character, and the "shortfall" that was the ultimatum's teeth is never mentioned again. Either the ultimatum was a bluff (then someone must call it — a bluff nobody names is a plot hole, not a reversal) or the gift is now withdrawn (then the town just lost the money and the room is celebrating anyway).

Compounding it: no trustee has a name, a face, or a stated reason in the entire book's climax. The board is a monolith that gavels, confers off-mic, and produces the compromise. SC2's own test — "can you name the planted effort that earned the rescue, or did the truth just arrive?" — is half-failed here: the *restoration* is earned (jar, report, stamp, twelve names in the second row — all planted, all paid), but the *vote* just arrives. And the trust's clinching announcement (ch28:267–275) is delivered by "a woman... in the third row" — the payoff of the whole pledge thread voiced by a character with no name, against SC2's letter ("saved by **named** community effort"). This is a finding, not a pass: a good chapter that lets the truth arrive by gravity cheapens SC2 at the exact moment the book most needs it honored.

This is the wave's single weakest page: **ch28, the vote, lines ~255–296.** The whole book aims at that page and it is the least dramatized page in the wave.

## The single highest-leverage fix (objection 1)
**Give the vote one face and make the bluff get called on the page.** One named trustee — someone the town can place, leaning Boyd going in — who, after Cal's testimony, names the thing out loud on a live mic: the ultimatum is a donor's bluff, and the board's compromise motion is the dare ("If half a gift is no gift, Mr. Boyd is free to take his whole one home"). Then Boyd's "The towers go up regardless" becomes a *visible, costed fold* instead of an unremarked contradiction, the board's flip acquires a stated reason instead of gravity, and the anonymous-monolith problem dies in the same stroke. Give the trust's announcement to that trustee's opposite number or to a named trust officer while you're in the scene, and objection 1 closes end to end for roughly a page of new material.

**Single sentence I'd cut first:** ch27:262 — "It was the longest inch in the county tonight" (and let "they had built it themselves, board by board..." stand or fall with it). The inch is the wave's finest image; that sentence walks on stage and explains it.
