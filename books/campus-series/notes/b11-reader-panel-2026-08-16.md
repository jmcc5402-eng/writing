## The headline

**The rebuild is real, not cosmetic.** Eleven beats the panel would
reread, a town they want to live in, and — the specific thing this
instrument was chartered to check — **the midpoint failure is
genuinely gone.** Ch 15 is not a procedure happening to Marisol; she
is the subject of every clause, the coup is partly her own doing, and
the chapter ends on a want with a direction. Ch 16 pays it.

Three things qualify that.

1. **The dead zone reopened in a worse position: ch 24–26.**
2. **The 2.1 repair pass left fingerprints** — at ch 14, 15, 17, 18
   the fix was applied as *added lines*, which is the sprinkle
   pattern.
3. **The story read and the frozen outline have drifted, and the
   author's approved register lives mostly in the document that is
   NOT canon.** Drafters get the outline.

## 1. Would genuinely lose a reader

### F1 — Three consecutive lead-apart chapters, straight after the peak

The 2026-08-13 audit killed a five-chapter dead zone at 11–15. A
three-chapter one opened at **24, 25, 26**, and it is worse-positioned
than the one that was repaired. The old gap *preceded* the first kiss
and therefore powered it — absence before a payoff is fuel. This one
*follows* the romance peak at 22–23. Absence after a payoff is drift.

Three chapters is exactly the audit's stated tolerance, **so the
finding is not the count, it is the position.** No instrument flagged
it because the target curve legitimately wants tension high at 24–26,
and the meter chart obediently reads ROMANCE 3/4/3 there as design. A
reader does not experience "the tension movement." She experiences:
*they finally got together, and now I've read three chapters where
they're not in the same room.*

**The fix is already inside the text and costs no new scene.** Ch 25
contains: *"the watermarked pages — DRAFT, UNSIGNED, every sheet,
**photographed with Cal's consent**."* Somewhere between 24 and 25
these two people met in a room, put the evidence of his ruin on a
table, and he told her to publish it. **That scene is currently
happening in a subordinate clause.** Stage it and the dead zone
becomes 24 and 26 — non-consecutive — and the book gains a beat where
he hands her the thing that can destroy him.

### F2 — The third step is never used by the couple

The book's central romantic object is a porch tread worn pale by a
hundred years of proposals: planted ch 5, revisited by Verna at 15 and
22. **Neither lead ever stands on it with the other.** Marisol stands
on it alone in ch 5 and tells no one. They dance on the porch at 22;
the kiss at 23 is at her gate under a live oak. The fair-play table
pays P13 thematically (20, 22, 28) and never romantically.

> P4: *"The whole town gets the step. The two people I read 300 pages
> for get a gate."*

No engagement required — standard 7 is satisfied by HFN and forcing a
proposal would fight the resolution discipline. It requires them on it
together, **once.**

### F3 — Nobody ever says it

Across 30 chapters neither lead makes a verbal declaration to the
other. He says one sentence at a podium about who was married on that
porch — *to a room.* She yields her time — *to a room.* He hands her a
mug, "which is his entire love language."

The restraint is the best idea in the characterization and should not
be removed. It needs exactly one release valve, and the book has none.

> P1: *"I loved that he can't say it. I would have loved one moment
> where he does."*

**One change closes F2, F3 and the weakest ending in the book:** put
them on the third step at ch 30, under scaffolding, and let him say
one clumsy sentence.

## 2. Would cost a reading session or a sample sale

### F4 — Ch 4 is the sample-killer; ch 18 is the nightstand chapter

**No DNF point.** The panel is held by ch 5 and the ch 9–10–11 run,
and by ch 11 all four readers are hooked. That is load-bearing, not
flattery: it means everything before ch 5 does the DNF work alone.

**Ch 4** sits at 13% — inside P3's abandon window — and contains no
Marisol, no want, no touch, a charming antagonist explaining money,
and a PR man made of greens powder.

> P3: *"I bought a romance and chapter four is a budget meeting with a
> dog in it."*

She doesn't close the book; she skips to ch 5 and ch 5 saves the sale.
**But a sample that ends at ch 4 does not convert.** Fix: Marisol
needn't be in it — Cal needs to want something other than to be left
alone. One beat where he catches himself checking whether the woman
with the wrench is in the yard while Boyd talks converts the chapter
from arithmetic to arithmetic-with-a-man-in-it.

**Ch 18** is where P2 puts the book down for the night: a transcript
taped in a break room, a deadline, a yard-fence conversation about
certifying officials of equal standing. Its only heat is the Ratchet
photo in the last sentence. Fix: move the photo off the last line and
let it *interrupt* the engineer conversation, so the wanting is inside
the procedure rather than appended to it.

### F5 — Register drift: the warm document is not the canonical one

**Where the two documents diverge, the outline is the colder, more
procedural one. The author is reading the warm one. Drafters get the
cold one.**

In the story read and **not** in the frozen outline: "a dress that has
opinions" (¶6); "the best she has looked all year and half the bar
notices" and the seltzer cooler (¶11); wine night on the porch rail
(¶17); the delivery app that has given up (¶15).

Drift runs both ways: ch 15's "which step to get engaged on" and the
entire RH2 plant exist only in the outline, and the story read clears
Tunk Ferrell at ¶25 of an accusation it never stages — **reproducing
the exact fair-play gap the 2.1 pass repaired.**

Three specific outline-side losses:

- **Ch 6**, the first-spark chapter, contains no physical wanting at
  all as a drafter will receive it.
- **Ch 11** buries the best ending in the book under the Tunk Ferrell
  red-herring apparatus, which the story read doesn't carry.
- **Ch 13** ends on a woman annotating a notebook; the story read ends
  it on the chill of the neighbours posting through Sunday dinner,
  which is better.

**Recommend a reconciliation pass at the frozen rung before any
drafter is briefed.**

### F6 — Card C5: chapters hand their last beat to a plot artifact

Scored across all 30: **20 A-grade endings, 6 B, 4 C.** Strong. But
the four C's cluster on one pattern.

**Three of the book's biggest romantic chapters end on machinery.**
Ch 6 (first spark) ends on a vendor credit. Ch 20 (the grief said
whole, the near-miss) ends on a thesis. Ch 21 (under the home stands)
ends on a deleted post. The swoon happens mid-chapter and the
machinery gets the door. A reader closes a chapter holding whatever
was last, and here she is repeatedly left holding a document.

Also: ch 16, 17 and 20 end on **thesis statements** a drafter must not
inherit ("The conflict survives the honesty. That's the book.").

**Ch 28 — the climax's last beat belongs to the villain.** The reader
waits 93% of the book for "I yield my time" and the podium signature,
and the final image is Boyd moving his next demolition up the
calendar. *The panel defers on content* — the Resolution discipline
says "Boyd unbeaten, House stayed not saved, the fight handed forward,"
and that is design. The finding is **order inside the chapter**, which
costs nothing: let Boyd move mid-chapter, in front of them, and end on
Cal and Marisol in that gym. It also flags gently against STANDARDS 22
(severability — dangling threads read as texture, never promise): as
placed, this lands closer to a promise.

**Ch 30 — the last line of a romance novel is a building metaphor.**
*"Good bones. The wiring's getting fixed."* Clever, pays P3, and it is
**the exact note the author objected to in his original ruling,
arriving at the very last moment of the book.** The story read's
version of the same chapter — *"The porch light stays on late. The
town, for once, minds its business — for almost an hour."* — is
warmer, funnier, ends on people, and is the approved register. Better
still: see F2/F3.

### F7 — Wine night is a noun, not a scene

The modern calibration names it first: *"ladies' wine night, alcoholic
seltzers, somebody overly focused on supplements, a DoorDash that gets
delayed."* In the frozen outline wine night appears **once**, as a
noun, in ch 25. It is never staged.

Consequence: **Marisol has no female friend on the page for thirty
chapters.** She has an oracle two generations up (Earlene), a
side-romance lead (Birdie), and twelve thousand strangers. For a
43-year-old divorced heroine written for a 35–45 reader that is the
most conspicuous absence in the book, and it is a FUN and HEART
absence at once. Cheapest fix: ch 7's board-reading skim becomes wine
night at her kitchen table, and ch 25's war room then pays off a room
we have already been in.

Related: **the modern calibration reads as a checklist, not a
texture.** Each of the author's four named examples appears exactly
once or zero times in the canonical document.

### F8 — Ch 23 needs re-deriving against the now-RULED standard 9

Three heat beats (16, 21, 23), none before the 50% mark, in a book
whose ruled ceiling is "everything up to the first piece of clothing
on the ground is on the page." Ch 23 currently reads: unhurried kiss
at the gate → cut to Sunday brunch. **That is the cut without the
approach.** The closed-door reader is not being denied the act; she
expects that. She is being denied the walk up to it, which is the part
standard 9 explicitly promises. Ch 23's marker still reads
`[HEAT 3 — door per TK ladder]` and the ladder is no longer TK.

## 4. The sprinkle check — the question the charter exists to answer

**Verdict: the rebuild is NOT banter sprayed over a procedural spine.
But the 2.1 repair pass was, in two of its five repairs.** The
re-dream (rev 2) was real work; the gate-response pass partly was not.

The 2.1 header states its own repairs verbatim: *"one line of wanting
each at 17–18."* METERS operating rule 2 says *"the fix happens by
re-dreaming the scene, never by nudging numbers."* A line of wanting
added to raise a sagging romance score **is** nudging the number — with
prose instead of arithmetic.

- **Ch 18's patch works and is load-bearing.** "wants to call her the
  way a man wants coffee at four a.m." plus the photo answered in nine
  seconds is the only reason ch 18 has a pulse. Note the irony: the
  patch is better than the chapter it was patching.
- **Ch 17's patch is decorative** and would survive being cut. It is
  stated, not dramatized, and competes with the chapter's real event.
  A reader would rather see him *fail* to find her across the
  boxwoods before she picks up the mic.
- **Ch 14 splits** — Ray's box load-bearing, Tick's pontoon pure
  decoration.
- **Ch 15's patch is load-bearing** and among the best additions in
  the pass.

## 5. What to protect in revision

**Eleven swoon beats**, named so a reviser knows what not to touch:
ch 9 ("he lets her be wrong so gently she wants to bet again" / "that
night she sets her alarm on purpose"); ch 10 ("She leaves the zinnias
on the porch rail. He waters them."); ch 11 (the look across the bar,
and the jukebox unplugged for an hour that nobody has noticed); ch 16
(the kiss in diesel and WD-40); ch 18 (the Ratchet photo, nine
seconds); ch 21 ("not touching harder than she has ever not touched
anyone"); ch 22 (crossing the porch in front of the whole town, the
fiddler slowing the tune unasked); ch 23 (the cap off; "asks whether
her friend would like his number"); ch 27 (the mug going cold on the
rail — it pays ch 9 and it hurts); ch 28 ("I yield my time to the
director of facilities"); ch 30 (the mug handed over unasked, "which
is his entire love language, and by now she is fluent").

**The inventory is strong. The book does not need more swoon — it
needs it redistributed.** Nine of eleven land at or after ch 16.
Before the midpoint there is exactly one swoon run — ch 9–10–11 —
between two deserts: **ch 1–8 (only ch 5 qualifies) and ch 12–15
(none).** A reader sampling the first 20% gets one great chapter and
one great look. She should get two.

**Protect above all:** ch 9–10–11 (carrying the entire first half
alone), ch 22, ch 23, ch 27's cold mug, and **Earlene, entire** — the
best-written character in the book and a genuine buy-the-next-one
asset.

## 6. The genre contract

**Paid, and paid well.** SC1 is outstanding — Delmar's smoke as the
book's weather, the Mule out of glasses, the Liars' Table as a press
release. SC2 is itemized and honest: the rescue is community effort,
no check from a stranger, and the winter answer is *found* by watching
a crew work. **SC4 is the strongest thing here** — they are fully
honest from ch 16 and still structurally opposed to ch 28, and ch 27
contains zero misunderstanding. A reader burned by third-act phone
calls will notice and say so in a review.

**Where the panel's findings collide with a wall, it defers.** The
largest: it would love the pen to stop being the engine, and it cannot
ask for that. SC4 is locked and the signature IS the load-bearing
obstacle — "the pen is the man" is why this conflict survives honesty
for thirteen chapters. **The finding is never "remove the document."**
It is only what the camera is holding when the chapter ends, and
whether anybody wants anybody while it happens.

## 7. Buy the next one

**Yes — four of four, and P1 preorders at ch 22.**

But be clear what she is buying: **Ashford and Earlene, not a couple.**
A week later she remembers the Mule running out of glasses, the third
step, "he waters them," Earlene on the Chamber of Commerce, the zinnia
wagon towed at parade speed. She remembers the town's *rooms* before
she remembers the word "Ashford" — SC1 working as designed, and a mild
note that the town's name does less work than the town's furniture.

**The mechanism that sells Book Two is the side pair.** Birdie × Eli
graduating into the ch-1 cameo slot converts the series signature from
bait into a dividend. Protect that.

**The risk to the preorder** is the hook the book ends on. If Book Two
reads as *another building, another signature, another fight with
Boyd*, the second purchase is the last one. `energy-bank.md` already
has the answer — the rivalry-week bonfire, the tornado-watch night,
the Millrow Burn — and none of them is a document. **Make Book Two's
premise legible as something other than a second condemnation before
Book One's back matter goes out.**
