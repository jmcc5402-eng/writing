# SUPERFAN REVIEWER — Wave 7 (ch25–28), Ashford Book 1.1, pre-acceptance read

Read staged: ch25 "Receipts," ch26 "Wind in the Upper Deck," ch27 "What It Costs," ch28 "I Yield My Time." Read against the season I've been living in since August and against the book's own promise (closed door, swoony, small-town, 35–45, HEA — STANDARDS.md items 7, 9, 12). No variance card drawn (deck is TK), noted per instructions.

Short version for the author before she reads it herself: this wave is why I preordered. Chapter 28 is the best chapter of the book and it's not close. I have one loud gripe and two watch-items, and the gripe is going to be in every three-star review, so hear it now.

## 1. STAR MATH — the wave as a chunk of the book

**70% five stars** — "I yield my time to the director of facilities" and the podium signature made a grown woman put her Kindle down and walk around the kitchen. **19% four stars** — loved everything, dinged it one star because the climax week of a romance contains zero kisses. **8% three stars** — "beautiful, but I attended more municipal meetings than dates." **3% one-two stars** — bought "romance," got watermark forensics and a trustees vote; these people were never going to be happy and one of them will misspell Marisol.

Wave rating from me personally: **5 stars, and it earns the book's 5 back after the ch24 gut-punch.**

## 2. THE REVIEWS

**★★★★★ — "Read the 1 a.m. chapter at 1 a.m., no regrets" — BarnDoorBecky**
"He put one hand flat on the stack and slid the whole of it across the bench to her side. 'Publish it,' he said. 'All of it. Me too.'" ME TOO. I gasped. This man hands her his entire life like it's a brake job and the book KNOWS that's the whole point — "The nerve of it never showed. It went into making those two motions look easy." And then chapter 28. She gets her three minutes at the podium — the woman who "had never once handed a floor away" — and she gives them to HIM. Both of their sad noble sacrifice offers from the night before, cancelled at once, in public, with a pen. He signs the report OUT LOUD. "It's the soundest building in this county, and I was married on its porch." I am not okay. Also Ratchet the dog lying across both their feet is the best character in fiction. Five stars, would rate the pen separately if I could.

**★★★☆☆ — "I wanted to love this (and mostly did) but WHERE IS THE KISS" — JenReadsAtRedLights**
Hear me out. I don't need spice — I chose this book BECAUSE it's the sweet kind. But they kissed in the shop back in chapter 16. They're together. And then in the four biggest chapters of the whole story I get: a shoulder lean at 2 a.m., a hand that stops "an inch short" on a folder, and a pen handoff. The book even teases me — the town invents "a kiss on the square that had not happened there yet." I KNOW, book. I noticed. Closed door means the door closes on something. The vote carries, the casseroles come uncovered, half the town is on its feet, and these two stand next to each other like coworkers. If that square kiss lands in the last chapters I'll come back and add a star. If it doesn't, I'm dropping to two and writing a longer version of this.

## 4. THE GRIPE, RANKED

1. **No kiss anywhere in the wave — costs a full star across the three/four-star band.** Ch25 shoulder lean, ch27 the inch-short hand, ch28 a pen. All three are gorgeous ON PURPOSE, and I understand the inch is the point in ch27 — but the climax chapter ends with the vote won, the town on its feet, and nothing for the couple but standing adjacent. The book itself plants "a kiss on the square that had not happened there yet" (ch25, last section). That is now a written promise with my name on the IOU. If ch29–32 pay it, this gripe evaporates retroactively. If the book ends without it, it's the one-gripe in every "wanted to love it" review. (For the record: this is under-delivering against the closed-door line, not over — nothing here brushes the bright line.)

2. **Boyd walks out unbeaten and escalating — costs nothing NOW, costs two stars at the end if unpaid.** Ch28 resolves the House and visibly does not resolve Boyd or Millrow ("The phase is tabled" is a tabling, not a win; the demolition survey call is a new clock). Mid-book that's delicious. But the book's own packaging promise is villain-beaten-on-page and no debt a reader can feel (STANDARDS 22, SC2). Flagging as the single biggest thing the remaining chapters owe.

3. **"[TK: which two — never named]" in ch25 (the two allies who quit loudly).** As staged, there's a literal bracket in the manuscript. And even named, "two allies quit" with zero identity reads as a thing I'm supposed to go back and look for. Reader-me went back and looked. Nothing was there. Cheap out or future plant — either way, as staged it's a snag.

4. **Kat's bag — half a star of fan irritation if it's nothing.** She keeps it on the bench all morning "and never opened it," orders a pecan waffle and never eats it. Chekhov's tote. The group WILL theorize. If the bag is just a bag, that sentence taught me to expect something and then didn't deliver.

5. **The middle of ch25 runs long for the skimmers — costs a star only in the two-star band.** The screenshot-triage stretch (roughly the Aisha deal-out through the reply-time chart) is where the DNF lives. I personally ate it up — Marisol debunking astroturf with bookkeeper energy IS the character — but it's the only stretch in four chapters where a tired reader checks the percentage bar.

6. **Ratchet skips the vote — a quarter star of pure comment-section noise, and I said I'd be 10% unfair.** The dog anchors ch25 and ch26, then vanishes for the two hardest chapters. Yes, you don't bring a dog to a trustees meeting. The Facebook group will still ask where the dog was.

What's NOT here, to the author's enormous credit, because I was braced for all three: no third-act breakup, no miscommunication — ch27 is the opposite, two people saying every hard thing out loud ("Say what it costs you" used twice, once in each direction, is the best structural swoon of the wave, and no, I don't get to say "structural," fine: it's the same dare traded back, and it wrecked me) — and no rich stranger's check saving anything. The House gets saved with jars, letters, a stamp from nobody's man, and a signature. Promise kept.

## 5. THE VERDICT

Preorder-worthy, and I don't say that at chapter 28 of anything. I stayed up with the 1 a.m. chapter and felt smug about it; I read the trustees meeting with my hand over my mouth in a way no zoning session has any right to produce; the cold mug on the porch rail hurt in the exact way I pay for it to hurt — sad because they're both being brave, not because anybody's being stupid. The dark chapter hurts right. The climax pays for twenty-seven chapters of a quiet man's quiet with one signature read into a microphone. All this wave owes me now is a kiss on the square with the whole county watching and Rex Boyd's polite head on a plate — and if the last chapters deliver those, book 1.1 is a 55-45 five-star split at retail and I'm buying 1.2 the night it lists, full price, no terms. I've paid it once. I'd pay it again.
