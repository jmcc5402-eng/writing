## 1. RANKING: **C > B > A**

**C wins** (`/home/user/writing/books/campus-series/drafts/2026-08-21/ch9-candidate-C.md`). This is the candidate where all four of us stopped being a panel and were just reading. The deciding line is the wrench beat: *"I know what I've had since the tent."* — followed by *"one full beat, and then another, long past where either of them could file it under hardware."* That's Cal confessing, without paying anything, that the debt was never about a tool — and her comeback, *"Be thorough faster,"* is the line the buyer-of-four screenshots and texts. C also has the deepest ache of the three (*"she had still owned a husband, technically, and even then she'd bought the thermos"* — the reviewer underlined that twice), the best unspoken notice (*"his eyes went once over her hair, down out of its knot for the first time in their acquaintance, and came back to the street without filing a report"* — a direct payoff of ch 8's loose-strand motif), and the strongest last page: *"Four-fifteen was a ridiculous hour to want something. She turned off the lamp and wanted it anyway."* The wanting is explicit, escalating, and hers. It banks exactly the gesture ch 11 will spend — *"found him already turned, already looking"* is the Blind Mule doorway look, pre-funded. One honest reader note: the 10 p.m. reader took ~45 lines to reach the smoke because the chapter opens inside the parents' group; she didn't skim it — the four a.m. mother (*"defend it like a casserole dish"*) is her own life — but it is the only stretch where the first private couple scene isn't yet the couple. Editor translation: the group opener earns its keep as ache-context and can be trimmed by a few lines, not cut.

**B second** (`ch9-candidate-B.md`). B has the best flirt *machinery* of the three and the single best laugh: *"So I lost on scheduling." / "You lost on Thursday."* — all four of us laughed out loud. Its stakes are genius in miniature — *"Loser pours." / "Poured. Past tense. You already poured." / "Next time," he said* — the bet itself manufactures the next morning. And it contains the thesis sentence of this whole slow burn: *"and she found she'd rather be owed than paid."* The empty-nest texture is precisely this reader (*"two doors open down the hall on two made beds"*; *"the first air in months that asked to be worn instead of survived"*). Why it loses: its peaks are slightly lower than C's — the wrench beat stays gentle (*"So do I," Cal said, and that was all he said*) where C's turns the room warm — and it has visible seams: the double tag at lines 264–266 (*"'She came with the arrangement,' Cal said. / 'You don't have to run off,' Cal said."*) reads like a drafting artifact, no chorus member ever appears on the page (Ray is dialogue-only; the wave gate asks for a named chorus in every chapter), and its carillon test runs at five where A and C both put it at six ([CHECK: no canon hour found in town-ashford.md §2]).

**A third** (`ch9-candidate-A.md`). A is the most beautiful of the three and the coolest, and per the VISION rule that ordering is fatal. Its litany is the best-written passage in the batch — *"Streetlights let go at five forty, all together, like they took a vote and it carried"* — and its ache paragraph is arguably the finest (*"It had been months since anybody had simply handed her something warm and let her alone inside it. Her eyes went hot for exactly one second, which the dark, an old friend of hers, kept."*). It even plays the C1 ration as a beat on purpose: *"'I'll write that down,' he said, and did not smile, and she heard exactly where the smile would have gone anyway"* — clever, ration-legal, and the reviewer admired it. But the reader's blunt version: "I loved the town and liked the man." Between the glance and her departure the couple current plateaus; the wrench beat is the lightest of the three — *"He's aware," Cal said* — a wink, not a pull; and the appeal beat (*"Weathered-golden was the phrase her mind produced"*) reads like the standard being filed rather than felt. Editor translation: A pleases; it doesn't yearn. In the book's one private-scene slot before two low-contact chapters, pleasing isn't enough. Its litany couplet above is the one A line worth an author's-choice steal — flagged here, kept out of the formal graft list, which is runner-up only.
