# Campus Series — The Chapter Meters

**Status: PROPOSED — author ratification via PR.** The romance meter
and its target curve are author-stated (PR #59 comment, 2026-08-14);
the other four meters and the operating rules are proposed around it.

## What this is

Five meters, each scored 0–10 for every chapter, at every rung —
outline first, prose later, revision again. The scores are cheap,
repeatable, and comparable across revisions, which is the point:
"is this book still about romance?" stops being a debate and
becomes a chart. Per the dreamer coda, **meters inspect; they never
generate** — see the operating rules.

## The five meters

1. **ROMANCE** *(author-stated).* The chapter's romantic charge:
   longing, closeness, contact, movement between people falling in
   love. The mains carry it; side romances feed it (that's where
   the early ups and downs come from — the author's design).
   *Anchor: 2 = the couple exists somewhere offstage; 8 = a beat
   the reader rereads.*
2. **FUN.** The 80s-college-movie energy — banter, appetite, event
   joy, the town's delight in itself. The register note's meter
   form. *Anchor: 2 = procedure on the page; 8 = a scene that makes
   the reader grin.*
3. **HEART.** The ache — longing, grief with weight, the
   lump-in-the-throat line. Distinct from ROMANCE (heat/closeness)
   and from FUN; this is what the 35–45 reader stays for.
   *Anchor: 2 = feelings reported; 8 = a line that costs something.*
4. **TENSION.** The page-pull — threat, secrets, clocks, can't-
   stop-now. *Anchor: 2 = nothing at risk this chapter; 8 = the
   reader ignores her stop.*
5. **TOWN.** Ashford delivered — rooms, seasons, chorus, the
   wish-I-lived-there factor (SC1 in meter form). *Anchor: 2 =
   could be any town; 8 = the town is a character in the scene.*

## Book 1 target curves (the author's shape, PR #59 comment)

- **ROMANCE:** waves early (4–7, driven by the leads AND the side
  romance), climbing through the middle; **super high around 75%**
  (9–10); **a dip by 90%** (2–4 — the dark moment); **skyrockets at
  the end** (10).
- **TENSION:** rises in waves, and runs INVERSE to romance at the
  90% dip — when the romance line falls, tension must be at its
  maximum; both resolve together at the end.
- **FUN:** a floor, not a curve — never below 4 for more than one
  consecutive chapter; event chapters (game day, festival) 8+.
- **HEART:** spikes at the vulnerability beats and the dark moment;
  at least three 8+ chapters per book, never adjacent to each
  other.
- **TOWN:** never below 4 (locked standard 3's meter form); 8+ in
  each season's signature-event chapter.

## Operating rules

1. **Blind to the dreamer.** Writers and outliners NEVER see the
   meters or targets while working — a chapter written to score is
   a flattened chapter (the same reason drafting is blind to
   neighboring chapters). Scoring runs after, by a separate
   instrument run.
2. **Deviations are findings, not autofixes.** A sagging line goes
   to the editor and the romance-reader panel as a numbered
   finding; the fix happens by re-dreaming the scene, never by
   nudging numbers.
3. **Scored at every rung.** Outline entries now; prose chapters
   later; every revision re-scored so drift shows as a diff between
   charts.
4. **Charts ship with every outline/adoption PR** — a sparkline
   row per meter, so the author sees the shape of the book at a
   glance before reading a word.
5. **Target curves are per-book, declared at premise time**, using
   Book 1's as the template; a book may vary its targets with one
   recorded line (warnings, not walls).
6. **The walls outrank the meters.** HEA, consent, R1–R2, fair
   play are not negotiable for a better-looking line.
