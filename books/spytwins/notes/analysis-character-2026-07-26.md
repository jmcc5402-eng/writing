# Spytwins Book 1 — Character Analysis (ch1–4 focus)

_Character analyst report, 2026-07-26. One of three top-down analyses of
`manuscripts/book-01-hawaii/manuscript.md`, read against
`characters/characters.md`, `concept/premise.md`,
`series-bible/story-engine.md`, and verified against — not deferred to —

## 1. Are the twins distinguishable on the page in ch1–4?

**By props: yes, cleanly.** The prop split is the best-executed character
work in the opening. Amanda: notebook (sketching petroglyphs in Ch1 within
minutes of arrival, Ch2, Ch3, doodling in margins and "under the table" in
Ch4) plus soccer ball (Ch2, with the Coach Chen / "powerful left foot"
backstory from canon rendered accurately). Andrew: tablet (Ch2 "Andrew
clutched his tablet, already plotting their route to the nearest
electronics store where he'd spotted some cool drones in the window"; Ch3
archives and photos; the Jenny/Jacob call arrives on *his* tablet) plus the
canon phone-longing, used well in Ch2: "We wouldn't be in such a rush if
Mom and Dad gave us phones."

One canon drift breaks the split: Ch4, "Amanda sat at a desk and fired up
her tablet." The tablet is Andrew's — one of only two props separating the
twins. The critique flagged this; confirmed on the page.

**By tells: half.** Canon gives Amanda a lip-bite ("bites her lip when
she's working a tricky problem or feels uncertain") and Andrew a hair/
cowlick tell ("plays with his hair / smooths his cowlick when nervous").

- Amanda's lip-bite appears once in ch1–4 (Ch3, fishhook match: "She bit
  her lip, a habit that always showed up when she was solving a
  particularly tricky math problem") — correctly deployed, correctly
  glossed. Book-wide: Ch3, Ch6, Ch9. Fine.
- Andrew's tell appears **zero times in ch1–4**. Grep-verified: the only
  occurrence in the whole book is Ch10 ("trying to smooth down his
  cowlick... he always played with his hair when he was nervous"). So the
  Ch10 gloss "he always played with his hair" pays off a habit the reader
  has never once seen. Ch1–4 offer several natural slots (first-day
  nerves, the staff panic in Ch3, lying by omission to Mrs. Kalani).
- A de facto third tell has crept in for Amanda: "eyes lit up," twice in
  ch1–4 alone, both with the same gloss ("her eyes lighting up the way
  they always did when she encountered a new puzzle," Ch2; again Ch3). It
  dilutes the canonical tell and should be cut or varied.

**By thinking style: partially in narration, weakly in dialogue.** Canon:
"Amanda is decisive and quick; Andrew is careful and curious... she finds
the pattern, he finds the proof." The narration honors this more than the
critique credits: Amanda declines Mrs. Kalani's tour and sets the pace in
Ch2 (decisive); Andrew lingers at the ukulele player, asks "What's
lilikoi?", asks the drone's model number, asks "why would someone carve
the exact pattern...?" and "how old are these carvings anyway?" (curious,
question-first). That is a real, if thin, cognitive split.

Dialogue is where it collapses. Strip the tags and most twin lines are
interchangeable exposition ("Look at this" belongs to both). The Ch2
tour-decline speech is entirely untagged — the reader cannot tell which
twin turns Mrs. Kalani down, in a scene that matters later (it feeds the
Ch3 "seen it just yesterday" continuity error the critique found). The
critique's tag-stripping complaint was aimed at Ch10; it applies to ch1–4
dialogue too, just less severely.

## 2. Andrew's agency — the deduction ledger

Every cognitive beat in ch1–4, by owner:

| Ch | Beat | Type | Owner |
|----|------|------|-------|
| 1 | Spots the property-line petroglyphs (via Blazer) | notice | Amanda |
| 2 | Finds the second petroglyph site ("stopped in his tracks") | notice | Andrew |
| 2 | "these symbols might be pieces of a bigger puzzle" | **inference** | Amanda |
| 3 | Archive lookup: same symbols "across the island," "five hundred years ago" | research | Andrew |
| 3 | Raises the security question ("The security system here is really good... motion sensors") | question | Andrew |
| 3 | Pulls up fishhook photo (enabling evidence) | research | Andrew |
| 3 | Petroglyph = fishhook match | **inference** | Amanda |
| 3 | Decision to withhold from Mrs. Kalani; mutes the call; return plan from Uncle Jacob's hint | judgment | Amanda |
| 3 | Spots footprints in the mud | notice | Amanda |
| 4 | Spiral = ceremonial bowl match (made silently) | **inference** | Amanda |
| 4 | Spots the faded map behind the filing cabinet | notice | Amanda |
| 4 | Recognizes fishhook + bowl symbols on the map | recognition | Andrew |
| 4 | Map = lava tubes | **inference** | Amanda |
| 4 | Dinner security question that triggers Mom's slip | question | Andrew |

**Verdict: the critique's claim is confirmed for ch1–4, with two
nuances.** All four inferential leaps are Amanda's. Andrew's contributions
are notices, lookups, and questions — valuable, but the draft never
converts them into a deduction. Worse, his one recognition beat (Ch4 map
symbols) is pre-empted: "Before Amanda could tell the boys about her
discovery" — Amanda has already made the spiral–bowl match silently, so
Andrew's moment is an echo the reader knows is second place.

The nuances: (a) Andrew *owns the security-method thread* — Ch3 motion
sensors, the Ch4 dinner question, and later the Ch9 chat with Kekoa where
he half-connects it himself ("Our mom was talking about security cameras
having blind spots..."). The thread pays off in Ch11 (key card, dead
zones) but Andrew is never allowed to close it. (b) Book-wide, "zero
deductions" slightly overstates: Andrew finds the "Dr. M. Lee" name and
runs the identification in Ch7, and argues the shipping-urgency logic. But
inside ch1–4, the critique is right on the page.

**The drone.** Grep-verified, every mention: Ch1 Andrew admires Kekoa's
Mavic 4 Pro ("Cool drone!... What model is it?") and leaves "still eyeing
the drone"; Ch2 drones in a shop window; Ch7 Kekoa "practicing with his
drone"; Ch12 Kekoa asks "One last flight?" of Andrew, "who had become
quite skilled at piloting over the past months," and "Together they
launched the drone." Andrew never once flies solo on the page, is never
shown learning, and the drone contributes nothing to the case. The skills
ladder's "Drone piloting — established in Book 1 (via Kekoa)" is not true
of this draft; Ch12 asserts the skill retroactively. This also violates
the engine's own rule of thumb: "the skill that solves the case should be
*set up earlier in the same book*" — here the skill neither solves
anything nor is set up. Confirmed exactly as the critique claims.

Also unused from Andrew's canon sheet in ch1–4 (and the book): "Sometimes
likes to be alone" — a free differentiation lever a rewrite could use
(e.g., Andrew processes a clue solo while Amanda thinks out loud).

## 3. The parents' spy-winks in ch1–4

Catalog of everything that could count:

1. **Ch1, ambient:** "Their mom Erin's phone buzzed for the third time in
   five minutes. 'Sorry, kids. Just one more email.'" Generic workaholic
   texture — establishes cover, not a clue.
2. **Ch4, the real one (both parents):** Andrew asks how a thief beat
   brand-new motion sensors and cameras. Dad: "Even the best systems can
   have weak spots if they're not set up properly." Mom: "most basic
   security systems have predictable blind spots, and if you know exactly
   where the camera angles create dead zones you could..." — "She stopped
   abruptly," laughed "a bit too quickly," covers with "I, uh, just heard
   a commercial about it on the radio," and flees to coconut pie. Amanda:
   "Sometimes you guys are so weird."

**Does ch1–4 plant a deniable clue? Yes — exactly one, and it is the
model.** The Ch4 dinner is precisely what `premise.md` locks in ("Early
books plant small, deniable clues the reader can enjoy on a re-read") and
matches Erin's canon sheet verbatim ("Knows suspiciously much about things
like security-camera blind spots — a planted clue"). It even does double
duty: it foreshadows the actual theft method (Ch9's cameras dark 8–9pm,
Ch11's dead zones). The kids' reaction stays perfectly calibrated — "weird
parents," not suspicion.

Gaps: it is the *only* wink in the first third; the canon motif of
"'clients' insist on meetings that can't happen over video" doesn't appear
until Ch7, and the parents' other deniable texture (Adam the
gadget-indulgent dad "partly out of guilt for missing their games," Erin's
fish-vs-meat grill gag) is absent from ch1–4 even though Ch1 has a grill
scene and Ch4 serves mahi-mahi. Winks land as winks only when the mundane
texture around them is established first.

## 4. Character requirements for a rewritten ch1–4

1. **Hold the prop split absolutely.** Notebook + soccer ball = Amanda;
   tablet + drone-longing + phone-longing = Andrew. Fix the Ch4 "her
   tablet" drift; no prop crosses twins without a story reason.
2. **Put Andrew's tell on the page twice before Ch10.** Hair/cowlick beats
   at natural nerve points (first day in Ch1; the Ch3 theft commotion or
   lying by omission to Mrs. Kalani), so the Ch10 gloss "he always played
   with his hair" pays off something the reader has seen. Keep Amanda's
   lip-bite at one or two uses; retire "eyes lit up... the way they always
   did" as her accidental third tell.
3. **Dramatize the canon thinking split.** Amanda leaps (conclusion-first,
   sometimes wrong-fast — "decisive and quick"); Andrew verifies (dates,
   sources, measurements — "careful and curious"). Include at least one
   ch1–4 beat where Amanda's quick pattern-read is wrong and Andrew's
   careful check corrects it, making "she finds the pattern, he finds the
   proof" visible rather than asserted.
4. **Give Andrew one genuine deduction Amanda has not already made.**
   Recommended: he already owns the security question (Ch3, Ch4) — let him
   close a piece of it, e.g. reasoning from his archive research or the
   center's website that the thief needed inside knowledge. And cut
   Amanda's silent spiral–bowl pre-empt in Ch4 so Andrew's map-symbol
   recognition is a first, not an echo.
5. **Drone-seeding beat (concrete, canon-fit):** In Ch1, extend the Kekoa
   meeting — Kekoa hands Andrew the Mavic's controller for a thirty-second
   first hover; Andrew nearly parks it in a palm tree while Blazer barks
   at it; Kekoa offers standing lessons around his surf-teaching schedule.
   This (a) creates a visible learn-the-skill arc that mirrors his
   lacrosse canon ("still working to get good at it"), (b) satisfies the
   ladder's "via Kekoa," (c) satisfies the engine's set-up-earlier rule so
   the drone can legitimately break the case mid-book (spotting the hidden
   entrance/staging from the air, per the critique's Rec 2), and (d) makes
   Ch12's "quite skilled" and the Tokyo-footage Book 2 seed earned.
6. **Thread drone progress through ch1–4** — one line per chapter is
   enough (Ch2's electronics-store window becomes Andrew pricing a
   battery for lesson two, etc.). After Andrew starts learning, no pilot
   beat belongs to Kekoa alone.
7. **Spy-winks: keep the Ch4 dinner untouched as the template; add exactly
   one earlier micro-clue,** drawn from canon motifs — a Ch1–2 "client"
   call that pointedly "can't happen over video," or Erin clocking the
   cultural center's camera placement in one throwaway line. Cap ch1–4 at
   two winks; the twins' reaction never exceeds "Sometimes you guys are so
   weird."
8. **Lay the parents' mundane texture under the winks.** Ch1 grill scene
   gains Erin's "more fish, less meat" gag (canon); Adam's gadget-guilt
   generosity gets one beat (canon) — which also gives the family a
   plausible future channel for gadget gifts (spine beat 4).
9. **Every twin dialogue exchange over four lines must pass a
   tag-stripping test.** Amanda: imperative, teasing, conclusion-first.
   Andrew: question-first, qualifying, spec-citing (model numbers, "five
   hundred years ago"). Tag the currently ownerless lines (the Ch2
   tour-decline speech).
10. **Keep Amanda's deduction crown, but stage each inference as visible
    collaboration:** for every Amanda leap in ch1–4, Andrew's evidence
    (photo, archive hit, measurement) appears on the page first and gets
    named in dialogue — the canon promise that the twins "complete each
    other's thinking," shown, not told.
