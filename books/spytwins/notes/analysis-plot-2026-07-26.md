## 2. The beat spine, audited (story-engine.md §2)

1. **Intro/setup** — DELIVERED. Location hook, petroglyphs by Ch1 midpoint,
   Blazer described in Ch1 as canon requires, strong Ch1 ender (Andrew
   staring at the carvings in fading light).
2. **The problem appears** — DELIVERED but off-page. All three thefts happen
   off-page and are *reported*, never witnessed or discovered by the twins:
   Ch3 ("The ancient fishhook display case was found empty this morning" —
   an announcement to a crowd), Ch4 (Kekoa: "Another artifact was stolen
   overnight!"), Ch5 (one clause: "they learned another artifact had
   disappeared"). The inciting crime of a mystery novel never occurs in
   scene.
3. **3–4 obstacles** — DELIVERED: storm (Ch3, Ch5), the dark/branching lava
   tubes (Ch6), the grounding (Ch8–10), "we don't even know who" (Ch3–6).
4. **A technical tool enters (gift from the parents' conferences)** —
   UNFULFILLED. Verified: no gadget is gifted anywhere in the book. Andrew's
   tablet pre-exists; the flashlights and drone are Kekoa's. The skills
   ladder's "drone piloting — established in Book 1" is also false on the
   page: Andrew flies nothing until Ch12 asserts he "had become quite
   skilled at piloting over the past months" — told, never shown.
5. **3–4 clues** — DELIVERED, honestly chained: fishhook/petroglyph match
   (Ch3), spiral/bowl match (Ch4), symbols-as-trail (Ch5), footprints (Ch3),
   torn note (Ch6), name on paper (Ch7), key card (Ch8), cameras dark
   8–9pm (Ch9), Blazer at the foundation stones (Ch9). Nothing resolves by
   luck. The Blazer scent chain (Ch1 nose → Ch7 peanut-butter note → Ch9
   whining → Ch10 tracking → Ch11 block) is the best-constructed setup in
   the book.
6. **2–4 red herrings** — ZERO. Critique claim CONFIRMED (see §4).
7. **3–4 risks** — DELIVERED: first descent (Ch6), stake-out and entering
   the building (Ch8), returning to the tubes while grounded on a permission
   obtained by misdirection (Ch10).
8. **Point of no return** — PRESENT but unframed: Ch10's hike under false
   pretenses is it, but the text never lets the twins weigh the cost.
9. **The major disaster** — MISSING. At no point does the case look
   unsolvable. Getting caught in Ch8 costs them roughly one day and no
   evidence. Nothing is ever lost, destroyed, or dead-ended.
10. **Final risk + resolution** — COMPROMISED. The twins earn the location;
    the ending is earned by adults. Mrs. Kalani's tracker finds them, her
    backup camera provides the proof, her police make the arrest, and the
    dog physically stops Dr. Lee. The twins recap their own investigation in
    past tense while evidence is bagged. Critique claim CONFIRMED.
11. **Secondary plot resolves** — MISSING. No secondary plot is ever
    formally run. Andrew's surfing struggle (Ch5) is dropped; Amanda's
    honesty thread ("She hated not telling the whole truth," Ch8; "They
    hadn't technically lied," Ch10) is built and never closed. The only
    runner that resolves is Blazer vs. the crabs (Ch1 → Ch12).

**Checklist quick audit (§1):** location ✓; atmospherics ✓ (storm ×2,
first-time surfing, grounding); family dinners ✓ (Ch1, 4, 7, 12; Dad
grills); Jenny & Jacob calls ✓ (Ch3, 7, 10 — the Ch10 call is load-bearing,
it gets the twins un-grounded); parenting moments ✓; practice time ✓
(soccer Ch7/9, lacrosse Ch6, surfing Ch5); Blazer in Ch1 ✓; pets as KEY
feature ✓; next-destination hint ✓ (Tokyo). Gaps: no gifted gadget (beat
4), no secondary plot, lesson ("come to adults for help") is imposed by the
parents rather than earned and named by a twin, and Moonbeam the cat never
appears at all (checklist allows "and/or," but the series mascot pair is
half missing).

## 3. Summary vs. played scene — the specific offenders

The book's structural disease is narration at synopsis density. The places
where the page summarizes what must be scene:

- **Ch2 opens by summarizing a week**: "The twins had spent most of their
  first week unpacking boxes."
- **Ch3, theft #1**: discovered via announcement to a crowd; the twins
  never see the empty case. Worse, the page claims "They had seen it just
  yesterday, gleaming under the display lights" — but in Ch2 they *declined*
  Mrs. Kalani's tour, and Ch3 is their first visit inside. The contradiction
  exists because the scene that should establish the fishhook was skipped.
  (Also Ch2's geography glitch: they decline the tour at the *library*, then
  the next paragraph has them walking "home from the cultural center.")
- **Ch3**: "Andrew and his dad did some internet research the night before"
  — a father-son scene, summarized in one line.
- **Ch5, theft #3**: "Later that afternoon, they learned another artifact
  had disappeared" — an entire crime in a subordinate clause.
- **Ch6 opens by summarizing three days**: "It took Andrew and Amanda three
  days to convince their parents." The negotiation — a classic middle-grade
  scene — is skipped. The descent itself is 597 words.
- **Ch7**: "They spent the rest of the afternoon planning their stake-out."
- **Ch9**: Amanda says of Blazer, "Remember last week? He kept trying to
  get to this same spot." That prior alert — a planted clue — was never
  shown on the page. A payoff is citing a setup that doesn't exist.
- **Ch12**: Andrew "had become quite skilled at piloting over the past
  months" — the entire drone skill, asserted retroactively.

## 4. Where the mystery stops being deductive

CONFIRMED, and it is precisely locatable. "Dr. M. Lee" is read off the torn
note at Ch7's opening — the exact midpoint. Before that moment nobody has
suspected anyone ("we don't even know who"); after it, no one but Lee is
ever considered. There is no suspect field, no false lead, no innocent the
reader briefly distrusts, and the culprit is never on-page before Ch8 —
the reader could not have played along even in principle. Deduction after
Ch7 concerns only *method and location* (hidden entrance, staging logic),
never *who*. Chapters 7–10 are pursuit: research him, stake him out, race
him. The story engine requires 2–4 red herrings; the count on the page is
zero. Mrs. Santos exists in exactly one line (Ch10: the neighbor "always
watching from her window") — a red herring the draft names and never uses,
exactly as the critique said.

**Where red herrings could be planted in Ch1–4 (specific):**

- **Mrs. Santos** — promote her from Ch10 to Ch1 or Ch2. She watches the
  twins arrive; give her one genuinely suspicious behavior in Ch2–4 — seen
  near the property-line petroglyphs at odd hours, or asking pointed
  questions about the cultural center's schedule — with an innocent
  explanation revealed by Ch9 [TK: her innocent reason — author's choice;
  she is not yet canon beyond one line].
- **Mrs. Kalani (deniable, reader-level)** — the page already supplies the
  material: she has keys and expertise; in Ch3 she ushers the twins into a
  research room "usually just for university students" and knows Amanda is
  "quite good with patterns" from an unshown talk with their mother; in Ch2
  she appears unannounced at the library doorway; and Ch11 reveals she has
  been covertly tracking the children and reviewing footage all along.
  Letting the twins privately wonder about her for a chapter is fair play
  that the ending already retro-justifies. Whether she can bear suspicion is
  the author's call [TK].
- **The Ch3 footprints in the mud** — currently generic. Let them appear to
  match a named local (the shave-ice vendor and the ukulele performer are
  both introduced in Ch2 and available as one-beat suspects) before proving
  innocent.
- **Dr. Lee, unnamed, in the background** — not a herring but the missing
  fair-play plant: a figure photographing the rock carvings glimpsed in
  Ch2–3, so the Ch7 name is a recognition ("the man from the trail!")
  instead of an introduction.

Additionally, the reveal should move: with herrings installed, the name on
the note can hold until Ch8–9 so the middle third contains actual
elimination of suspects.

## 5. The ticking clock

First appearance verified. There is **no time pressure of any kind in
Ch1–6.** The clock enters in three steps:

- **Ch7 (soft)** — Lee's social posts, "vindication coming soon"; Andrew:
  "we have to do it soon... before he ships them off"; chapter ends "We
  have to do it tomorrow night" (the stake-out).
- **Ch8 (hard)** — the overheard call: "Yes, tomorrow night... The final
  pieces... I've arranged the shipping..."
- **Ch9 (operationalized)** — Amanda's staging deduction: the artifacts
  must be at the site *now*; go this afternoon, beat the truck.

So the clock starts at ~58% of the book. STATUS.md's applied fix ("single
ticking clock, tightened deadline logic") is real — Ch8→Ch10 deadline logic
is coherent — but the fix tightened a clock that still doesn't exist for
the first six chapters. The only early time frame is the three-month stay
(Ch1), which is season-scale, not case-scale.

## 7. Structural requirements for a rewritten Ch1–4

1. **Put the twins inside the cultural center before the first theft.**
   They accept (not decline) Mrs. Kalani's tour in Ch2 and see the fishhook
   in its lit case. Fixes the Ch3 "seen it just yesterday" contradiction and
   the Ch2 library/center geography error, and makes the loss personal.
2. **Play the theft discovery as a scene.** The twins at or near the empty
   case — glass, absence, Mrs. Kalani's face — not an announcement overheard
   from a research room.
3. **Build a suspect field of two to three by the end of Ch4.** Mrs. Santos
   promoted to Ch1–2 with one suspicious behavior (innocent explanation
   [TK]); a deniable question mark over Mrs. Kalani drawn from evidence
   already on the page (access, foreknowledge, turning up unannounced);
   optionally one one-beat suspect via the Ch3 footprints. Each lead must be
   raised and later disproven — restoring deduction to the middle third.
4. **Put the culprit on-page, unnamed, in Ch2–3** (a figure photographing
   the carvings near the trail), so the mid-book name is recognition, not
   introduction — and the name reveal can slip to Ch8–9.
5. **Start a case-scale ticking clock in Ch1–4** [TK: mechanism], so Ch8's
   "tomorrow night" escalates an existing deadline instead of creating the
   book's first time pressure at 58%.
6. **Deliver spine beat 4: a technical tool arrives as a gift** from the
   parents' conference or Uncle Jacob in Ch1–2 [TK: which gadget], and it
   must be used meaningfully in the back half.
7. **Begin Andrew's drone lessons with Kekoa on-page in Ch1–4**, so drone
   piloting is earned (the skills ladder claims it), Andrew is
   differentiated from both Amanda and Kekoa, and a late drone beat can
   break the case.
8. **Show Blazer's first alert at the foundation stones** as a played scene
   in Ch2–4 — the setup Ch9 currently cites from off-page.
9. **Establish the security system on-page** during the Ch2 tour — cameras,
   motion sensors, and a passing mention that outside consultants set the
   system up years ago — planting the key card and blind-spot payoffs.
10. **Launch the secondary plot in Ch1–2** (Andrew's surfing/balance is the
    on-page candidate) and open Amanda's honesty thread early, so both can
    close in Ch11–12 and the lesson is earned by a twin, not assigned by a
    parent.
11. **Convert summarized time into scene.** The first week (Ch2), the
    father-son research (Ch3), and the three-day negotiation (Ch6) become
    played scenes or one-line transitions attached to scenes; every clue
    discovery in Ch1–4 must happen in scene, on the page.
12. **Preserve what works:** the Ch1 Blazer description and petroglyph
    hook, the Ch1 chapter-ender, the Ch4 dinner (Mom's blind-spot slip —
    the book's best scene and the arc's best seed), and the
    worst-possible-moment rhythm of the Jenny & Jacob calls.
