## 3. NEW findings introduced by the rebuild (ranked by sink-likelihood)

**N1 — The incident arithmetic does not reconcile across the doc's own three ledgers.** The day spine says "her fourth and last prank D-12" — but the quarters put only two pranks in her column on the page (signs D-02, geese D-03; incident #3 is explicitly his: "crueler, cruder, and better informed"). Her third is nowhere; her fourth exists only in the spine. Meanwhile the escalation ladder lists six rungs with no D-12 prank in it at all, and the ingredient audit claims a "six-incident ledger" that matches neither. Worse: a prank by her on D-12 — the day *after* the public accusation (D-11) and two days after Amanda's promise — is a plot-critical character act the quarters never narrate, and it collides with OQ 5's account of her back half ("quietly working the same doors and gathering her own equipment back in"). Bar failed: in a fair-play mystery whose master clue is *sorting incidents into two columns*, the doc's own three tallies must agree on who did what, when. This is the top rung-2 gate condition.

**N2 — The climax contradicts its own timetable.** "It is ten past five in the morning... Somebody with a phone is already here." *Then:* "The first boat lands, the ramp clangs, and a pleasant man in good shoes walks off it" — and he is met at the hall "with... a rented bike leaning against the wall behind him." As sequenced, the missing bike was rented *before* the first boat landed, so either someone else took it (never resolved), or he came by another door (which breaks the case), or the boat actually landed before 5:10 and the prose order misleads. Bar failed: the timetable is, by the book's own line, "the one thing out here that cannot lie" — the climax cannot fuzz the very hour that convicts.

**N3 — The signal geography is wired backwards.** Ch11 needs the club's guest network because the canoe is "the only place on this island where a tablet can find a signal" — but the ch2 plant has Dad "logging his own account into Andrew's tablet" at the dock kiosk, which requires the tablet to be online at the dock. If the dock has signal, the dawn canoe is unnecessary and the beat is decoration; if it doesn't, the plant can't be planted. One of the two scenes is currently impossible. (Compounds the existing [CHECK] on walk-up vs app-only, which the doc does carry.)

**N4 — The middle's load-bearing fact is explained only in an optional annex.** The grandmother's silence ("She was out. She was alone. She won't discuss it") is what damns her — and the only account of *why* lives in OQ 5, a PROPOSAL the author may decline. The Q4 confession does not retroactively cover it: admitting forty years of pranks does not say where she was at first light on D-13. Bar failed: the premise body must carry its own causal spine; an open question may tune the middle, not hold it up.

**N5 — A plot turn runs on unattributed information.** "Their friend has found out, from somebody else, that Amanda has known about the shed for three days." Only Amanda, Andrew, and the grandmother knew. If the grandmother told the family, that is a major character action buried in a passive clause; if anyone else knew, it's an unplanted plant. "From somebody else" is the premise admitting it doesn't know.

**N6 — The lock ledger overstates its own compliance.** Q1 apparatus reports the locked sentence as "Held" while amending it — the author locked "mortified to be *enrolled* in island day camp"; the rebuild delivers a junior-counselor *posting* and labels the change a repair inside a "Held" line. The change is good (it was my own T2.6 fix) and it is disclosed — but "Held" invites a skim-approval of an amended lock. Bar: a locked sentence that has been modified must be flagged as AMENDED, PENDING RATIFICATION, not held. (Q2, Q3, Q4 are genuinely held; Q4's "genuine pranks were islander's work" is kept with real wit.)

**N7 — Procedural: the wink waiver cites an authorization that doesn't exist yet** — "per the book-8 suspicion dial (author, [TK date on approval])." A waiver pending its own approval is a request, not a waiver. Harmless if the author ratifies at this gate; flagging so it can't silently harden into precedent.

**N8 — Calibration flag, for kid-reader-panel, not a premise failure.** The fence artifact — "a photograph of the approach path with times written on it and one place circled" — is in-world slander designed to *imply* aviation menace, which is clever, but the page still puts an implied threat-to-aircraft in front of 8–12s. ## 4. First-timer pass — what a stranger stumbles on that a canon-reader doesn't

- **The two columns are never printed.** The whole middle runs on sorting incidents into hers vs. his, and a cold reader has to reverse-engineer the sort from four scattered surfaces (quarters, ladder, spine, audit) — which is also how N1 got in undetected. A canon-reader autocompletes; a stranger, and possibly the author, cannot. One incident table with a "whose hand" column would have caught N1 before I did.
- **Unglossed jargon with no pointer.** "The millimeter is 'none'", "the book-8 suspicion dial," "B1 reference build," "Kekoa trade," "Moonbeam debt" — a stranger hits all five with no in-doc gloss or file path. Most apparatus terms are cited to sources; these are not. The write-for-a-stranger standard is mostly met by the story text and mostly missed by the series shorthand.
- **"The sandbag summers" lands cold at first use** (rebuild preamble, line 12) two pages before the back cover grounds it. Inside the story text proper the grounding order is fine.
- **The twelve-day silence between two people sharing a live secret.** A stranger notices that after the shed doorway ("She hands Amanda the twine, and says nothing at all") the grandmother and Amanda do not transact over the secret again until the ch10 ask — roughly D-11 to D-17 with the book's two secret-keepers never in a scene together. A canon-reader trusts drafting to fill it; a stranger reads it as a hole where the book's most charged relationship should be.
