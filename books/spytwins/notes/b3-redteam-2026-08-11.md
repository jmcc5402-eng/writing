## What is strong (stated once, so the rest calibrates)

The prose clears jacket-copy standard — "the loudest thing all day is
a screen door" is the best sentence in three books of planning docs.
The car-free wagon-and-ferry world is real SC3 gold; nothing on the

## THE SINGLE MOST DANGEROUS WEAKNESS

The entire solve rests on **"the only way in is the ferry"** — a claim
the premise's own research note refutes three separate ways. A mystery
whose foundational fact is false in the specific real place it is set
does not survive its first Toronto school visit.

---

# TIER 1 — would sink the book

## 1. Book 3 is Book 2 in a different coat

Beat for beat: small property mischief; authority shrugs; the twins'
friend('s grandmother) wrongly accused; a dated community VOTE as the
clock; a public promise as the point of no return; dismissal at the
adults' meeting as the setback; a narrowing that yields exactly one
dawn; a dawn stakeout pitched to parents with the B1 walkie net and
adults as perimeter; the culprit handled with grace, not punishment.

The franchise promise is "new city + new mystery + new skill each
book" — the *engine* repeats, the *plot silhouette* must not. Books 2
and 3 are the two an acquiring editor reads to decide whether forty
of these exist. Also: "adults dismiss the kids" is now the obstacle in
all three books.

Kids don't articulate this; they say "it was okay" and don't ask for
book 4. Booksellers do articulate it.

## 2. The solve's foundation is factually false — and the refutation is in the book's own research file

`notes/toronto-research-2026-07-30.md` refutes "the only way in is the
ferry" three ways:

- **The pedestrian tunnel** (research §5): open since 2015, under the
  Western Gap, and **it serves the airport alone** — the exact
  location of the staged incident, reachable on foot from the
  mainland twenty-four hours a day, by a man whose employer owns the
  tunnel. The premise never acknowledges it exists.
- **Water taxis** (§2): on-demand, not timetabled.
- **The yacht club's own member ferry** (§7) — at which this book
  stages its Q4 set piece, without noticing it is a second dock.

A fourth problem the research implies: the first morning *arrival*
boat on a commuter island carries islanders coming off night shifts,
park and camp staff, deliveries. "No islander needs a boat to be
home" is backwards.

**And the squeeze:** a 650-person community small enough to convict a
stranger by timetable would have clocked a man in city shoes on the
dawn boat the *second* time, out loud, at the dock. Either the island
is small enough for the timetable to convict — and the islanders make
him first — or big enough to hide in, and the timetable proves
nothing. The book needs both.

Fair play is a WALL, not a warning.

**Fix:** put the tunnel on the page in Q1 as the adults' door the kids
can't use, then make the deduction *"he didn't take the tunnel
because his badge would log him"* — the tunnel becomes the theme's
best plant instead of the plot's fatal hole.

## 3. There is no investigable case

- **The crime scene is ruled off-limits** — the fence line is "a
  BOUNDARY, not a venue." The twins investigate a scene they may
  never stand in, which is *why* Andrew ends up counting bicycles at
  a different dock.
- **Two clues across twelve chapters** (the fence-line forgery, the
  first-boat narrowing). B1 ran a symbol system, a mapped trail, a
  paper-trail ID and a shipment clock; B2 ran mark height, stencil
  edges, the rain pattern, panel keys and the corner syllable. This
  is independent of the density dial — density governs explanation
  load; clue count governs whether there is a book.
- **Nobody to suspect:** a 200-year-old ghost and a dog fighting
  geese. Neither is a person who could have done it. The only human
  suspect is the woman the jacket copy tells us is innocent.
- **The antagonist arrives on the last page** — a suit with no face,
  no name, no scene, no want. B2 got away without a villain because
  Rin was met in ch2 and rooted for by ch11. This is not "no
  villain"; it is an ABSENT villain.
- **The first culprit is retired in a subordinate clause** — three
  escalating incidents, the whole content of Q1–Q2, attributed to a
  person who never appears, by a deduction never shown.

## 4. The trap has no bait

By vote morning the fixer has won — the petition is collapsing, names
are coming off. The premise then has him make a third trip, on the
public ferry, in daylight, carrying incriminating equipment, into the
one place everyone gathers. The twins have no channel to him and so
cannot have lured him. The climax works only because the antagonist is
stupid on a schedule.

B1's Lee had a shipment racing the ceremony; B2's Rin had the last
rain dawn. B3's fixer has nothing.

**Fix:** one more incident planned for vote morning itself, stated in
Q3, so the twins deduce *when* he must return, not merely *how*.

# TIER 2 — a reviewer draws blood

5. **(C6) The hook does not compete.** Shelf-mates all have an object
   of desire — a hidden literary game, a stolen painting, a global
   hunt. B3's nouns are pranks, blame, grandmother, vote. Nothing to
   *find*: a defense case, not a hunt. The jacket's best writing is
   all setting; every mystery sentence is flatter than every island
   sentence — diagnostic that the appeal is a place, not a case. And
   there is no twelve-word hand-sell line anywhere in the doc.
6. **(C6) The series is aging DOWN while its readers age up.** Day
   camp with the little kids, canoe lessons, petting farm, hedge
   maze. Against Framed! and Book Scavenger (twelve-year-old leads,
   art heists, cipher games), B3 reads younger than B2, which already
   read younger than B1. *Fix:* make camp a junior-counselor posting
   — same humiliation, same roam warrant, but they carry
   responsibility instead of being mistaken for little kids.
7. **The headline question is decided offstage and may go
   unanswered** ("[TK: does the petition win]"). Twelve chapters
   train the reader on a countdown to a vote held in a room the twins
   aren't in. Meanwhile the climax, the clearing and the deadline all
   land on vote morning, leaving ch12 nothing but the epilogue the B1
   reference build forbids.
8. **Nothing converts "jets" into something a ten-year-old loses.**
   B2 needed one Kenta line ("the first camera points at OUR
   shutter"). B3 has no equivalent — the vote is a calendar.
9. **Amanda's lesson contradicts her characterization, and Coach Chen
   is the device for the third book running.** She is "annoyingly,
   immediately good" in a canoe, then the climax asks perseverance to
   pay off. And Chen is canon as her *soccer* coach — he cannot have
   coached paddle technique; B2 already ran the present-tense
   warm-up. *Fix:* let the canoe beat her for two chapters; Chen
   supplies something transferable and soccer-true.
10. **The single wink is the book's thesis in Mom's mouth** — "cash
    and a bicycle, that's how you move around a city," in a book
    whose climax catches a man *because he had a phone*. Not
    deniable at book 3, against the book-8 suspicion dial. *Fix:*
    move the wink off the theme.
11. **The ride-code payoff does nothing**, and the proof that
    convicts is adult-read. What is the unlocked bike FOR? And "every-
    thing with an app leaves footprints" — read by whom, given two
    kids without phones? Risks a climax where the twins hold a canoe
    while adults hold the evidence.
12. **Niagara is a postcard, and the geography won't pay the clock
    bite.** The same three-beat sensory image appears four times
    (mist heard, rumble felt, scale) — a tell that the doc has no
    Niagara material. And it is ninety minutes each way: "days they
    can't spare" (L94) contradicts "let the case wait a day" (L272),
    and a Canadian reader knows it.
13. **The proposed adult ally is a walking solution** — a deckhand
    "who knows every face on the first boat" could identify a repeat
    stranger in chapter three. *Fix:* make him new to the route; he
    knows the boat, not the faces.

# THE HIGHEST-LEVERAGE FIX

**Make the grandmother guilty of the funny half.**

She has been pranking that airport herself — cheerfully, brilliantly,
since the sandbag summers. The twins' problem is not that an innocent
woman is blamed. It is that the woman being blamed **did half of it**,
and the half she didn't do is the half that could end her.

One swap fixes most of Tier 1:
- **The clone dies.** "Prove the person you love is guilty of the
  small thing and innocent of the big one" is not a plot this series
  has run, nor one on the comp shelf.
- **The locked quarters survive nearly verbatim** — suspicion still
  lands on the grandmother; the genuine pranks are still an
  islander's, just a *named* islander the reader has loved for eight
  chapters.
- **The case becomes investigable** — the [TK retiree] vanishes; the
  middle gains a real question (*how much did she do?*) with a
  suspect in scenes, with motive, on the page.
- **The honesty beat gets teeth** — clearing her means exposing her,
  which converts a throwaway clause into the emotional climax.
- **The hook gets its twist** — "prove their friend's grandmother
  didn't do the scary thing, which means proving she did all the
  funny ones."
- **The stakes get kid-sized** — a grandmother her granddaughter is
  proud of, who has been lying to her about it.

It does not fix the tunnel (#2) or the missing bait (#4); both need
their own one-line repairs, named above.
