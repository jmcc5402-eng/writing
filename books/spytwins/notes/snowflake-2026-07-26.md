## One-page overview

**One sentence:** When ancient artifacts start vanishing from a Maui
cultural center, twins Amanda and Andrew Wilson decode a petroglyph trail
that leads them — one step behind the thief and one step ahead of everyone
else — to a hidden sacred site, never suspecting that their consultant
parents are secretly spies.

The Wilsons land on Maui for a three-month consulting posting: Amanda
(notebook, soccer, pattern-brain), Andrew (tablet, lacrosse, gadget-hungry,
no phone until high school), parents Erin and Adam, and Blazer, the
Lab-Shepherd with a champion nose. Day one delivers the whole engine: Blazer
flushes petroglyphs at the property line, teen neighbor Kekoa gives Andrew a
wobbly first turn on his drone controller and offers standing lessons, his
grandmother Mrs. Kalani invites them to the cultural center she works at,
neighbor Mrs. Santos watches it all from her window, and a gift gadget
[TK which] arrives from Dad's conference. A "client call that can't happen
over video" passes unnoticed at the first grill dinner.

On their first solo outing the twins accept Mrs. Kalani's tour: they see the
prized bone fishhook gleaming in its case beside the ceremonial bowl, clock
the new cameras and motion sensors "set up by outside consultants years
ago," and learn the case-scale deadline [TK mechanism — e.g. the exhibit
closes/travels on a fixed date] that will squeeze every later choice.
Walking the trail home they pass an unnamed man photographing rock carvings
and find a second petroglyph site that Amanda reads — too fast, until
Andrew's careful photo check corrects her — as pieces of a bigger puzzle.

Then the fishhook vanishes, and the twins walk into the discovery: the empty
case, Mrs. Kalani's stricken face, the police. The loss is personal now.
Amanda matches the fishhook to her sketches; Andrew, working his own thread,
deduces that intact sensors plus no forced entry means inside knowledge. A
suspect field forms: Mrs. Santos and her odd-hours behavior (innocent reason
[TK]), the unnamed photographer, and a flicker of unease about Mrs. Kalani
herself — keys, foreknowledge, turning up everywhere. A second theft, a
faded office map, and Blazer whining at odd-fitting foundation stones point
the pattern toward the lava tubes as the deadline shrinks.

The middle third eliminates suspects while the trail hardens: a third theft,
a first descent into the tubes, a torn note naming "Dr. M. Lee" — whom the
twins recognize as the photographer from the trail. Lee is a disgraced
archaeologist stealing "proof" of his rejected migration theory. A botched
stake-out gets the twins grounded just as Lee's shipping deadline lands;
Amanda's staging deduction (the artifacts must already be at the site) and a
well-timed Jenny-and-Jacob call get them up the mountain in time. In the
carved chamber, Amanda's decoding reveal stuns Lee; Blazer blocks his
escape; Mrs. Kalani — sharper than she looks — arrives with police, tracker,
and backup-camera proof. Artifacts restored, honesty lessons landed, surf
balance finally found, drone skills earned on the page, and a hint of Tokyo
— while the reader alone smiles at Mom's suspicious fluency in
security-camera blind spots.

## Four-paragraph overview

**Setup.** The Wilson family arrives on Maui for three months, and chapters
1-2 assemble every piece the back half will spend: Blazer and the
property-line petroglyphs, Kekoa and the first drone lesson, the gift gadget
[TK which], Mrs. Santos at her window, and — on an accepted tour of the
cultural center — the fishhook in its lit case, the security system with its
old-consultant history, and the deadline [TK mechanism] hanging over the
exhibit. The twins find a second carving site on the trail, pass a stranger
photographing rocks, and Amanda begins to suspect the symbols are a designed
pattern rather than decoration.

**Escalating middle, first half.** The fishhook theft breaks as a played
scene with the twins in the building, and the mystery becomes personal:
Amanda's sketch matches the stolen piece, Andrew reasons the thief had
inside knowledge, and by the second theft the twins hold a live suspect list
(Mrs. Santos, the photographer, a guilty flicker about Mrs. Kalani) plus a
faded map that reads as a trail into the lava tubes. Blazer's alert at the
center's foundation stones, the third theft, and a nervy first descent —
modern litter, a torn note, footsteps in the dark — turn theory into
pursuit, while the deadline keeps shrinking.

**Escalating middle, second half.** The note yields a name the twins already
know by face — Dr. Marcus Lee, the photographer from the trail, a dismissed
professor whose posts promise "vindication coming soon" — and the remaining
suspects fall away as his motive locks in. The stake-out catches Lee using a
key card inside the center and betrays the twins to Mrs. Kalani and their
parents: grounded with the clock nearly run out, they sleuth from
confinement (cameras dark 8-9 p.m., sealed underground storerooms, the
hidden entrance Blazer found) until Amanda's staging deduction — the
artifacts must be at the site *now* — makes that afternoon the last chance.

**Resolution.** Un-grounded by Uncle Jacob's timely advocacy and traveling
on a half-truth that costs Amanda real guilt, the trio follows the decoded
trail to the ceremonial chamber where all three artifacts wait for pickup —
and Dr. Lee is waiting too. Amanda's reveal that she read the same
petroglyphs he spent two years decoding breaks his certainty; Blazer blocks
his bolt; Mrs. Kalani arrives with the police, her tracker, and backup
footage; and the access-card explanation closes every loose end. The
artifacts return to their cases, the twins own what bending the truth cost
them, Andrew flies the drone he has earned, and Tokyo beckons.
