# Spytwins, Book 1 — *The Petroglyph Mystery*

_Outside critique, 2026-07-26. Read against `concept/premise.md`,
`series-bible/story-engine.md` and `characters/characters.md`. The
manuscript was not edited; everything here is a proposal._

---

## Part 1 — Critique

**What this actually is.** Not "Nancy Drew meets a family of undercover
spies." That's the pitch, and it oversells the spy half. On the page is a
warm, well-mannered artifact-theft mystery for the young end of the band —
eight to ten, not twelve — narrated in a pleasant adult voice, in which
two observant kids and a very good dog out-notice one sad man. The spy
premise occupies about six lines. That's by design, but it means Book 1
has to win on the mystery alone.

**The number that decides everything.** 10,294 words in twelve chapters.
Middle-grade mystery is acquired at 25,000–45,000. *Spy School*, *City
Spies*, *Framed!* and *Book Scavenger* are novels; this is a third of one.
It reads like what it is — a correctly shaped story told at synopsis
density, every scene the right scene and every scene ending three beats
early. Chapter 6, the first descent into the lava tubes, is 597 words:
strange tunnel, someone else's trash, footsteps in the dark, lost, out,
before a nine-year-old's pulse has moved.

**Opening.** Chapter 1 starts on a weather report ("The hot Hawaiian sun
beat down as...") and recovers fast: petroglyphs by page two, Blazer
described where canon requires, and a real chapter-ender in Andrew
"staring at the rocks... now barely visible in the fading light."

**Does the mystery play fair?** It plays fair. It barely plays. The clue
chain is honest and nothing resolves by luck — petroglyph to fishhook
(Ch3), spiral to bowl (Ch4), trail as map (Ch5), the torn note (Ch6), the
key card (Ch8, paid off Ch11), cameras dark 8–9pm (Ch9). The Blazer chain
is the best-built thing in the book: nose (Ch1), peanut-butter note (Ch7),
whining at the foundation stones (Ch9), scent in the tunnel (Ch10), growl
(Ch11). Five chapters of setup, and it lands.

But `story-engine.md` §5 asks for 2–4 red herrings and this draft has
**zero**. Dr. Lee is named in Chapter 7 — the exact midpoint — and from
that second he is the only person anyone has suspected. No suspect field,
no wrong turn, no innocent the reader briefly distrusts. Everything after
Ch7 is pursuit, not deduction. A whodunit in which nobody is suspected is
a chase with a magnifying glass on the cover. Mrs. Santos, introduced in
Ch10 as the neighbor "always watching from her window," is a red herring
the draft names and never uses.

**The twins.** The tells are locked and used — lip-bite, cowlick, notebook
versus tablet. Function is another matter. Amanda makes every deduction,
including reading the petroglyphs to Dr. Lee's face in Ch10; Andrew looks
things up and holds a flashlight. Kekoa is *also* the tech/drone/local
kid, so Andrew is squeezed from both sides. Strip the tags from the cave
dialogue and you cannot tell the twins apart.

**Voice.** The best minute in the book is the Ch4 dinner — Mom's "most
basic security systems have predictable blind spots," the too-fast laugh,
the coconut pie, Amanda's flat "Sometimes you guys are so weird." Comedy,
character and the forty-book arc in six lines. Elsewhere the prose tells:
"Her hands trembled with excitement"; Amanda's eyes "lit up" four times,
twice with the identical gloss "the way they always did."

**Pacing and ending.** The farewell chapter (736 words) is longer than the
first cave descent (597); the stake-out, the suspense centerpiece, is 641
— second shortest in the book. Then Mrs. Kalani solves it: her tracker,
her backup camera, her police. The twins are *found*, then recap their own
investigation in past tense while adults bag evidence, and Dr. Lee is
stopped by the dog. Obedient to "come to adults for help" — but the
detectives are spectators at their own climax.

**Verdict.** The engine is commercial and the bones are sound; this is not
a book with a story problem. It is a complete, correct skeleton of a good
middle-grade novel, not yet written at novel length or novel temperature.
Two thirds of the finished book is missing, and `STATUS.md` calling Book 1
"complete" is the most expensive inaccuracy in the repo. Not submittable,
six to nine months from it — but that gap is drafting, not rethinking.

## Part 2 — Recommendations

Items 2–6 are small to execute and large in effect. Item 1 is the gate;
item 7 protects the other thirty-nine books.

**1. Reclassify, then dramatize six scenes to length.** Change Book 1 in
`STATUS.md` from "Complete" to "first pass / skeleton, 10.3K of a ~30K
target." Then, rather than expanding globally, take six scenes to
1,200–1,800 words each: Ch3's discovery of the theft (the twins should
*see* the empty case, not hear an announcement), Ch6's lava tube, Ch8's
stake-out, Ch9's grounded run, Ch10's confrontation, Ch11's resolution.
Model them on the Ch4 dinner. Cost: months — do everything below inside
it.

**2. Give Andrew the drone, and make it the case-breaker.** Kekoa lends
him the Mavic around Ch5–6; Andrew flies the ridge and spots what nobody
on foot could — the hidden entrance, or a vehicle staged on a fire road.
This fixes four things: Andrew stops being a sidekick; the skills ladder's
claim that "drone piloting — established in Book 1" becomes true (he
currently flies nothing, and Ch12 merely asserts he "had become quite
skilled"); the twins' *thinking* differentiates, not just their props; and
Book 2 is seeded, since Kekoa already asked for Tokyo footage. Cost: one
scene plus four threaded lines. Best ratio here.

**3. Plant two red herrings; move Dr. Lee's name to Ch9.** Use the two the
draft half-built. Mrs. Santos gets three appearances and one suspicious
behavior that proves innocent. And let the twins privately wonder about
Mrs. Kalani — she has keys, expertise, ushered them into a room "usually
just for university students," and knew Amanda was good with patterns
before they told her. Then delay the "Dr. M. Lee" reveal two chapters so
the middle third contains deduction. Cost: two short scenes, eight
threaded lines. Whether Mrs. Kalani can bear suspicion is the author's
call.

**4. Let the twins win the climax.** In Ch10 Amanda already stuns Dr. Lee
by proving she read petroglyphs he spent two years decoding. Build the
ending on that: she talks him down using his own theory plus Uncle Jacob's
Ch7 lecture on documentation and context — the stolen artifacts prove
nothing, because removing them destroyed the provenance. Keep Blazer's
block; let Mrs. Kalani arrive *after* the decision. Rule 3 in `CLAUDE.md`:
the twins must earn the answer. Right now they earn the location and an
adult earns the ending. Cost: ~800 words.

**5. Land the secondary plot and name the lesson.** `story-engine.md` beat
11 requires the secondary plot to resolve. Andrew's surfing struggle
appears in Ch5 and vanishes. Amanda's honesty thread is the better
candidate, three-quarters built — "She hated not telling the whole truth"
(Ch8), "They hadn't technically lied" (Ch10) — but it never closes. Give
her one moment of volunteering a truth that costs her, and one line at the
Ch12 ceremony naming it. Cost: an afternoon.

**6. Continuity and mechanics — the copy-edit pass is not done.** Log in
`CHANGELOG.md`:
- **Ch2 geography error.** The twins are at the *library* when Mrs. Kalani
  invites them and they decline; the next paragraph has them taking "the
  hiking trail home from the cultural center."
- **Ch3 contradiction, downstream of it.** Having declined the tour, they
  cannot have seen the fishhook "just yesterday, gleaming under the
  display lights." Ch3 is their first visit.
- **Ch4 canon drift.** "Amanda sat at a desk and fired up **her** tablet"
  — the tablet is Andrew's, one of two props that separate the twins.

Also: hyphens stand in for em dashes throughout, dialogue starts lowercase
after a tag at least three times, and the file isn't wrapped at 80 columns.

**7. Series: start a ledger before Book 2 is outlined.** Add
`series-bible/ledger.md` — one row per book: skill *actually earned on the
page*, gadget received and from whom, arc clue planted, lesson learned,
next-destination hint. Filling in Book 1 honestly exposes two gaps: spine
beat 4 ("a technical tool enters — a gift from the parents' conferences")
is **unfulfilled**, and the skills ladder overstates what Book 1
delivered. Arc 3 promises a gadget per book and Arc 4 spends thirty books
of skill in one rescue; neither is writable off an aspirational ledger.
Cost: an hour now, five minutes per book after.

**One series idea, as a question.** Ch7 gives the parents a Big Island
meeting that can't happen over video; Ch10 has them leave for a client
meeting "until after dinner" — then appear inside a lava tube. Made
slightly more pointed, that timing is a deniable clue of the kind
`premise.md` describes, at zero cost. Whether those are the same meeting
is currently ambiguous — [TK: author to decide].
