## Part 1 — Critique

**Verdict: better than canon ch9–12, decisively; good middle-grade
chapters in absolute terms; and the climax now belongs to the twins.**
SC2's one-line ruling: every deduction and the capture itself is a
kid's, on the page. Amanda's staging leap is verified by Andrew off
Lee's dropped sketch (ch09 l.196–225, paying T22 at last); the trap is
planned in a whispered huddle the reader attends (ch10 l.218–295);
Andrew's click-beacon is built from his own ch6 failure and tested
on-page before it's needed ("Because last time it mattered," ch10

worked, didn't it?", ch11 l.270–276); and Amanda releases the leash
herself (l.153–156). Kalani arrives with tapes and tracker — proof
hers, win theirs, exactly per the locked decision.

**Pacing (card C1).** The set accelerates where it must. Ch9 turns
confinement into compression — "I timed the route" (l.111), "Eleven
minutes of lunch break left" (l.294), and the polarity flip "We're not
early, Andrew. We're almost late." (l.278). Ch10's radio test is a
pause the climax buys back double, and the ch10/ch11 break —
mid-scene, on "click, click. / Still okay. Still okay." — is the best
structural choice in the book. Ch11 intercuts stall and beacon like
parallel film cutting. The one stall: after the capture, five
explanation beats run consecutively (tracker, tapes, card, storerooms,
peanut butter, ch11 l.239–302) — the speakers changed from canon but
the recap *shape* survived. Ch12 earns its quiet honestly: nearly
every paragraph pays a listed debt (mats, surf, drone, helicopter,
crabs, more-fish), so stillness reads as accounts closing.

## Part 2 — Recommendations

1. **Fix the three mechanical breaks before the adoption read.**
   "Two days ago" → "last night" (ch10 l.13–15, two lines); the
   tracker dot frozen "at the tube entrance, an hour old" (ch11
   l.244–245, one line); one clause explaining who called the parents
   (Kalani from the station is free and in character). Cost: under an

   true, not merely near-true.
2. **Author rules on the sheen, once, globally.** Bundle the three
   dialogue instances (ch8 Dad, ch11 "good haircut," ch11 "wobble when
   you fib") with the narration gradient and decide keep/soften as one
   voice policy — not line by line across three reviews. Cost: one
   sitting with old/new pairs supplied.
3. **Rule the three pending wink-adjacent beats** (ch8, "Jakarta.
   Probably.", "known since the lawn"). I'd keep Jakarta (J&J
   worldliness is premise canon), keep ch8 as parenting, and soften
   lawn-dad only if the author wants Dad surprisable. Cost: minutes.

   scent exchange to the walk out of the tubes, and let the officer's
