## Verdict

Better — clearly, and not by a little. The old chapters 1–4 ran 3,535 words;
these run 5,694, inside the snowflake's target, and the added words are scenes,
not padding. The theft is now walked into instead of announced over a PA
(ch03 l.53–69 vs ms l.138), the suspect field my critique said didn't exist now
does (ch04 l.148–170), and Andrew finally makes a deduction Amanda doesn't hand
him (ch03 l.82–99). In absolute terms these are good middle-grade chapters —
publishable-shape, finally at novel temperature — with one asterisk: three
load-bearing beats are literal brackets. A gift is opened onto a placeholder
("Inside the box was [TK gift gadget...]", ch01 l.185–187) and the ticking
clock exists twice as stage directions (ch02 l.158–161; ch04 l.85–87). Right
per the no-invented-canon rule, but it means the chapters cannot yet be felt
end-to-end: the deadline has no pressure because it has no date.

## Voice

Dialogue voice is preserved, because the drafter kept it verbatim where it
counted — the whole mahi-mahi dinner survives to the syllable (ch04 l.189–215
vs ms l.212–224), and the chapter-1 ender is intact (ch01 l.209–211). The
narration is another story. The old book barely uses simile; the new pages
average one per scene, and they are conspicuously better than anything the
author wrote: "steady as a dinner plate" (ch01 l.85), "sat in her stomach like
a swallowed ice cube" (ch03 l.123), "the leash twanged like a guitar string"
(ch04 l.41), "the color of a bruise" (ch03 l.238). Pair the lip-bite lines:
ms l.156, "a habit that always showed up when she was solving a particularly
tricky math problem," becomes "the way she did when a problem turned out to
have teeth" (ch03 l.136–137). That is a sheen. It is tasteful, kid-right sheen
— the new Blazer opener, "declare war on a sand crab... which Blazer clearly
took personally" (ch01 l.3, l.14), reads like the author on a good day — but
the author should be told the narration is now wittier than theirs and asked
whether to keep it. Hard rule: the voice is the point.

## New problems

1. **Unsupported evidence.** Andrew's deduction leans on "The front door was
   locked when we got here — Mrs. Kalani unlocked it for us, remember?" (ch03
   l.92–93). No such moment exists — the twins walk in on her at her desk, a
   volunteer already seated (ch03 l.42–49, l.66). The rewrite's best beat cites
   a scene the rewrite forgot to write.
2. **POV break.** "And far behind them on the trail, / faint as a bird, a
   camera clicked" (ch02 l.221–222). This book has never left the twins' heads,
   and the line edges past what Ch8+ confirms about Lee — requirement 6 says
   build only from that. Cut it or own the device deliberately.
3. **The library contrivance** — Mrs. Kalani happening by with flyers (ch02
   l.102–105) is thin; the drafter flags it themselves (DRAFT-NOTES q.6).
4. Ch4 still hand-waves Kekoa's Monday schooling (carried [CHECK], DRAFT-NOTES
   l.150–152).
