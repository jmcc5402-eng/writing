## 1. THE REVIEW

Outlet: **Kirkus Reviews (starred)** — chosen because Kirkus's starred
middle-grade reviews are the ones librarians, parents, and jacket copy
quote, and its register (present tense, plot-forward, an italic
verdict line) suits a fair-play mystery.

> **THE PETROGLYPH MYSTERY**
> _Spytwins, Book 1_ — [TK author] ([TK publisher], [TK pub date])
> ★ Starred review

"The first thing Blazer did on Maui was declare war on a sand crab."
From its opening line, this series debut knows exactly what it is:
warm, quick, and sneakily precise. Twins Amanda and Andrew Wilson have
three months on Maui while their consultant parents work — long enough
to meet Kekoa, the teenage drone pilot next door; his grandmother Mrs.
Kalani, of the cultural center down the road; and the petroglyphs
carved into the black rock at their own property line. Then the
center's prize, a bone fishhook carved five centuries ago, vanishes
from beneath working cameras, and Amanda's sketchbook holds something
impossible: the stolen hook's exact shape, carved into a hillside half
a mile away.

What follows is a fair-play mystery of uncommon integrity. Amanda
reads patterns and leaps; Andrew photographs, measures, and doubts.
★ When Amanda misreads a spiral and her brother's photos correct her,
she grins and concedes, "Okay. That's better than what I said" — and
in seven words the book pulls off what most series never manage: two
kinds of smart, both necessary, neither embarrassed by the other. The
clues are laid where any attentive reader can walk them — a spiral
that is not quite the same spiral, footprints half full of rainwater,
security cameras that went dark for exactly one hour, and Andrew's
early deduction, stated plainly: "I'm just saying it's not luck.
Nobody is that lucky." ★ By the time the twins spring their trap in a
carved chamber under the mountain, sharp readers will be shouting at
the page because they got there half a step ahead — which is, of
course, the whole point. ★ And the showdown delivers the rarest
middle-grade thrill: a kid looks a beaten expert in the eye, and the
man who staked his career on these carvings can only stammer, "You...
you read them too? It took me two *years*..." Kids will gasp. So did
we.

Maui is no backdrop. Lilikoi and shave ice, lava tubes and sacred
sites, and a mystery that could not be moved to any other island on
earth: its map is carved into this one. The book even earns its
conscience — a half-truth sits in Amanda's stomach "like a swallowed
ice cube," and the lie costs her something real before the end.

Then there is the wink. Asked how she knows so much about security
systems, the twins' mother — who has just explained exactly how camera
dead zones work — laughs "a bit too quickly" and offers coconut pie.
★ The twins shrug it off ("Sometimes you guys are so weird"); the
grown-up sharing the read-aloud will sit up straight, having just been
handed a secret the heroes don't have — and, apparently, forty books
to keep it in.

★ With Tokyo winking from the final pages and a family secret
engineered to run forty volumes, this is that rarest thing: a series
opener that reads like a promise — and shows every sign of keeping it.

_A fair-play stunner with a double bottom: kids get the mystery,
parents get the wink, everybody gets Book 2._ (Mystery. 8–12)

## 3. FINDINGS

### (a) ★ moments no current super concept covers — candidates

- **CONCRETE SCENE — "The Two-Years Scene."** Every book climaxes in a
  named beat where a twin says aloud the thing an adult expert spent
  years failing to see, and the adult's certainty breaks on the page.
  Book 1 already owns it (ch10, "It took me two *years*"). SC2 covers
  the method; nothing names the payoff *image*. This is the series'
  cliff-catch.
- **CONCRETE SCENE — "The Kid-Sprung Trap."** The capture itself is a
  trap the twins design and spring on the page — the ending image
  belongs to the kids, every book. Currently the scene belongs to Mrs.
  Kalani's tracker (T09). ★2 and ★5 both die without it; no SC states
  it as a scene obligation.
- **The costly truth.** The lesson must cost a twin something real, on
  the page ("swallowed ice cube" → a paid consequence). S06 is a
  ledger rule, not a concept; the emotional spine has no SC.

### (b) Super concepts generating no ★

None sits at zero, but **SC3 never generates a ★ alone** — it tingles
only when fused with the mystery (the map carved into the island;
★3/★5 shared credit). Not yet suspect, but watch Book 2: if Tokyo's
setting doesn't *produce the puzzle*, SC3 is decoration. Consider
sharpening its chapter test toward "the place IS the puzzle."
