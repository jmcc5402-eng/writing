# Spytwins — The Concept (Locked)

_This is the finalized, canonical concept for the series. It formalizes the
author's original notes ("Book template notes") into a single reference. Where a
decision resolves an inconsistency across older drafts, it is flagged as a
**[Canon decision]**._

---

## Logline

Twin siblings travel the world with their globe-trotting consultant parents,
solving a mystery in every new city using a growing kit of investigative skills
and gadgets — never realizing that their parents are secretly spies for a
mysterious government agency.

## The one-sentence hook

_"Nancy Drew meets a family of undercover spies — except the kids don't know
their parents are the spies... yet."_

## Audience & tone

- **Primary readers:** ages 8–12 (middle grade). Book 1 reads squarely for
  ~9–11-year-olds.
- **Tone:** warm, funny, adventurous, and smart. Real stakes but never scary;
  problems are solved with brains, teamwork, and curiosity — not violence.
- **Values baked in:** family, cultural respect and curiosity, honesty (the
  twins wrestle with when they've bent the truth), perseverance, and learning.
- **Educational bonus:** each book teaches something real — a culture, a place,
  a skill, a bit of science or technology — without ever feeling like homework.

## Why it works as a franchise

The series has a **repeatable engine** (new city + new mystery + new skill each
book) sitting on top of a **serialized secret** (the parents' true identity).
That combination gives it both the endless-episodes quality of a long kids'
series *and* a binge-worthy long arc — which is exactly what makes IP travel
well into graphic novels, comics, and screen.

---

## The master arc — 40 books, 4 mini-series of 10

The series is designed as **four ten-book mini-series**, each raising the stakes
and the twins' capabilities:

| Arc | Books | Focus | Skills & tools | Stakes |
|-----|-------|-------|----------------|--------|
| **Arc 1 — First Cases** | 1–10 | Introduce the characters and the world. One self-contained mystery per city. | Basic investigative & technical skills (observation, patterns, research, simple gadgets). | Local mysteries — missing artifacts, sabotage, small-town trouble. |
| **Arc 2 — Wider World** | 11–20 | More advanced skills, more exotic and challenging settings. | More sophisticated techniques; the twins get genuinely capable. | Bigger, more complex cases; higher personal risk. |
| **Arc 3 — The Gadget Years** | 21–30 | Mom and Dad gift increasingly cool gadgets — **one signature gadget per book.** | A defining spy-gadget drives each story's solution. | Cases start brushing up against the parents' hidden world. |
| **Arc 4 — The Rescue** | 31–40 | **The twist pays off: the parents are kidnapped, and the twins must save them.** | Everything they've learned across 30 books, put to the ultimate test. | The parents' lives; the truth about who they really are. |

### The central secret (the "iceberg")

The parents, **Erin and Adam**, present as management consultants who relocate
the family every few months for client work. **They are actually operatives for
a mysterious government agency.** This is seeded quietly throughout (Mom knows
too much about security-camera blind spots; "clients" insist on meetings that
can't happen over video; Uncle Jacob's oddly worldly expertise) and pays off
across Arc 4.

**[Canon decision]** The reveal is a slow burn. Early books plant small,
deniable clues the reader can enjoy on a re-read; the twins themselves don't
piece it together until late in the series.

---

## The per-book formula

Every book is a **standalone mystery** (a new reader can start anywhere) that
also advances the twins' skills and the long arc a little. Each book:

1. Drops the family in a **new city** for a few months' "consulting" assignment.
2. Presents a **local mystery** rooted in that place's real culture/geography.
3. Has the twins solve it using **a skill or gadget** that levels up from the
   last book.
4. Delivers a **lesson learned** by one of the twins.
5. Ends with a **hint of the next destination.**

The detailed checklist, structure, and idea menus live in the
[series bible](../series-bible/story-engine.md).
