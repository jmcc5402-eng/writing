# Spytwins — Go-to-Market Roadmap (Agile)

> **[Author decision, 2026-07-28] Strategy pivot: the KU-velocity path.**
> Spytwins goes the continuous-delivery route (the Emerson / *6th Grade
> Ninja* model), AI-centric production at a target of one book per month:
> **no launch until four books are done**, then a continuous cycle of
> release and marketing — Kindle Unlimited, series velocity, parent-facing
> channels. Traditional querying is off the table for this book; the
> author's stated goal is work in the world on a continuous cadence, not
> a multi-year long-shot. Operating playbook:
> `studio/gtm/spytwins-ku-velocity-2026-07-28.md`. The format ladder below
> still describes the long game; the 2026-07-28 GTM overview's audit of
> this doc still applies (the webcomic rung remains COPPA-broken).

_The plan for bringing Spytwins to market one small, validated increment at a
time. The goal of each stage is to **learn something and de-risk the next
stage** — not to bet everything up front. Formats stack: each success makes the
next format easier to sell._

---

## The core idea

Treat Spytwins like a startup building a product. Ship the **smallest valuable
thing**, get it in front of real readers, learn, and reinvest. The natural
format ladder the author described:

> **Kids' book series → graphic novel → comic → TV / screen**

Each rung reaches a bigger audience and costs more to make — so we climb only as
each rung proves demand.

## Stage 0 — Foundation _(in progress)_

**Goal:** one clean, canonical source of truth and one finished book.
**Increment:**
- ✅ Concept, series bible, and character canon locked (this repo).
- ✅ Book 1 cleaned and continuity-fixed.
- ⏳ A proofreading/polish pass on Book 1.
**Done when:** Book 1 is a clean manuscript you'd be proud to hand to a stranger.

## Stage 1 — Prove the writing (Kids' book series)

**Goal:** validate that kids and parents love the *story*, cheaply.
**Increments, smallest first:**
1. **Beta readers** — get Book 1 in front of ~5–15 kids in the 8–12 range (and
   their parents). Watch where they lean in and where they drift. This is your
   cheapest, highest-value signal.
2. **A pitch package** — a one-page synopsis, the series overview (we have the
   raw material for both), and the first 3 chapters. Reusable for agents *or*
   self-publishing.
3. **Choose a path:**
   - **Traditional:** query literary agents who rep middle-grade series.
   - **Self-publish (KDP):** commission a cover, publish Book 1, and let sales +
     reviews be the market test.
   - A hybrid: self-publish to build proof, then approach agents with traction.
4. **Write Book 2** while Book 1 is being read — a series needs momentum, and
   publishers buy *series*, not one-offs.
**Learn:** Do young readers finish it and ask for the next one? Which characters
and beats land? That tells us whether to invest in richer formats.
**Done when:** you have real reader signal (finished reads, reviews, or an
agent's interest) on at least Book 1.

## Stage 2 — Prove it visually (Graphic novel)

**Goal:** test whether the world is as compelling *illustrated* — the bridge to
every screen format.
**Why now:** middle-grade graphic novels are one of the hottest categories in
kids' publishing, and a graphic novel is the cheapest way to prove the *visual*
IP before anyone spends TV money.
**Increments:**
1. Adapt one strong, visual book (Book 1's lava tubes, surfing, and petroglyph
   map are very illustratable) into a graphic-novel script.
2. Commission sample art / a short proof-of-concept (a few finished pages) before
   committing to a whole book.
3. Pitch or self-publish the graphic novel.
**Learn:** Does the art find an audience the prose didn't? What's the visual
style of Spytwins?
**Done when:** you have a defined visual identity and evidence the illustrated
form sells.

## Stage 3 — Serialize & build a fanbase (Comic / webcomic)

**Goal:** turn readers into a *following* with regular, bite-sized episodes.
**Increments:**
1. Run short comic episodes on a webcomic platform / social channels — low cost,
   high frequency, direct audience feedback.
2. Use the audience you build as **proof of demand** for a screen pitch.
**Learn:** How big and how engaged is the fanbase? A comic following is concrete
leverage in a TV/film room.
**Done when:** you have an audience metric a producer would care about.

## Stage 4 — Screen (TV / streaming)

**Goal:** the big-reach format — an episodic kids' adventure show.
**Why it fits:** the series is *built* episodic (new city + mystery each book)
with a serialized secret (the parents) — the exact shape streamers want for kids'
animation or live-action.
**Increments:**
1. A **series bible** (we already have the spine) + a **pilot script** (adapt
   Book 1).
2. A **sizzle / pitch deck** leaning on everything proved in Stages 1–3.
3. Approach producers, animation studios, or agents with the full track record.
**Done when:** the IP is packaged and pitchable with real proof behind it.

## Guiding principles

- **One increment at a time.** Finish and validate before climbing the next rung.
- **Reuse everything.** The concept doc, series bible, and character canon feed
  every format — write once, adapt many times.
- **Keep books coming.** Every stage is stronger with a growing library behind it.
- **Let readers vote.** Beta reader reactions and early sales should steer where
  we invest next — not guesswork.
