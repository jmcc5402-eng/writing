## Snowflake spine

### One sentence

When painted marks start appearing at dawn on the shutters of a Tokyo
fish-market row and their new friend is blamed,
Amanda and Andrew must decode the marks
before a festival-day vote installs security cameras —
and discover the "vandal" is a little kid repainting, panel by panel,
the shutter mural their late grandmother made before the renovation.

### One paragraph

The Wilsons land in Tokyo, and the twins befriend a kid whose family
runs a stall in the outer fish market [CHECK: Tsukiji Outer Market vs
Toyosu naming] — just as a painted mark appears on a shutter: barely a
crime to the koban, an earthquake to the market.
**Disaster 1:** more marks come, always dawn, always low, and paint on
their friend's hands makes HIM the accused.
**Disaster 2:** the scandalized association schedules a security-camera
vote for festival day — a public clock the twins can't move.
**Disaster 3:** the twins' own evidence (mark height, stencil edges,
rainy-morning timing) points to "someone small who knows the market" —
and the adults hear it as more proof against their small,
market-raised friend.
With one rainy dawn left before the festival, the twins stake out the
shutter row, catch a little kid mid-panel — repainting the mural a late
grandmother once painted across the row — and turn a culprit into a
commission: marks decoded, vote flipped, shutters repainted officially
for the festival. No villain; vandalism-that-isn't.

### The four quarters (locked text, with chapter mapping)

**Q1 (ch1–3).** _"The twins arrive in Tokyo, befriend a kid whose
family runs a fish-market stall, and a painted mark appears on a stall

**Q2 (ch4–6).** _"More marks — always dawn, always low — and while

The pattern hunt: height, stencil edge, rain timing all planted;
Amanda's code lens fails productively; the accusation lands at the
midpoint and the twins publicly commit.

**Q3 (ch7–9).** _"The scandalized association moves to install
security cameras — vote at the festival — and the clues (mark height,

The vote is scheduled; the tuna-auction dawn; the decode breaks open —
the marks are panel keys to a lost row-length mural — and the twins'

**Q4 (ch10–12).** _"A dawn stakeout catches a little kid painting
back, panel by panel, the shutter art their late grandmother made
before the renovation — and the twins turn a culprit into a commission:
marks decoded, vote flipped, shutters repainted officially for the
festival."_
One rainy dawn left; the one-shot stakeout; chopsticks at the drain
grate; the sketchbook; festival day flips the vote into a restoration.
