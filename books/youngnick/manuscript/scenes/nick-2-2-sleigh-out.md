The sunlight was starting to drift in over the rocks that Nick had assembled to
block the cave entrance.

The sleigh was sitting in front of the pile of rocks, just inside the cave.

Nick was actually annoyed at himself. He'd spent the last few hours freeing the
sleigh. He didn't realize until the sleigh was out that he'd spent a lot of
energy on this. And he didn't have any food. After he had unearthed the sleigh
and dragged it to the front of the cave, Nick wolfed down the last two strips of
jerky. But that had been two hours ago.

The sleigh was peculiar, but unless he could eat it, it didn't help his current
predicament. He pushed himself through the rocks at the edge of the cave and
regarded his situation. The sun was out, so that was nice. There was a steep
cliff straight ahead that went down to the raging river. There was a deep
embankment to his right and a ledge to the left that went up a hill. For the
moment, that was his only option.

**((Section about his first few days in Bronston, how things had been bad, no
one was happy))**

He walk the few yards up the ledge and peered over the crest of the rise. It was
a long open area that led down the mountain. It was covered in snow and ended at
what appeared to be a forest a mile or so down the hill. That was good. A forest
meant food and shelter.

Since he didn't have any extra gear, Nick decided to set out. If he could make
it to the forest, he felt confident he could survive.

He took a few steps into the snow, but then fell up to his waist. The snow was
deep, and not packed tightly. This would be a long hike.

Then he remembered...the sleigh! He might be able to ride it down the hill. With
the excitement of seeing the forest, and possibly some cooked rabbit for lunch,
he had forgotten all about it.

**((Describe the sleigh int he day time)) ((Include the carvings where the

He carefully pushed the sled along the ledge up to the top of the hill. He
should be able to sit on top of the snow and possibly sled down the hill.

Other than the dark red stain of the wood, the sled was thypical. It had a long
hand brake in the middle to slow the sled down. When used with dogs or deer the
driver would yell to the animals and then pull on the break. Trained animals
stopped quickly once they felt the extra friction of the hand brake.

At the top of the hill the sun was bright, but it wouldn't be light for long at
this time of year. Nick looked around and noticed an eagle flying in the
direction of the forest, likely heading towards its nest to warms it's eggs.

Nick set the sled at the top of the hill and pushed off. To his good luck, the
sled didn't sink. It had enough surface area to rest on top of the snow. With a
long branch he found in the cave, Nick gave a second big push and the sleigh
started to pick up speed. As he headed for the forest a mile away, Nick started
to pick up speed.

"This might actually work," Nick whispered to himself, still thinking about a
roasting fresh rabbit over a fire.
