The kick sent Nick flying off the cliff. His left arm and shoulder slammed into
a rock below and propelled him to the left. Nick knew he was in trouble. He
clawed the cliff trying to slow his fall, but he was flipping too quickly.

From the corner of his eye he spied the branch of a large bush. He reached out
too late and missed it. By torquing his body though, Nick changed his trajectory
and slammed into a loose grouping of rocks. The water was about 30 feet below.
Nick ended up on his bottom sliding towards the water. He dug his heels in the
ground, but there didn't seem anyway to stop his momentum.

Nick rolled on his stomach and used his hands to slow his descent. He leaned to
the left and saw a hole in the ground, it must be an eagles nest. As his feet
slipped off the cliff, Nick plowed his arm into the eagles nest. The hole was
deep and his arm went in up to his armpit. He stopped quickly, but pain shot
through his arm. He didn't think it was broken, but he could barely feel his
anything below his shoulder.

Nick's arm was stuck in an eagles nest and his waist was hanging off the cliff,
but anything was better than falling into the freezing river only only 8 feet
below. He climbed to his left and spotted a grouping of large rocks. As he
rolled over he looked up to the ledge. He could just make out the silhouette of
a man peering over the ledge. They were looking for him. Luckily, the rocks
blocked the view and he was way too far down for them to follow. They must be
sure he is dead.

Nick laid for a few minutes. What had just happened? Why had they done this? He
knew he wasn't loved by the Grin brothers, but couldn't imagine why they would
kill him.

In the three weeks since his return to Bronston, AK, he'd done three excursions
to the mountains for samples. This one was the riskiest, but they felt it could
have the biggest payoff.

Nick remember the sample, he'd left it by the fire. Not that it mattered
anyways.

Nick glanced up the mountain, it had been about thirty minutes since he'd been
pushed. He considered climbing back up the steep hill, but the drop offs were
too sharp. He wouldn't be able to climb up without equipment.

Looking left and right, Nick regarded his situation. To the left was an icy
slope that led to the river. To his right were more rocks. That would be his
only chance of getting off this cliff without falling into the water.

The river was certain death. Men fell into freezing rivers on occasion and never
lived. The few times someone climbed out of the water, they never made it more
than a few hours. The water was too cold. Nick's father had always warned that
freezing water shuts down the heart.

Nick edged further to the right. He couldn't see past the largest boulder in
front of him, but if he could climb to the top, he would have a better view of
his surroundings. He couldn't move too fast without sliding the rest of the way
into the river.

As he edged over, he started to think back to the warmer weather of California.
Why had he left? Did he really think he would make any money back in Alaska? It
didn't seem to matter right now. The only thing that mattered now was reaching
the rocks about 4 feet to his right.

Suddenly, the dirt below him gave way and he started to slide. Without thinking,
Nick twisted his body and lunged for the rocks. With both arms he grabbed onto a
large rock . He realized that if this rock came loose, he would fall directly
into the river.

It held.

Nick pulled himself up to the rock, this was way too much adventure for one day.

From this vantage Nick finally felt safe. There was a rocky hill that seemed
climbable. For the moment, Nick stopped to catch his breath.

Laying on the boulder, Nick noticed his shoulder was throbbing. He questioned
why he had returned to Alaska. He convinced everyone in California it was for
the money. But that wasn't true. Money would be nice, but he wasn't convinced
any gold remained. Honestly, Nick wasn't sure why he returned. It had been years
since he'd left Bronston, but there were so many questions that remained. The
mine. The city. The Northern Lights. He longed to see them again. And of course,
that conjured memories of Heather. Did he really care about the Northern Lights,
or did they just remind him of her?

He'd only seen her once since he got back from California, that seemed like ages
ago, but in reality it was only a few days earlier. He had seen her across the
town as she left the market, but didn't have the nerve to call out to her. Nick
wasn't even sure she remembered him.

For the last three years, Nick had worked as a lumber jack in Northern
California. Though it could be exciting, he often spent hours by himself.
Remembering his time as a kid with Heather helped pass the time. They used to
spend hours climbing rocks like this. Nick, Heather, and the Grin brothers. They
were quite the group. But that was years ago, before Nicks father died, before
the earthquake, and before the Grins became killers.

A cold wind brought his mind back to the current situation. The sun was setting
now, if he was going to get off this mountain he would need to find shelter
quickly. Night time in Alaska was dangerous, for the cold, and the predators. If
he didn't find shelter, he would surely freeze to death. Thoughts about Heather
would have to wait.

He crawled over a few rocks, being careful that each was firm. Another cold wind
blew making Nick's nose and ears shout with pain. Luckily though, he turned to
his right and saw exactly what he was looking for.

A cave.
