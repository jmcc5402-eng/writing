**Nick flies back devastated. sees the reindeer, tries them out**

With the alvalanche the grins decide to leave early. They star packing the boat
right away. Nick watches from a hiding spot and is devastated. The town will
lose its gold.

He starts to fly back to his camp. He sees a heard of reindeer, the same herd
that mills around his camp. He has an idea. Reindeer are used to pull heavy
sleighs, there is a chance they could pull the heavy gold.

Ties up the reindeer and heads off. He can fly much faster. This might actually
work !

Nick was out of ideas. There wasn’t time to rally the town to charge upon the
Grins hidden dock. Even if he could, the Grins were well armed and obviously not
above harming people. The townspeople didn’t have weapons and were not trained
to fight. And Nick wasn’t trained to lead them.

To make it worse, Nick didn’t have any food. He had returned from the dock to
find his stock of eggs and rabbit legs depleted. He cursed himself again as he
crept through the forest he’d been camping in. He checked on his rabbit traps.
So far, nothing.

Catching game this time of year was difficult. The rabbits buried themselves in
their nests and the polar bear were out on the ice. Nick felt like he was the
only animal in the forest. Except maybe the occasional caribou he sighted. But
that was a dream unlikely to come true. Caribou were huge and required much more
than a simple trap to catch.

Gazing into the woods, Nick saw a large male sauntering along, probably looking
for berries. Nick remembered when he was a kid and the mine was at its peak. Men
would tie up dozens of caribou to huge sleighs to haul the tons of dirt from the
mine. The caribou were dangerously strong. Once tied together they heave endless
loads of dirt. The hard part was taming the beasts.

Right now, Nick was thinking about eating one, he was starving. He was also dead
tired. He sat on a log and pondered his situation. No food, no gold, ruined
city. The sleigh was his only bright spot. But how could he use it to help the
people? He didn’t even consider the fact that the town must think he had been
dead for weeks. If he showed up now explaining that the Grins had stolen all the
gold, he was sure he’d be accused of taking advantage of the town at their
lowest moment. Besides, how would he explain where he had been?

Nick looked up and noticed the caribou had veered closer to his current
location. It wasn’t normal for them to come this close to humans. Except, of
course, when they were strapped to a bridle. He had a length of rope with him.
Would it be useful to rope this huge animal? For what reason, he thought. Nick
considered the rocky terrain around the Grins cabin. If he could distract the
Grins somehow, would the caribou be able to haul the gold up the hill? It would
never work, he thought, shaking his head. He would never have time to load the
gold. And the Grins were sure to be at the dock preparing their departure.

Nick continued to brainstorm. If he loaded less gold and the sleigh could
levitate an inch or two, the caribou might be able to provide enough power to
get them up the hill. In a perfect situation that might work, but what about
distracting the Grins?

If only the caribou could fly. That would make life easier. But as long as Nick
could remember, he’d never encountered a flying caribou before, or a horse, cow,
or pig...as the saying went. But, Nick thought, he’d also never encountered a
flying sleigh before three weeks ago.

What would happen if he tied the caribou, or multiple caribou, to the front of
the sleigh? Common sense dictated that a sleigh that could not lift multiple
crates of gold would not be able to lift mutiple large animals either. But Nick
decided he’d had enough of common sense. This was worth a try. A half dozen
caribou could haul almost a ton of dirt. Who says two caribou wouldn’t be able
to pull ten crates of gold? Caribou increase the pulling power of a sleigh on
the snow. Why wouldn’t caribou increase the pulling power of a sleigh through
the sky?

Nick knew he had lost his mind, but he also felt it was worth a try. It couldn’t
make anything worse.

In a matter of hours, he had strung up three caribou to the front of the sleigh.
The animals weren’t happy about it, but they eventually succumbed to the rope
and powerful knots Nick had tied.

There was a reason that caribou were used to pull sleighs. They had a natural
desire to move forward. So it wasn’t difficult for Nick to entice the animals to
get started. They started at a slow walk, whisking the sleigh behind them on the
snow, almost without effort.

The team approached a clearing and Nick reached for the lever. If this worked,
they’d be flying through the air, if not, the sleigh would likely yank the
caribou’s neck. It wouldn’t be pretty, they would likely stampede and drag Nick
along with them. He decided there wasn’t time for second guessing, if the
caribou charged off, it was probably fate.

With a quick look up to ensure a clear takeoff, Nick pulled back on the lever
and yelled ‘Ho…!’. For a moment Nick was disappointed. The sleigh crept off of
the ground, but it did not appear to affect the caribou. He could land now and
give up, proceed with plan B. But he knew, there was no plan B. So with a second
call to the caribou, Nick pulled back hard on the lever as far as it could go.

The rear of the sleigh kicked up as the front was pulled down by the weight of
the three large animals. Nick’s hands clenched the sleigh as he was lifted
almost horizontal, staring at the ground twenty feet below. Only the force of
the sleigh’s movement kept him pinned to the sleigh.

But then something happened. As fast as the rear of the sleigh had kicked up,
the front of the sleigh bucked up and Nick was jolted backwards. Much like his
initial flight, Nick found himself sitting on the back bench. He looked over the
sides, expecting to see caribou hanging by their necks dragging behind the
sleigh, but he saw nothing.

Nick lept to his feet and gazed in amazement at what was in front of him. The
sleigh was flying through the air at top speed. Directly in front of him were
three flying caribou pulling him along.
