A slow rain had started. This could be bad. If Nick had to spend the night in
wet clothes he would surely catch pneumonia. Of course, if he slipped, he'd
spend the rest of his short life at the bottom of the river.

Concentrating on every move, Nick continued across the cliff towards the cave.
He couldn't afford to grab a loose rock, or slip in the icy mud.

The river below him was still daring him to fall.

At the moment he was resting his body on a collection of rocks about six feet
from the opening to the cave. The opening to the cave was to his left and a
steep decline down to the raging river to the right. He almost hopped inside the
cave, happy to take a rest, but his instincts made him stop. No reason to rush,
he didn't want to make it this far just to be torn apart by a polar bear or
wolverine.

**((Woliverne jumps out at him, but falls down the cliff, or nick wacks him))**

After a few minutes of waiting and listening, he felt it was safe to proceed.

The cave had a base about six feet wide by three feet deep, leading to the
opening. This area seemed to be sturdy so he stopped to catch his breath. He
looked at the opening to the cave, about six feet wide and five feet tall. He
would be able to walk, but would need to crouch down. At this moment, crouching
didn't seem to be much of a setback.

The cave was set in the rocks in a position that it couldn't be seen from above
and might not be visible from either side. This made Nick feel a little safer on
the off chance the Grins came looking for him.

He could see about eight feet into the cave before it became pitch black. The
first thing he saw gave him a glimmer of hope. There were pine needles packed
into the bottom corner of the cave. Animals in this area knew to make pine nests
in caves like this. The pine needles would offer some warmth and insulation..

Caves like this got very cold so it wouldn't buy him much to continue deeper
into the mountain. Because the cave was so dark, he didn't attempt to crawl very
far back. He knew the best course of action would be to start a fire. But after
a quick look around, Nick erased the thought from his mind. He didn't have any
wood, let alone kindling to make a spark.

Nick considered himself an expert woodsmen. Growing up in Alaska until he was
ten and spending the next ten years in the woods of California had provided all
the experience needed to live in the wilderness. However, this situation was
somewhat different. The weather here was deadly. Staying dry and out of the wind
was his only need.

**((Intro into Nick and Knots))**

Without a fire, the first step to making it through the night was to block the
entrance from the wind. He gathered rocks for about twenty minutes and stacked
them in front of the entrance. With the wind no longer blowing, Nick started to
make a bed. The needles made for good insulation so he gathered as many as he
could. He packed them in sleeves and pant legs. He wanted to take off his shoes,
but worried his toes would get too cold.

**((Nick sees the Northern Lighths, Nick remembers Heather))**

Feeling somewhat content with his sleeping arrangement, Nick laid down for the
night. As his head lowered to the ground, he remember he had some jerky in a
pocket. He hadn't noticed how starving he was. Of the four strips he had, he
decided to eat two. This food wouldn't last him long, but he knew he wouldn't be
able to sleep on an empty stomach. After slowly savoring the jerky, he closed
his eyes. Within seconds he was asleep.

Nick's dream was very simple that night. He was sleighing back down the
mountain. He could see the base camp ahead of him and smell meat on the outdoor
grill. The red glow of the grill was getting bigger as he got closer.

Suddenly he woke up. He wasn't on a dog sleigh, he was still in the cave.
However, he could still see the red glow of the grill from his dream. It seemed
to be coming from deeper within the cave. He turned towards the entrance, but it
was pitch black. He had no idea what time it was. How long had he slept?

Nick focused his attention on the red glow coming from the cave. It wasn't
bright enough to light a path, but it was definitely there. What could it be?

It wasn't the color of a fire and it didn't seem to be that far away. It was a
dull reddish hue. Nick slowly stood from his sleeping position. He entire body
ached. There was no sense laying still he thought. His mind was racing. What
could be making that glow?

He carefully stepped deeper into the cave. He could almost stand up straight, he
estimated the ceiling to be about five and a half feet, about seven inches
shorter than his six foot frame. As he moved in closer, the light got larger. He
still couldn't make out what it was. Could it be buried?

He stepped slowly, barely able to see. He used the rocky wall on the right for
balance. More than once, Nick's foot got snagged by a rock protruding from the
ground. He almost tripped a few times, but eventually found himself standing in
front of a large mound of dirt blocking the path to the cave.

The red glow was coming from beneath the dirt.

The wind blew against the rocks at the opening of the cave and Nick remember
that his clothes were mostly soaked from the fall. Once the sun came up survival
would be his highest priority, but for the moment he was entranced by this
glowing mound of rock and dirt.

Nick had never seen anything like this. The dirt felt warm and he was very cold,
so he started to dig at the dirt with his hands. As he brushed the dirt and
rocks to the ground, the glow got brighter. His hand finally came upon something
that didn't fit. It wasn't a rock, this appeared to be metal or maybe something
softer, like wood. He dug around the glowing piece in the dirt. As the dirt
moved away, he started to get an idea of what this was. It was a handle of some
sort.

Nick spent the next number of minutes digging away at the handle. He found a
rock on the ground that helped him move the dirt and gravel faster. It didn't
take long to get a better idea of what this was. Below the handle was a piece of
glowing wood. He kept digging.

It finally made sense, but it made no sense at all.

In the middle of the Alaskan mountain side, glowing in the dirt of a cave, was
an old sleigh. A red glowing sleigh.
