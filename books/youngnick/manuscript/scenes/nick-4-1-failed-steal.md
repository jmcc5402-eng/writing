Two days had passed since Nick discovered the towns gold hidden in the secret
vault concealed in the cliff. For most of that time, Nick had remained hidden
behind the bush at the top of the cliff observing the situation. The Grins had
just returned. The town assumed they went to Juno, but now Nick didn’t know what
to think.

The cabin and vault were left alone most of the day. Nick continually kicked
himself for not running down and retrieving the gold. But each of these thoughts
was interrupted by someone. During the first afternoon, Duff had arrived and
continued the construction he had started on the dock. He was building a plank
that bypassed the dock. The new plank would reach from the ground directly to
the boat. Nick assumed this was for transporting the entire stock of gold.

At night, Duff was joined by Dwayne, another of the workers from the mine. They
spent each evening playing cards and drinking whiskey. They never paid attention
to the mountain cave safe. Nick wondered if Donald knew about the high quality
security work being provided.

Nick lay still behind the shrubs at the top of the cliff. The vantage point was
excellent, but spying on the camp would not get the towns gold back. He needed
to act. Tonight would be the night.

Nick slipped down the path from his viewing point and edged along the mountain
side until he reached the shrubs that concealed the vault. The shrubs blocked
the view of the vault from most people , but in this case it also obscured Nick
from the cabin. With the key he took two days earlier, Nick inched the door
open, hoping to avoid the high pitched screech. Nick felt the freezing rock
scrape along his back as he squeezed through the door into the vault.

The plan was simple. Load the gold, at least as much as could fit, from the
vault to the sleigh. The sleigh was currently at the top of the cliff, Nick
decided to preposition the gold by the door before he flew the sleigh into
position. That way, if something went wrong, the sleigh wouldn’t be exposed.

After 15 minutes of work, twelve crates of gold were stacked by the door to the
vault. Nick slid out of the metal door and crouched behind the bushes. With his
knees in the snow, Nick gazed over to the cabin ensuring Duff and Dwayne were
still occupied. After a deep breath, Nick rose towards the cliff and the sleigh.

At that moment however, he saw Duff stand up and turn towards the vault. Nick
crouched behind the shrubs, he was a sitting duck! If he tried to scurry back to
the sleigh, Duff would see him. What was going on? While watching the dock the
last two days, Nick never noted the guard making regular checks on the safe.

Nick watched as Duff walked slowly towards the vault. He cursed himself for
being in this situation. He didn’t have a gun or even a knife. Nick waited
silently, watching Duff approach. He covered his face with his scarf, when Duff
got close he would jump him, push him down, and sprint up the hill to the
sleigh.

Nick could not believe this turn of events. If the Grins found out someone, let
alone Nick, was creeping around the vault, they would surely pack up the gold
and leave. Nick was worried about getting pummeled by the two guards, but most
of all he was sad for the town.

Duff turned and yelled something back towards the house. It didn’t seem like he
was being very security conscious. Then he slowed down and turned toward the
mountain. He was about 15 feet from Nick, but mostly concealed by another row of
bushes. Was there a second secret vault? Nick kicked himself for not checking.
Could that be a weapons vault? Nick would have to give himself up if Duff had a
gun. What would happen if the sleigh got into the hands of the Grins?

Nick turned his head when he heard something unexpected. Was there a stream over
by Duff? Was he coming to get water?

Suddenly it became clear. Nick had to cover his mouth to muffle a snicker. Duff
wasn’t coming over to check on the safe, he was peeing.

Nick slowly got up when Duff returned to the porch. Then he heard louder
talking, what was going on? Nick looked closer and saw that Jim Grin had arrived
at the cabin. He had taken the cliff road along the coast. What was he doing
here? He’d come once while Nick watched. This was not good, Jim would probably
want to check the safe.

At that moment Nick felt something under his feet. Huh? The ground started to
shake. An earthquake.

Nick fell to one knee, this was pretty bad. He fell back on the safe door, the
door slammed shut. The Grins would have certainly heard the sound, but when Nick
glanced over he saw them running into the house. Not smart in an earthquake, the
cabin didn’t look very sturdy.

From above the house, Nick heard a thunderous rumble. Nick imaged a stampede of
horses, and they were coming straight for him. Nick looked up and cringed.
Avalanche.

The avalanche 10 years ago changed the fate of this town forever. Would this one
change it again? Nick was starting to think fate was not on his side. All he
wanted to do was help this town that he loved. But it didn’t seem that Mother
Nature was on his side.

A high pitch crack skewered Nick’s eardrums. He covered his head and dove for
cover. When he got his bearings, he realized this was a familiar sound. He
scanned the area and ensured he was not in danger. There was a large oak tree in
front of the house that had been uplifted by the earthquake. The top of the
leaning tree had too much weight and snapped the trunk like a twig.

The huge tree clobbered the ground in front of the cabin. Maybe the Grins were
smart to rush inside because a giant limb shattered the porch where the men had
been sitting. It appeared that the front door was blocked shut. This might buy
Nick some time.

Nick scurried up the mountain back to the sleigh. With the Grins trapped in the
cabin, he wasn’t going to give up on the gold so easily.

Ten minutes later, the sleigh was packed full with twelve crates of gold. Nick
glanced back at the cabin, but didn’t see any movement. The guys were either
content to stay inside or couldn’t get out. Either worked for Nick.

Nick mounted the sleigh and pulled the handle to take off. The sleigh jerked and
shuttered. Then stopped. That hadn’t happened before.

Nick leaned forward, yanked the handled again. The sleigh’s front lifted off the
ground, but then slumped back to the ground. What was going on? Nick tried a
third time. Nothing. This could not be happening. The sleigh had worked
perfectly for three weeks and this is when it decides to have mechanical
difficulty!

Nick looked around frantically. How could this be happening? Did the avalanche
damage the esleigh? He jumped off and inspected the machine. He didn’t see any
damage.

Nick glanced towards the cabin. The front door was blocked, but Duffs armed was
flailing out the opening trying to make room. Nick knew they would check the
gold the moment they got out.

Nick’s temper got the best of him and he stomped his foot on floor of the
sleigh. At that moment there was a small aftershock. Three of the crates fell of
the sleigh and landed in the snow. As Nick turned to inspect the fallen crates,
he pulled on the ignition handle. The sleigh lurched forward. This time further
than before. Nick whipped his head back to the front. He tried the lever again.
The sleigh took off into the air for a brief moment. Nick glanced at the gold in
the snow. Was the extra weight of the gold affecting the sleigh? He hadn’t
considered whether the sleigh could handle heavy loads.

When the sleigh landed, Nick stacked the rest of the crates on the ground and
attempted to take off. The sleigh elevated into the air without delay. Nick’s
mind raced with possibilities. What should he do? Should he take multiple trips
to the top of the cliff? It would only take about five minutes.

Nick heard yelling. The Grins were sawing a branch through the front window of
the cabin. Where did they find a saw? The branch held the front door closed. If
they cut the branch, they would be free.

It appeared a fire had started in the cabin. This must have kick started them
into action. Nick knew that fires were the main cause of death from an
earthquake.

Moments later, the three men stumbled out of the front door. Climbing through
the rest of the fallen tree, they crawled onto the gravel walkway in front of
the house. James Grin stood up and yelled, ‘well, don’t just stand there, check
the vault!’

Duff bolted for the vault, but realized he didn’t have the key. James had one on
his belt loop. He handed it to Duff, who dashed off for the vault thirty yards
away.

The vault door creaked open and Duff gasped. Jim started to jog over and yelled,
“What’s wrong?”

“Nothing boss, no worries,” Duff replied. “It just looks like the crates fell
off the racks during the shake up. One, two,” he started counting. “...five,
six...yeah, no worries, looks like their all here.”

“Well, ‘looks like it’ won’t cut it for Donald. Stack them all up and do a
proper count.” James said, already knowing the Donald was probably having a
heart attack wondering about the gold.

James poked his head into the vault. The gold crates were scattered all over the
floor. Some of the gold had spilled out, but Duff was right, it looked like most
of it was here. ‘Man,’ James thought, ‘the earthquake really shook the mountain
good.’

The wind whipped in his face, Nick was furious. How did he not think of testing
the sleigh with a large load? He had spent years hauling logs in the forest and
one of the first rules was to not overload your trailer. Now the Grins, and the
towns gold, were leaving in a day or two and nothing would stop them. They were
probably going to leave right away, after this shock. Nick couldn’t think of
anything that would stop them. This would be the end of the city. Everyone would
starve.

Nick remembered he still had the sleigh. He could find a way to help the people,
even if he couldn’t return the gold they had broken their backs for. He could
help them trade with the Inuits or maybe haul food from the city. The sleigh
could be used in the mine. Maybe he could haul small amounts of gold to the bank
in Juno. This time they would get real cash in return. It would take a while,
but he could help the town get back on its feet.

The next moment Nick’s optimism disappeared. The smile under his scarf was
replaced as his eyes widened and his mouth hung open in disbelief. He was
passing over the mine, but something was very wrong.

He didn’t think things could get any worse, but they just did4. There would be
no hauling gold for the mine. There would be no nothing for the mine.

As Nick circled around the mine he saw a cacophony of dirt, wood, and snow mixed
together. The entrance to the mine was not just covered, the entire mine had
caved in.
