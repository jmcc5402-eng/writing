“How were the numbers this week?” Don asked.

“Down again,” James responded gazing down at his feet. He rarely looked is older
brother in the eye. “We've never been down three weeks in a row, and this is 5.”

Donald looked around the cabin. He hated this place. It was freezing and small.
They owned the mine, but lived like servants.

“Alright then, this is the time.” Don said. “It's time to wrap things up and
head for the mainland. If someone finds out the mine is cashed, we’ll have a
heck of a time on our hands. And probably our necks in a noose.”

The two started talking about the preparations. The boat was ready at the secret
dock. They would pack what little they had, head to the secret dock, load the
gold, and be on there way.

They would stop at port Lola about 80 miles south for supplies for the long
journey to San Francisco. Then it was inland.

**((CHange to dialogue, like: ‘You thought more about what we’re gonna do after
Frisco” James said.’))** They weren't 100 percent sure of the plan after San
Fran, but they both felt heading east was safest. The last thing they wanted was
to run into anyone from Bronson, or even Alaska, again.

**((Add dialoge to explain))**They didn't feel terrible about leaving the town.
In fact, it was almost a gift. The town could take over the mine, they would
have more control. At least that is what Don told his younger brother to
rationalize the plan. They both knew the mine had been losing money for a while
and the gold was drying up, if not gone.

The Grins had been padding the totals so the town folk wouldn’t know the mine
was going dry. Donald carefully advertised certain numbers to the miners. Though
each individual was bringing in less gold, the mine was working more efficiently
and the overall totals were higher, he would say.

“We’re just worried,” a group of miners confided in Donald one afternoon the
previous spring. “We’re spending every dime of our savings to live up here and
our gold is going to some bank. It seems like we’re pulling in less metal every
week.”

In these situations Donald shined. He retrieved from his desk detailed accounts
of the gold mined over the last months. He spoke fast and used a lot of numbers.
Most of the miners didn’t have much education so they believed everything he
said. When Donald showed them a graphic of the ‘increased production’ over the
last few months, every nodded with approval.

Don would describe the payout from the bank. “With the coming annuity payout,
each participant will receive four payments over a one year period.” He would
calculate a few numbers on a piece of paper and then in a big show, circle the
final number. Holding the paper above his head he was exclaim, “Each participant
will walk away with four payments of $24,000. How does that sound?”

It wasn’t hard to convince people. The more gold found now would mean more money
in the pocket once the annuity was returned. In less than 18 months, each miner
would be in control of almost $100,000.

The sound of footsteps on the front porch made both men freeze. Jim grabbed his
gun, “who the hell could that be,” he whispered. Don went to the door and
quickly opened it, leaving Jim and his gun a clear shot at the visitor. Don
noticed Jim's look of confusion and slowly peered around the door.

He never expected to see the person standing in the doorway.
