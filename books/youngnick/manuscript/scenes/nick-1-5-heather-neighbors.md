The market was usually crowded on the weekends, but recently things had been
slow for everyone. Heather had just dropped off her letter for the ferry company
at the general store and was considering picking up extra flour.((Excitement,
apprehension for going, etc))Her father's birthday was the next week and she
would love to make him a cake. They couldn't afford to spend extra money on
desserts, but her father was famous for his sweet tooth so it might be worth it.

Growing up her mother would make delicious cakes with flour and chocolate from
the Inuit traders. Heather would wonder where the natives got chocolate from
because it doesn't grow in this cold climate. Her father always said, 'Anyone
can get anything, once gold is discovered. Men will travel thousands of miles to
take part in the fever.'

Heather had read how the Inuits were master tradesmen. They'd been doing it for
a thousand years. The traders traveled all of the land trading from port to
port. Most miners went home penniless, without a pinch of gold. The traders,
however, were happy to have an endless supply of customers. ((Possible story
Heather read about traders in school?))

Heather heard old Rob, the store clerk, talking with a few other men. Miners
weren't working on Saturday recently and everyone was worried the mine was going
dry. But this conversation was more upbeat. The men said some of the telltale
words,

'sample',

'success',

'2-3 years of metal in the ground',

'...well past the summer and the payout',

'...just fine until the new machinery is here.'

"Money?", one of the men belted out. "According to the news, we're gonna have
more money than we ever dreamed of. The Grins just returned and the sample was
positive. They said the last time a sample was this clear, we found more than 18
pounds over the next six months."

"The whole town is going ape. They can't wait to get up there and start the
machinery."

This news made Heather a bit less apprehensive about paying for the university.
When the annuity was due next summer, many folks were worried the mine would be
tapped. Heather secretly smiled and thought, 'it sounds like there will be
steady income for another year at least. Maybe then she could convince daddy to
join her on the mainland.'

Heather wondered about the 'annuity' that was held in the Juno Bank, located in
Juno, Alaska. The bank was a partner with Grin mining, the company that owned
the mine in the town, and where Jim had worked for the last twenty five years.
Four years earlier, Grin Mining made a financing deal with Juno Bank. The bank
provided funds for six hydraulic drills. In return, Grin created an annuity with
its employees as collateral.

**((Details on the annuity))**

"In only five years we'll be 'fully vested', then we will be partners in the
mine", Jim had told a skeptical Heather four years earlier.

The annuity allowed for each employee to be fully vested in five years, but
until then, 75% of their earnings would be retained by the bank collecting
interest.

The town had learned to live meagerly. Especially the last few years, as the
price of food had skyrocketed. An avalanche had closed off the pass to get to
Juno so all cargo and travelers had to go by boat. This was very expensive.

As she was walking out, she noticed the men lowered their voices.

"It was a tragedy. And he had just returned a week or two ago. I hadn't even
seen the boy, except for once before they went off on the expedition."

The men stopped talking and smiled as Heather approached the counter. She had
known all of these men since she was a little girl.

"What was the tragedy? I hadn't heard anything," Heather asked.

The group of men all looked at each other and then down at the floor.

"I don't mean to be the bearer of bad news," Old Rob began, '...and you might
not even remember him, but Nick Johnson was killed during the expedition,"

A flood of memories hit Heather at once. Nick had been her best friend for years
until he left when they were twelve. She didn't even know he was back. How long
had he been back? Why hadn't he contacted her?

The small sack of flour fell from her hand and hit the floor. A cloud of dust
billowed up to her knees. She turned and slowly walked out the door. An empty
feeling ran throughout her entire body. Though she would never admit it, she had
always assumed that one day Nick would be the man she married.
