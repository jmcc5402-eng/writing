It was amazing!

The sleigh was cruising about twenty feet off the ground. Nick was in shock. How
could this be?

The ride was smooth and nothing like Nick had ever experienced. Unlike normal
dog sledding, there was an eerie silence. The sound of the snow and wind was all
he heard.

Nick was staring out the side of the sled looking at the ground. The snow below
look more beautiful than he had ever noticed before. It was like an endless
sheet of white. His mother used to brag about how white grandma could make the
bedsheets. She was always jealous. Well, no bedsheet had ever been this white.

How high could he be? At least as high as a tall pine tree, Nick imagined.

Pine trees. Nick had forgotten about the forest he was heading towards. He
looked ahead just in time to notice the line of trees that were fast
approaching. He was 20 feet in the air, but the line of trees was at least 40
feet tall. Worse, he didn't know how to steer a flying sleigh!

He frantically looked around and didn't notice any other controls. Holding the
sides of the sleigh he jerked his body to the left, hoping to change the
momentum of the flying craft. Nothing happened. He tried again. This time he
jerked his body so hard he lost his footing and fell over.

For the second time in an hour he was in a sleigh heading towards certain doom.
At least the first time he could have jump out of the sleigh and only ended up
with a few bumps and bruises. If he jumped out now, he'd break his neck.

To pull himself up, Nick reached for the main hand brake. It didn't stop the
sleigh when he was sledding towards the ravine, at least it could help him up.
Like the first time Nick pulled the lever, he expected it to remain firmly in
place. However, this time it gave way and moved easily.

At the same time the sleigh rotated sharply to the left. Nick grasped the lever
with both hands as the sleigh rotated 90 degrees. Now the floor was almost
vertical! Nick held onto the lever for dear life. His feet slid out from under
him and were dangling in mid air. This wasn't a brake, it was a lever to control
the sleigh in the air!

With the trees fast approaching Nick released one hand from the lever and
reached for the front edge of the sleigh. Nick quietly thanked the California
redwoods for providing him ten years of tree climbing. He slowly pulled his body
up and managed to center the lever. The sleigh slowly rotated back to normal.

Looking up, Nick saw he was still facing the trees. They were coming on fast.
Judging from the turn he just maneuvered, Nick guessed he couldn't turn sharp
enough to miss the trees. He had an idea. Nick pulled back on the lever. The
front of the sleigh started to turn up.

He pulled back further on the lever. The sleigh was now facing upwards about 45
degrees.

He kept his hand on the lever as he felt the tops of the trees just scrape the
bottom of the sleigh.

Two close calls in one day. Two too many.

He moved the handle to the left. He moved it to the right. Nick learned to lean
with the sleigh to sharpen the turns. He could go up and down. This thing was
fun to fly!

There was one problem.

How to land.
