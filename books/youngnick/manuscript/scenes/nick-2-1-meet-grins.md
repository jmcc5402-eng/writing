"Fifty six, fifty seven, fifty....nine, thats about right," said Donald Grin as
he stared at the mechanical scale sitting on the desk in front of him. Donald,
or Don, as most people called him, had been sitting there for over an hour with
his brother James counting the days take of gold. The cabin was cold even with
the fire burning in the corner. Don scoffed at the memory of his father
reminding him to keep the cabin in good repair. "Look," Don said to himself, "it
ain't easy to run this mine, let alone make sure every crack in the windows are
sealed. And Jim sure never does a darn thing without me yelling at him to get it
done." But he knew he only had himself to blame.

James Grin was sitting next to Don and packaging the gold in sacks ((FIND OTHER
WORD)). A few years younger and a few inches shorter, Some would say he was a
few eggs short of a dozen too. James always had trouble staying on task. While
waiting for Don to finish weighing each nugget, James kept himself occupied with
a flyer about New York City.

'Look at this Don, dancers and dinner on every street corner,' James said and
flashed the picture towards Don.

'It won't be long now, brother. We'll be there soon, but get that magazine out
of my face. I need to focus, maybe you could too.' Don shook his head. "Then
maybe we could get home before the path freezes over."

The Grins had been sitting in that little room and counting gold for almost
fifteen years. And before they were counting, they were watching their father
count the gold.

'I'm trying to decide if I'm going to miss this place or not. We've got a lot of
memories here', says James.

'And not a single good one. I hope the name Bronston never crosses my mind
again', scoffs Donald.

Life hadn't been easy for the Grin bros. Their mother died when they were young,
four and six respectively. Since their Father was the owner of the mine, they
had to spend all their time with him at work.

They, especially Donald, grew up feeling under appreciated.. They watched their
dad rule this roost for ten years, but didn't have much to show for it. It
didn't help that growing up the other miners treated Nick Anderson as the
beloved one. His father was the second in command at the mine. And for some
reason, everyone loved him. Except for James and Donald.

'Have you heard from Duff today? We can't go another week without progress. All
this talk about New York won't mean a thing if we can't even make it to Frisco,'
Don blurted out.

'He came by this morning and said we'd be ready in less than two weeks. And once
the cargo is in place we are on our way,' James replied. Then he quietly asked,
"'Has there been any more talk about Nick?"

'What about? He was a great man. Nick Johnson falling off that cliff was the
worst moment of my life. At least thats what I keep repeating around town. There
is nothing left to say about that." Don continued, "Sometimes less men return to
camp than leave. Everyone knows that. And either way, memories are short around
here. Everyone is just thinking about more gold."

Don raised the small sack of gold in his hand.

"Thats been an easy way to change the subject. The best thing we got going for
us is the metal...and this towns endless hope for more it."
