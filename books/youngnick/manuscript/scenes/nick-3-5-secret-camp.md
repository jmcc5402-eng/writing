A week passed and Nick made two more trips to the Grin’s cabin and two more
trips to the town. Last night had a few surprises, while sneaking off of Mrs.
Hannigan’s porch, she opened the door. Nick dashed off her deck and behind her
house. She didn’t follow, but he slouched down in the shadatw for almost an hour
to make sure no one else would see him.

One thing kept bugging him, why did the Grins try to kill him? He knew they were
crooks, but honestly, skimming ten percent of the towns gold was not that bad.
What drives a man to kill? Money...power...love...could that be it?

Nick recalled before he moved to California that Donald Grin had strong feelings
about Heather. During the last months of his time in Alaska, Donald barely ever
spoke with Nick.

“Whats wrong with him?” Nick asked James one day walking home from the creek.
“He’s been a real jerk for the last few months.”

“Well, he’d kill me if I told you, but he’s in love with Heather,” James said,
kicking a rock on the path. “And well, everybody knows that you’re gonna marry
her one day.”

“Married! What are you talking about, I’m never getting married!” Nick crinkled
his nose and they both laughed. They didn’t talk much the rest of the way home.
‘Me and Heather?’ Nick pondered. ‘I don’t plan on staying in this small town. So
if they both want to stay, more power to ‘em.’

Years later while hauling logs in California, Nick regretted that conversation.
Did James repeat to Donald what Nick had said. Or to Heather? Did Heather ever
hear that Nick never wanted to marry her. He wished he knew. What a stupid thing
to say. He should have stood up for himself and said that he was going to marry
Heather. They were gonna move to California and live on the coast. She’d become
a doctor and he’d be a sheriff.

Nick finished his breakfast and decided he needed to take some action. Every
other Friday the Grins load the gold onto their boat and make the trip to the
capital. It couldn’t hurt to get a better idea of what they were up to.

An hour later Nick felt the familiar wisp of cool air on his face as the sleigh
wisped through the air above the Grin mine. The Grin’s caravan of a carriage and
two horses heaved two crates of gold onto the main road towards the dock. The
horses and carriage would be easy to follow along the main road and the arrival
of dusk would provide good cover for the flying sleigh.

About a mile before the town’s dock, the Grin’s small caravan detoured onto a
thin rocky path that paralleled a winding creek. The path headed towards the
cliff road. Nick followed. Twenty minutes later they arrived at a small bay with
a dock and a boat. What was this? A secret dock?)

Nick watched as the Grins loaded the crates of gold off of the carriage onto a
dolly. They rolled the dolley towards a section of the mountain about twenty
feet from the house. James hopped off the carriage and sprinted towards the
small cabin. A few seconds later he reappeared and bolted for the carriage. Nick
could hear Donald making an angry remark. Nicked watch the men move shrubs aside
along the mountain, revealing a large metal door. Very interesting, Nick
wondered to himself, must be a supply room. Or supply cave.

The Grins struggled to push the dolly through the silven doorway. After sometime
the Grins rolled the dolly out of the cave and dragged it over the gravelly path
towards the boat.

There was a long plank connecting the earth and a dock. The dolly barely fit,
but smoothly rolled across the plank and onto the dock. Another plank was fitted
on the dock to load the dolly directly onto a series of storage comparts on the
boat. A wood container with a section about three feet high received the crate
from the dolly.

After loading the gold, the Grins returned to the cabin. The Grins seemed to
follow every difficult task with a healthy dose of rum. Nick guessed he had a
few minutes to survey the boat.

Nick crept along the line of bushes that separated his position from the boat.
Unless the Grins were suspicious of someone, he would probably be safe.

Roughly 30 feet from the plank to the dock, Nick eased himself down the
embankment that led from the path to the water. The rocky decline ending with
splashing water provided a quick flashback to his adventure falling down the
cliff in the mountains. Nick shook his head and decided to focus on the task at
hand. Hopefully, this investigation would give him a better idea to why the
Grins acted so deadly.

Nick slid down the hill and then carefully navigated the rocky terrain until he
reached the dock. He climbed aboard the ship and crouched down to avoid being
viewed from the cabin. His knee scraped along the wooden deck of the boat as he
squirmed towards the payload. Nick grimaced as the the latch on the crate
squeaked loudly. Gratefully, the squeak was muffled by the sounds of the choppy
waves banging on the side of the boat.

Inside the crate, Nick gazed at dozens of sacks of gold nuggets. He inspected a
sack, not exactly sure what to look for. He reached deeper into the crate
squeezing a number of sacks.. Something wasn’t right. The sacks on the bottom
felt different than the first one he inspected.

A large wave rocked the boat to the left and white. Nick’s left leg slipped on
the wet deck and he momentarily lost his balance. Squatting down, he observed
the surrounding area. He couldn’t see anyone in the cabin, but he didn’t see the
Grins anywhere else. He needed to find out what was in the bottom of that
create.

He gripped a bag at the bottom of the crate and pulled it towards the top.
Opening it, he saw black. This was gold, this was coal. He quickly squeezed and
inpsected a number of sacks. The eight at the top were gold, but the two dozen
or so at the bottom were all coal.

Nick was perplexed. He had witnessed them load the gold onto the dolly back at
the mine. He was certain those bags were full of coal. Many had come from the
secret vault under the desk. He estimated at least 20 lbs of gold. This couldn’t
be more than 3. (Just then Nick heard the cabin door open, the Grins were
walking towards the boat)

What was going on here? Why were the Grins hiding coal with the gold?

Nick heard the door to the cabin open. Luckily it screeched louder than the
latch. The Grins were locking the cabin.

Nick hopped back onto the dock and belly crawled off the edge, landing on the
embankment he had descended earlier. He lay completely still behind a rock,
mostly concealed from the dock and the dock. The Grins crossed the plank and
boarded the ship without looking around. Ten minutes later, the medium size
cruiser was heading down the river towards Bronston and the channel.
