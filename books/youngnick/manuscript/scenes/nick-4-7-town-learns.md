Jim hadn’t seen Heather since the earthquake hit and he was starting to worry.
For the last 30 minutes he had been helping piece together Joe’s market shop.
The roof of the market had tumbled off its joists and crushed half of the market
below. Two people were underneath, but after a hurried search, they were found
alive.

When Jim stuck his head into Mrs. Murphys he expected to see Heather inside
helping to clean up, but Mrs. Murphy said that Heather hadn’t shown up that day.
Jim wished her well and turned down the street towards their house.

Most of the houses were only slightly affected by the quake. The fault line must
be further in the mountains or closer to the water.

He rushed into the house and yelled for Heather. No response. Where could she
be, Jim thought, glancing around the house. He knocked on her door and when no
response came, he opened it. He was hoping to see her standing there, but the
room was empty.

On the bed was a large envelope. These must be the papers from the university,
Jim thought. On closer inspection, however, Jim noted the papers had nothing to
do with the school. These were bank papers. Inside the large envelope were a
number of smaller envelopes, addressed to various towns people. Jim knew the
post office occasionally combined letters in a single envelope, especially if
they were damaged or traveling to the outskirts-like Bronston.

Jim turned his head as he heard footsteps patter through the house. “Any site of
her?”, Joe asked poking his head through Heathers doorway. Joe seemed hesitant,
he probably felt uneasy about entering a ladies room.

“She’s not here,” Jim replied. Then holding up the documents he said, “but look
at this, somehow Heather received a package of everyone’s bank holdings. The
Grins always said they receive one invoice for the whole town, I’ve never
received a statement like this before.”

After Joe tore open the letter addressed to him, he replied, “Somethings wrong
with these anyways, this says I’ve only got $52.52 in my account. I swear that
was the same amount I had the day we signed onto the annuity.”

Jim searched for an envelope addressed to him, then his eyes widened, “Do you
think Heather read these? I can’t find our statement anywhere. She said these
were docs from the university. What the heck is going on?”

“I don’t know, but this town needs some answers. Where is the money I’ve been
busting my back for for the last five years,” Joe said. He gather the envelopes
and shoved them into the larger envelope.

The two men heard shouting in the street. When they exited the house they saw a
few other men having a heated discussion. Bill James was panting, he’d just
returned from the mill up mine road. He said the whole thing had collapsed. The
men all decided to go to the mine to see if there was any damage.

Jim asked if anyone had seen Heather. Bill glanced over and said, “I saw her
walking up mine road yesterday, I thought it was a bit odd her heading up there
herself. I asked if she wanted company, but she shot me a look meaner than a
bull dog.”

The earthquake had damaged the mill, what state would the mine be in. The men
were getting riled up. On top of that, Joe had started handing out the bank
invoice. Everyone’s account was empty.

“The Grins started this arrangement with the bank. Where the heck are they now,”
somebody yelled.

The Grins lived up at the mine. Heather had gone to the mine. Suddenly, Jim went
to get his gun. If Heather had gone to confront the Grins, there could be
trouble.

Standing in his doorway he yelled out, “Lets find out whats going on at the
mine. Is it still standing, and lets have a long chat about our money with
Donald Grin”.

The group followed him towards the mine.

CHAPTER

The men discovered the mine cabin buildings empty. In the Grin’s office, they
began rummaging through anything they could find. They uncovered a printing
press that looked like it had been used for printing bank invoices.

“This place has been swept clean. Where the hell are the Grins,” Joe yelled,
standing in the doorway of the cabin.

Jim had just returned from the Grins living bunk. It was empty too. “They’re
skipping town and I think they have Heather.” Jim said, his voice cracking a
bit. He was trying to keep his composure, but he’d never been this scared.

Just then, one of the men ran up from the mine. “You’d better come see this”, he
said, waving the men towards the mine.

As they hustled around the bend towards the mine, Jim considered their options.
They hadn’t seen the Grins anywhere near town. Since the pass was blocked they
couldn’t escape by land. They must be at the dock.

Jim stopped in his tracks. He felt like he got the wind kicked out of him. He
was staring at the entrance to the mine. The place he had worked for almost
twenty years. The place that would pay for a brighter future for his daughter. A
place that put food on the table for every family in this town.

The entrance to the mine, as well as most of the surrounding mountain, had caved
in. The mine was gone.

“The Grins must be leaving by the dock,” Jim said to the group of men. “The boat
was still there this morning, so they are either hiding out, or have already
shipped off. I think they have Heather. I’m begging you men to help me find
her.”

Halfway to the dock, someone pointed to smoke rising over the trees. There was a
fire. This was normal in the dense forest, but only after a lightning storm.
“That smoke is coming from the cliffs, what the hell could burning over there,”
somebody wondered.

When they happened upon an overgrown path with recent wagon trails, Jim got
suspicious. He didn’t believe in coincidences. Why was there a fire burning in
an uninhabited part of the cliffside. “That must be them,” Jim said. His jaw was
clenched tight, he pointed at five of the men from the town, “You head towards
the dock, stop those Grins, tie’em up if you have to. The rest of us are going
to head towards the fire. It might be their camp.”

The cabin appeared to be recently inhabited, even though it was slowly burning
to the ground. A large tree had fallen in front of the cabin, destroying the
porch and blocking access to the front door. One of the men climbed in through a
rear window, but quickly exited after determining the cabin was empty.

Just then, Duff appeared came running down the cliff road. His face was white.
Two of the men grabbed him and brought him to Jim and Joe. Before they could say
anything Duff blurted out, “Donald’s gone mad, I didn’t want to hurt anyone, I
thought we was just taking the gold.”

Jim grabbed him by the collar, “Where is Heather”, he said. His eyes were narrow
and his nose was only inches from Duff’s.

“Up at the cliff! Donald’s got her, thats when I ran, I don’t want to be part of
anybody getting hurt.”

Jim pushed Duff over and ran up the cliff road, Joe and the other men followed
behind.

When he emerged from the trees Jim stopped suddenly. He was about 100 yards from
the tip of the cliff. He could see Donald Grin holding a knife in front of a
tall pine. Behind the pine was someone else. It was Heather.

Jim called out, but the unending sound of the choppy river water drowned out his
voice. He reached out his arm in a futile attempt to stop Donald and started to
run towards the cliff.

Jim was getting old, but could still run well. But in this instance, it wasn’t
fast enough. In the time it took him to cover the 100 yards, he saw Donald jump
around the tree and grab Heather. They were facing towards the river, it
appeared like he was yelling at someone.

He saw a commotion and Heather swing back around the tree. At that moment, the
ground began to shake. Jim’s leg scrape a large rock that had become unsettled
and he toppled to the ground. He heard something crack. Excruciating pain ripped
through his left leg. He tried to the stand, but the tremor and his leg caused
him to roll over in pain.

He gazed up in terror, hoping to see Heather safe. She wasn’t.

Donald Grin had disappeared. The tall pine had uprooted and was hanging off the
cliff. Heather was clinging to the tree, her legs dangling above the deep
crevice above the river. Jim began dragging himself closer to her with his arms.
His leg was bleeding and the movement was painful. He kept his eyes on Heather
and screamed her name.

Heather turned her head towards Jim. He could see her glancing around in fear,
frantically calling for help. He wasn’t sure if she saw him.

And then she fell.
