There was a large rock on a creek bed hidden from road about a half mile from
the town. A flat area had been carved in the rock facing the creek, perfect for
two people to sit and throw stones into the water. Heather sat there alone.

For years, Heather had come here when she wanted to think about her mother. The
sound of the creek flowing over the rock bed below was comforting. No one ever
came around here. Salmon didn't swim in this part of the creek.

But today she wasn't thinking about her mother, she was thinking about Nick
Johnson. In one instant he went from being a glimmer of a memory to being here
in Bronston to being dead. It seemed like a dream. She hadn't seen him in ten
years.

She was ashamed of herself. After she had run out of Old Rob's market, she
hadn't felt remorse that Nick died. She felt hurt. She felt hurt that when he
returned to Bronston he didn't find her. That was why she was ashamed. What type
of terrible person thinks of herself first when hearing that another has died.

Why had he come back? He was in California. Heather had read about the wonderful
climate and the beautiful redwood trees. Why would he return to this mine town?

She wasn't coming back. This place was evil. The mine was evil, the Grins were
evil, gold was evil. Greed seemed to be the only emotion felt by anyone in this
town. Thats why Nick had always held a place in her heart. He was the only one
who wasn't caught up in the fever of gold.

Nick and Heather spent countless hours tossing pebbles into the stream dreaming
about the future. Heather talked about her dreams of becoming a doctor. Nick
imagined adventures he would lead. Gold and money were never a topic. (EXPAND

And now Nick was gone. She had to leave Alaska. She had to find someone else in
this world who wasn't entranced by gold. The moment her fare to the continent
arrived, she would head to the Grins and insist they take her to Juno. If they
refused, she would trek across the pass. The avalanche had closed off the main
pass, but there were other ways. The Inuits could do it. She would hire a guide.
It wouldn't take long, only three or four weeks.

When she arrived in Juno, she would withdraw the money her father had saved and
sail to Seattle. From there is was a short trip to San Francisco. Bronston would
never cross her mind again, except for letters she sent to her father. He would
see that life on the continent was worth the trip. He would come to live with
her. He would be their when she finally became a doctor. He would be their when
she finally cured typhoid.

Together, they would move on with their lives. They would sit and discuss
science and math, just like when she was young. Gold would not be the only topic
of conversation.
