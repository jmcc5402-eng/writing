Nick didn't know what to do. He had an amazing flying sleigh. His trip to the
Inuits had been a success. Should he head over the mountain, back to
civilization and find a normal job? He could just imagine all of the things he
could do with the ability to fly.

One thing was still bugging him. Why did the Grins push him over the edge? He
remembered them being bullies, but didn't think they would become killers.

He put out the fire he had built in the cave. Maybe he could find some answers
at the mine.

The Grins never thought too much of security, but it would not be easy to ambush
the mine. It wasn't easy to get up to the mountain. Let alone down.

At the edge of the woods, Nick peered across the clearing to the main office. It
was a small cabin that appeared to be slowly falling apart. There was smoke
coming out of the chimney, so Nick assumed someone was inside.

In the shadows of the trees, Nick approached the house. His years in the woods
taught him how to avoid sticks and leaves that would make a sound. Even without
daylight, he could glide across a field and not disturb a dear.

Nick snuck up and heard the Grins talking. He watched them count the receipts
that the miners turned in. Then they weighed the gold to make sure everything
matched. Immediately, Nick saw that they were cheating. The Grins were skimming
a bit of gold off of each pack they counted.

Under a counter, the Grins had stashed a sack of coal pieces. The miners knew
the weight of the gold they turned in each day. As the Grins counted and weighed
each miner's gold they replaced some gold pieces with coal. When the miners
double checked the weight of the locked bags before shipment, the weight was
correct, but the miners didn't know that some of the gold in the bag was
actually coal.

Honestly this didn't surprise Nick. The problem with a mine like this is that
you have trust the owners. And if you didn't, there wasn't much to do about it.

Nick saw them lock the gold into the large safe and copy the receipts next to
it. This was a way for the workers to check that their gold was being weighed
correctly. It all made sense. The workers felt good that the Grins were taking
care of them, but all the while the Grins were probably skimming an extra 10
percent.

The Grins put the skimmed gold into a safe and then into a hidden compartment
under the desk. No one even knew it existed.

Until now.
