As usual, Heather woke up early. She started to dress and then remembered,
'Today is daddy's birthday.'

It was a bittersweet feeling. Many of the neighbors would be coming over tonight
for the celebration, but they were all so poor no one had any money to buy a
present. And this year, there wasn't enough flour to make a cake or cookies. She
knew her father was happy to have friends over, but she also knew he had a sweet
tooth!

Her father's nickname was Sweet Tooth. When she was a kid, the first thing he
did with his paycheck each week was buy two bars of chocolate. One for him and
Jane, Heather's mother, and the other for the three kids. It was the best moment
of the week. Heather would dream about it all week long.

But after the avalanche, and the death of Jane three weeks later, Jim lost his
appetite for chocolate. It wasn't just that the town was cut off from the
traders, Jim had changed. He was no longer the happy go lucky guy that everyone
knew, he was serious and sad.

Heather boiled water for tea and then went outside to sweep the porch. As she
walked outside, she saw Ms Hannigan across the street on her front porch
beaming. Ms. Hannigan gave Heather a big smiling wave. Heather wondered what
made her so happy this morning, though it was nice to see a smile on someones
face every now and then.

Reaching for the broom, Heather's foot bumped into something. It was a small
package. Right away she knew what it was, but she hadn't seen anything like this
since she was a little girl. It was a package of chocolate. The kind that was
bought from the local traders. Since the avalanche, 'had it been tens years?',
chocolate had been hard to come by. People rarely see traders and without extra
money, it is never worth buying sweets..

((She once asked her father where the chocolate came from. He said when gold is

'Nice day for a treat, Sweet Pea!', hollered Miss Hannigan from across the
street. Heather could see she was chewing a big chunk of chocolate.

"Where did this come from?" replied Heather.

"No time for worrying dear, just take a bite!" the old lady said.

Heather marvelled that Ms. Hannigan didn't question the fact that packages of
chocolate had appeared on their porch. But that was an indication of the life
that Ms. Hannidgan had lived. She knew that if something good happened, just be
happy and move on. Around here, it doesn't happen often.

Heather looked down the street and saw Mr. Bramley sweeping his floor. Mr
Bramley was another older man still working at the mine. He loved to talk
Heather's ear off about how the gold was running out. "Before long I'll have
that boat I've been saving for," he would tell her. "I'm taking the whole
deposit the second I can and sailing for warmer weather. The gold ain't lasting
longer...you here me??!"

Heather always smiled and listened, but without much faith. She had been
listening to his dreams for almost 10 years and he hadn't made much progress.
Mr. Bramley knew he could finally afford the boat once he cashed in his annuity
next summer. He only had to wait another year, pack up, and he would be back on
the mainland before the winter snow.

Heather took the package of chocolate and started walking down the street. She
noticed that most of the houses on the street, about 10, had a package waiting
or an owner looking baffled.

A group of the neighbors were chatting at one of the corners. After discussing
the mystery, someone suggested everyone bring a piece of chocolate to the
birthday celebration that night.

Heather thought that was a great idea. Then she remember the boiling water on
her stove.

"I need to get home!", she yelled as she shuffled up the street towards her
house.
