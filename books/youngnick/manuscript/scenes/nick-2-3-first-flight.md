The slope of the mountain was perfect. Steep enough to keep Nick moving, but not
too steep that he lost control. He had gone about 500 yards and was thinking
about the future. The near future. He couldn't just go back to town, the Grins
would hear about it and come after him. He would try to make it back to town and
live in the woods. He would sneak in at night and try to get some help.

Nick still couldn't believe the Grins tried to kill him. He never would have
described Don and James as 'kind hearted', but he couldn't believe they were
killers. Maybe his mother was right. That Grin family was no good. Nick always
assumed she was bitter because Gerred Grin was the boss and didn't treat Nick's
father very well. Even though Nick's father was the real brains of the
operation.

There must be something going on that Nick didn't know about. His only
advantage, Nick thought, was the fact that the Grins thought he was dead.

The sled was working well. Nick was standing, but there was a small bench seat
behind him. There was also a section in the back for equipment. Next to the
bench seat, along the edge there was another lever. Must be another break,
possibly an anchor of some sort. As a child in Alaska, Nick had sled
extensively, but only in two sleighs. His father's small two seater and the
large sleigh owned by the mine. This sleigh reminded him of the mine sleigh.

The wind was whipping into Nick's face. He didn't have a mask on like he
normally would when sledding. He squinted his eyes to keep the ice and snow out.
Though it was cold, Nick was feeling pretty good. He was grateful to be heading
towards a forest. After the events of yesterday, Nick felt his situation was
looking up.

Suddenly everything changed. The sleigh had crested a small hill and Nick had a
good view of the path in front of him. The problem was the hill got much
steeper. Even worse, it appeared there was a dip in the snow ahead. Hopefully
only a small hill, Nick thought, but it could be a cliff or revine. As Nick
picked up speed he got a better view. Not good. There was a steep divot in the
snow. He wouldn't be able to jump over without slamming into the other side. It
was about 200 yards away. Nick found himself speeding towards a certain crash!

The brake. Nick slowly pulled the brake lever. He felt the brake touch the snow
on the underside of the sleigh, but it didn't slow him down. The snow was too
powdery to create any friction. He gave it another yank. No luck. The sleigh was
still moving fast. His only chance was to jump.

The cliff was getting closer, if he didn't jump now he was worried his momentum
would take him over the edge and who knows what was on bottom, rocks, river, or
worse. Nick looked around desperately searching for something to slow the
sleigh. H spotted the second brake lever. If it was an anchor it may go deeper
into the ground, maybe enough to turn or stop him. He had enough time for one
last attempt.

He sat down on the bench and placed his hand around the handle. He was surprised
at how well the grooves fit his hand. He slowly pulled back on the lever.
Nothing happened.

He pulled back further waiting to feel any friction on the ground. Nothing. This
was no use. He would have to jump. Why did he waste so much time trying to stop
this goofy sleigh? Nick looked down and pulled his coat tightly around his body.
He would jump and roll to soften the landing.

Nick turned his body to face the opening on the right, ready to jump, but
noticed something was wrong. He felt the sleigh slowing on the snow below him.
He was sitting down so he couldn't see the cliff, but the friction of the snow
was lessening. The sleigh was slowing down, did the brake finally catch?

He stood and peered over the front of the sleigh. He couldn't see the cliff
anymore. It was as if the brake had lifted the front end of the sleigh up. There
was barely any friction to the ground now, but it didn't seem like he was going
any slower. If anything, the wind was picking up. He leaned forward over the
front of the sleigh...what was going on?

The front of the sleigh suddenly bucked, throwing Nick back onto the bench. His
head banged against the back of the bench. Pain throttled through his head and
down his spine. Dazed, he grabbed onto the second brake to keep from falling. He
couldn't feel the ground below him at all now.

Had they stopped?

He looked out the side of the sleigh. Below him was the deep embankment he was
trying to avoid. There were jagged rocks about forty feet down. Crashing would
have been certain death.

Was he on a bridge over the ravine?

Nick gaped to the left and then to the right the sleigh.

He looked forward again. This couldn't be.

The sleigh was flying.
