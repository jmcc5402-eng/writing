**Nick gets the gold and sees Heather escaping**

Nick landed the sleigh and the three caribou at the top of the cliff where he
could safely observe the dock. He solved one of his problems, how to transport
the gold, but he still had no idea how to distract the men below and load the
gold.

Nick noticed that the construction on the dock was completed. It looked like a
new plank that accommodate the rolling crate the gold was stored in. The new
plank connected the rocky ground directly to the ship. It completely bypassed
the dock. Nick decided he needed a closer look.

Praying the animals stayed quiet, Nick landed the sleigh on the gravely beach
down the embankment roughly 50 yards from the dock. He had flown the sleigh up
river and then hugged the embankment as he guided the team to this location.
Nick felt confident that the trees and bushes along the embankment would conceal
the sleigh by screening the view of the men working on the dock.

Right then, all the men on the dock looked towards the vault. Nick saw Duff and
James rolling the large dolley of gold towards the new plank. Nick scanned the
path the dolley would take. Across the gravel road, onto the plank, over the
dock, and then onto the boat. Nick was desperate to find any place he could
intercept the dolley.

Nick looked hard at where the plank connected to the boat. The plank ended at
the wall of the boat about 4 feet higher than the boat’s deck. If there was any
way he could land on the boat’s deck, the gold might be able to roll directly
onto the sleigh. Of course, this seemed very unlikely. After attempting to
murder him a few weeks back, Nick didn’t think the Grins would assist him in
stealing their stolen gold.

As Nick watched the men roll the gold from the vault to the bridge, he
considered the odds. There were two Grins brothers and two other guys. All had
guns and none were very nice. The chances of him grabbing the gold seem very
low.

Right then, a loud crash made everyone’s head turn towards the cabin. A light
flashed, then smoke puffed through the windows.

The front door opened, there appeared to be a commotion inside. Duff went
running through the door, but a moment later fell backwards out the door. he
stumbled on one of the branches still strown across the porch and then tumbled
onto his backside.

Nick didn’t believe what happened next.

Heather came running out. Her two hands gripping a rope, or maybe a belt. She
glanced in either direction and then bolted up the cliff road.

She sprinted away from the house up the coastline.

James and Duff were standing close to the dock, mouths gaped open. A second
later Donald Grin limped through the front door of the cabin and screamed, ‘Get
her!’

All at once everyone darted up the coastline in the direction of Heather.

Nick was stunned. Where did Heather come from? Could she be part of the heist?
What was she doing with Donald in the cabin?

Nick didn’t have an answer to any of those questions, but he knew one thing.
Heather was in danger. As Nick knew, the Grins were not above murder, and
apparently not kidnapping either.

Nick’s eyes darted back to the dock. The crate of gold was left unguarded. Nick
realized he had a chance to get the gold.

Without hesitating, Nick barked at the caribou and yanked back on the lever of
the sleigh. The sleigh jerked forward and seconds later was levitating above the
boat. Gradually pushing the lever forward, Nick brought the sleigh straight down
onto the deck of the boat. The rear of the sleigh scraped on the main mast, but
no damage was done. The first caribou rested his feet on the forward stairs, but
somehow, the team fit.

Nick leaped out of the sleigh and scurried across the plank. Letting out a large
groan, Nick bent his knees and heaved the dolley onto the plank. Once on the
freshly cut pine, the dolley glided over dock and came to a stop at the boat.

This thing was heavy, it was probably a million dollars in gold. There was a six
inch gap between the plank and the wall of the boat. Then there was another two
or three inch gap to the cargo area of the sleigh.

Remembering the Grins, Nick peered up the cliff road and saw two men returning
to the area. They were still a half a mile away, but Nick knew he was short on
time. And it wouldn’t take long for them to realize something was out of place
when they noticed a sleigh with three reindeer on the deck of their boat.

Eyeballing the gap from the plank to the sleigh, Nick closed his eyes and gave
the dolley a hard shove. He pushed the tall crate as hard as he could and gasped
as it shot off the plank and landed squarely in the back of the sleigh. Nick
couldn’t believe his luck, but he knew he wasn’t out of danger yet. The two men
with guns were approaching quickly.

Nick sprung himself off the plank landing with a bang in the sleigh. He
whispered to the deer, “come on guys, we’ve only got one shot at this.” He
pulled back on the lever of the sleigh and yelped at the animals.

But nothing happened. This couldn’t be. He was certain the reindeer would give
him the power he needed to move the gold. In a louder whisper he pleaded between
his teeth, “come on guys, you can do it.”

The deer with his feet on the stairs glanced back and let out a loud snort. He
kicked his front feet up and strained forward. The other two followed suit.

Nick looked up. The two men were close, they were currently blocked by a large
shrub, but that would only last a few more seconds.

With a final loud huff, the deer started to move and the sleigh followed.

Nick let out a short sigh of relief, but then the unthinkable happened. The
withered rope Nick had used to bridle the reindeer tore apart. The reindeer
bucked forward, but were now only held to the sleigh by one rope on their left
side. Nick’s entire body shook with horror. How could this be happening?

Chapter

The guards were at the foot of the plank searching for the tower of gold. Nick
could hear their voices over the crashing of the waves against the boat. They
must have thought James pushed the dolley back to the vault because they turned
briefly towards the cliff. Their guns glistened in the mid day sun.

Nick dove over the front of the sleigh, mounting the closest caribou. The animal
bucked violently, not used to a human on his back. Nick gripped the animals
rough mane and didn’t let go. Gripping the caribou tightly with his feet, Nick
reached forward for the rope hanging from the front caribou. The ropes splintery
threads scraped Nick’s middle finger as he tried to move the rope closer. With a
final effort Nick jerked his body forward and wrapped his hand around the rope.
Pulling his body back upright, Nick grabbed the bridle from the caribou he was
sitting on and brought the two pieces of rope together.

He considered what type of knot to make, there was no time and he had very
little lead. ’The baker’. It wasn’t the strongest, but it was the fastest.

Left over right, under the middle, he got it.

Nick saw the heads of the mean approaching the dock. There wasn’t time to go
back to the sleigh. He patted the caribou and whispered ‘you’ll get an extra
treat for this.’ Then, while sitting on the caribou, he reached back and
released the brake of the sleigh and gave a slight kick to the deer.

Once again, the first caribou led the charged. A steam puffed out of the
carbou’s nose and then the team slowly lifted off the boat.

Nick let out a huge sigh of relief. The sleigh lifted over the boat, then dipped
down and almost touched the water. As the skis touched the water, the sleigh
gained energy and took off.

They were safe and they had the gold. Nick could not believe it. As the sleigh
skimmed low to the water, Nick glanced back and saw Duff and James frantically
searching for the dolley. Nick gave a quick laugh as he patted the caribou neck.

He whipped across the river and started to head up stream, but suddenly
remembered something,

Heather.
