**Heather learns her bank account is emtpy, she suspects something is very
wrong**

The large envelope on the table caught Heather’s eye the moment she walk into
the small kitchen. It was addressed to her without any return address. With a
thin knife she slit open the envelope and was surprised to see another envelope
from the Bank of Juno inside.

Heather retrieved the inner envelope and slid her fingers across the top. It was
wilted and damaged. Heather suspected it had gotten wet. On these occasions, the
post office protects the damaged envelope by protecting it ith a new envelope.
The outer envelope is addressed and sent to the destination.

After carefully recovering the letter, Heather tried to remembered the last time
they recieved a letter from the bank. It had been at least a year. Each
participant in the annuity was sent a yearly statement, but in the five years of
the program, only two had arrived. The Grins, who carried the mail, downplayed
the problem, noting that the mail in Alaska was notoriously ineffective. Most of
the towns folk didn’t seem to care because they received statements from the
Grins.

Heather’s lips curled down in a frown as she considered how the Grins influence
reached so far into everyones affairs. She remembered hearing a rumor that the
Grins spent each return trip from Juno scouring everyones mail for juicy love
letters.

She had sent two letters to Nick since he moved to CA, but hadn’t received
anything in return. She brought it up to Donald once, but he played coy. There
could be a million reasons Nick didn’t write you back he had said. She walked
away disgusted about the possibility of Donald reading her letters. Not that
there was anything juicy in them. Heather mostly rambled about how she wanted to
move to California one day to study to be a doctor.

Returning her attention to the letter in her hand, Heather guessed this must be
a receipt of some sort. She would be leaving for the city in just a few weeks.
Maybe these were her tickets to the ferry, though she had been told they would
be kept at the ferry company. The thought of the trip to Juno and then the
mainland sent a shiver of excitement down her spine. She was so excited. It
wouldn't be long and she would be on that ferry steamer heading to San
Francisco.

Letting her mind drift, she pondered life at the university. Was she ready? She
knew she was bright and loved to read, but folks from the mainland were probably
much smarter and sophisticated than a girl from a small mining town.

After unfolding the paper, her mind quickly focused on the terse language
included. As she read the letter, she became more and more confused.

*‘Funds absent from account’*

*‘Charge for over drawn liability’*

The letter said, the tickets for the ferry could not be purchased because of
insufficient funds in the account. More importantly, it showed the account only
held $34.42. Her father promised more than 3000 dollars, not just 34!

Where could the money be?

**(( include a story about someone who had inquired about the money and then
mysteriously died.or had gone to the mainland and never come back and may have

Heather thought about the situation. What if there was no money in the accounts,
where could it be?

‘Hey sweetie, what you got there?’ her father said, coming in the front door.
Heather pulled the paperwork close to chest. She wasn’t sure what to tell her
father. Had he been lying about the money? What would he do when he found out it
was gone?

Gaining her composure, she flashed a smile to her father and said, “Papers from
the university!” and ran off to her room. Her father chuckled and scratched the
back of his neck as she shuffled past him. He considered asking for more
details, but remembered how much she enjoyed poring over a letter in the privacy
of her room. He’d ask at dinner.

Heather paced the perimeter of her tiny room. How could she find some answers?
She slumped her shoulders remembering only two people in the town deal with the
bank. She gathered her coat and looked out the window. There was still enough
light left in the day. She slipped out the front door and tromped up the street.
She was heading towards the mine.
