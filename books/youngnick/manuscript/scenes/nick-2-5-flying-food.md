Nick pulled his scarf over his mouth. It was freezing. He felt like he was on
the fastest dog run of his life. Though there weren't any dogs. He spent the
next hour enjoying the flying machine. He learned how to turn left and right,
how to slow down, and how to dive like an eagle.

((INSERT STORING ABOUT chasing/playing with/diving with eagles))

On the second eagle dive Nick realized he was famished. He hadn't eaten since
the tiny bit of jerky he had at day break. He looked around and only saw snowy
mountains. There was nothing around except ice. Worse, he didn't have any gear.
No knife, spear, let alone a rifle.

As he prepared for another eagle dive he had an idea "Eagle!, I could find eagle
eggs".

Eagle eggs were a rare delicacy. They were large and full of yoke, but because
of the bird's high nests, people rarely got a chance to eat the delicious egg.
((Confirm this))

In this part of the country, eagles were everywhere. Nick remembered an eagles
nest close to the cave he slept in. He maneuvered the sleigh around and set out
for the cave.

As he hoped, the nest was full of brand new eggs, ripe for the picking. Nick
landed the sleigh at the opening to the cave and climbed up to the nest. He took
three of the eight eggs. That should be enough for now.

Back in the cave, he skillfully poked a hole in the first egg and sucked out the
yoke. He'd been eating eggs in this manner since he could remember.

His fathered loved fresh eggs. He showed Nick how to carefully use your smallest
fingernail to create a hole without cracking the entire shell. Nick and his
father would search for eagle eggs before trading trips to Okikoke. The local
traders were always willing to trade good meat or fish for eagle eggs.

On many occasions while climbing the redwoods of California, Nick made a quick
snack out of the robin. Though not as large as eagle eggs, the robin eggs
brought back happy memories of his father and their time in Alaska.

((INSERT STORY HIS FATHER TOLD ABOUT TRADERS))

As Nick was eating the eggs he began to think of the Inuit traders. The pass to
the valley wasn't far west of here. As a young child he had traveled with his
father through the valley. One time they trekked all the way to Juno. But that
was before the avalanche. Since the avalanche, and the earthquake that caused
it, the valley was unpassable.

Of course, that was unpassable on foot. With the sleigh he could fly over. He
could find the Inuits and trade these eggs for some real food. The thought of
eating nothing but eagle eggs made this plan very desirable.

Nick stomped on the eggs he was holding and walk to the sleigh. The sun was
shining brighter now and for a brief moment Nick felt warmth on his cheeks. That
warmth spread to his entire body. Something about the nice weather, the sleigh,
and the eagles eggs made him feel good inside. Perhaps returning from California
wasn't such as bad idea. This strange discovery must be some sort of sign.

It occurred to Nick, he couldn't leave Bronston now. Something was wrong in that
town. Why had the Grins done what they did? What had they been talking about in
that tent that caused them to push Nick over that cliff?

He would have to find out. He didn't return to Alaska for gold. He never thought
he'd get rich. He returned because he loved this place. He loved the people, the
creeks, the eagles, and even the mine. If the Grins were doing something to harm
this town, Nick would have to find out and put a stop to it.

Nick spent the next hour tracking down eagles nests and gathering eggs. He was
careful not to take too many from any single next. Once he had enough to trade,
he pointed the sleigh towards the valley. As the sleigh picked up speed Nick
thought of Bronston. He was going to help this town, no matter what that meant.
No one had seemed happy in Bronston these days and everyone was hungry. At the
very least he could bring good food to the town from the traders. At the very
best, he could figure out what had changed and help change things back to the
way they were.
