**Nick returns to Cave**

Nick returns to the orbital cave trying to decide what to do next. He decides
not to interfere with heather who has headed off to school.

He sees something else glowing where the sleigh was. He digs it out. It's a
glowing rock with a compass rose (another theme) with the north glowing. When he
turns south it stops glowing. He decides to flollow the compass north.

The caribou nibbled the pieces of apple Nick fed to them. They were the true
heros, Nick thought. Somehow, the addition of the caribou increased the speed of
the sleigh, it seemed almost five fold. Nick didn’t think he would’ve been able
to catch Heather without them.

Rain started to come down so Nick headed into the cave for shelter. The animals
seemed to almost enjoy the freezing rain.

After starting a fire, Nick considered what he should do next. He couldn’t get
his mind off of Heather. He left her on the dock with the gold, but he decided
not to stay. Maybe he chickened out. He rationalized that he didn’t want to make
the sleigh public knowledge. If he ever did that, he would need to carefully
plan how.

He started to drift off. The fire was warm and he finally ate a legitimate meal.
Much like his first night in the cave, he had a dream. This time it was about
Heather.

Heather was walking towards him on the city dock. She was wearing a beautiful
dress with a knit sweater over her shoulders. Nick could see lights in the
background, it must be the spring festival. His eyes were focused intently on
Heather. She wore a ruby necklace around her neck. Nick was so happy. He reached
out for Heather, he wanted to tell her that she was gorgeous.

As the two approached each other, the ruby began to glow. Nick was enticed by
the deep red ember. She was right in front of him and he touched her hand. This
was all he ever wanted. His childhood, their families, everything in his life
led up to this moment.

The ruby was bright, it almost blinded him. He obscured the glare with his arm.
Then his eyes opened.

He wasn’t on the dock and Heather wasn’t here. He was in the cave. But the ruby
still glowed. Nick had a flashback to the night so many weeks ago when a deep
glowed led him to discover the sleigh.

He got to his feet and felt his way deeper into the cave. He saw the place where
he discovered the sleigh, but this glow was deeper in. He carefully stepped
further into the darkness, the glow was his only light.

When it was close enough to touch, he reached out. Once again he began to scrape
away the dirt. The earth was freezing on his gloveless fingers. This glows was
smaller than the sleigh, but it was brighter, more concentrated.

Nick’s fingernails touched something hard in stark contrast to the soft clay of
the earth. He dug faster around the object. It was small, not much larger than a
plum. Or was it a coin?

With a final effort, he removed the object from the wall. It felt warm. It was
smaller than his palm and oval shaped. It wasn’t spherical, but it was rounded.
It reminded him of a good stone for skipping across the water, but it was a bit
too thick.

Nick rubbed his thumb across the stone to clear away the dirt. The more he
rubbed the warmer the stone began to glow. Nick could make out something
inscribed on the stone. It was a circle. He cleared more dirt, finally it was
clear. It wasn’t a circle, it was a compass.

The cave was still very dark and Nick could not see anything except for the
stone. He turned to the opening of the cave, hoping to use the fire for a better
inspection. When he turned, something happened. The glow of the rock dimmed.
Suddenly, Nick was in the pitch black. He turned again, back towards the depth
of the cave. The stone began to glow again.

Right then,something occurred to Nick. He examined the size of the stone, he
turned it in his hands as he gazed at it. He then turned around and hustled to
the front of the cave.

The sleigh was waiting at the opening of the cave. Nick jumped on and slid his
hand across the wood deck. He inspected the ornate grooves. The middle groove
drew his attention. Could it be? At this point, Nick didn’t question any
coincidences.

He slowly moved the glowing stone over the grooves. The middle groove was the
exact same size. When the stone was an inch above the groove, Nick felt
something. The stone was being pulled towards the groove. It felt like the
magnets he had seen in California. He loosened his grip on the stone and it was
pulled into the groove. It fit perfectly.

At that moment, a flash of red, starting with the stone, spread throughout the
entire sleigh. It only lasted an instant and Nick thought that if he had blink
he would have missed it.

Nick looked down at the stone. It’s glow was very dim. He remembered how turning
the stone made it glow. Without thinking, Nick grabbed the reigns, yelled to the
caribou, and took off into the air.

As Nick ascended he thought of Heather. He wanted to find her, tell her
everything, and take her with him. An eery feeling spread through his body, the
fact that the stone fit into the sleigh, this seemed like something bigger.

Once in the air, Nick whipped the sleigh around to his left, away from Bronston,
towards the North. The stone grew brighter the more he turned. He looked up and
saw the stars above. The North Star was shining bright to his left.

The sleigh kept turning, but once the North Star was straight ahead, something
happened. The stone glowed bright red. The sleigh stopped turning. It felt as if
the sleigh was attached to a set of tracks, like the trains used to haul
sequoias to San Fransisco. Nick tried to angle the sleigh back towards the cave,
but it didn’t budge.

Nick gazed at the stars, something seemed different. They started to shake. Then
they were moving. Faster than before. Faster than he’d ever gone before. He
looked over his shoulder, the mountain and the cave were no where in sight. He
turned towards the front in time to see a bright flash straight in front of him.

A grey cloud formed where the flash began. The sleigh shot forward, it was
accellatoring. Nick had lost control of the sleigh. It was flying itself. The
next thing he knew, at a speed that seemed faster than lighting, the sleigh
entered the cloud.
