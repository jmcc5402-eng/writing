There was a knock at the door. When Heather opened it, she saw Mrs. Hannigan
standing in front of her smiling.

Heather had lived in this town for almost 19 years, and Mrs Hannigan, nor anyone
else, had ever knocked on her door this early in the morning.

The only time she could remember something like this was the morning after the
avalanche. She had felt the earthquake at night, everyone had, but there wasn’t
much damage at home and it was so dark there wasn’t much that could be done in
the middle of the night.

Shaking her head to wash away the memories, Heather opened the door. In front of
her stood Mrs. Hannigan. She was all smiles. She was dangling a small bag in
front of Heather. It was the small kind baggy that people held eggs in. A small
burlap sack with a tie string.

“Surprise surprise Sweet Pea!”, she called out. Heather forced a smile at the
name of her old nickname. Sweet Pea had been Heathers nickname since she was
tiny. It was a spinoff of her father’s nickname, ‘Sweet Tooth’.

“Somebody is smiling down on us!” Heather could tell Mrs hannigan was enjoying
this. Heather still had no idea what was in the bag.

“If this keeps up we won’t need that money from the bank!” Ms Hannigan held the
small bag, but pointed to the floor of Heathers porch. On a chair was another
bag.

Ms. Hannigan reached over and picked the sack off the chair and held it out to
Heather.

“Looky here, Sweet Pea!” Mrs Hannigan always used phrases such as ‘Looky here’
or ‘how ya doin.’ Heather’s father told her that Mr. Hannigan had always spoken
like that. When he died, Mrs. Hannigan kept on the tradition. Mining folks had
strange ways of remembering loved ones.

Heather stepped forward and took the sack that Mrs. Hannigan was offering. After
glancing down the street, she opened it. It took her a second to figure out what
was inside, it was still a bit dark outside. Was it a rock? Why was Mrs.
Hannigan giving her a rock? Heather pulled the rock out. It wasn’t a rock.

It was gold.

She held the nugget of gold in her hand. Of course, Heather had seen a lot of
gold over the years, but it didn’t usually appear on one’s doorstep.

Heather was a bit too pragmatic to get excited. Last week was chocolate, this
week gold. The town had already started talking about fierys and angels leaving
the chocolate. What stories would this weeks discovery bring?

A chill rushed over Heather’s body. Something strange was happening. It was
wonderful to have gold appear on the front porch, but the Grins controlled all
the gold from the mine. What would happen if they found out?

She quickly put the sack in her pocket and decided she didn’t want to find out.
