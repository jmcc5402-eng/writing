## Chapter 1

He was probably the first human to ever step foot in this part of the Alaskan
mountain side. Mountain goats may have tried, but honestly, what reason would
they have to hike up to this point. Of course, Nick had a reason: gold.

The ground sample Nick would extract would indicate to Grin Bros Mining the
potential for gold on this side of the mountain. The sample technique wasn’t
perfect, but it had proven very accurate the past few years.

After scaling the final few rocks, Nick reached into his pack to grab his
equipment. Suddenly the wind picked up. With his one free hand, Nick pulled his
stocking cap on tighter. He was not dressed for the snow or rain and was looking
forward to the descend back to the base camp. Going down should be easier that
climbing up, right?

Though Nick returned to Alaska over three weeks earlier, he still hadn’t gotten
used to the biting cold. After ten years living in Northern California his blood
had thinned.

‘Had it been ten years?’, Nick thought to himself.

He quickly did the math. He left Alaska when he was eleven and he just
celebrated his twenty first birthday last month. It wasn’t a celebration to
remember. He’d been alone and freezing on the bottom deck of the barge he took
from Seattle to Juno.

Focusing on the task at hand, Nick begins to takes the sample when it started to
snow. He thought this might happen and was happy it held off until now. After
drilling a few holes into the rock, he used his small trowel to pack a few small
containers with earth.

When he finished, Nick took quick look around. He sees an eagle off in the
distant. It darts straight down toward the ground, but pulls up at the last
moment. What ever animal it was searching for must have scurried away in time.

Ice forms very fast and on any surface in these conditions so Nick used extreme
caution as he climbed down the rocky peak and back to the ledge. With about 5
feet to go, a rock gives way and he loses his balance. Nick reached out and
grabs the root from a bush to keep from falling off the cliff. His face slams
against the freezing ground and both legs dangle off the ledge.

As it tends to do, ice makes its way down his neck. The sting of the ice on his
chest makes him cringe. That does not feel good. The wind picks up and Nick
squeezes the root to avoid slipping into the deep crevice below. Nick’s heart
was pounding, in his chest and in his ears.

Nick puts his foot on the loose rock, but it gives way and falls completely off
the ledge. Nick lets out a yelp and sees sees the rock fall. He didn’t hear it
land. Maybe his cry muffled the sound he thought, but maybe this crevice is just
that deep. That is not a good sign.

Keeping his vice grip on the root, Nick digs his fingers into the dirt of the
ledge. The ground is ice cold, but manages to get a grip. With his left foot
placed on a sturdy rock, Nick uses the root and the ground to pull himself up.

Working as a logger on the beautiful redwoods had its danger, but at least it
wasn’t freezing.

With the sample kit safely packed away, Nick stands up and heads back to the
base camp. The snow lets up as he moves down the mountain. At the camp, Nick
sees smoke coming from the tent and can’t wait to warm himself by the fire. He
stops and removes his pack outside the ent. He can just hear the Grin brothers
bickering inside. ‘....San Fran...’ ‘........three more weeks?....’ ‘...if just
one person finds out...’ Nick shakes his head and thinks, ‘ten years later and
those two are still going at it.’

A minute later James Grin backed out of the tent, turned around, and looked
straight at Nick. His face had a surprised expression, but Nick could tell the
younger Grin was upset about something. The Grins weren’t expecting Nick back
for a few hours, if he was going to make it at all. James and Nick made eye
contact and without a word James went back inside.

Nick spent a few minutes organizing his equipment and then knelt down by the
fire to cook some jerky. The dogs were digging themselves burrows to sleep for
the night. Nick was surprised the group was spending the night at the camp. He’d
hurried back hoping they would trek back to the mountain base camp before night
fall. One advantage to traveling in the evening was that the sleighs traveled
fast on the icy trails. The morning often brought fresh snow which slowed the
dogs down.

Suddenly, Nick heard footsteps approaching. He turned to look, but all he saw
was a dirty glove swinging a large wooden log at his head. The blow hit him
right on the forehead. He fell back trying to defend himself, but two strong
arms grabbed him from behind.

The second man hit him with two body blows and Nick doubled over. He was picked
up and thrown across the camp...towards the cliff.

Nick rolled over to break free from his attacker, but a boot hit him square in
the back. Nick groaned. What was going on? Was the camp getting ambushed? They
have nothing of value. Where were the Grins? He started to cry out, but the two
strong arms launched him towards the edge of the cliff.

He dug his his hands into the ground to slow his momentum and came to a stop
with his head hanging over the cliff.

He looked down and saw a raging river about 100 feet below. Falling would not be
good.

But at that moment, both men kicked Nick at the same time, sending him over the
edge towards the water.

He flipped in the air and crashed into the mountain side. Flailing his arms and
legs as he fell, he looked up and saw his assailants.

It was the Grin brothers.

## Chapter 2

The kick sent Nick flying off the cliff. His left arm and shoulder slammed into
a rock below and propelled him to the left. Nick knew he was in trouble. He
clawed the cliff trying to slow his fall, but he was flipping too quickly.

From the corner of his eye he spied the branch of a large bush. He reached out
too late and missed it. By torquing his body though, Nick changed his trajectory
and slammed into a loose grouping of rocks. The water was about 30 feet below.
Nick ended up on his bottom sliding towards the water. He dug his heels in the
ground, but there didn’t seem anyway to stop his momentum.

Nick rolled on his stomach and used his hands to slow his descent. He leaned to
the left and saw a hole in the ground, it must be an eagles nest. As his feet
slipped off the cliff, Nick plowed his arm into the eagles nest. The hole was
deep and his arm went in up to his armpit. He stopped quickly, but pain shot
through his arm. He didn’t think it was broken, but he could barely feel
anything below his shoulder.

Nick’s arm was stuck in an eagles nest and his waist was hanging off the cliff,
but anything was better than falling into the freezing river only only 8 feet
below. He climbed to his left and spotted a grouping of large rocks. As he
rolled over he looked up to the ledge. He could just make out the silhouette of
a man peering over the ledge. They were looking for him. Luckily, the rocks
blocked the view and he was way too far down for them to follow. They must be
sure he is dead.

Nick laid for a few minutes. What had just happened? Why had they done this? He
knew he wasn’t loved by the Grin brothers, but couldn’t imagine why they would
kill him.

In the three weeks since his return to Bronston, AK, he’d done three excursions
to the mountains for samples. This one was the riskiest, but they felt it could
have the biggest payoff. Nick remember the sample, he’d left it by the fire. Not
that it mattered anyways. Nick glanced up the mountain, it had been about thirty
minutes since he’d been pushed. He considered climbing back up the steep hill,
but the drop offs were too sharp. He wouldn’t be able to climb up without
equipment.

Looking left and right, Nick regarded his situation. To the left was an icy
slope that led to the river. To his right were more rocks. That would be his
only chance of getting off this cliff without falling into the water.

The river was certain death. Men fell into freezing rivers on occasion and never
lived. The few times someone climbed out of the water, they never made it more
than a few hours. The water was too cold. Nick’s father had always warned that
freezing water shuts down the heart.

Nick edged further to the right. He couldn’t see past the largest boulder in
front of him, but if he could climb to the top, he would have a better view of
his surroundings. He couldn’t move too fast without sliding the rest of the way
into the river.

As he edged over, he started to think back to the warmer weather of California.
Why had he left? Did he really think he would make any money back in Alaska? It
didn’t seem to matter right now. The only thing that mattered now was reaching
the rocks about 4 feet to his right.

Suddenly, the dirt below him gave way and he started to slide. Without thinking,
Nick twisted his body and lunged for the rocks. With both arms he grabbed onto a
large rock . He realized that if this rock came loose, he would fall directly
into the river.

It held.

Nick pulled himself up to the rock, this was way too much adventure for one day.
From this vantage Nick finally felt safe. There was a rocky hill that seemed
climbable. For the moment, Nick stopped to catch his breath.

Laying on the boulder Nick noticed his shoulder was throbbing. For some reason
this made him think of Heather. He’d only seen her once since he got back from
California, that seemed like ages ago, but in reality it was only a few days
earlier. He had seen her across the town as she left the market, but didn’t have
the nerve to call out to her. Nick wasn’t even sure she remembered him.

For the last three years, Nick had worked as a lumber jack in Northern
California. Though it could be exciting, he often spent hours by himself.
Remembering his time as a kid with Heather helped pass the time. They used to
spend hours climbing rocks like this. Nick, Heather, and the Grin brothers. They
were quite the group. But that was years ago, before Nicks father died, before
the earthquake, and before the Grins became killers.

A cold wind brought his mind back to the current situation. The sun was setting
now, if he was going to get off this mountain he would need to find shelter
quickly. Night time in Alaska was dangerous, for the cold, and the predators. If
he didn’t find shelter, he would surely freeze to death. Thoughts about Heather
would have to wait.

He crawled over a few rocks, being careful that each was firm. Another cold wind
blew making Nick’s nose and ears shout with pain. Luckily though, he turned to
his right and saw exactly what he was looking for.

A cave.

## Chapter 3

Heather arrived home from Mrs. Jones’s earlier than usual. Her father, Jim, was
sitting on the front porch enjoying the cool evening. She smiled and said, “I
decided, I’m going to enroll in the University.” Jim closed his eyes, leaned
back, and smiled.

“This is the proudest moment of my life”, he said. He gave her a hug. “Come on,
lets celebrate”.

Jim had been trying to persuade Heather to apply to the university all winter
long. She was almost twenty and he worried a bright girl like her would be
miserable if she got stuck in this mining town. More importantly, he had the
money. He’d been saving for this for years. And from what he’d heard recently,
the gold was not going away.

“But won’t you need the money,” Heather had responded the first time Jim brought
up the subject.

“I haven’t needed it for the last four years, I’ll be fine if I never see it”.

Heather knew Jim could manage on next to nothing, but she didn’t want her father
working at Grin mining the rest of his life.

Heather had dreamed about heading to the mainland since she was little. It
started with her father. He would sit by her bed and tell stories of his younger
days in San Francisco. He had studied to be a doctor, but the pull of gold was
too strong. He left the big city after eighteen months in school to chase the
gold rush in Alaska.

When Heather was little, Jim would quiz her on the different parts of the body
and other scientific questions. Night after night they would sit together
talking. “ She was a natural,” Jim would say, “she had an amazing memory.” She
could recall tiny details about a subject in the blink of an eye.

Since those days, Heather dreamed of becoming a doctor. She would be the first
to cure typhoid. Typhoid, that terrible disease that took her mother away when
she was eight. Those terrible months when her mother was suffering and
eventually succumbed to the disease only strengthened her determination.

Now she would pack up and leave the only town she had ever known. She was
nervous, but excited. The small bookstore in town barely had enough books to
fill a shelf. And the newspapers only arrived monthly when the Grins returned
from Juno.

Living in San Francisco would be much different. At the university she would be
surrounded by peopled searching for knowledge. Unlike most folks in Bronston,
who only cared about digging deeper into the mines, folks in the city would want
to discuss science and math. They would set their sights higher than the next
days dig. In San Francisco, she would be surrounded by people like herself.

But her dad was resolute on staying in Alaska. He didn’t want to make the long
trip, he didn’t want to find a new house. He didn’t love working in the mine,
but he didn’t want to start a new life.

Heather was worried the mine would kill him, like it had done to so many of her
friends fathers. He needed to stop working. But she knew he wouldn’t stop until
she was done with school. On some level he was right, they needed the money from
the mine to afford life at the university.

“Tomorrow morning write a letter to the cruise line to reserve your fare”, Jim
said. The cruise line to the continent would be able to receive funds for the
tickets from the bank. It would take a few weeks for confirmation, but by mid
December, Heather would have the tickets to Portland in her hands.

The cave was set in the rocks in a position that it couldn’t be seen from above
and might not be visible from either side. This made Nick feel a little safer on

He could see about eight feet into the cave before it became pitch black. The
first thing he saw gave him a glimmer of hope. There were pine needles packed
into the bottom corner of the cave. Animals in this area knew to make pine nests
in caves like this. The pine needles would offer some warmth and insulation..

Caves like this got very cold so it wouldn’t buy him much to continue deeper
into the mountain. Because the cave was so dark, he didn’t attempt to crawl very
far back. He knew the best course of action would be to start a fire. But after

wood, let alone kindling to make a spark.

was his only need.

Without a fire, the first step to making it through the night was to block the
entrance from the wind. He gathered rocks for about twenty minutes and stacked
them in front of the entrance. With the wind no longer blowing, Nick started to
make a bed. The needles made for good insulation so he gathered as many as he
could. He packed them in sleeves and pant legs. He wanted to take off his shoes,
but worried his toes would get too cold.

Feeling somewhat content with his sleeping arrangement, Nick laid down for the

pocket. He hadn’t noticed how starving he was. Of the four strips he had, he

able to sleep on an empty stomach. After slowly savoring the jerky, he closed

Suddenly he woke up. He wasn’t on a dog sleigh, he was still in the cave.
However, he could still see the red glow of the grill from his dream. It seemed
to be coming from deeper within the cave. He turned towards the entrance, but it
was pitch black. He had no idea what time it was. How long had he slept?

Nick focused his attention on the red glow coming from the cave. It wasn’t
bright enough to light a path, but it was definitely there. What could it be?

It wasn’t the color of a fire and it didn’t seem to be that far away. It was a
dull reddish hue. Nick slowly stood from his sleeping position. He entire body

He carefully stepped deeper into the cave. He could almost stand up straight, he

## Chapter 7

The sunlight was starting to drift in over the rocks that Nick had assembled to
block the cave entrance.

The sleigh was sitting in front of the pile of rocks, just inside the cave.

Nick was actually annoyed at himself. He’d spent the last few hours freeing the
sleigh. He didn’t realize until the sleigh was out that he’d spent a lot of
energy on this. And he didn’t have any food. After he had unearthed the sleigh

jerky. But that had been two hours ago.

The sleigh was peculiar, but unless he could eat it, it didn’t help his current
predicament. He sat for a moment and pondered why there was a sleigh buried in a

Since he didn’t have any extra gear, Nick decided to set out. If he could make
