## CHAPTER 1

Nick looked down into the frozen gorge below. All he could see was black.

‘If it starts to rain, I’ll probably freeze to death’, he thinks to himself.

He was almost to his destination, a formation of rocks about 40 feet further.
The problem was, the path soon became a thin ledge. Before he reached the sample
area, he’d be scaling rocks.

He was probably the first human to ever step foot in this part of the Alaskan
mountain side. Mountain goats may have tried, but honestly, what reason would
they have to hike up to this point. Of course, Nick had a reason: gold.

The ground sample Nick would extract would indicate to Grin Bros Mining the
potential for gold on this side of the mountain. The sample technique wasn’t
perfect, but it had proven very accurate the past few years.

After scaling the final few rocks, Nick reached into his pack to grab his
equipment. Suddenly the wind picked up. With his one free hand, Nick pulled his
stocking cap on tighter. He was not dressed for the snow or rain and was looking
forward to the descend back to the base camp. Going down should be easier that
climbing up, right?

Though Nick returned to Alaska over three weeks earlier, he still hadn’t gotten
used to the biting cold. After ten years living in Northern California his blood
had thinned.

‘Had it been ten years?’, Nick thought to himself.

He quickly did the math. He left Alaska when he was eleven and he just
celebrated his twenty first birthday last month. It wasn’t a celebration to
remember. He’d been alone and freezing on the bottom deck of the barge he took
from Seattle to Juno.

Focusing on the task at hand, Nick begins to takes the sample when it started to
snow. He thought this might happen and was happy it held off until now. After
drilling a few holes into the rock, he used his small trowel to pack a few small
containers with earth.

When he finished, Nick took quick look around. He sees an eagle off in the
distant. It darts straight down toward the ground, but pulls up at the last
moment. What ever animal it was searching for must have scurried away in time.

Ice forms very fast and on any surface in these conditions so Nick used extreme
caution as he climbed down the rocky peak and back to the ledge. With about 5
feet to go, a rock gives way and he loses his balance. Nick reached out and
grabs the root from a bush to keep from falling off the cliff. His face slams
against the freezing ground and both legs dangle off the ledge.

As it tends to do, ice makes its way down his neck. The sting of the ice on his
chest makes him cringe. That does not feel good. The wind picks up and Nick
squeezes the root to avoid slipping into the deep crevice below. Nick’s heart
was pounding, in his chest and in his ears.

Nick puts his foot on the loose rock, but it gives way and falls completely off
the ledge. Nick lets out a yelp and sees sees the rock fall. He didn’t hear it
land. Maybe his cry muffled the sound he thought, but maybe this crevice is just
that deep. That is not a good sign.

Keeping his vice grip on the root, Nick digs his fingers into the dirt of the
ledge. The ground is ice cold, but manages to get a grip. With his left foot
placed on a sturdy rock, Nick uses the root and the ground to pull himself up.

Working as a logger on the beautiful redwoods had its danger, but at least it
wasn’t freezing.

With the sample kit safely packed away, Nick stands up and heads back to the
base camp. The snow lets up as he moves down the mountain. At the camp, Nick
sees smoke coming from the tent and can’t wait to warm himself by the fire. He
stops and removes his pack outside the tent. He can just hear the Grin brothers
bickering inside.

‘....San Fran...’

‘........three more weeks?....’

‘...if just one person finds out...’

Nick shakes his head and thinks, ‘ten years later and those two are still going
at it.’

A minute later James Grin backed out of the tent, turned around, and looked
straight at Nick. His face had a surprised expression, but Nick could tell the
younger Grin was upset about something. The Grins weren’t expecting Nick back
for a few hours, if he was going to make it at all. James and Nick made eye
contact and without a word James went back inside.

Nick spent a few minutes organizing his equipment and then knelt down by the
fire to cook some jerky. The dogs were digging themselves burrows to sleep for
the night. Nick was surprised the group was spending the night at the camp. He’d
hurried back hoping they would trek back to the mountain base camp before night
fall. One advantage to traveling in the evening was that the sleighs traveled
fast on the icy trails. The morning often brought fresh snow which slowed the
dogs down.

Suddenly, Nick heard footsteps approaching. He turned to look, but all he saw
was a dirty glove swinging a large wooden log at his head. The blow hit him
right on the forehead. He fell back trying to defend himself, but two strong
arms grabbed him from behind.

The second man hit him with two body blows and Nick doubled over. He was picked
up and thrown across the camp...towards the cliff.

Nick rolled over to break free from his attacker, but a boot hit him square in
the back. Nick groaned. What was going on? Was the camp getting ambushed? They
have nothing of value. Where were the Grins? He started to cry out, but the two
strong arms launched him towards the edge of the cliff.

He dug his his hands into the ground to slow his momentum and came to a stop
with his head hanging over the cliff.

He looked down and saw a raging river about 100 feet below. Falling would not be
good.

But at that moment, both men kicked Nick at the same time, sending him over the
edge towards the water.

He flipped in the air and crashed into the mountain side. Flailing his arms and
legs as he fell, he looked up and saw his assailants.

It was the Grin brothers.
