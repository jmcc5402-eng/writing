## CHAPTER 1.1

Tonight was the night, Nick said to himself.

When he returned from this expedition he would do it. He had traveled back to
Alaska for the opportunity. If he could scale down a frozen cliff like this, he
should be able to drum up the courage. It wouldn't be hard. He just needed to be
confident. And if things didn't go his way, he could always return to
California. Tonight was the night.

Tonight he would propose to Heather.

Nick peered into the frozen gorge below. A burst of adrenaline coursed through
his chest. All he could see was black.

He broke off his stare and turned his gaze inwards to the rocky cliff he was
repelling down. His eyes locked on the thin ledge sixty feet below. If the rocks
held, it wouldn’t be a difficult descent. From the ledge, he’d be able to move
horizontally back to the trail. In two hours he’d be warming coffee by the fire.
If there was time, they might make it back to the base camp tonight.

Bracing himself against the cliff in front of him, Nick reached into his pack
and retrieved a rope and three spikes.

Just then, a sharp wind approached from the left and tiny ice particles attacked
Nick’s face.

His body tensed from head to toe. It felt like falling face first into a bed of
dry pine needles. Nick cringed. “Keep on moving,” he said to himself and rammed
the spike into a soft section of earth between the rock. Maintaining his balance
between his left hand and two feet, Nick knotted his rope around the spike.

Moments later, Nick clung to the mountain thirty feet above the ledge. His right
hand wrapped around another safety spike he had managed to cram into the earth.
The rest of his weight was centered on a rock about two feet below.

The sun had appeared between the overcast clouds and Nick appreciated the brief
splash of warmth on his face. He looked down, eyeing his target destination, and
planned his course of attack. With his left hand he gripped a triangle shaped
crag half an arms length below his head. The hand grip held firm and he placed
all of his weight on his left foot, currently on a sturdy rock.

Nick’s right foot descended and searched for a place to rest. His boot scraped a
potential target using a technique he learned years ago. It was always
beneficial to contact a foothold before applying too much weight. In the case,
the ragged rock didn’t budge. Nick put his foot on the rock and began to lower
himself.

Suddenly something unexpected happened. The rock holding Nick’s right foot began
to collapse.

Even in the arctic temperature, sweat seeped from Nick’s hands and his upper
body tensed. His right foot was crumbling into the rock. Trying to stay calm,
Nick tightened his hand grips, his left foot searching for it’s previous resting
place. The foot found a divot in the mountain, not it’s previous spot, but it
would have to do because Nick’s right foot was now dangling and he was
supporting himself entirely via his hands.

As he moved his weight back to his left foot, Nick realized he made a mistake.
The hold his left foot rested on was too thin. In an instant, his foot slipped.
The momentum caused his left hand to lose it’s grip and his face to smack into
the cliff. A searing pain shot through Nick’s scull, initiating at his cheek
which had slammed into a freezing shard of icy rock.

Nick’s head rang like a clock and his vision blurred. He tried to close his
eyes, but was overcome by a wave of nausea. His head was spinning, but worse,
his right hand slipped from the rock it held. Without thinking, Nick’s left hand
lunged forward, but this frantic maneuver shifted his weight and his left hand
lost control of the rock it had held. In an instant, Nick began to fall.

The panic of falling pulsed through Nick’s entire being. The pain in his head
was replaced by chilling realization that this may be the last fall of his life.

Nick’s right shoulder slammed into a rough rock about four feet below that was
jutting out of the cliff. A scorching pain seared through Nick’s arm, but the
impact changed the momentum of his fall. Nick’s upper body tilted forward and
made contact with the rock in front of him. Nick reached out his hands, clinging
for anything to slow his descent.

Though sliding downward, Nick spotted one of the safety spikes about six feet to
his right. Without hesitating, he lunged. His right hand seized the spike, but
his shoulder screamed in pain and he lost his grip. With a twist of his upper
body, however, Nick reached up wth his left hand and caught the metal rod.
Nick’s body swung to the right and then back to center, but this time his vice
grip held.

For a brief moment, Nick felt safe. But because his body was angling downward,
his shoulder pack began to slide. The bag was heavy. It contained the rest of
his spikes, the hand drill he had used, as well as the earth samples he had just
collected. If he lost the bag, the entire journey would be for nothing. Nick
felt the strap inching down his arm.

To stop the bag from falling, Nick straightened his body by pulling up on the
safety spike. But this spike wasn’t one of his, it might have been five years
old. Nick wasn’t sure when the last time someone had climbed this mountain, but
he knew one thing, this spike was not secure.

Nick winced and spit as bits of dirt and ice crumbled onto his face. Looking
right he spotted a sharp rock. He would need momentum to reach it. He swung
slightly to the left and kicked off the mountain. At that moment the spike came
loose. Nick flailed for the new rock and grabbed it with his right hand. As the
spike fell from his left hand Nick found a solid foothold.

As the adrenaline wore off, Nick realized his shoulder was reeking with pain. He
moved his arm in a small circle, it wasn’t broken, but it must be dislocated. He
would need one of the Grin brothers to knock it back into joint when he returned
to the campsite. It would hurt like heck, but less than the time a few years ago
when he’d done it himself.

Nick trudged back to the mini-camp. The path was slow and wet and Nick’s
shoulder throbbed. He thought about the fall he just took. He wouldn’t last long
in Alaska if he kept volunteering for the riskiest jobs. But what else could he
do? There didn’t seem to be open slots in the mine, like he had planned.

And what about Heather? Nick didn’t want to admit it, but she was the reason he
had returned. Was she with Donald Grin? Donald hadn’t mentioned anything about
her, at least to him. Tonight at camp he would inquire. And then, either way,
when he got back to town, he would drum up the courage to talk with her. The
girl he knew would never fall for Donald.

Rounding the last bend, he saw smoke drifting out of the makeshift tent at the
camp site. There was also a fire struggling to stay lit. The dream of hot coffee
just might come true.

The camp stood on a ledge of the mountain Nick was descending. The ledge was
only ten feet wide, with a steep cliff leading up on one side and a steeper drop
off down the other.

As Nick approached the camp he heard the river gushing against the rocks one
hundred feet below. That sound, coupled with the view of the mountains in the
distance, reminded him how much he loved this place. Even a throbbing shoulder
and frozen feet couldn’t ruin a picturesque Alaskan panorama.

Kneeling in front of the fire, Nick let his pack slip off his shoulder. Man that
hurt.

He shook his head and smiled when he heard the Grin brothers yelling at each
other in the tent. Something about San Francisco...or maybe a boat that was
being repaired. Some things never changed. As kids, those two dreamed of the
California gold rush, always bitter to be stuck in the freezing Yukon.

He was glad he had a brief moment of silence to warm by the fire. The smell of
the ash reminded him of the nights out with his father. But that was before
everything changed, before the avalanche.

Reaching into his pocket for his cold strips of jerky, Nick heard footsteps
behind him. For some reason, the hair on the back his neck came to attention.

The footsteps turned into a run.

A shot of fear bolted through Nick’s body. Something was wrong. He got up on one
knee and turned his head, but it was too late.

A dirty glove was swinging a wooden club - directly at Nick’s face.

## CHAPTER 1.2

It all happened so fast.

Nick’s nose exploded. Blood everywhere.

For a brief moment, Nick found himself on all fours, the burning fire only
inches from his face. But then, a large frozen boot pummeled nick in the
stomach.

There was no time.

Two arms picked him up from behind and tossed him to the left. Snow scraped up
Nick’s sleeves.

Nick stumbled onto all fours again and realized his head was hanging over the
cliff. He could see the froth of the river as it crashed against jagged rocks.
Nick heard his attacker approaching so he rolled to the left.

It didn’t matter. There were two of them.

The Grins?

Another brutal kick to the ribs sent Nick backwards.

He grasped for anything to hold onto, but his body was facing upwards.

Another kick. That was all it took.

Nick tumbled off the cliff’s edge towards the icy water below.

Nick knew he was in trouble. He clawed the cliff trying to slow his fall, but he
was flipping too quickly.

From the corner of his eye he spied a large bush. He reached out, but his arms
just sped through the thin branches. By torquing his body though, Nick changed
his trajectory and slammed into a loose grouping of rocks. Nick ended up on his
bottom sliding towards the water. He dug his heels in the ground, but there
didn’t seem anyway to stop his momentum.

The water was 30 feet below and approaching fast.

Nick rolled on his stomach and used his hands to slow his descent. He leaned to
the left and saw a hole in the ground, it must be an eagles nest.

The drop off to the river was only a few feet away.

As his feet slipped off the cliff, Nick plowed his arm into the eagles nest. The
hole was deep and his arm went in up to his armpit. His body stopped, but pain
shot through his already aching shoulder.

The river crashed against the rocks. Foamy white topped currents splashed
against Nick’s feet.

Nick’s face was plastered in freezing snow, his arm was crammed in a deep
burrow, and his legs were dangling off the cliff. He turned his head and coughed
out shards of ice and dirt that filled his mouth. He took his first real breath
since the fall.

Nicks head was spinning. he felt like a church bell that was ringing twelve
noon. The fall had stopped, but his body was still in motion, at least it seemed
to be.

He was dazed and couldnt hear himself breathe. The roar of the river encompased
his whole being. Once at a county festival a canon was shot a few feet away. He
couldn't hear for ten minutes. This was worse.

With his ear pressed against the ice, Nick looked down and regarded his
situation. Though hanging by a thread, he had avoided the river.

The river was certain death. Nick’s father had always warned that freezing water
shuts down the heart.

At that moment, a wave of fear rushed over Nick’s body. His attackers, where
were they? He shifted his head away from the river and up the cliff.

Large rocks obscured the view, but parts of the ledges horizon were visible.

Would the attackers follow him? He remained still and glared up the mountain.
The ledge was about one hundred feet above. He studied the ledge for movement.

Suddenly he saw something. Between a crevice of one of the jagged rocks, he
could make out the silhouette of a man.

They were looking for him.

Nick pressed the side of his head against the lip of the cliff. He would be
difficult to spot this far down the cliff, especially with the rocks in the way,
but he didn’t want to take any chances. He would have to wait until they left.

With his ear pressed against the freezing snow, Nick considered what had just
taken place. Was the camp overrun by bandits? That didn’t seem likely this high
in the mountains.

Was it the Grins? Nothing else made sense. Though he didn't like the idea of
accusing them without proof.

He could think of a few reasons the Grins didn’t like him, but none that would
result in him being shoved off a cliff to a frozen death at the bottom of a
river.

Nick glanced at the river. It was daring him to fall in. He decided this was not
the time to solve a mystery. He needed to move, but what were his options?

It had been about fifteen minutes since he’d been pushed, was it safe to move?
Nick wasn’t sure. It wouldn’t take long to dismantle the camp and head down the
mountain. And with the gold sample in hand the Grins wouldn’t have a reason to
stay. What about the sample? The Grins were probably rifling through the pack
right now. That sample was supposed to prove that the mine would yield gold for
at least two more years.

Looking left and right, Nick decided that if he was going to see any of that
gold, let alone the next morning, he'd better get moving. Nick examined the
terrain, considering if he could climb back up, but the drop offs were too
sharp. He wouldn’t be able to make it without equipment.

Equipment. He'd left his pack by the fire: food, supplies, and of course, the
letters that Heather had written him.

The letters were years old and anything but juicy, but the thought of Donald
holding them burned a fire in Nick’s gut. Heather loved to write about her plans
to become a doctor. Nick responded two or three times, but a few years back, the
letters stopped. He figured she had finally forgotten about him. Nick kept each
one though. He would read them during the longs days by himself working as a
logger.

Since he didn’t have a permanent residence back in town, he brought them with
him everywhere. He thought they might give him a good reason to start a
conversation with Heather. At the moment however, they were just another thing
that had gone wrong that day. He needed a plan.

With his arm entrenched in the hole, Nick’s body was pointing down the river. He
didn’t have a good view of the terrain behind him. He twisted his neck to take a
glance. With his peripheral vision, Nick spotted a pack of large boulders about
ten feet up and ten feet over. They might provide cover from the wind and snow.

With pain flaring through his shoulder, Nick pulled himself onto the slope he
had just fallen down and crawled towards shelter.
