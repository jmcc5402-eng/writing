## CHAPTER 3.1

The door squeaked slightly and Nick froze.

His heart was pounding. He tried to listen for someone moving, but all he could
hear was the pulsating between his ears. After a moment, he continued into the
office.

Inside he glanced around the premise, then slowly closed the door. Turning again
to face the room, he noted the moonlight splashing through the raggedy windows.
He wouldn’t need a light.

Nick’s mind raced. What was he doing here? He wasn’t a thief. He didn’t know how
to break into safes. He told himself he was there to find his father’s belt and
he wasn’t going to take anything that wasn't rightfully his. Then he thought
about the folks in town. They were forced to eat gruel every night because they
couldn’t afford beef, returning their stolen gold wouldn’t be a crime. He
briefly pondered how his father would handle the situation. Though an honest and
law abiding man, he wasn’t one to blindly follow the rules. If Nick could help
the people in town at a small cost to a couple of crooks, it was his duty to
help. He continued into the room.

The room consisted of a large oak desk, a number of cabinets, and a long sofa.
Behind the desk was a small table that held a scale and a large safe. Inside the
desk, Nick knew, was a hidden compartment that held gold and the key to the
safe. He would contend with the hidden compartment after looking for the belt.

The room was baren and a quick search convinced him the belt was not there. Nick
considered his options. He could attempt to enter the Grin’s cabin or just focus
on the gold. The Grins lived in a small cabin connected to the office by a long
porch. He decided it would be impossible to search the cabin without being
caught. The belt would have to wait for another day. He would wait until one of
them took a trip to the city, then he might have a chance. Pushing the belt out
of his mind for the moment, he strode to the desk.

He recalled Donald retrieving the key from a panel on the left side. Kneeling
behind the desk, Nick’s fingers scoured the wood for a clue to open the hidden
compartment. It was too dark to see beneath the desk, so he ran his hand under a
large section of oak. His fingers grazed a seam in the wood and searched for a
mechanism for opening it. Nothing.

After a few minutes he was getting frustrated. He knew if the Grins walked in,
he would be dead meat. Desperate for a clue, Nick opened the drawer to the desk.
It was full of the normal items, paper, ink, and receipts. He slid his hand
along the bottom of the drawer. Something nicked his palm. Nick squeezed his
eyes and tried to see what it was. Another seam. This one was slightly raised
and gave Nick an idea. He pressed down on the seam while simultaneously feeling
the seam underneath.They were connected.

He gently squeezed the two together and then heard a click. The panel of wood
beneath the door popped out from the desk a quarter inch.

Nick’s heart jumped. This was it, the secret compartment. He pulled it open. The
panel eased out on a well oiled track.

Inside the panel was a small safe and numerous small burlap sacks full of coal,
black powder on the edges gave them away. Along the inside panel there was a row
of keys. The keys were labeled: Safe, Secondary, Safe 2, Secondary.

He grabbed the first key and put it into the small safe. Nothing happened. It
must open the large safe on the ground. Thinking he would open that next, he
quickly placed it in his pocket and reached for the second key. It worked.

Lining the small safe were gold sacks, neatly aligned in rows. The Grins were
very organized.

After inspecting the sacks, Nick decided to play it conservative. He would take
a small amount of gold from each bag. He thought about placing bits of coal in
each sack to maintain it’s weight, but reconsidered. He reasoned the Grins were
less likely to weigh the skimmed gold. And if they did, they’d probably blame
each other, they’d never know it was him.

For a moment he considered the point of this adventure. He didn’t need gold, he
actually hated gold. But the town was getting swindled. If he could give a
little back, life might get a bit easier in Bronston. He wouldn’t be here long
anyways. Now that he knew Heather was with Donald, he just wanted to help out
the townsfolks, get his belt, and get out of Alaska.

He moved as fast as he could. Nick reached into the safe to grab the second to
last bag, but it was upside down. As he pulled the sack out of the safe, all of
the gold spilled. Half of the metal ended up in the safe and the other half on
the ground. Nick let out a short gasp and cursed himself for hurrying.

Just then he heard a noise. His jaw clenched shut. He glanced at the gold spread
out around him, he was red-handed. Nick froze. He peered through a crack in the
desk. through the foggy window, nick saw a dog approaching the door. A chill of
fear rushed through Nick’s body. He didn’t know they had guard dogs. He silently
chastised himself for this entire foolish act. He had no business being here.
And The fact that he didn't think to learn they owned a guard dog was proof of
his incompetence.

Nick held still as the dog tromped past the window. Surprisingly, Nick had a
momentary feeling of nostalgia. He recognized the dog. It was Old Gus.

Old Gus was a sleepy hound that had been loafing around the mine since Nick was
a kid. Nick and the Grin brothers spent hours playing chase and catch with the
dog. One time, Nick accompanied the Grins on an elk hunt. Old Gus went too,
though he wasn’t much help. He prefered to sniff the bushes and lay in the sun.

Then Nick realized this could not be Old Gus. That dog must have died years ago,
but the resemblance was uncanny. This must be an offspring. Nick smiled and
whispered to himself, “Hello, Young Gus.”

If Young Gus came inside he would quickly see Nick. And Nick didn’t want to take
the chance that Young Gus was as lazy as his father.

Nick was crouched behind the desk holding his breath. Hounds had great senses of
smell, but they also loved to sleep. Did the dog smell him? Or hear drop the
gold? Nick didn’t move.

Thirty seconds later, the dog moved on. Nick crept to the window and watched
Young Gus meander down the porch to the Grin’s bunk house. The dog went inside.
Nick waited a moment and then decided it was time to leave.

He had one and a half bags of nugget, he estimated two bags would be enough for
everyone in town to get a little piece. He got right to work and finished
filling the second bag.

After ensuring the office looked untouched, Nick crept out the front door and
made his way to the woods. He didn’t feel safe until he felt the cool wind
pressing against his face as the sleigh lifted off the ground. The night seemed
to be a success. The next day he would sneak into town and leave a donation on
everyone’s doorstep.

Nick held onto the edges of the sleigh as he veered to the left and towards the
mountains. Reaching for his scarf, he put a hand in his pocket. He felt
something hard and rigid. A wave of fear pulsed through him.

His fingers examined the object in the pocket. He had forgotten to return the
key to the safe.

## CHAPTER 3.2

Nick was exhausted and his fingers and toes were frozen. The flight back to the
cave had taken forever. Lying on the ground he wished he had started a fire to
warm his extremities, but he had been too tired.

The night had been a success, only one minor incident. After placing the sack of
gold on Mrs. Hannigans porch, a floor panel creaked. Nick hopped off the porch,
but was amazed how quickly the elderly women had opened the door and peered into
the freezing night. He slouched in the shadows for thirty minutes to ensure she
was back in bed before he moved on.

The last house he had passed was Heathers and her fathers. He stared at the
familiar wooden panels for a long time wondering how she had gotten caught up
with the Grins. She had been a shining star as a child. So bright and full of
life. Even back then she dreamed of moving to San Francisco to become a doctor.
During Nick’s long journey to California with his mother, he imagined a day that
he would surprise her in San Francisco and they would reunite forever.

But back in the cave, Nick regretted to believe that dream was gone. She was
with Donald now. She must have given up on becoming a doctor. Nick just couldn’t
believe it. What had she seen in him? He was dark and brooding. Totally self-
consumed. Totally greedy, willing to steal money from the town he was supposed
to be supporting. She had just wanted to help people. It didn’t make sense.

And why weren’t they married yet? They were both of age. What were they waiting
for? Maybe she was pushing it off. Maybe she was turned off by the fact that
Donald was stealing the towns gold. Maybe she didn’t know. That made sense. Why
would Don tell her, she was not likely helping the operation?

Nick stepped through what he knew of the Grins. They made a living by managing
the mine and the town. They controlled almost all of the goods that arrived at
the dock. They already made good money, what did they do with the skim? Maybe
they gambled it away when they went to Juno?

Donald had mentioned Heather held a job at the merchant market. Donald said she
spent time on the job receiving shipments of goods from the cargo boat the grins
operated. It must be a freezing job out on the dock. Why would she work if
Donald was rich?

She must not know about the skimming.

Though he was laying on frozen rock and could barely feel his toes, Nick’s body
filled with warmth. He had hope. If he told Heather about the skimming she would
be shocked. She would call off the engagement at once.

Nick would be there for her.

It wouldn’t be long until they fell in love. He would rekindle her desire to
become a doctor. They could move back to California. They wouldn’t see Donald
Grin ever again.

He began to formulate a plan. He had to tell her about the skimming. He would
have to find her alone, it wouldn’t be easy, this was a small town. But this
time the Grin’s wouldn’t get in his way. This time he had the advantage, this
time he had the sleigh.

## CHAPTER 3.5

*“I'm Waiting for Don on the boat,”* Heather had said. Remembering her words
felt like an ice pick in the chest. It was all over.

At least now he knew, Heather was in love with Donald. He couldn’t hear exactly
what she had said, but he saw the look on her face. It was the look of someone
waiting for their love to arrive. She was riveted by the arrival of the boat.
And the arrival of Donald.

She had almost seemed disgusted by the sight of Nick. It was not what he had
expected. He planned on her coming towards him under the dock and embracing him
for a long kiss. That didn’t happen.

He stared up at the ceiling of the cave. His body ached. Not a painful ache, but
an ache of emptiness.

Rain was sprinkling down and the sky was cloudy. A fitting day, Nick thought. He
tried to sit up, but his body wouldn’t listen to his mind. What was point, his
conscientious muttered, and Nick couldn’t think of a good one.

A few hours later, Nick finally rose. He was hungry. He started a fire and began
roasting the rabbit legs he had stored. He moved slowly, as if in a dream. He
saw his hands start the fire and tend to the food, but his mind was elsewhere,
in a fog. His senses had deserted him. The meat had no smell and no taste. Smoke
from the fire burned his eyes, but he didn’t blink. He chewed the rabbit and
gazed at the rain. He felt alone.

When Nick rose the next morning, he decided to leave Alaska. The only person who
cared for him was in California, he should be with her, his mother.

But he couldn’t leave without the belt. And now he didn’t care if the Grins saw
him or not. He would break into their house, steal the belt, and run. If they
didn’t shoot him in the back, they would never see him again.

The problem was finding the belt. Nick had searched the office and it wasn't
there. The bunkhouse was the only other place Nick could conceive they would
keep it. This was assuming they hadn’t tossed it in the river, or sold it in the
city. There was only one way to find out. That night he would head back to the
Grin mine.

## CHAPTER

Nick recalled before he moved to California that Donald Grin had strong feelings
about Heather. During the last months of his time in Alaska, Donald barely ever
spoke with Nick.

“Whats wrong with him?” Nick asked James one day walking home from the creek.
“He’s been a real jerk for the last few months.”

“Well, he’d kill me if I told you, but he’s in love with Heather,” James said,
kicking a rock on the path. “And well, everybody knows that you’re gonna marry
her one day.”

“Married! What are you talking about, I’m never getting married!” Nick crinkled
his nose and they both laughed. They didn’t talk much the rest of the way home.
‘Me and Heather?’ Nick wondered. ‘I don’t plan on staying in this small town. So
if they both want to stay, more power to ‘em.’

Years later while hauling logs in California, Nick regretted that conversation.
Did Donald ever hear what Nick had said? James likely spilled the beans. And
what about Heather? What a stupid thing to say. It was probably the reason she
went for Donald. He should have stood up for himself and said that he was going
to marry Heather. That they would move to California and live on the coast.
She’d become a doctor and he’d be a sheriff.

Now, as Nick flew the sleigh towards the Grin’s mine, he tried desperately to
focus on anything other than Heather. Luckily, as he approached the mine he saw
something that grabbed his attention. A wagon pulled by two horses was turning
off the Mine road and heading towards the city.

Nick knew this was a perfect opportunity to search the bunkhouse for the belt,
but something made him follow the wagon. He told himself he should head to the
bunkhouse and search for the belt. But his subconscious drove him towards the
wagon, fueling the tiny flicker of a chance that he would see Heather again.

He maintained a distance well behind the wagon. Even though he was in the air,
he knew there was a chance they would spot him.

Right then, the wagon stop. James Grin hopped out and began to clear a number of
bushes on the side of the road.

Nick pulled back on the sleigh and ascended higher. He led the sleigh in a broad
circle higher to provide a view of wagon. Once higher in the sky, Nick made out
a thin and rugged trail that led towards the river, a few miles to the east. The
trail was not well traveled, likely because of the trees and bushes concealing
it from the main road.

The wagon turned and continued down the trail. James reassembled the bushes and
trees to obscure the path and scurried towards the wagon, jumping on while it
was still moving. Apparently the driver didn’t feel it was necessary to wait.

Nick’s interest was piqued. Where were they going? He stood on the sleigh as it
levitated in place and stared at the wagon disappearing down the mysterious
trail. He considered his options. He could speed back to the empty mine and
search for the belt. If he found it, he could continue on to California, ending
this painful chapter of his life. If he followed the Grins, he knew it wouldn’t
end well. He would probably see Heather and Donald together. They might be going
to meet her right now.

As he considered what to do, Nick dropped the sleigh down to the intersection of
the main road and the hidden trail. He wondered why James went to all of the
trouble of concealing this path. That wasn’t normal. What were they hiding?

The trail descended slightly downward and then turned. Tall pines, oaks, and
underbrush conceded a slight path down the middle. Without knowledge of the
entrance, this trail would be impossible to spot by the casual traveler.

Nick lifted the sleigh from the ground to investigate from another angle. He
found himself following the trail, scouring the ground for anything of interest.

Moments later however, he heard the sounds of the wagon wheels grinding over the
rocky ground. His eyes shot forward and saw the wagon twenty feet ahead. He took
in a quick breath and then slammed back on the lever, bringing the sleigh to a
hault.

Nick realized his decision was made. He would follow the grins to see where this
hidden trail ended.

## CHAPTER

Nick felt the freezing ground on his chest. His body was in a prone position
peering off the edge of a short cliff. His eyes were unblinking, staring at the
scene below.

The sleigh was lightly concealed in a clump of tall bushes a few feet away. If
the Grins spotted him, he could make a quick get-away.

Below Nick was a crescent shaped cove. At the apex of the cove was a small dock
and boat. The cliff lurked forty feet above. The dock was well positioned in an
obscure nook of the cove so it would be difficult for a boat to notice from the
river.

Down below, the Grins loaded crates off the wagon onto a dolly. They rolled the
dolly towards a section of the mountain twenty feet from a small cottage. James
hopped off the wagon and sprinted towards the cabin. A few seconds later he
reappeared. Donald barked an angry remark, but James wasn’t phased. The men
moved shrubs aside along the mountain, revealing a large metal door.

Very interesting, Nick wondered to himself, first a secret trail and now a
secret vault.

Donald opened the door and the other men struggled to push the dolly through the
silven doorway. After sometime, the Grins rolled the dolly out of the cave and
dragged it over the gravelly path towards the boat.

There was a long plank connecting the earth and a dock. The dolly barely fit,
but smoothly rolled across the plank and onto the dock. Another plank was fitted
on the dock to load the dolly directly onto a series of storage comparts on the
boat. The three foot high compart received the crate from the dolly.

The cargo was loaded and the Grins meandered to the cabin. It appeared the Grins
followed every difficult task with a healthy dose of rum.

Nick decided he had a few minutes to survey the boat. After carefully sliding
down the steep hill, he crept down a path that separated his position from the
boat. Concealed by a line of underbrush, he was well covered unless the Grins
suspected someone of approaching.

Roughly thirty feet from the dock, Nick eased himself down the embankment that
led from the path to the water. During the rocky decline, Nick’s mind flashed
back to his adventure falling down the cliff in the mountains. He shook his head
and decided to focus on the task at hand.

Nick slid down the hill and then carefully navigated the rocky terrain until he
reached the dock. He climbed aboard the ship and crouched down to avoid being
viewed from the cabin. His knee scraped along the wooden deck of the boat as he
squirmed towards the payload. Nick grimaced as the the latch on the crate
squeaked loudly. Gratefully, the sounds of the choppy waves muffled the sound.

Inside the crate, Nick gazed at dozens of sacks of gold nuggets. He inspected a
sack, not exactly sure what to look for. It was the standard size and weight.
This made sense. The Grins made regular trips to the big city to deposit the
mine's gold. Nick had heard some men discussing some type of savings plan. What
was it called? An annuity.

He reached deeper into the crate, squeezing a number of sacks. Something was
wrong. The sacks on the bottom felt different.

A large wave rocked the boat to the left and right. Nick’s left leg slipped on
the wet deck and he momentarily lost his balance. Recovering, he took a moment
to observe the surrounding area. He couldn’t see anyone in the cabin, but he
didn’t see the Grins anywhere else. He needed to find out what was in the bottom
of that crate.

He gripped a bag at the bottom of the crate and pulled it towards the top.
Opening it, he saw black. This wasn't gold, it was coal. He quickly squeezed and
inspected a number of sacks. The eight at the top were gold, but the two dozen
or so at the bottom were coal.

Suddenly, Nick heard something. His cackles rose and he ducked down
instinctively. Creeping his head above the crates he saw the three men from the
cabin walking towards the boat. They were almost to dock. This investigation
would have to be put on hold.

Nick belly crawled to the end of the boat and slipped over the side.

## CHAPTER 3.

Nick’s left foot was freezing, but he didn’t move. He had dashed off the boat
and nearly broken his leg landing on the rocky river embankment. Just as the
Grins had come into sight, he dove behind a large boulder. Moments later a rush
of surf batted up the river bank and soaked his leg. He could barely feel his
toes.

As the boat was preparing to leave, Nick considered what he had seen onboard.
Why was the boat loaded with coal? Coal was *imported* into Bronston, not
exported. Nick had assumed the shipment was one of the regular deposits the
Grins made to Juno. Could that shipment be supplying coal to the town? Nick
didn’t think so. Coal arrived from a steamer about four times a year.
Transporting a few crates worth didn't make sense. Especially separated into
tiny bags.

Nick poked his head over the rock to watch the boat push from the dock. Until
that moment, he hadn’t questioned the existence of this small harbor. As the
boat slid out of view, Nick glanced around. Why did the Grins have two harbors?
Was this something to do with trading coal?

He thought about what had transpired since he arrived at the cove. He had
watched the Grins roll the dolly, stacked with crates, off the wagon into the
vault. The men then rolled the dolly, still loaded with crates, onto the boat.
Nick had assumed they were the same crates.

His assessment of the process only conjured additional questions. What had
happened in that vault? Was it used to store the coal or more gold? And why did
the Grins need it? Coal didn’t need to be stored in a safe. The safe at the mine
seemed more than adequate to hold the gold waiting to be transferred to Juno.

What about the gold the Grins had skimmed? They probably wanted a separate
location to store it. Nick did some quick calculations in his head and estimated
the skimmed gold, even if they had been skimming for five years, would only
amount to eight to ten pounds. The vault seemed a bit excessive for that amount.

What is they were skimming more than Nick knew? That seemed unrealistic. If they
had skimmed more, the whole operation would have been exposed. The miners
carefully inspected and weighed the bags when they were wrapped for deposit. And
later, the town received statements from the bank corresponding with the
deposits.

Nick decided he had to see what was in the vault. He also wanted to search that
small cabin. There was always a chance he would find his belt.

After peering over the lip of the embankment, Nick hustled to the bush that
concealed the vault door. It was large and metal. Frowning, Nick realized he was
missing something important.

The key.

## CHAPTER X

Nick’s finger blindly felt for the latch...click. The door swung open. Nick
breathed out slowly and then crept into the small cabin. He looked around,
struggling to see through the fading sunlight shimmering in through the windows.

He entered into a rear workshop. Carpentry tools were scattered about the floor
and hung on the walls with hooks. The main room was small and unkept. There was
an office area with a desk and a number of cabinets and then a fireplace,
sitting area, and small eating nook.

Nick guessed this room was used for guzzling rum and not much else. There were
two couches that appeared to be used as beds.

Starting in the workshop, Nick searched for a key to the vault. Nothing. He
moved into the next room. The eating area was sparse...but the office, something
looked familiar.

The desk.

This was the same desk as in the mine cabin. Would it have the secret
compartment?

Nick leapt towards the desk and knelt down. Opening the draw he once again
placed his hand at the bottom of the drawer and under the wooden panel at the
bottom. And once again the compartment clicked open.

Inside of this drawer Nick saw the key. His eyes opened wide and he grabbed it.
He couldn’t believe his luck.

Moments later he stood in front of the vault. His heart was pounding as he
turned the key.There was a loud screech as the door budged open. Nick grimaced
and though the door needed to be greased.

Nick poked his head into the room and his mouth gaped open.

It was a long corridor stretching back at least thirty feet. The room was
roughly ten feet wide with large shelves lining each side. Each shelf held
various crates, the same crates the Grins had loaded from the dolly.

Nick brushed his hand along the crates as he drifted down the dusty hallway. He
gazed to the left and right taking in this unique storage facility. The shelves
were attached directly to the rock. The entire room was carved from the cliff
side.

Nick hated to admit it, but he was impressed. The Grins must have built this
vault. It appeared to be constructed in the last ten years so.

But why?

Nick focused on a crate midway down the corridor. Gripping the sides he tried to
lift it, it was heavy. He grabbed a crowbar from the wall and began to pry the
top off of the crate. After wedging each side, the top released.

Throwing the top on the ground, Nick looked into the crate. His brow furrowed in
confusement.

The crate was full of gold sacks. These were not the small sacks from before,
but larger bank-style sacks. The side of each was labeled ‘3lbs’. Picking one
up, Nick guessed this was accurate.

The crate contained about ten of these large sacks. This was a lot of gold.

Suddenly, Nick’s eyes shot wide open. He stepped back feeling dizzy. He broke
his gaze off of the crate in front of him and slowly rotated his head left and
right.

A thought rippled through his brain, could each of these crates hold this much
gold? Nick began to feel queasy.

He snatched the crowbar and head towards the next crate. It was full of gold.
The next, and the next. All full of gold.

Nick stopped for a moment and counted the crates, he could see thirty six. His
mind was spinning.

But just then he heard something. Fear pulsated throughout his body.

It was someone on horseback.

He lunged for the doorway. Luckily only a crack was open. He glared out,
searching the surroundings for the rider.

At that moment, Nick was grateful for the shrubs concealing the vault. He could
see Duff dismounting his horse next to the dock.

It was unlikely Duff noticed the cracked vault door so he had some time. Nick
slowly pulled the door further shut and considering what to do.

In a flash, he ran down the corridor and placed the tops on each of the crates.
It would be obvious they had been opened, but at the moment he didn’t want to
draw any attention by banging them shut.

Holding his breath, he glanced out the cracked vault door. He didn’t see Duff.

He waited.

Finally, a noise came from the cabin. Duff was dragging two saw horses from the
cabin to the dock. He looked ready to do some work. After setting up the saw
horses, he went back to the cabin.

Nick didn’t miss a beat. When Duff entered the cabin he slipped through the
vault door. Quickly glancing towards the cabin, Nick then shouldered the vault
door closed. It shut with a bang, but there was no other choice.

Staying low, Nick scurred along the cliffs edge away from the cabin and dock.
After a few paces, Nick judged it safe to turn around.

Duff was harmlessly setting up his workspace on the dock.

At the top of the cliff, Nicked lingered for a moment and saw that Duff was
making some additions to the dock gangway. What was he working on?

Moments later, as the sun began to set, Nick felt the cold air brisk past his
face. The sleigh was cruising above the trees, angling towards the cave.

Nick finally understood why the Grins tried to kill him.

## CHAPTER

Nick wanted to scream. His right fist pounded into his left hand. How could he
have missed this? It’s all a lie. Everything has been a lie. The annuity, a lie.
The big payment for the town, a lie. All the talk about the mine lasting two or
three more years, it was a lie too.

Nick wasn’t sure what to believe anymore. The only thing he knew for certain was
that he and Heather were not going to be together.

The two caribou were back. Nick watched them munch grass for a while. He didn’t
want to think about anything. The big animals calmed him. They weren’t much
different than the huge oxen used back in California. But these beasts didn’t
have the answer to his problems. It didn’t seem anyone did.

What could he do? He was just one man. The Grins were rich and powerful, they
were feared, and they had Heather. If Nick started an uproar in the town, it
wouldn’t hold any weight. He’d also be dead before he knew it. The Grins
obviously didn’t put murder below them, the moment they saw Nick they’d kill
him. And since they *were* the law of this town, there wouldn’t be much
consequence.

Nick considered his options. Sabotage? He could blow up the secret vault. But
that would destroy the gold, and any chance the town had for getting it back. He
could sneak into town and talk with Jim Richards, Heather’s dad. He would
listen. Or maybe ol’James. But that was risky, if anyone saw him it would be a
madhouse and the Grins would surely find out.

He could steal the gold. Would that work?

The mountain goat bucked its way down the cliff to the grassy knoll. What would
he do? Nick stared at these animals. They were strong. They would take the gold.
They wouldn’t hesitate.

The vault was left unattended for hours, maybe days at a time. He could fly in,
open the vault, load the sleigh, and escape.

If he showed up in town with a large enough sample of the stolen gold, the folks
would have to listen to him. And if he had all the gold, that would be better.

Nick began to plan. It was a final attempt. Grab the gold and bring it to town.
If Jim and James saw him, especially with a wagon full of gold, they would know
that something was wrong. They would stop and listen to him.

Nick mapped out the cove in his mind. He could come in from the river and bring
the sleigh in close to the cliff. He would land next to the vault, the bushes
would provide cover. Though that was unlikely to be needed. If he was seen, the
gig was up.

Nick thought about the dock. Duff was making an addition to it. What was the
point of that? He couldn’t think of a reason, but he didn’t like it. At any
point the Grins could decide to leave town. That was a another reason not to
head to town, if the Grins got wind of a commotion, they could probably take off
in a manner of hours.

When should this happen? His mind was spinning. So many details and only one
chance to get this right. Time is of the essence Nick thought, but he also tried
to have patience. There was only one chance at this.

He turned up the mountain and towards the cave. He would head to the cove
tonight.

## CHAPTER X

Two days had passed since Nick discovered the towns gold hidden in the secret
vault concealed in the cliff. For most of that time, Nick had remained hidden
behind the bush at the top of the cliff observing the situation. The Grins had
just returned. The town assumed they went to Juno, but now Nick knew they only
came to this secret cove.

The cabin and vault were left alone most of the day. Nick continually kicked
himself for not running down and retrieving the gold. But each of these thoughts
was interrupted by someone. During the first afternoon, Duff had arrived and
continued the construction he had started on the dock. He was building a plank
that bypassed the dock. The new plank would reach from the ground directly to
the boat. Nick assumed this was for transporting the entire stock of gold. That
could only mean one thing, they’re planning on leaving soon.

At night, Duff was joined by Dwayne, another of the workers from the mine. They
spent each evening playing cards and drinking whiskey. They never paid attention
to the mountain cave safe, but they also never left. This altered the plans.
Nick would have to load the gold during the night, when the workers were drunk,
or even better, asleep.

Nick crouched behind the shrubs at the top of the cliff. The vantage point was
excellent, but spying on the camp would not get the town’s gold back. He needed
to act. Tonight would be the night.

Nick slipped down the path from his viewing point and edged along the mountain
side until he reached the shrubs that concealed the vault. The shrubs blocked
the view of the vault from most people, but in this case it also obscured Nick
from the cabin. With the key, Nick inched the door open, hoping to avoid the
high pitched screech. Nick felt the freezing rock scrape along his back as he
squeezed through the door into the vault.

The plan was simple. Load the gold, at least as much as could fit, from the
vault to the sleigh. The sleigh was currently at the top of the cliff, Nick
decided to preposition the gold by the door before he flew the sleigh into
position. That way, if something went wrong, the sleigh wouldn’t be exposed.

After 15 minutes of work, twelve crates of gold were stacked by the door to the
vault. Nick slid out of the metal door and crouched behind the bushes. With his
knees in the snow, Nick gazed over to the cabin ensuring Duff and Dwayne were
still occupied. After a deep breath, Nick rose towards the cliff and the sleigh.

At that moment, however, he saw Duff stand up and turn towards the vault. Nick
froze. His heart jumped and he felt a wave of fear rush down his throat. He was
a sitting duck. If he tried to scurry back to the sleigh, Duff would see him.
The branch was providing some cover and it was dark so Nick stood still.

What was going on? While watching the dock the last two days, Nick never noted
the guards making regular checks on the safe.

Nick watched as Duff walked slowly towards the vault. He cursed himself for
being in this situation. He didn’t have a gun or even a knife. Nick waited
silently, watching Duff approach. He covered his face with his scarf, when Duff
got close, he would jump him, push him down, and sprint up the hill to the
sleigh.

Nick could not believe this turn of events. If the Grins found out someone, let
alone Nick, was creeping around the vault, they would surely pack up the gold
and leave. The gold would be gone and the town would be devastated. At least the
townsfolks could continue to mine, though less gold than before, the mine still
managed to bring in a healthy chunk of metal.

Duff turned and yelled something back towards the house. It didn’t seem like he
was being very security conscious. Then he slowed down and turned toward the
mountain. He was about 15 feet from Nick, but concealed by another row of
bushes.

Was there a second secret vault? Nick kicked himself for not checking.

Could that be a weapons vault? Nick would have to give himself up if Duff had a
gun. What would happen if the sleigh got into the Grin’s hands?

Nick turned his head when he heard something unexpected. Was there a stream over
by Duff? Was he coming to get water?

Suddenly it became clear. Nick had to cover his mouth to muffle a snicker. Duff
wasn’t coming over to check on the safe, he was peeing.

When Duff returned to the porch, Nick’s heart slowly returned to normal speed.
Without moving his feet, he rotated his head around to regain his bearings, and
composure.

Then he heard louder talking. He froze again. What was going on?

Nick looked closer and saw that James Grin had arrived at the cabin. He had
taken the cliff road along the coast.

What was he doing here? He’d come once while Nick watched. This was not good,
James might want to check the safe.

Before Nick decided what to do he felt something under his feet.

Huh?

The ground started to shake. An earthquake.

Nick fell to one knee, this was pretty bad. He fell back on the safe door, the
door slammed shut. The Grins would have certainly heard the sound, but when Nick
glanced over he saw them running into the house. Not smart in an earthquake, the
cabin didn’t look very sturdy.

From above the house, Nick heard a thunderous rumble. Nick imaged a stampede of
horses, and they were coming straight for him. Nick looked up and cringed.
Avalanche.

The avalanche 10 years ago changed the fate of this town forever. Would this one
change it again? Nick was starting to think fate was not on his side. All he
wanted to do was help this town that he loved. But it didn’t seem that Mother
Nature was on his side.

It didn’t appear anything was coming down to this level. Composing himself, Nick
shoot a look to the cliff above him. Falling rock was a huge danger in these
situations.

Then, a high pitch crack skewered Nick’s eardrums. He covered his head and dove
for cover. When he got his bearings, he realized this was a familiar sound. He
scanned the area and ensured he was not in danger. There was a large oak tree in
front of the house that had been uplifted by the quake. The treetop snapped the
trunk like a twig. The huge tree clobbered the porch in front of the cabin.

Maybe the Grins were smart to rush inside because a giant limb shattered the
porch where the men had been sitting. It appeared that the front door was
blocked shut.

This might buy Nick some time.

Nick scurried up the mountain back to the sleigh. With the Grins trapped in the
cabin, he wasn’t going to give up on the gold so easily.

Ten minutes later, the sleigh was packed with twelve crates of gold. Nick
glanced back at the cabin, but didn’t see any movement. The guys were either
content to stay inside or couldn’t get out. Both worked for Nick.

Nick mounted the sleigh and pulled the handle to take off. The sleigh jerked and
shuttered. Then stopped. It didn’t move. That hadn’t happened before.

Nick leaned forward, yanked the handled again. The sleigh’s front lifted off the
ground, but then slumped back to the ground.

What was going on?

Nick tried a third time. Nothing. This could not be happening, Nick though,
clenching his fists and squeezing closed his eyes. The sleigh had worked
perfectly for three weeks and this is when it decides to have mechanical
difficulty. Impossible.

Nick looked around frantically, searching for a solution. Did the avalanche
damage the sleigh? He jumped off and inspected the machine. He didn’t see any
damage.

Nick glanced towards the cabin. The front door was blocked, but Duffs armed was
flailing out a window trying to cut away the impeding branches. They would check
the gold the moment they got out.

Nick’s temper got the best of him and he stomped his foot on floor of the
sleigh. At that moment there was a small aftershock. Three of the crates fell of
the sleigh and landed in the snow. As Nick turned to inspect the fallen crates,
he pulled on the ignition handle. The sleigh lurched forward. This time further
than before. Nick whipped his head back to the front. He tried the lever again.
The sleigh took off into the air for a brief moment. He glanced at the crates
lying in the snow. Was the extra weight of the gold affecting the sleigh? He
hadn’t considered if the sleigh could handle heavy loads.

Nick stacked the rest of the crates on the ground and attempted to take off. The
sleigh elevated into the air without delay. Nick’s mind raced with
possibilities. What should he do? Should he take multiple trips to the top of
the cliff? It would only take about five minutes.

He heard yelling. The Grins were sawing a branch through the front window of the
cabin. The branch held the front door closed. If they cut the branch, they would
be free.

Smoke drifted from the rear of the cabin. A fire had started. This must have
kick started them into action. Nick knew that fires were the main cause of death
after earthquakes.

Moments later, the three men stumbled out of the front door. Climbing through
the rest of the fallen tree, they crawled onto the gravel walkway in front of
the house. James Grin stood up and yelled, ‘well, don’t just stand there, check
the vault!’

Duff bolted for the vault, but realized he didn’t have the key. James had one on
his belt loop. He handed it to Duff, who dashed off for the vault thirty yards
away.

The vault door creaked open and Duff gasped. James started to jog over and
yelled, “What’s wrong?”

“Nothing boss, no worries,” Duff replied. “It just looks like the crates fell
off the racks during the shake up. One, two,” he started counting. “...five,
six...yeah, no worries, looks like there all here.”

“Well, ‘looks like it’ won’t cut it for Donald. Stack them all up and do a
proper count.” James said, already knowing that Donald was probably having a
heart attack wondering about the gold.

James poked his head into the vault. The gold crates were scattered all over the
floor. Some of the gold had spilled out, but Duff was right, it looked like most
of it was here.

## CHAPTER X

The wind whipped in his face. Nick was furious.

How did he not think of testing the sleigh with a large load? He had spent years
hauling logs in the forest and one of the first rules was to not overload your
trailer. Now the Grins, and the towns gold, were leaving in a day or two and
nothing would stop them. They were probably going to leave right away, after
this shock. Nick couldn’t think of anything that would stop them. This would be
the end of the city. Everyone would starve.

And Heather. She was gone. The Grins would pack up and leave and she would go
with them.

Nick remembered he still had the sleigh. He could find a way to help the people,
even if he couldn’t return the gold they had broken their backs for. He could
help them trade with the Inuits or maybe haul food from the city. The sleigh
could be used in the mine. Maybe he could haul small amounts of gold to the bank
in Juno. This time they would get real cash in return. It would take a while,
but he could help the town get back on its feet. It was a long shot, but this is
why he’d returned to Bronston, to make some positive change.

The next moment, Nick’s optimism disappeared completely. The hopeful grin under
his scarf was replaced as his eyes widened and his mouth hung open in disbelief.
He was passing over the mine, but something was very wrong.

He hadn’t think things could get any worse, but they just did. There would be no
hauling gold for the mine. There would be no nothing for the mine.

The sleigh circled above the Grin mine like a hawk preying on a rabbit. As Nick
circled around he saw a cacophony of dirt, wood, and snow mixed together.

The entrance to the mine was not just covered, the entire mine had caved in.
