## CHAPTER 3.1

The door squeaked slightly and Nick froze.

His heart was pounding. He tried to listen for someone moving, but all he could
hear was the pulsating between his ears. After a moment, he continued into the
office.

Inside he glanced around the premise, then slowly closed the door. Turning again
to face the room, he noted the moonlight splashing through the raggedy windows.
He wouldn’t need a light.

Nick’s mind raced. What was he doing here? He wasn’t a thief. He didn’t know how
to break into safes. He told himself he was there to find his father’s belt and
he wasn’t going to take anything that was rightfully his. Then he thought about
the folks in town. They were forced to eat gruel every night because they
couldn’t afford beef. Returning their stolen gold wouldn’t be a crime. He
briefly pondered how his father would handle the situation. Though an honest and
law abiding man, he wasn’t one to blindly follow the rules. If Nick could help
the people in town at a small cost to a couple of crooks, it was his duty to
help. He continued into the room.

The room consisted of a large oak desk, a number of cabinets, and a long sofa.
Behind the desk was a small table that held a scale and a large safe. Under the
desk, Nick knew, was a hidden compartment that held gold and the key to the
safe. He would contend with the hidden compartment after looking for the belt.

The room was baren. A quick search convinced him the belt was not there. Nick
considered his options. He could attempted to enter the Grin’s cabin or just
focus on the gold. The Grins lived in a small cabin connected to the office by a
long porch. He decided it would be impossible to search the cabin without being
caught. The belt would have to wait for another day. He would wait until one of
them took a trip to the city, then he might have a chance. Pushing the belt out
of his mind for the moment, he strode to the desk.

He recalled Donald retrieving the key from a panel on the left side. Kneeling
behind the desk, Nick’s fingers scoured the wood for a clue to open the hidden
compartment. It was too dark to see beneath the desk, so he ran his hand under a
large section of oak. His fingers grazed a seam in the wood and searched for a
mechanism for opening it. Nothing.

After a few minutes he was getting frustrated. He knew if the Grins walked in,
he would be dead meat. Desperate for a clue, Nick opened the drawer to the desk.
It was full of the normal items, paper, ink, and receipts. He slid his hand
along the bottom of the drawer. Something nicked his palm. Nick squeezed his
eyes and tried to see what it was. Another seam. This one however, was slightly
raised, and gave Nick an idea. He pressed down on the seam while simultaneously
feeling the seam underneath.They were connected.

He gently squeezed the two together and then heard a click. The panel of wood
beneath the door popped out from the desk a quarter inch.

Nick’s heart jumped. This was it, the secret compartment. He pulled it open. The
panel eased out on a well oiled track.

Inside the panel was a small safe and numerous small burlap sacks full of coal,
black powder on the edges gave them away. Along the inside panel there was a row
of keys. The keys were labeled: Safe, Secondary, Safe 2, Secondary.

He grabbed the first key and put it into the small safe. Nothing happened. It
must open the large safe on the ground. Thinking he would open that next, he
quickly placed it in his pocket and reached for the second key. It worked.

Lining the small safe gold sacks were neatly aligned in rows. The Grins were
very organized.

After inspecting the sacks, Nick decided to play it conservative. He would take
a small amount of gold from each bag. He thought about placing bits of coal in
each sack to maintain it’s weight, but reconsidered. He reasoned the Grins were
less likely to weigh the skimmed gold. And if they did, they’d probably blame
each other, they’d never know it was him.

For a moment he considered the point of this adventure. He didn’t need gold, he
actually hated gold. But the town was getting swindled. If he could give a
little back, life might get a bit easier in Bronston. He wouldn’t be here long
anyways. Now that he knew Heather was with Donald, he just wanted to help out
the townsfolks, get his belt, and get out of Alaska.

He moved as fast as he could. Nick reached into the safe to grab the second to
last bag, but it was upside down. As he pulled the sack out of the safe, all of
the gold spilled. Half of the metal ended up in the safe and the other half on
the ground. Nick let out a short gasp and cursed himself for hurrying.

Just then he heard a noise. Nick froze. He peered through a crack in the desk
and saw a dog approach the door through a window. A chill of fear rushed through
Nick’s body. He didn’t know they had guard dogs.

Nick held still as the dog tromped past the window. Surprisingly, Nick had a
momentary feeling of nostalgia. He recognized the dog. It was Old Gus.

Old Gus was a sleepy hound that had been loafing around the mine since Nick was
a kid. Nick and the Grin brothers spent hours playing chase and catch with the
dog. One time, Nick went accompanied the Grins on an elk hunt. Old Gus went too.
He wasn’t much help. He prefered to sniff the bushes and lay in the sun.

Then Nick realized this could not be Old Gus. That dog must have died years ago,
but the resemblance was uncanny. This must be an offspring. Nick smiled and
whispered to himself, “Hello, Young Gus.”

If Young Gus came inside he would quickly see Nick. And Nick didn’t want to take
the chance that Young Gus was as lazy as his father.

Nick was crouched behind the desk holding his breath. Hounds had great senses of
smell, but they also loved to sleep. Did the dog smell him? Or hear drop the
gold? Nick didn’t move.

Thirty seconds later, the dog moved on. Nick crept to the window and watched
Young Gus meander down the porch to the Grin’s bunk house. The dog went inside.
Nick waited a moment and then decided it was time to leave.

He had one and a half bags of nugget, he estimated two bags would be enough for
everyone in town to get a little piece. He got right to work and finished
filling the second bag.

After ensuring the office looked untouched, Nick crept out the front door and
made his way to the woods. He didn’t feel safe until he felt the cool wind
pressing against his face as the sleigh lifted off the ground. The night seemed
to be a success. The next day he would sneak into town and leave a donation on
everyone’s doorstep.

Nick held onto the edges of the sleigh as he veered to the left and towards the
mountains. Reaching for his scarf, he put a hand in his pocket. He felt
something hard and rigid. A wave of fear pulsed through him.

His fingers examined the object in the pocket. He had forgotten to return the
key to the safe.

## CHAPTER 3.2

Nick was exhausted and his fingers and toes were frozen. The flight back to the
cave had taken forever. Lying on the ground he wished he had started a fire to
warm his extremities, but he had been too tired.

The night had been a success, only one minor incident. After placing the sack of
gold on Mrs. Hannigans porch a floor panel creaked. Nick hopped off the porch,
but was amazed how quickly the elderly women had opened the door and peered into
the freezing night. He slouched in the shadows for thirty minutes to ensure she
was back in bed before he moved on.

The last house he had passed was Heathers and her fathers. He stared at the
familiar wooden panels for a long time wondering how she had gotten caught up
with the Grins. She had been a shining star as a child. So bright and full of
life. Even back then she dreamed of moving to San Francisco to become a doctor.
During Nick’s long journey to California with his mother, he imagined a day that
he would surprise her in San Francisco and they would reunite forever.

But back in the cave, Nick regretted to believe that dream was gone. She was
with Donald now. She must have given up on becoming a doctor. Nick just couldn’t
believe it. What had she seen in him. He was dark and brooding. Totally self-
consumed. Totally greedy, willing to steal money from the town he was supposed
to be supporting. She had just wanted to help people. It didn’t make sense.

And why weren’t they married yet. They were both of age. What were they waiting
for? Maybe she was pushing it off. Maybe she was turned off by the fact that
Donald was stealing the towns gold. Maybe she didn’t know. That made sense. Why
would Don tell her, she was not likely helping the operation.

Nick stepped through what he knew of the Grins. They made a living by managing
the mine and the town. They controlled almost all of the goods that arrived at
the dock. They already made good money, what did they do with the skim? Maybe
they gambled it away when they went to Juno?

Donald had mentioned Heather held a job at the merchant market. Why would she
work if Donald was rich? She must not know about the skimming.

Though he was laying on frozen rock and could barely feel his toes, Nick’s body
filled with warmth. He had hope. If he told Heather about the skimming she would
be shocked. She would call off the engagement at once. Nick would be there for
her. It wouldn’t be long until they fell in love. He would rekindle her desire
to become a doctor. They could move back to California. They wouldn’t see Donald
Grin ever again.

He began to formulate a plan. He had to tell her about the skimming. He would
have to find her alone, it wouldn’t be easy, this was a small town. But this
time the Grin’s wouldn’t get in his way. This time he had the advantage, this
time he had the sleigh.

## CHAPTER 3.5
