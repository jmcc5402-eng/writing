# Young Nicholas — Story Bible & Master Plan

*The secret origin of Santa Claus.*

> **Working logline:** At nineteen, a young outdoorsman returns to the run-down
> Alaskan mining town of his childhood to find the people starving and his
> childhood love promised to the man who owns the mine. Pushed off a cliff and
> left for dead by people he trusted, he survives — and discovers a hidden,
> glowing sleigh whose markings match his late father's belt. As he secretly
> gives the swindled townsfolk back their stolen gold, he answers a question
> millions of children ask every December.

This document is the single source of truth for the project: who the characters
are, what happens, what the world's rules are, the open decisions still to make,
and the multi-platform plan for getting it out into the world.

---

## 1. The Hook — why this story matters

*Young Nicholas* is a **Santa Claus origin myth** disguised as a frontier
survival-adventure. Every familiar Santa element is "explained" through the plot,
which is what makes it commercial and evergreen:

| Santa tradition | Its origin in our story |
|---|---|
| The flying sleigh | Nick discovers a hidden, glowing sleigh in a mountain cave |
| The reindeer | Nick encounters them after his lowest moment and "has an idea" |
| Gifts that appear overnight | Nick secretly returns the town's stolen gold — **the town wakes to treasure** |
| Giving in secret | Nick can't let anyone know he's alive, so he must give anonymously |
| The red suit / warm glow | The glow of the belt medallion and the sleigh |
| The North Pole | The final medallion warps Nick away to parts unknown |
| "Saint" Nicholas | An ordinary young man choosing, again and again, to do good quietly |

**The keystone image:** a poor, swindled town waking up to find the gold they were
owed mysteriously returned in the night. That *is* the first Christmas morning —
and it's already in your outline ("The town wakes to gold").

---

## 2. Themes

- **Quiet courage vs. loud power.** Nick's father never fought the Grins and Nick
  wrongly thought him weak; Nick's arc is learning that standing up quietly, and
  at the right moment, is the truest strength.
- **Giving without credit.** The hero can take no recognition — the purest form of
  generosity, and the seed of the Santa legend.
- **Freedom vs. obligation.** Grace must free herself from the duty and control
  others place on her to choose her own life.
- **Hope as currency.** The town survives on the *promise* of the annuity; the
  villains weaponize that hope. Nick restores it.

## 3. Characters

### Nick (Young Nicholas) — protagonist
- ~19–21. Returned to Alaska after ~10 years growing up in Northern California
  (worked as a logger among the redwoods). Skilled outdoorsman, climber, gentle,
  honest — "as straight as an arrow."
- **The wound:** His father was pushed around by old man Grin and never fought
  back. Nick read this as weakness and was angry at his father the day he died.
  He's carried the fear that he, too, is weak — and Donald Grin needles this
  constantly.
- **The arc:** From balking (he can't even tell Grace he loves her) to finding the
  courage to face Donald and save Grace. He likely learns his father's
  restraint was strength, not cowardice (possibly a hero during the earthquake
  that killed him).
- **Carries:** His late father's **belt**, engraved with emblems/a medallion — the
  key to the sleigh's magic.
- *Continuity note:* surname appears as both "Anderson" and "Johnson" in drafts —
  **needs to be locked** (see §7).

### Grace — deuteragonist / love interest
- ✅ **Name locked: Grace.** *(She was "Heather" in the earliest drafts; we've
  standardized on the newer "Grace" everywhere going forward.)*
- Nick's childhood sweetheart. Fiercely smart with a remarkable memory.
- **Dream:** To leave for San Francisco and become a doctor — specifically to fight
  typhoid, the disease that killed her mother.
- **The wound:** She feels bound to care for her widowed father, and the Grins
  (especially Donald) use her father's dependence on the mine to control her.
- **The arc:** She stops letting other people rule her thoughts, confronts Donald,
  and claims her own future.
- **The misunderstanding engine:** At the dock, each wrongly concludes the other
  doesn't care — Nick thinks she's waiting for Donald; Grace thinks Nick only came
  back for gold. Neither learns the truth (that Nick returned to *propose*) until
  the climax.

### Donald "Don" Grin — primary antagonist
- Co-owner of Grin Bros. Mining with his brother. Grew up feeling unappreciated in
  the shadow of Nick's beloved father. Craves power and being "on top"; an
  "unending piece of his soul is missing."
- Skimming — actually **stealing all** — the town's annuity gold, planning to
  vanish to New York / San Francisco by secret boat. Courting/coercing Grace.

### James Grin — secondary antagonist
- Donald's younger brother. Dimmer, distractible, dreams of New York City
  glamour. Talks too much (leaks Nick's secret to Don). Follows Don's lead.

### Duff — the henchman
- The Grins' muscle. One of the men who pushes Nick off the cliff and later comes
  down to confirm the kill. Physical threat in the climax.

### Jim — Grace's father
- Widowed miner. Once studied medicine in San Francisco before chasing the gold
  rush. Gentle, frugal, resigned to Alaska. Saving his annuity share for Grace's
  education. Doesn't want to uproot his life.

### Nick's father (deceased)
- Second-in-command at the mine; beloved by the townsfolk, resented by the Grins.
  Honest but not a blind rule-follower. Died in an earthquake/avalanche. Left Nick
  the engraved belt and the survival creed:
  *"Shelter, fire, food in the cold. Shelter, food, fire in the warm."*

### The town of Bronston
- A collective character: starving, patient, sustained by hope for the annuity
  payout. Their overnight windfall is the story's emotional payoff.

## 4. World & Setting

- **Place:** Bronston, a remote Alaskan gold-mining town, reachable by barge from
  Seattle/Juneau. Frozen cliffs, crevices, a river/Yukon, northern lights.
- **Era:** Gold-rush frontier (late 1800s / early 1900s feel).
- **The mine & the annuity:** The town's wealth is pooled in an annuity — a big
  future payout everyone is counting on (a boat, travel, kids' schooling, Grace's
  college). The original banker, Tim Jones, died; the Grins took control of the
  deposits and statements. That's how they steal it: **they control the books.**
- **Key locations:**
  - *The Mountain* — where Nick takes samples and is thrown off the cliff.
  - *The Cave* — hidden high on the mountain; holds the glowing sleigh, its wall
    engraved with the same emblems as Nick's belt.
  - *The Rock / Creek* — where Nick and Grace carved their names as kids (Nick
    later adds a heart around them).
  - *The Grins' cabin & office* — desk with a hidden compartment, safes, the gold.
  - *The secret cove & dock house* — where the Grins' escape boat and the real
    stolen gold are hidden.
  - *The Dock* — the town's link to the outside; site of the central
    Nick-and-Grace misunderstanding.

## 5. The Magic — how Nick becomes Santa

The magic is doled out slowly and tied to objects, not spells:

1. **The belt.** Nick's father's belt bears engraved emblems (and a medallion).
2. **The cave.** Its wall carries the same emblems — the sleigh is bound to the
   belt's lineage.
3. **The sleigh.** Hidden, glowing; Nick learns to ride, then *fly* it (first by
   accident, nearly crashing).
4. **The medallion & the glow.** The medallion begins to glow near the sleigh; a
   beam/northern-lights effect connects cave to cave.
5. **The reindeer.** Encountered after Nick's lowest point — the idea that
   completes the "Santa" picture.
6. **The time warp.** In the end, Nick sets the belt's medallion into the cave's
   engraving and is warped away — *"One thing Nick knew for certain: he had no
   idea where he was."* (Series hook → the North Pole.)

**Rule of thumb:** keep the magic minimal, physical, and earned. It should feel
like *discovery*, not sorcery — that's what makes the Santa reveal land.

## 6. Plot — the four-book structure

Adapted from your `Childrens v1` outline and the numbered scene drafts. Structured
in four movements (your "Books"), each ending on a strong turn.

### BOOK 1 — The Setup
- **Nick:** Returns from a sample climb → is ambushed and pushed off the mountain →
  survives the fall, evades Duff → makes camp in a crevice → treks up and finds the
  **cave** → **discovers the sleigh.** *(The Opportunity.)*
- **Grace:** Decides to go to college (at the creek) → plans to convince her father
  to come to California, writes to Nick → at the feedstore, learns **Nick has
  "died."**

### BOOK 2 — The New Situation
- **Nick:** Rides the sleigh toward town → nearly off a cliff → **learns to fly** →
  scavenges food, plans to help the town → circles their initials on the rock →
  **discovers the Grins are skimming the gold.** *(Change of Plans.)*
- **Grins:** Introduced — plotting their escape to New York.
- **Grace:** Dreams of California, grieves Nick → she and neighbors find the
  (chocolate/gold?) gift → resolves to leave; plans a stop at the rock on her way
  to collect ferry tickets.

### BOOK 3 — Progress → Point of No Return
- **Nick:** Searches the Grins' house for his belt → **secretly gives the town its
  gold back** → tries to reach Grace.
- **The Dock (the big misunderstanding):** Grace waits for the mail boat; Nick
  calls to her; each misreads the other; Donald and the boat arrive; Nick bolts.
- **Nick:** Believing Grace loves Donald, resolves to grab his belt and leave →
  follows the Grins → finds the **cove and the escape boat** → finds the **real
  vault of stolen gold** → realizes *this* is why he was pushed. *(Point of No
  Return.)*
- **Grace:** The town wakes to gold → she learns the annuity account is **empty** →
  suspects the worst → goes to the mine (stops at the rock, sees the heart around
  their names).
- **Turn:** Nick sneaks in to steal the gold back → an **earthquake** hits → he
  escapes but **flies away without the gold.** *(Major Setback.)*
- **Grace captured:** Confronting the Grins during the quake, she's taken hostage.
  Donald reveals Nick came back **to propose to her.** She resolves to escape,
  starts a fire, grabs the belt, and flees up the mountain.

### BOOK 4 — Climax & Aftermath
- **Nick:** Flies back devastated → sees the **reindeer**, gets an idea → tries
  again and spots Grace. *(The Final Push.)*
- **Town:** Jim searches for Grace; sees her fall.
- **Climax:** Nick goes after Grace — the rescue.
- **Aftermath:** Nick brings Grace safely to town; they confess their love; she
  returns his belt. Nick slips away to **hide the sleigh** — sets the medallion in
  the cave engraving — and is **warped away.** *(Series hook.)*

## 7. Decisions — locked & proposed

The unresolved forks from your notes. **Locked** = settled. **Proposed** = my
recommendation, baked into the plan but easy to change if you disagree.

1. ✅ **LOCKED — Love interest is Grace** (formerly Heather).
2. 🟡 **LOCKED (leaning) — The Santa reveal is a *wink for the reader*.** The word
   "Santa" is never said. The reader assembles the clues (sleigh, reindeer, gifts
   overnight, the North Pole warp) and gets the delight of realizing it themselves.
   *We can revisit; see the trade-offs in the note below.*
3. 🔵 **PROPOSED — Nick's surname stays light / rarely used.** Drafts have both
   "Anderson" and "Johnson." For a Santa origin, leaning on **"Nicholas"** (→ *Saint
   Nicholas*) is stronger than any surname. Recommendation: use **Anderson** if a
   surname is needed, but keep it in the background.
4. 🔵 **PROPOSED — Gold reveal: reader thinks "some," truth is "all."** The reader
   believes the Grins are only skimming; the full theft is revealed at the cove. The
   **earthquake** triggers the Grins' escape, so the tension doesn't depend on the
   reader knowing how much was stolen. *(This is your own note's preferred fix.)*
5. 🔵 **PROPOSED — Ticking clock: "the boat leaves in 3 days."** Set the hard
   deadline the moment the Grins decide to run, to drive Book 3–4 urgency.
6. 🔵 **PROPOSED — Keep the magic minimal and object-based** (belt, medallion,
   glow, sleigh, warp). Discovery, not sorcery — that's what makes the reveal land.
7. ✅ **LOCKED (author, 2026-07-26) — The magic object is a hybrid: the compass
   stone, found by Nick, whose markings echo his father's.** The manuscript's
   glowing compass-inscribed stone (which the draft has and the belt-based §5
   does not) is canon: Nick finds it himself — the magic answers his choices,
   not his bloodline. The connection to his father is *recognition*, not
   inheritance: its markings echo the emblems Nick remembers from his father's
   belt. The belt may remain as a keepsake, but it unlocks nothing on its own.
   §5's beat-level details (medallion, warp trigger) need reconciling to this —
   [TK: rework §5 steps 1, 2, 4, and 6 around the stone].
8. 🟡 **LOCKED (delegated ruling, 2026-07-26) — Audience: older all-ages
   frontier myth, not middle-grade 8–12.** The author delegated this call to
   the analysis run; the ruling (rationale in
   `notes/snowflake-2026-07-26.md`): keep the adult leads, the proposal plot,
   and the annuity swindle, and raise the prose register to match — the
   Klaus / Baum / Guinn lane. The 2026-07-26 draft chapters are written to
   this ruling. Easy to veto; if it stands, [TK: amend §11's "Novel
   (middle-grade)" rung to match].

> **On the "wink vs. explicit" reveal (the one you were unsure about):**
> The wink is almost always the stronger, more magical choice, and it's the norm
> for the best modern Santa-origin books — but it carries one risk worth naming:
> if it's *too* subtle, a young reader (or a browsing parent/bookseller) might miss
> the Santa connection entirely, which is also your biggest marketing draw. The
> usual solution: keep the *story* a wink, but let the *title, cover, and jacket
> copy* make the Santa promise clearly. Best of both worlds. We'll keep the wink as
> our working choice and confirm once we draft.

## 8. Multi-Platform Plan — the agile ladder

The strategy: **develop the core IP once (this bible), then express it in the
cheapest-to-produce, fastest-to-market format first**, learning and building
audience at each rung before investing in the next.

> The reusable asset is not any single format — it's the *world, characters, and
> the Santa-origin concept*. Every format below is an expression of this bible.

| Rung | Format | Why this order | What you already have |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Picture book** | Fastest "out the door," most giftable/seasonal, proves the concept | `Childrens v1/v2` outlines ✅ |
| **2** | **Graphic novel** | Bridges picture book → prose; visual world-building; older kids | Full scene beats to adapt ✅ |
| **3** | **Novel (middle-grade)** | The complete story, deep character arcs | Books 1 & 3 "Final," Book 4 draft, ~30 scene docs ✅ |
| **4** | **Series** | The warp-away ending opens North Pole / how-Santa-began sequels | Built-in hook ✅ |

**Beyond books (later):** the concept is naturally suited to animation/film, a
seasonal (Christmas) release rhythm, and merchandise — but those are downstream of
proving the story in print.

### Honest note on the picture-book rung
A picture book is short in words but **carried by its art**, which is the slow,
costly part. Decide early how illustration gets made (you illustrate, hire an
illustrator, or use AI-assisted art as a proof-of-concept dummy). We can also ship
an even faster **"proof" rung first** — a polished illustrated short or a story
"dummy" — to test the concept before committing to full illustration.

## 10. Comps & differentiation (know the neighborhood)

"Santa origin" is a proven, beloved category — which is good news (readers want it)
and a caution (you need to stand out). The most relevant recent works:

- **_Klaus_ (2019, Netflix film).** The closest cousin. A newcomer arrives in a
  bitter, feuding far-north town; secret gift-giving gradually heals the town and
  gives rise to the Santa traditions. **Overlap:** secret gifts transforming a
  cold northern community into the Santa legend. **You must know this one.**
- **_A Boy Called Christmas_ (Matt Haig, 2015; film 2021).** A boy journeys north
  and becomes Father Christmas. The benchmark for modern middle-grade Santa-origin.
- **_The Autobiography of Santa Claus_ (Jeff Guinn)** and the classic
  **_Life & Adventures of Santa Claus_ (L. Frank Baum, 1902).** Older, but proof the
  "how Santa began" story has deep roots.

**How _Young Nicholas_ is distinct (our differentiation):**
- **Setting:** American **gold-rush Alaska**, not a generic European/arctic village.
- **Genre engine:** a **survival-adventure + heist + romance**, not a gentle fable.
  Nick is pushed off a cliff, learns to fly a sleigh, and foils a robbery.
- **The hero *is* Nick** — he becomes Santa himself, rather than assisting a Santa
  figure (as in _Klaus_).
- **A grounded villain plot** (the annuity swindle) gives real, worldly stakes
  under the magic.
- **A built-in mythology** (the belt/medallion/warp) that opens a series.

> **Action item:** watch _Klaus_ and read _A Boy Called Christmas_ if you haven't —
> not to copy, but to make sure every beat of ours feels like *only ours*.

## 11. Tone & voice

- **Genre:** Middle-grade adventure with warmth, humor, peril, and heart. Ages ~8–12
  for the novel; younger for the picture book.
- **Feel:** *Frontier grit meets Christmas wonder.* Cold, hunger, and real danger
  make the eventual warmth and giving land harder.
- **Voice sample (your own words, lightly kept):**
  > *"Shelter, fire, food in the cold. Shelter, food, fire in the warm."* — Nick's
  > father's survival creed. This is the register: plain, practical, quietly wise.
- **Golden rule:** earn the magic. The more real the cold and the swindle feel, the
  more the flying sleigh and the overnight gold feel like grace.
