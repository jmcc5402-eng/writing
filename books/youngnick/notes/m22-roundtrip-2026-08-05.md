## 1. ONE SENTENCE

A wronged 21-year-old gold-camp surveyor, left for dead by his crooked
employers, discovers a buried flying sleigh and secretly plays Robin Hood /
proto-Santa for his dying Alaskan mining town — recovering the stolen gold,
saving the girl, and then being pulled by a magic compass stone toward the
North Star into an unknown portal.

## 2. THE PROMISE

**Frame:** Close-third adult(ish) survival-adventure opening — no frame
story, no narrator device, no "let me tell you how Santa began." The book
opens as a straight wilderness thriller: "He was probably the first human to
ever step foot in this part of the Alaskan mountain side... Of course, Nick
had a reason: gold." (Ch 1). The Santa connection is a **dramatic-irony
Easter-egg engine**, never named: reader recognizes the pieces (red sleigh,
reindeer, gifts on porches, "Ho…!", the North) one by one; no character ever
says "Santa," "Christmas," or "Claus" anywhere in the text.

**Genre promised by the opening:** man-vs-nature survival plus a
betrayal/crime plot (the Grins throw Nick off a cliff by end of Ch 1). The
magic arrives only in Ch 5 (the glow) and Ch 8 (flight). Romance is promised
early via Heather's POV (Ch 3–4: "she had always assumed that one day Nick
would be the man she married") and then **deliberately not consummated** —
they never exchange a word on the page.

**Who knows what, when:** Nick knows everything and tells no one. The town
knows nothing — it mythologizes him in real time ("fierys and angels," "a
miracle"). Heather glimpses him once at the rescue and is gaslit by structure:
"Nick who?" — "Maybe it was a dream, but I remember seeing him" (Ch 22). The
reader is the only one who knows both halves. The book's implicit thesis: the
Santa myth is what a town invents to explain one hidden good man.

**The opening also promises:** a POV alternation (Nick / Heather / Grins),
a mystery ("what were the Grins hiding?") solved at midpoint, and a
gold-economy plot (the annuity) that carries the town stakes.

## 4. WORLD FACTS

**Geography and places**
- **Bronston, AK** — the mining town; spelled "Bronston" throughout except
  once "Bronson" (~L1171, "run into anyone from Bronson"). "In the three
  weeks since his return to Bronston, AK" (Ch 2).
- **Juno** — the capital/banking town (Juneau, consistently misspelled):
  "the barge he took from Seattle to Juno" (Ch 1); "the 'annuity' that was
  held in the Juno Bank, located in Juno, Alaska" (Ch 4).
- **Port Lola** — "about 80 miles south," Grins' planned supply stop en
  route to San Francisco (Ch 13/14 seam, ~L1166).
- **Okikoke** — trading settlement: "trading trips to Okikoke" (Ch 9 fused,
  ~L836).
- **The valley pass** west of the cave, route to Juno, "unpassable" since
  the avalanche "and the earthquake that caused it" (Ch 9 fused, ~L846).
- Mainland references: Seattle, Portland ("tickets to Portland," Ch 3), San
  Francisco / "Frisco" / "San Fran" / "San Fransisco," New York City
  (James's flyer, Ch 6), Northern California redwoods; "trains used to haul
  sequoias to San Fransisco" (Ch 23).
- Local features: the raging river below the cliffs ("about 100 feet below,"
  Ch 1); the cave hidden mid-cliff; the mine up "mine road" with a mill; a
  town dock plus a **secret dock** in a small bay off the cliff road with a
  camouflaged vault: "moved shrubs aside along the mountain, revealing a
  large metal door" (Ch 15).

**Era markers (in-world)**
- Gold-rush Alaska: Jim "left the big city after eighteen months in school
  to chase the gold rush in Alaska" (Ch 3). Barge/steamer travel, dog
  sleds, oil-lamp-and-moonlight interiors, mail carried by hand, monthly
  newspapers ("the newspapers only arrived monthly when the Grins returned
  from Juno," Ch 3). Typhoid is incurable ("She would be the first to cure
  typhoid," Ch 3). Hydraulic drills financed by a bank (Ch 4). No phones,
  no cars, guns and rum. Dollar figures ("$24,000... almost $100,000," Ch
  13/14; "$34.42," Ch 14; "$52.52," Ch 20) are wildly large for the
  implied ~1900s period — unresearched-era tell.

**The economy / the crime**
- The annuity: "The bank provided funds for six hydraulic drills. In
  return, Grin created an annuity with its employees as collateral...
  until [vested in five years], 75% of their earnings would be retained by
  the bank collecting interest" (Ch 4).
- The truth: "There was no annuity. There were no bank accounts. They were
  storing all the gold in a secret vault at the cliffs" (Jim, Ch 22); plus
  a skim: coal swapped for gold at weigh-in (Ch 10), fake statements from
  "a printing press that looked like it had been used for printing bank
  invoices" (Ch 20). The mine itself is dying: "the mine had been losing
  money for a while and the gold was drying up, if not gone" (Ch 13/14).

**Rules of the magic (as established by THIS text)**
- The sleigh glows red and radiates warmth when buried: "a dull reddish
  hue... The dirt felt warm" (Ch 5). "Other than the dark red stain of the
  wood, the sled was hypical [sic]" (Ch 7).
- Flight is controlled by the second lever: "This wasn't a brake, it was a
  lever to control the sleigh in the air!" (Ch 9 fused). Lean to steer;
  pull to climb; it dives "like an eagle."
- **Weight limit:** unaided, the sleigh cannot lift heavy cargo — "Was the
  extra weight of the gold affecting the sleigh?" (Ch 17). This is the
  engine of the caribou discovery.
- **Caribou rule:** harnessed animals fly and multiply power: "Directly in
  front of him were three flying caribou pulling him along" (Ch 18);
  "Somehow, the addition of the caribou increased the speed of the sleigh,
  it seemed almost five fold" (Ch 23). The word **"reindeer" appears only
  in Ch 19** (~L1866, L1878ff: "a sleigh with three reindeer on the deck of
  their boat") — otherwise always "caribou."
- **The stone:** a warm, glowing, plum-sized oval inscribed with a compass,
  buried deeper in the same cave; glows when turned toward true direction
  ("He turned again, back towards the depth of the cave. The stone began to
  glow again," Ch 23); magnetically seats into the middle groove of the
  sleigh's deck ("It fit perfectly"); once aimed at the North Star it takes
  over: "It felt as if the sleigh was attached to a set of tracks... Nick
  had lost control of the sleigh. It was flying itself" (Ch 23) — then a
  flash, a grey cloud, and entry at impossible speed. The deck has multiple
  "ornate grooves" (Ch 23); an author note earlier says "((Include the
  carvings where the stones will eventually fit))" (Ch 7) — stones plural,
  a planned multi-stone system this draft never renders.
- No rules given for: who made the sleigh, why it was buried, whether the
  magic has a cost, why the cave has two relics.

**Nature (as this text believes it)**
- Freezing river = certain death: "Nick's father had always warned that
  freezing water shuts down the heart" (Ch 2).
- Eagle eggs are a tradeable delicacy (author note "((Confirm this))," Ch
  9 fused). Polar bears and wolverines are cave threats (Ch 5); "the polar
  bear were out on the ice" (Ch 18). Caribou haul mine dirt in teams (Ch
  18). Robins' eggs eaten in the California redwoods (Ch 9 fused).

## 7. THE ENDING

The rendered ending is a **double ending**: the town plot closes completely,
and a second, mythic door opens in the final chapter — and the manuscript
stops exactly on the threshold.

Closure (Ch 22): "'Thats yours sweety,' Jim said... 'Turns out the Grins
suckered us all. There was no annuity. There were no bank accounts... The
whole town feels rich.'" And the myth seals over Nick: "'What about Nick?'
Heather asked. / 'Nick who?' / 'Nick Johnson. Maybe it was a dream, but I
remember seeing him.'"

Nick's choice (Ch 23): "He left her on the dock with the gold, but he
decided not to stay. Maybe he chickened out. He rationalized that he didn't
want to make the sleigh public knowledge."

The door (Ch 23, final lines): "once the North Star was straight ahead,
something happened. The stone glowed bright red... It felt as if the sleigh
was attached to a set of tracks... Nick had lost control of the sleigh. It
was flying itself. The next thing he knew, at a speed that seemed faster
than lighting, the sleigh entered the cloud. Cha" — and then the blank
Chapter 24.

What this points toward: the sleigh and stone are artifacts of something at
the **North** (Pole), and they are summoning their finder. The book this
text wants to be ends its first volume with Nick — already a gift-leaving,
reindeer-driving, red-sleigh-flying secret legend — being conscripted by the
magic itself into whatever Santa's origin actually is. The romance is
deliberately parked (Heather bound for San Francisco believing she dreamed
him), which reads as sequel fuel, not oversight. The empty Chapter 24 is the
single loudest fact in the file: the draft died at the exact moment the
survival story had to become the myth.

## 8. LOOSE WIRES

**Setups that never pay off (in this text alone):**
- The ground sample "he'd left it by the fire" (Ch 2) — the Grins evidently
  spun it into the town-wide "positive sample" rumor (Ch 4), but the object
  and the fraud-of-the-sample are never confronted.
- "((Include the carvings where the stones will eventually fit))" (Ch 7) —
  stones **plural**; exactly one stone is found and seated. The other
  grooves are an unfired arsenal.
- Ch 13's cliffhanger: "He never expected to see the person standing in the
  doorway." No chapter ever shows who it was or what happened in that room;
  Heather-as-captive is inferred, never dramatized.
- Heather's two siblings ("the other for the three kids," Ch 9 fused) —
  vanish from the book.
- Gus Anderson's avalanche-morning visit and the "dream they shared [that]
  had just been dashed" (Ch 12) — heavy foreshadowing energy (the fathers
  knew something — about the cave? the mine? the sleigh?) with zero payoff.
- Nick's mother — invoked twice in memory (Ch 8, Ch 9 fused), never placed:
  alive? in California? Does anyone tell her that her son "died"?
- Donald's love for Heather (Ch 15) — set up as the deeper murder motive
  ("Money...power...love...could that be it?") but the climax runs purely
  on gold; the love angle never surfaces in Donald's behavior.
- James Grin, Dwayne, and Duff's fates — arrests happen only inside the

  the rendered prose leaves them at large. Likewise "Word spreads... that
  the grins were arrested" exists only in the notes, and contradicts them.
- The town's future — the mine is destroyed and the gold is a finite

  Mr. Bramley's boat, tagged twice, never gets its beat.
- The eagle of Ch 1 / eagle-dive motif — decorated but never structurally
  used (e.g., no eagle assist at the climax).
- Nick's public resurrection — he is legally dead in Bronston; the text
  never spends this (no confrontation with the "tragedy" story the Grins

- The secret dock's SECOND possible vault Nick fears in Ch 17 ("Was there a
