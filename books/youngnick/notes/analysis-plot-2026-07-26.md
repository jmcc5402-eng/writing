## 2. Which beats belong in ch1–4, and in what order

Candidate beats from the brief: return to town, the Grins, the push,
survival, the cave/sleigh/stone discovery.

**Ruling: N-N-N-G — Push / Ledge / Sleigh / Town.** Keep Nick's thread
continuous through the sleigh discovery, then cut to Grace and the
town. Reasons:

1. **The promise of the premise moves up to end of ch3.** The glowing
   sleigh is the Santa-origin promise; at ch5 it is late, at ch3 it is
   early enough to hold a browsing reader through a quieter ch4.
2. **Chronology runs forward.** The cave night is the night of the
   push; the market death-news scene requires the Grins to have
   returned to town and spread the story — days later. The current
   order (push day → push evening → town-days-later → push night)
   jumps forward and then back. N-N-N-G is strictly forward.
3. **It matches the bible.** §6 Book 1 runs Nick's beats (climb →
   push → survival → cave → sleigh, "The Opportunity") and *ends* the
   Grace thread on "learns Nick has 'died.'" The reordered ch4 is
   exactly the bible's Book-1 Grace terminus.
4. **Every chapter ends on a turn:** the Grin reveal / "A cave." /
   "A red glowing sleigh." / the flour drop and "the man she married."
5. **Cost of the trade:** Grace's first chapter moves from position 3
   to 4. Acceptable — she still appears inside the first four chapters,
   and her dream, the town's plight, and the false hope all land in one
   scene-driven chapter instead of two summary-heavy ones.

**Where "return to town" goes:** nowhere as a full chapter. Nick's
actual return (three weeks before the push) is backstory in both the
draft and the bible — §6 opens Book 1 mid-expedition. But the bible's
arc statement ("from balking — he can't even tell Grace he loves
her") is currently *two lines of summary* in Ch2. Ruling: render the
return-to-town as one dramatized memory-scene inside ch2 — the dock or
market, Grace across the street, the balk — planting the arc's starting
position without revealing the proposal intent, which is Donald's
Book-3 reveal and must stay off the page here.

**Where the Grins go:** their counting-house scene (current Ch6) stays
OUT of ch1–4 and opens ch5 — the bible places the Grins' introduction
in Book 2. Inside ch1–4 the scheme exists only as the overheard tent
fragments (ch1) and the too-good sample news reaching town (ch4): the
plant and its first false payoff.

**Where the stone goes:** not in ch1–4 — the manuscript finds it in
current Ch23 and decision 7 does not move it. What ch1–4 owes it is the
*recognition engine*: at the sleigh discovery, describe the carvings
and the empty middle groove (the author's own TODO at current Ch7:
"((Include the carvings where the stones will eventually fit))"), with
markings Nick half-recognizes but cannot place. The belt is named
nowhere in the manuscript; per decision 7 it unlocks nothing, so ch1–4
plants only Nick's *memory* of his father's belt emblems, so the
late-book "recognition, not inheritance" moment has something to
recognize.

## 4. Canon conflicts and defects inside the opening (flag, don't fix)

1. **Who pushes Nick.** Bible §3: Duff is "one of the men who pushes
   Nick off the cliff and later comes down to confirm the kill." The
   manuscript has exactly two attackers and the reveal "It was the Grin
   brothers"; the kill-check is a silhouette from above, nobody
   descends. The Ch1 punchline argues for Grins-only with Duff on the
   kill-check, but the bible currently says otherwise.
   [CHECK: reconcile bible §3/§6 vs. manuscript Ch1–2 on Duff's role
   before the opening is redrafted.]
2. **The overheard fragments don't match their replay.** Ch16 recalls
   whole sentences from the tent ("There won't be time in San
   Fransisco!", "I don't care about the damn redwoods, we have to keep
   moving") that Ch1 never gives the reader. The ch1 fragments must be
   rewritten so the Ch16 realization is a fair-play replay.
   [CHECK: align Ch1 fragments with Ch16's recollection.]
3. **Nick's return motive.** Ch2: "Did he really think he would make
   any money back in Alaska?" implies a gold motive; Ch9-Nick says "He
   didn't return to Alaska for gold"; the bible says he returned to
   propose (a Book-3 reveal). Ch1–4 must plant longing, not gold, and
   not the proposal. [CHECK: unify the motive lines in ch1–2.]
4. **Age/timeline arithmetic.** Ch1: left at eleven, just turned
   twenty-one. Ch5: "Growing up in Alaska until he was ten and spending
   the next ten years" in California. Ch2: "For the last three years"
   a lumberjack; Ch8: "ten years of tree climbing." Ch4/Grace: he left
   "when they were twelve." Bible: ~19–21, ~10 years away.
   [CHECK: fix one arithmetic before redrafting ch1–2.]
5. **Surname in the death-news line.** Ch4 says "Nick Johnson"; §7.3
   proposes Anderson, kept in the background. The death announcement
   is the one opening line that *needs* a surname.
   [TK: surname per §7.3 in the ch4 news line.]
6. **Import damage at the seam.** "A red glowing sleigh. C" is a
   swallowed chapter break — evidence the discovery already ended a
   chapter in the source. The reordering above restores that.
7. **Audience-dependent density.** The ch4 annuity exposition (75%
   retention, vesting, financing deal) and the ch1–2 peril intensity
   both depend on the audience ruling in progress. The beat plan below
   is order-stable under either outcome; the [TK] items mark where the
   ruling changes execution, not structure.
