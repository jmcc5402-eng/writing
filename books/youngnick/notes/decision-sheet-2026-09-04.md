## D1 — The ticking clock

**The question.** Does the back half of the book run on a hard, stated
deadline that the reader can count down — and if it does, what is the
deadline, when in the story is it announced, and which character says it
aloud?

**What it blocks.**

- `STORY_BIBLE.md:245-246` — decision 5, still 🔵 PROPOSED: "Ticking
  clock: 'the boat leaves in 3 days.' Set the hard deadline the moment
  the Grins decide to run, to drive Book 3–4 urgency."
- `THREADS.md:649-650` (T02) and `THREADS.md:1032-1033` (T28) both carry
  the same open [CHECK]: rule it or retire it.
- `THREADS.md:651-655` (T02 OWED) — "the escape itself — the Grins never
  actually leave, fight, or fail on the page... §7.5's ticking clock was
  never installed."
- The two ★ Write items. Grace's second act (`TODO.md:39-42`) is a
  captivity whose length and pressure are a clock statement; the archive's
  own draft dialogue for it is a clock line — RECON quotes Donald as
  "that boat is leaving in a few hours and your're gonna be on it"
  (`notes/drive-reconciliation-2026-07-27.md:255-257`). The rebuilt climax
  (`TODO.md:44-46`) needs to know how much time Nick has.
- Every Grins-POV brief after ch5, and every chapter of the heist
  sequence the trunk holds at ch15–ch19.

**The options.**

*Option A — install the hard three-day clock as §7.5 proposes.* Buys the
sharpest possible Act 3–4 drive and a countable countdown a reader can
hold. Costs: it has to be announced by somebody, in a scene, which means
a Grins chapter must exist at the moment of announcement; and it competes
with two clocks already in adopted prose. Ch5 has Duff's word — "Under
two weeks. Once the cargo's in place, we're on our way."
(`manuscript/manuscript-v2.md:1424-1425`) — and the Thursday boat, run
against it explicitly: "Two weeks, against a boat that came every
Thursday. He ran the two clocks against each other once, cleanly, and put
them away." (`manuscript/manuscript-v2.md:1429-1431`). Dropping a
three-day clock on top of two-weeks-and-Thursday makes three clocks, and
the two-weeks line would have to be re-read as a lie or superseded.

*Option B — promote the clocks already on the page: the Thursday boat is
the metronome, Duff's "under two weeks" is the outer wall, and the
countdown becomes hard only when the Grins name the sailing date.* Buys a
clock the reader has been carrying since ch5 without being told it was a
clock — which pays off exactly the way this book likes to pay things off.
Costs: nothing on the page yet says which Thursday, so the tightening
scene still has to be written; and "under two weeks" is loose enough that
the middle of the book can sag if the tightening comes late.

*Option C — retire §7.5; run the back half on consequence pressure
instead of a deadline.* Buys freedom from calendar bookkeeping. Costs the
book its urgency engine at exactly the point THREADS says it is missing,
and leaves T02's OWED — the Grins never leave, fight, or fail — with no
structural reason to resolve.

**Recommendation: Option B.** The clock the book actually wants is
already installed and already sourced to a character who would know it.
Duff's two weeks sets the outer wall from ch5; the Thursday boat gives
the reader a repeating tick with a date attached; and the hard countdown
starts the moment Donald names the sailing — which should be the same
scene in which he decides to run, exactly as §7.5's instinct says
("the moment the Grins decide to run"), but stated as a Thursday rather
than as three days. This preserves the adopted prose word for word,
converts an existing line into the book's spine rather than adding a
third timekeeper, and it hands every later brief a countable number.
Grace's captivity then has a natural length and a natural terror: she is
being held until the boat.

**Reversibility: EXPENSIVE if reversed after drafting resumes, CHEAP
today.** Nothing after ch5 is written. Once ch6 through the climax exist,
changing the clock means re-timing every scene in them.

## D2 — The calendar: the month, and the elapsed-day spine

**The question.** In what month does Day 0 (the push) fall, and how many
days pass between the push and the climax at the cliff?

**What it blocks.**

- `plots/ch6-brief.md:433-434` — open [TK] number 6: "the month — season
  locked to late Oct–Nov play, month unlocked per gate N16; no month word
  on the page." The next chapter to be drafted carries this unresolved.
- `plots/ch6-brief.md:160-162` — the brief has to hedge daylight:
  "Short daylight — 'it wouldn't be light for long at this time of year'
  (mined)... the month is still [TK]."
- `drafts/2026-07-26/DRAFT-NOTES.md:185-186` — "[TK: lock the calendar
  month (~late Oct–Nov played: sleet, hard cold, open raging river, short
  days, no polar night, no northern lights)]."
- `drafts/2026-07-26/DRAFT-NOTES.md:192-193` — the exact day offsets of
  ch4's two scenes from Day 0 are deliberately vague.
- Every remaining chapter: weather, river ice, daylight hours, and
  whether the northern lights the bible lists in §4
  (`STORY_BIBLE.md:129`) can appear at all.

**What canon already fixes.** Adopted prose puts a dated horizon on the
page: "By mid-December she would hold tickets to Portland in her own two
hands" (`manuscript/manuscript-v2.md:975`), reached by a letter whose
"confirmation took a few weeks coming back up the water"
(`manuscript/manuscript-v2.md:972-974`). Working backward, ch4 sits
roughly four to six weeks before mid-December — early-to-mid November —
and Day 0 is a few days before ch4 (`THREADS.md:155`, "Grace; days
later"). The season is therefore already decided by the page; only the
word is missing.

**The options.**

*Option A — name the month in prose (e.g. November) once, early.* Buys
absolute clarity for every drafter and reader. Costs a small amount of
myth-distance: the book so far has kept dates as weather and boat days,
which suits the register (`STORY_BIBLE.md:449-453`).

*Option B — never name a month; lock the calendar in the bible only, and
keep the page running on the Thursday boat, mid-December, and weather.*
Buys the register intact and matches the surname ruling's logic — legends
do not carry paperwork (`STORY_BIBLE.md:232-240`). Costs nothing on the
page, but only works if the bible carries the real dates so drafters stop
guessing.

*Option C — leave it open.* Costs a recurring hedge in every brief and
guarantees a weather continuity failure somewhere in the back half.

**Recommendation: Option B, with the day-spine written into the bible.**
Add to §4 a short, page-invisible calendar: Day 0 in the first week of
November; ch4 four to six days later; the mid-December ticket horizon as
the book's outer date; and a stated total elapsed time from the push to
the cliff. The prose keeps saying "boat day" and "before the freeze" and
never says a month, which protects the myth register; the drafters get a
number. This is the twin of D1 and should be ruled in the same sitting —
a clock without a calendar is half a ruling.

**Reversibility: NEAR-PERMANENT for the season, CHEAP for the month
word.** The season is already load-bearing in adopted prose (the
mid-December ticket line, the open unfrozen river, short daylight); it
cannot be moved without reopening ch4. Naming or not naming the month is
a small, late, reversible choice.

**The options.**

*Option A — the save is a knot Nick has already used badly earlier, tied
right this time under load.* Buys the arc in one physical action: the
climax pays a mistake the book already made him make. Costs a rewrite
obligation in the earlier scene, so that the wrong version is on the page

father's knot is not magic, so it is legal, but it must read as taught
skill, not inheritance.

*Option C — leave the mechanics to the drafter of the climax.* Costs a
guess at the highest-stakes moment in the book, and every plant the knot
needs sits in chapters that will be drafted before the climax is.

**Recommendation: Option A, tied to the baker knot already on the page.**
Rule that the baker knot — chosen at the heist for speed over strength —
is the knot that fails or nearly fails, and that at the cliff Nick spends
the seconds he does not have to tie the strong one instead, on the wrong
side of the sleigh, with his bad shoulder taking the load. The cost is
the thing the book needs the climax to charge: time, and pain, and the
choice not to be fast. This uses only what canon already names, it makes
SC3 fire at the exact moment the dream review says it must, and it makes
"restraint as strength" a physical act rather than a stated theme.

**Reversibility: MODERATE.** The climax is unwritten, but the plants live
in chapters that will be drafted first — so the longer this stays open,
the more likely the ladder gets built for a rung that changes.
