# Young Nicholas — Review of the Rewritten Chapters 1–4

*A junior critic's second read, 26 July 2026. Primary text:
`drafts/2026-07-26/ch01–ch04`; compared against `manuscript/nick-manuscript-22.md`
old ch1–5, the critique and snowflake of this date, and `STORY_BIBLE.md`
(§7 decision 7 controlling).*

---

## (a) Verdict

These four chapters are better than the original opening — not marginally,
categorically. The tense instability is gone (past throughout), the double
cliff-scare is fixed exactly as prescribed (the slip is now one caught beat and
"one look, a cold one" at the crevice, ch1 L98–108), and the material that was
essay is now scene. In absolute terms: a publishable-quality opening with one
real disease, diagnosed below. Grace's chapter is the best of the four; the
porch scene (ch4 L9–106) is the first stretch of this project that reads like a
finished book. None of the four is worse than its original.

## (b) Voice: preserved at the spine, ghosted at the skin

The load-bearing lines survive, and the wryness is genuinely Nick's: "He knew
better. That was the trouble with going down" (ch1 L23) is a fair adult
translation of the old "Going down should be easier that climbing up, right?"
(ms. L31–32). "Which proved mostly that he owned a rock" (ch3 L28) is the old
voice, matured.

But a sheen has crept in, and it has a fingerprint: the simile template "the
way X does Y" appears **24 times in 8,099 words** — "the way a man leans on a
rail he has tested twice" (ch1 L14), "the way old men drive a nail twice" (ch2
L237), "the way you know a word in a dream" (ch3 L251), "the way you hear about
weather in another town" (ch4 L275). Any one is good; two dozen is a
ghostwriter's tic the author never had. Worse, the two lines my critique said
to protect were both smoothed: "It finally made sense, **but** it made no sense
at all" (ms. L477) became "**and** it made no sense at all" (ch3 L261) — the
"but" is the joke — and "The river below him was still daring him to fall"
(ms. L377) became "the river, which had not stopped inviting him down" (ch3
L13–14). "Daring" is the author; "inviting" is a copyeditor. Compare also
"He was picked up and thrown across the camp...towards the cliff" (ms.
L104–105) with "picked up whole and slung across the camp, and the direction he
was slung was the cliff" (ch1 L242–244): the new line is better *written* and
less *his*.

## (c) The recommendations, verified

- **People in rooms talking (rec 2): delivered.** Nick has more spoken lines in
  ch1–2 alone (the eagle, ch1 L77; James, ch1 L182–198; Old Rob, ch2 L228–266)
  than in the entire 23,376-word original. The Nick–James exchange is new and
  earns its place — "James laughed at everything... and he didn't laugh" (ch1
  L192–194) converts the old silent "eye contact" (ms. L85–89) into playable
  dread.
- **Density: delivered.** Old ch3's essay ("Jim had been trying to persuade
  Heather... he had the money", ms. L230–234) is now dialogue in which the
  decision is Grace's own claim: "Not because you've been at me about it all
  winter... I decided because of Mother" (ch4 L38–40). Old ch4's vesting
  seminar (ms. L326–339) is capped at two sentences (ch4 L208–212), as
  directed.
- **The father wound (rec 5): delivered** in three touches (creed L55–65, belt
  memory L83–88, the anger L112–119, all ch1) plus the trapline catechism (ch3
  L74–83). No medallion, no glow, no cave-wall emblems — decision 7 is complied
  with; the carvings and empty oval socket sit on the sleigh itself (ch3
  L229–246).
- **Not delivered:** the total runs 8,099 words against the brief's 8,500–10,500
  floor; and the critique's "the book starts at the footsteps" was overruled by
  the snowflake, defensibly — the front half now carries plants, not throat-
  clearing.

## (d) New problems

1. The simile-tic and the two smoothed signature lines (above) — the register
   ruling licensed *raising* the voice, not replacing its jokes.
2. New Nick is aphoristically witty ("It's a friendly mountain, if you don't
   believe anything it tells you," ch1 L189) — fluent banter sits oddly on a man
   whose defining failure is that words desert him; the balk (ch2 L256–262)
   lands, but the contrast needs managing, not luck.
3. Production notes printed mid-scene: the [TK] block at ch3 L22–25 sits at the
   exact peak of the predator pause and kills it; "Nick [TK surname]" inside
   Rob's spoken line (ch4 L249) does the same to the death announcement.
4. Small unflagged texture (Rob's redwood banter, ch2 L238–243) is invented,
   though canon-consistent; the drafter's notes flag most but not all of it.

## (e) Three highest-leverage next moves

1. **Lock the drafter's blocking questions and write them into the bible** —
   balk upgrade, pushers/Duff, surname, ticket route, plus the pending §11
   audience amendment and CHANGELOG entry. Ch5+ cannot be drafted cleanly over
   these open holes.
2. **A de-sheen pass on these four chapters:** cut the "the way X" template to
   a third of its count and restore "but it made no sense" and "daring him to
   fall" verbatim. One session; protects the author before this register
   hardens into the book's default.
3. **Draft ch5, The Counting House, next.** The villains were the old draft's
   thinnest element; the new register has proven it can do survival and grief
   but not yet Donald. Test it before twenty more chapters stand on him.
