# Opening Tournament — Book 1 lineage (2026-08-05)

**PROPOSED — creative-poke instrument, author decision required; nothing
here overrides RECON §4 or bible §7.**

The author's top-starred TODO item is the Book 1 lineage: stay on the
v2/trunk opening and mine the 2015 line, or adopt the 2015 frame (RECON
§4). The material is old and the author wants fresh creative pressure on
it. This tournament puts the two existing openings in a fair fight with
two freshly designed alternatives and scores all four against what makes
this book win: the SUPERCONCEPTS, the §1 hook, the §10 comp shelf, and
the §6/§12 series structure.

**One honest correction to the brief.** The archive contains no opening
where the reader knows the *Santa* connection from line one (verified:
no Santa-frame material anywhere in `manuscript/`; the only "Santa"
mentions are the 2013 loose-ends sheets' multibook "Is he currently
Santa?" question). What the 2015 line actually is — per RECON §4 and
`drafts/yn-1-doc-2015.md` — is the **proposal frame**: the reader knows
Nick's *motive* from line one ("Tonight he would propose to Grace").
Entrant B is reconstructed as what the material really is, not as a
Santa-from-line-one frame that was never written.

**Constraints respected by all entrants.** Grace (§7.1). The wink —
"Santa" never said (§7.2). The compass stone found by Nick; the belt a
keepsake that unlocks nothing (§7.7). Older all-ages register (§7.8).
The cliff mirror is canon: *the book opens with Nick pushed off a
cliff* and ends with him catching Grace at one (§7.9, SC2) — so no
entrant may move the push out of the opening movement. Wake-to-gold
stays understated (§7.10). Invented details are marked [TK].

## The entrants

### ENTRANT A — the v2 cold open (as drafted 2026-07-26)

`drafts/2026-07-26/ch01-the-push.md` — "The Push." Linear: the sample
climb, the creed as working arithmetic, the near-fall and the silent
crevice, the descent to camp, the overheard Grin argument (redwoods /
New York / "if just one person finds out"), the James exchange with the
sum running behind his eyes, jerky at the fire — then the glove, the
log, and the push. Ends: "It was the Grin brothers." No proposal intent
on the page (the snowflake's deliberate design — the reader learns
Nick's motive at Donald's Book-3 reveal). Written to the older
all-ages register; the belt appears as memory only, unlocking nothing.

### ENTRANT B — the 2015 proposal frame (honest reconstruction)

Reconstructed from `drafts/yn-1-doc-2015.md` ch1–2 (the polished
submission package, Grace throughout). The frame, verbatim from the
source:

> Tonight is the night, Nick said to himself. ...
> Tonight was the night.
> Tonight he would propose to Grace.

Then: the near-fall on the descent ("Focus lover-boy"), the dislocated
shoulder, the trudge to camp, the engagement needle on the page —
"Could she really be engaged to Donald Grin?" — the overheard argument,
the club, the fall, and (downstream, ch3) Duff descending to confirm
the kill. The reader holds Nick's motive from line one, so the dock
misunderstanding and the "Nick has died" news run on dramatic irony
instead of surprise. What the frame carries that canon now forbids: the
belt "A deep red amulet was woven into the buckle" (decision-7
contamination), and an MG register the §7.8 ruling has retired. Scored
here as the frame honestly rebuilt — proposal intent from line one,
amulet stripped, register raised — since scoring the 2015 prose as-is
would be a straw man.

### ENTRANT C — fresh: "The Fall Is the First Flight" (in medias res)

*A third door: not a frame, not a linear cold open — the book opens
airborne.*

**Beats.** Line one, Nick is already off the cliff. The chapter runs
the fall in stretched time — the trained body doing the trained things
— braided with tight retrospect that pays out the same information as
A in reordered pieces: the sample climb, the creed, the silent
crevice, the camp, the scraps of argument, the glove and the log.
The braid closes as the retrospect catches up to the present: he
strikes the mountainside, sights the burrow hole, jams his arm in to
the armpit, stops. He looks up. Two shapes against the last of the
sky, shapes he has known his whole life. The Grin brothers. The book's
first page and last page are now the same shape: a body in the air
above a river, and the question of who catches it.

**First lines (sample, §11 register):**

Nick was falling.
He had time to know it, which was the worst part.
A man who slips knows nothing till he lands;
a man who is thrown gets the whole ride.
Below him the river ran black between its shelves of ice,
a hundred feet of dark air coming up patient,
and Nick did with the fear what his father had taught him to do:
he put it in line behind the work.
Cover your head. Take it on the shoulder. Eyes open —
falling men shut their eyes, his father said,
and dead men keep them shut.
[TK: the "eyes open" falling rule is invented — no source in bible or
manuscript; replace or ratify]
Half a breath ago he had been warming jerky at another man's fire,
as near to easy as he had been since Seattle.

**Known risk.** The braided timeline is a step away from §11's "plain,
practical" linearity; it must be executed as a man's mind actually
works in a fall, not as a literary device, or it cheapens SC4.

### ENTRANT D — fresh: "The Men at the Top" (villain-side opening)

*Maximally different from C: exterior where C is interior, linear
suspense where C is fractured, and the reader is placed above the cliff
instead of below it. James Grin's POV.*

**Beats.** Camp on the ledge, late light. James has spent the afternoon
hoping the mountain will do it for them — mountains do it all the time,
and nobody asks questions about the weather. Donald inside with the
books [theft scale kept vague per §7.4 — "three more weeks," "the
bank," never an amount]. The order to bed the dogs down here instead of
pushing on: James understands what the order is. Then Nick appears on
the high rocks — early, alive, coming down sure-footed like the
mountain owes him the use of itself — and James's dread turns physical:
the thing they were afraid of was exactly this competence. The exchange
(A's dialogue seen from the other side); James almost says something,
looks at the tent, doesn't. Donald: it's time. The log, the push. James
cannot make himself look over the edge; Donald does, for a long time,
and then says something cold [TK: Donald's line]. [TK: pusher identity
written here as the two Grins per m22/v2 — RECON delta 11 (Duff
descending) is an open Decide item this entrant would force early.]

**First lines (sample, §11 register):**

James Grin had spent the afternoon hoping the mountain would do it.
Mountains did it all the time.
A rock let go, a rope did not hold,
a man came down a cliff the fast way instead of the slow one,
and nobody in Juno ever asked a question about the weather.
[CHECK: Juno/Juneau spelling — RECON delta 2]
But the light was going long and yellow across the snow,
and the dogs were digging in for the night,
and there, small and sure on the high rocks,
coming down like he had all the time in the world, came Nick.
Alive. Early, even.
James found he could not make his feet go to the tent.

**Known risk (structural, and it is serious).** Bible §6 has Nick
realize *at the cove* why he was pushed. A chapter inside James's head
either shows the motive (killing the Book-3 realization) or visibly
withholds it (reader feels cheated). D can only blur this, not solve
it.

### Reasoning, one sentence per cell

**SC1 — the best story ever, subtly unraveled**
- **A (4):** The two dog *sleighs* drawn nose to tail, the emblems on a
  dead father's belt, the gear-squaring habit — grains hidden in plot
  necessity, and the reader could not say the word out loud.
- **B (2):** The frame spends its opening on romance rather than
  disguise, and its one legend-object is the amulet-belt that
  decision 7 now forbids.
- **C (4):** Its grain is the deepest one available — the first "flight"
  of the man who will fly is a fall — but the compressed retrospect has
  less room for A's quiet camp-texture grains.
- **D (3):** Villain POV can hide the sleighs and the swindle, but the
  legend lives in Nick's craft and Nick is an object here, not an agent.

**SC2 — the ending where everything flies**
- **A (5):** Delivers the canonical mirror's first panel at full weight,
  and the silent crevice-rock plants the dread of deep falls the climax
  must answer.
- **B (3):** The push lands, but the chapter half-spends the cliff on a
  warm-up near-fall, and "Focus lover-boy" cheapens the thing the ending
  is built on.
- **C (5):** Opening line one in the air makes the mirror structural —
  the book opens falling and ends catching — the strongest possible
  frame for decision 9.
- **D (4):** The push seen from above is chilling and the mirror holds,
  but the fall's interiority — what the climax's catch will answer — is
  surrendered.

**SC3 — Nick, the relatable outdoorsman**
- **A (5):** Drilling, bare-fingered tamping, testing each hold twice,
  the creed run as live arithmetic — learnable skill shown on the page,
  not claimed.
- **B (3):** Spikes and holds are present, but Nick survives by lunge
  and luck ("without thinking," "frantic"), which is precisely what SC3
  forbids.
- **C (4):** The trained fall (cover the head, take it on the shoulder)
  shows craft under pressure, but compresses out A's deliberate
  working-method texture.
- **D (3):** Nick's competence arrives only by reflection — James
  dreading how good he is — which is a real effect but not skill shown
  on the page.

**SC4 — frontier-real: the magic obeys the cold**
- **A (5):** Every beat is rope, rock, ice, and hunger; the chapter is
  maximally tense with zero magic, which is the test verbatim.
- **B (3):** Physically grounded but the MG-register exclamations and
  jokey asides soften the cold the concept depends on.
- **C (5):** Nothing on the page but physics and training; the fall is
  the cold's own syllabus.
- **D (4):** Murder logistics in the cold are tense without magic, but
  the tension is human menace more than survival physics.

**The hook's promise (§1)**
- **A (4):** Perfect disguise as frontier survival-adventure, but the
  logline's other engines — the starving town, the love promised to the
  mine owner — are absent by design.
- **B (4):** Runs the romance engine from line one and arms the dock and
  death-news with dramatic irony, but delivers none of the disguise the
  hook calls the reason the book matters.
- **C (3):** Honors the survival-adventure promise instantly, but its
  compression leaves even less room than A for the town, the swindle,
  and Grace.
- **D (3):** Establishes the worldly stakes (the bank, the escape)
  earliest of the four, but pre-spends the "why was I pushed" question
  that §6 answers at the cove.

**The comp shelf (§10)**
- **A (4):** Total genre commitment is itself a surprise — Klaus-
  adjacent readers expect whimsy and get a frontier thriller — though a
  browsing reader gets no early handshake (which the jacket, per §7's
  note, is meant to supply).
- **B (2):** A lovelorn young man in the frozen north is exactly what
  the shelf already stocks; it surprises nobody.
- **C (5):** Nothing on the Santa-origin shelf opens with the hero
  thrown off a cliff in sentence one — maximal shelf surprise, in
  precisely the direction of the book's differentiation.
- **D (4):** Opening inside the murderers is genuinely shelf-strange —
  Klaus has a villain but never opens there — though it reads noir
  before it reads wonder.

**Series load-bearing (§6/§12)**
- **A (3):** Plants the Grins' escape argument and the redwoods thread,
  but carries no series-scale anchor for Volumes 2–4.
- **B (3):** The proposal frame powers Volume 1's (and Volume 3's) love
  story, and its downstream Duff kill-check is a genuine gift — but the
  frame itself plants nothing beyond Volume 1.
- **C (3):** Structurally A's equal, plus a falling-to-flying motif the
  volumes could rhyme on — but nothing explicit for the four-book
  fuse.
- **D (4):** Volume 4 is "the Grins return"; opening Volume 1 inside
  James builds exactly the villain equity that shape needs, and seeds
  the James confession that plugs the ch22 knowledge leak (THREADS
  T22).

## Winner: ENTRANT A — the v2 cold open (30/35)

The fresh pressure confirms the v2 opening rather than unseating it —
narrowly. A wins because it is the only entrant that scores top marks
on all three concepts the author has LOCKED (SC2's mirror at full
weight, SC3's craft shown not claimed, SC4's magicless tension) while
still hiding SC1's grains in plain sight; and because it is the lineage
every 2026 ruling, plant, and locked decision was built on, so it wins
on price as well as points — adopting any rival reopens decisions 7–10
and re-checks every downstream plant. Its real weaknesses are the two
the scoreboard shows: the hook's town/romance engines are off the page
(the critique's "nobody speaks" problem), and it carries no
series-scale anchor. Both are exactly what the grafts below repair.
Worth saying plainly: C at 29 is not a moral victory for the incumbent
— it is evidence that *opening airborne* is the strongest single idea
in the field, and A can steal it without surrendering its structure.

## The grafts (tournament rule: runners-up's best beats into the winner)

1. **From C — first runner-up, primary graft: one beat of flight
   inside the fall.** In A's final fall, give Nick a half-breath of
   terrible weightlessness rendered as flight-that-isn't — the body
   airborne above the river, the trained mind noting it — before the
   mountainside takes him. The climax's true flight then answers the
   book's first airborne second (SC2's mirror sharpened to a point),
   and it costs A three lines, not its structure. A plant, not a
   spend: the thunder stays whole.
2. **From B — the engagement needle, not the frame.** One line at the
   fire, in Nick's head, in the raised register: the rumor he cannot
   get a straight answer about — could Grace really be promised to
   Donald Grin? This puts Grace and the romance engine on page one and
   arms the dock misunderstanding, while preserving the snowflake's
   late proposal reveal (motive stays off the page; a rumor is not an
   intent). RECON delta 11's neighbor already lists the needle as
   canon-wanted — this graft has independent support.
3. **From D — the almost-warning.** In the James exchange, one gesture
   legible only in retrospect: James starts to say something, looks at
   the tent, and doesn't. It deepens the "sum being run behind his
   eyes," humanizes the dimmer Grin the four-volume shape needs alive
   and interesting, and quietly plants the Duff/James confession beat
   (THREADS T22, TODO).
