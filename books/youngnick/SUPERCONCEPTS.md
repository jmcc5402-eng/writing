# Super Concepts — Young Nicholas

The 3–4 concepts that make this book win. Not the plot — the reasons this
Santa origin and not another one. Every chapter must *value* them: not
necessarily incorporate, never cheapen. Editorial agents judge chapters
against this file.

Status: **PROPOSED** — extracted from `STORY_BIBLE.md` (§1, §7 decisions
7–8) and the 2026-07 critique/thread-map evidence. The author locks each.

---

## SC1 — The wink: Santa is never said

Every familiar tradition is *earned by the plot*, and the word is never
spoken. The reader assembles sleigh + reindeer + overnight gifts + the warp
and gets the delight of realizing it themselves. The wink is the difference
between a myth and a checklist.

**Chapter test:** does the chapter earn its tradition through story
pressure, or explain it? Does anything come within a wink of saying the
word?
**Today:** per `THREADS.md`, two traditions fully earned, three partial,
two owed — and the ones that work are exactly the ones plot *forces*
(anonymous giving because he must stay dead).

## SC2 — The keystone: a town wakes to its gold

The swindled town waking to find its stolen gold returned in the night IS
the first Christmas morning. One image carries the entire commercial
premise; cover, pitch, and ending all point at it.

**Chapter test:** does this chapter raise the debt, the despair, or the
secrecy that the keystone morning will pay?
**Today:** the book's biggest debt — the scene exists only as outline
pasted into ch20 and a summary in ch22. Writing it is the highest-leverage
act in the workspace.

## SC3 — Sainthood is earned, not inherited

An ordinary young man chooses, again and again, to do good quietly — and
takes no credit. The magic answers his choices, never his bloodline
(§7 decision 7: the found stone; recognition, not inheritance). This is
the theme with evergreen pull: anyone could be this Santa.

**Chapter test:** does anything here come to Nick by entitlement, luck, or
lineage that he should have chosen or earned?
**Today:** the manuscript already voted for this (no belt); the bible's §5
belt beats are the remaining contamination, flagged in T16.

## SC4 — Frontier-real: the magic obeys the cold

Survival physics ground the wonder — rope, rock, hunger, and weather stay
true even after the sleigh flies. The draft's own best move ("Why wouldn't
caribou increase the pulling power of a sleigh through the sky?") treats
magic as a practical problem. This is what separates it from soft Santa
books and earns the older all-ages register (§7 decision 8).

**Chapter test:** would this scene still be tense with the magic removed?
Does every magical beat cost something physical?
**Today:** the strongest concept on the page — the v2 chapters prove the
register; protect it from whimsy as the Santa elements accumulate.
