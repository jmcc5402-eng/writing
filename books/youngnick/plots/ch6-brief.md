## BEATS (8; plants and pays named — thread IDs per THREADS.md;
## beats 7–8 are the [TK-A] merge material)

1. **OPEN — the last of the digging, the last of the food.**
   Daylight seeping over the rock wall. Nick finishes freeing the
   sleigh and only afterward feels what it cost; he eats the last
   two strips of jerky and the arithmetic hits zero (v2 ch3's
   "short and unfriendly" sum, now paid out). SC4: hunger is the
   chapter's clock, and the reader — holding two town clocks Nick
   can't see — feels all three. CARRIES the bad shoulder (gate
   N10: it bills him for the digging).
2. **The sleigh in daylight (T17 COLLECTED).** First full look
   with sun on it: the dark red wood, the glow faint under
   daylight, the carvings — and his fingers find the empty oval
   socket again. One touch, one beat of the half-known shapes
   sliding away again (v2 l.840–848 recalled, not re-solved), and
   he lets it go: riddles don't burn calories. RECALL, not
   re-plant; no decoding (T16 guard). The practical inventory —
   hand brake, second lever, bench, cargo bed — reads like a
   workman assessing a tool (SC3).
3. **The survey and the question.** From the ledge: cliff to the
   river ahead, embankment right, the crest left — and past it the
   snowfield running a mile down to a forest. Forest means food
   and shelter; the creed's order holds (T09, arithmetic only).
   And once, aloud, the question the book is allowed to ask and
   not answer: who buries a sleigh inside a mountain? (S01's one
   asking — nothing answered, no theories indulged.)
4. **The snow votes.** He steps out and sinks to his waist.
   Unpacked powder, a mile of it, on no food — walking is a
   day-and-a-night he doesn't have. The sleigh stops being a
   riddle and becomes equipment: enough deck to ride the surface,
   a slope to work with, a brake to control it. The plan is craft
   the reader can check (SC3's test, shown not claimed).
5. **The run.** Push-off with a branch; the sleigh rides on top of
   the powder and takes speed; for a few hundred yards the day is
   actually good — the one warmth this chapter is allowed, kept
   short. One small father touch: the two-seater he learned on,
   the mine's big sleigh this one resembles (T09; body-silence
   guard absolute). Optional texture: an eagle working toward the
   forest (T26, paid motif, no new plant). ADVANCES Nick's plan in
   passing: reach the forest, then work toward town by night —
   they think he's dead, and that is his only advantage (T21,
   mined trunk l.609–623).
6. **The mountain calls the bluff.** Over the crest the slope
   steepens and the snow ahead shows a seam — a divot, a ravine,
   something with a far wall. Speed compounds. He pulls the hand
   brake: nothing — powder too loose to bite (SC4: the physics are
   the antagonist). Second yank, nothing. Two hundred yards.
7. **The jump is priced; the lever is spent.** He weighs jumping
   at this speed — rocks under the snow, the coat pulled tight,
   roll and hope — a real price, honestly computed (SC3/SC4; the
   no-luck rule's setup: he has chosen the jump and is turning to
   make it). One option left first: the second lever, the one
   whose grooves fit his hand. He pulls it with nothing to lose.
   Nothing happens — and then the friction goes quiet under him.
8. **CLOSE — "The sleigh was flying."** The hiss of the runners
   fades with the wind still full in his face; the nose bucks and
   throws him into the bench, head cracking wood (the cost lands
   with the wonder); he grips the lever, looks over the side, and
   the embankment's jagged rocks are forty feet DOWN. Not a
   bridge. Nothing under him at all. Land the chapter flat on the
   impossible fact — the trunk's own proven ender, cousin to "A
   red glowing sleigh." — with no trailer-voice, no reaching
   forward (banned move). PAYS the second-lever setup (beat 2);
   PLANTS nothing new: an airborne man who cannot land is the next
   chapter's inheritance, stated by his situation, not by the
   narrator.
